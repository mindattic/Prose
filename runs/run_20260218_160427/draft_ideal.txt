**SCENE**

The clinic smelled of ozone and old blood. My left eye’s tactical overlay painted the corridor in wireframe green. The stolen core’s signal pulsed behind a door marked ‘PHYSICAL THERAPY’. I moved. My synth-muscles made no sound. The katana’s hilt was cool against my palm.

A boy sat on a therapy table, legs dangling. He held the core—a silver spindle—against his chest. Wires snaked from his neck jack to a jury-rigged box on his lap. His eyes were wide, liquid with fear. He looked fourteen. Maybe fifteen.

“Don’t,” he whispered. His thumb hovered over a toggle on the box.

I stopped at the room’s threshold. My overlay identified the device. A dead-man switch. Standard extortion tech. I calculated the distance. I could cross it before his thumb completed its downward arc. The katana could separate his hand from his wrist. Clean. Certain.

“It’s not for me,” he said, the lie brittle. “My sister. She’s in the ward. They’ll repo her lungs.”

My audio filters caught the hum of life support. A chorus of it. Down the hall. The overlay mapped the signals. The boy’s box was linked to a local network. Not to a bomb. To the clinic’s power grid.

<IDEAL-LINE 1>
*A code is only real when it costs something.*

“The switch is tied to the ward,” I said. My voice was flat. A statement.

He flinched. Nodded. “You kill me, it cuts power. You try to take the core, I cut power.” He swallowed. “Just let me get her out. Then you can have it.”

The core’s owner paid me enough to retire. The boy’s sister was a statistic. A debt-holder’s problem. My finger traced the *tsuba* of the katana. The metal was smooth. Perfectly balanced. A tool for decisive cuts. Not for this.

<IDEAL-LINE 2>
*The blade’s purpose is to end threats, not to negotiate them.*

I heard a wet cough from the ward. A respirator’s steady sigh. The sensory motif of the hum deepened. It was the sound of borrowed time. I took one step inside. The boy’s thumb twitched. The hum stuttered for a microsecond. A warning. He was shaking.

“I will cut the signal,” I said. “You will live. But you will not walk again.”

He didn’t understand. Not yet.

<IDEAL-LINE 3>
*Clarity is a mercy. False hope is a slower cruelty.*

I moved. Not the full *iaijutsu* draw. A single, precise step. My left hand shot out, locked around his wrist. Bone and thin cable under my grip. He gasped. The core clattered to the floor. My right hand drew the katana. The *shing* of polished steel was very loud in the small room.

The blade was a line of cold light. I did not aim for him. I aimed for the thick data cable snaking from the box to the wall conduit. The cut had to be perfect. Sever the signal cascade without triggering the fail-safe. The katana was a scalpel. I was a surgeon.

<IDEAL-LINE 4>
*Mastery is the elimination of excess. In motion. In consequence.*

I cut.

The katana parted the cable. A spray of sparks like orange pollen. The box died with a soft pop. The boy screamed. A short, sharp sound. Then silence. He slumped back, eyes open. Unharmed. But the neural feedback from the severed line had burned through his motor-jack. A permanent short circuit. His legs were now architecture.

The hum of the life support never stopped. I had been precise. The ward’s patients lived.

I sheathed the katana. The click of the guard meeting the scabbard was final. I picked up the core. It was warm from his hands.

A nurse appeared in the doorway. Her eyes went from my blade to the boy. To his still, unresponsive legs. “What did you do?” Her voice was low. Accusing.

<IDEAL-LINE 5>
*Explanation is a form of apology. I do not apologize for necessary acts.*

I walked past her. The corridor air felt colder. As I passed the open door to the ward, I saw her. A girl, maybe ten, in a bed by the window. Clear tubes in her nose. Her small chest rose and fell in time with the respirator’s hum. She was looking at the ceiling. Alive. Her brother had saved her. For now.

He would never walk to her bedside.

<IDEAL-LINE 6>
*The consequence is the truth of the choice. Carry it, or be crushed by it.*

I exited the clinic into the acid-yellow dawn. The core was secure in my inner pocket. The payment would clear. My hand rested on the katana’s hilt. The weight of it was different now. It was not the weight of a weapon. It was the weight of the boy’s stare. The weight of a cut measured in decades, not millimeters.

The hum of the city replaced the clinic’s hum. The same sound. A different meaning. It was no longer the sound of life sustained. It was the sound of the machinery I served. The sound of a choice, made. Irrevocable.

<IDEAL-LINE 7>
*Dignity is not in the act, but in bearing its full cost without looking away.*

I did not look back.

**MOMENTS OF RESTRAINT THAT COST**
- Not killing the boy immediately, which introduced risk and moral complication.
- Not attempting to negotiate or deceive him, which forfeited a potentially easier path.
- Using the katana for a precision disable instead of a lethal strike, which required immense skill and focus but guaranteed a living victim to witness the result.
- Offering no comfort or explanation to the nurse, absorbing her hatred to expedite the closure of the incident.
- Denying himself the narrative of "saving the ward," by immediately acknowledging the specific, permanent harm he chose to inflict instead.

**SENSORY MOTIF: THE HUM**
1. **First:** The hum of life support is a neutral backdrop, a sensory detail of the clinic's environment.
2. **Second:** The hum stutters as the boy threatens it, transforming into an active threat—the sound of innocent lives in the balance.
3. **Third:** Leaving the clinic, the hum of the city replaces it. The sound is now the echo of the choice—the perpetual, indifferent machinery of the world where such calculations are necessary.
