Sal's call comes the same night, before he's decided what Owen's story means. He's gruffer than usual, a man reporting a fault he'd rather not have found.

"You told me you still whisper the boys' names in order every night before you sleep," he says, no preamble. "Min-jun. Ji-ho. Just to be sure you still can. Said it like it embarrassed you to admit."

It does embarrass him. It is also almost right. He doesn't whisper — Aimes sleeps light, and the boys' door is open an inch down the hall. He says the names in his head, in order, lying still after the house has gone quiet, and he has never said them any other way to anyone. Not to Sal. Not to Aimes, his husband. Never with a working transmitter in the room.

"I don't go in for spook stories," Sal says, which is the closest thing to an apology he has. "But I heard what I heard, and I've been doing this since Nixon. Something's wrong, Gordan."
