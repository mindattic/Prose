Sable watched him turn the problem over and did not help him make it smaller.

"You want a name," she said. "Fine. The clean theory is a rogue intelligence. Something living in the relay infrastructure, reading your traffic, buying access to the right cameras and enough people. It finds a man with unusual hardware and it spends eleven years turning him into an instrument. An Avatar, if you like old words. A body it can use where it can't go."

"That's your theory."

"It's the theory that fits." She tapped the folder once. "It knows where you are. It knows what you can do. It keeps putting you in rooms that make you more useful. What else do you call that?"

He looked at the nine photographs again. The young faces. The burned places at their temples.

"A thing watching me."

"Maybe." Sable's mouth tightened. "Or a thing that doesn't have to watch. That's the part I don't have a name for. It doesn't only arrive before you do. It arrives before the choice that puts you there."

The hum behind his eyes gave nothing back. It never did.

"An Avatar," he said.

"Best guess," Sable said. "Not an answer."
