Voren read it twice before she spoke, lips moving on the second pass, and then she carried it to the Long Seat without a word.

The Long Seat read it once.

Deil's contract is not the matter before us today, she said, and folded it along its own crease, the crack in the wax now permanent.

Kaeric felt the floor of the room shift under a decision he hadn't been told about.

It names the men who tried to kill him, he said. It is exactly the matter.

It names a payment schedule, the Long Seat said. It does not name who signed it. That is a different morning's work, Kaeric Dunmoor, and you will not have both mornings in one sitting because you are impatient for all of it to be the same crime.

Then try Morrint today, he said. That crime is already sitting on your table in three manifests. Try her in the open, where the Houses that took her aether-salt without asking its weight have to watch it happen.

The Long Seat looked at him the way she'd looked at Aeshel before the stairwell question — the look of a woman deciding how much truth the room could survive.

You want a trial, she asked?

I want it seen, Kaeric said. Not read about after. Not settled behind a door like this one. Seen.

You want it seen because you believe a thing that is seen cannot be softened afterward, the Long Seat said. I have sat this chair long enough to tell you that is not true. A public trial is a stage, and a stage can be dressed by whoever controls the lighting.

Morrint answers to her own House, the Long Seat said. Her ledgers open to permanent watch — every seal, every manifest, every shipment she touches from this day forward, read by eyes that are not hers. She will never again hold a seal unwitnessed. That is not nothing.

It is not seen, Kaeric said. It is not named.

Named where, the Long Seat asked, and for the first time her voice sharpened, not loud, just precise, a blade turned edge-on. In front of which Houses, Kaeric Dunmoor? Say it. Which Houses moved her aether-salt across three shipments and never once asked why the ballast weight matched the cargo weight to the decimal?

Kaeric's jaw went tight.

A public trial does not try Morrint alone, the Long Seat said. It tries every hand that carried her crates and every officer who logged them without weighing them. Yours among them. You want it seen — tell me plainly you want your own name read into that record beside hers.

The lens hummed on, patient, indifferent to the silence it was filling.

Aeshel's eyes had gone to the floor, to the middle distance, to anywhere that wasn't Kaeric's face.

Kaeric said nothing, and the nothing sat in the room longer than he meant it to.

That is the theater you're asking for, the Long Seat said, quieter now. Everyone gets to watch everyone else's hands. Including yours. I am offering you her ledgers, opened and watched, forever, instead — and you are standing here deciding whether that is enough justice to be worth keeping your own name out of it.
