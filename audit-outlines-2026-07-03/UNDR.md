# Structural Audit — Underclan (UNDR) {#SS-UNDR-AUDIT-2026-07-03}

## Outline

**Ch01 — The Breath of the Deep**
1. Glim's morning air-check, candle-count, routine of the Hollow established.
2. Glim/Sorrel harvest scene; Sorrel warns him the Surfacing will show "what stays"; the unnamed "third" (a Brave who never came back) is referenced but not named.
3. Crossing with the Oarsman; Glim forgets to have his Fare ready — first time the arm has ever barred him; establishes the Oarsman needs the Fare, not just tolerates it.
4. The hum explained via memory of First Hearing / Knuckle's face when Glim was small; Glim stops at the jetty edge, unsettled about the coming Surfacing.
5. Fungus-harvest; Lark is coughing and hiding it; Glim tells her to report to Knuckle — she doesn't commit either way.

**Ch02 — The One Word**
1. Teaching-chamber introduced; Vesh corrects Glim's candle placement without looking.
2. Vesh's core theology lesson ("the surface forgets, the Deep remembers"); a false candle-alarm mid-lesson that Glim handles by reflex; Lark shaken that she "didn't feel it."
3. Sorrel asks about the One Word/broadcast; Vesh leaves the theological question open (never confirms the Current hears them); private aside — Vesh reframes "surface-stain" as "origin," tells Glim the Surfacing exists to let him return to the dark "in a new way."

**Ch03 — The Fare**
1. Knuckle tells Glim the origin story: found him crying silently at a pump junction at the Skin-border twelve years ago; the hand-on-head gesture is now understood as inherited memory.
2. That night, fragmented sense-memories surface: a huge noise, a hand's grip going from certain to tight to gone, an unnamed smell. Never a face.
3. A crossing years earlier: candle-light catches an object in the Oarsman's Fare-basin — a photo/likeness ("small and pale, large dark eyes") that Glim says "looked like the face in the fragments." He tells no one. Carried four years, unresolved.

**Ch04 — The Surfacing Called**
1. Knuckle moves the Surfacing up three cycles specifically to get Glim gone before Grale can build a case; confirms Glim is "not surface-stained," just precocious.
2. Sorrel warns him precisely what the light will do to his eyes and body.
3. Knuckle's ritual blessing (grip sequence); "keep moving, you can always keep moving."
4. Knuckle tells the "three outcomes" teaching with Grale silently present; Grale's reaction is unreadable — "a man who wanted to be wrong and wasn't sure yet if he was."

**Ch05 — Surface-Stained**
1. Grale confronts Glim directly at pack-check: "I've watched you my whole life and I've watched what stays."
2. The climb: heat, unfamiliar air-complexity, approaching pressure of surface noise.
3. At the final junction, Glim chooses the unfamiliar/riskier left route over the known safe one, specifically to test whether he can act without calculation — reaches the grating; sees a fixed white-blue light for the first time (light that burns vs. light that lives).

**Ch06 — The Lid Off the World**
1. Surface sensory overload: undirected noise, no ceiling, ambient omni-directional light; hum thins to near-nothing but is still present; he chooses not to retreat.
2. The Burning Eye rises; total whiteout, described as "indistinguishable from blindness."
3. Recovery; realization that the surface's indifference (not malice) is what "up there you are no one" means; he reaches the grating and chooses to go back down.
4. One level down he is caught by floodlights and Lamplighters — his dark-adapted advantage is erased by total light; captured at the last possible moment.

**Ch07 — The Smell Before the Face**
1. Candle confiscated and left to die from mishandling; taken up through the Skin in a vehicle by hunters who treat the catch as routine sport.
2. Handed to a second, bureaucratic set of surface people; sensory disorientation of manufactured light/no shadow, no hum; he withholds information by silence.
3. Reunion: Noor in the doorway — recognition by smell before sight; both cry involuntarily.
4. Noor's caretaking begins (clothes, food, water); he refuses her spoken name for him; learns "eat/come/sleep" by context; will not give his tapped name.
5. [Marked Noor POV] Noor watches him eat in his ritualized order; her body remembers the shape of his three-year-old grip; she resists reaching for him because he flinches; the surface media has already started running a "Children Beneath GLMZ" story.

**Ch08 — A Name He Will Not Answer**
1. Days in Noor's dwelling; he catalogues surface objects/customs without judgment; taps his name on the windowsill — she can't hear it, correctly.
2. Day four: a device shows a police-sketch-style image of his face and drone/crawler footage of tunnel architecture — "someone had written down the way down." He hides that he understands this is happening and begins planning his return.
3. Officials visit and question him ("Where did you come from," "Are there more of you"); he stonewalls; catalogues their faces and light-sources the way a Brave catalogues new territory.
4. Night six: Noor tries her name for him again, then offers her own name in exchange; he responds by tapping his real name on the floor instead of speaking the surface word — a given gift, not an answer.

**Ch09 — The Way Down**
1. Night eight: his candle has died from surface mishandling and neglect; feeling he can no longer maintain Brave-discipline in the surface's "wide, unfocused" attention mode, he escapes through a window and returns to the grating.
2. Noor follows him down into the Works, unheard until three levels in; he delivers (in Underclan speech she can't understand) the rule that going down is a choice with no way to unmake it; she doesn't turn back.
3. He leaves her at the Works-level, tells her (in broken surface speech) "I watch. You stay. Safe," and leads her the rest of the way to Homewater in the dark, holding her hand.
4/5. [Duplicate beat, same content twice — see Finding 9] They arrive; the hum affects him, not her; he reflects that this is what the surface felt like to him, and he is "still here."

**Ch10 — Light Comes Down**
1. Return to the Hollow is met with calculated silence, not welcome; Grale immediately brands him "Surface-tainted" as a verdict, not a taunt; Sorrel escorts Noor in.
2. Third day: Lamplighters raid the Hollow directly, from two junctions simultaneously, total whiteout of the entire community (not just Glim); a candle is confiscated and goes silent; the clan retreat-chains into the Old Deep.
3. Lark's cough worsens and is now explicitly framed as the price of contact; Sorrel confirms these latest surface visitors are not Lamplighters but "the Mission," offering medicine and "help."
4. Vesh refuses surface medicine outright; Glim publicly contradicts him for the first time in his life; Vesh concedes only to "ask the Mission what they want in return."

**Ch11 — When the Candles Sicken**
1. The Mission's price is registration — being entered into the surface's count; Vesh names the real fear: a hand "sure it is feeding you" is unbeatable, unlike a hunter.
2. Slip (age ~7) contracts the fever badly; Noor identifies it as urgent and requests the Mission's medicine be brought immediately.
3. Slip dies before the medicine helps him; ritual sending-off; his candle set adrift on the water. Lamplighters raid again mid-ritual, forcing the clan to retreat past the Old Deep into the never-before-entered Tartarian Empire, because there is nowhere else to flee.
4. Noor is swept into the retreat chain by accident (Glim grabbed her hand in the whiteout without knowing whose it was); she keeps pace and doesn't panic, which surprises him.

**Ch12 — The Ghost-Country**

2. Vesh reveals (implicitly, by omission and by Glim reading his face) that he has been dying of the Fever throughout the flight; he gives Glim his bone teaching-marker as a death-gift.
3. Glim goes alone into a deeper hall and feels a single, unrepeated low pulse distinct from the hum — held privately, "not explained, not dismissed."

**Ch13 — The Second Word**

3. Full document handoff; Dross agrees to help in exchange for future advocacy on the covenant; alliance sealed.

**Ch14 — A New Fare**

## Structural Findings

1. **The bible's central mystery engine — the Tartarian navigation maze — is planted nowhere and never dramatized (Ch10 beat 2 → Ch11 beat 3 → Ch12 beat 1).** §2 and §5 of the bible require this to be "the climax's first antagonist," explicitly planted early: "a Brave who went as deep as possible and came back wrong about where they had been; a fragment of carved stone with no mapped source; the Engine Guild's oldest member who says there is a depth past which the tunnels stop making sense." None of these plants appear in Ch1–9. The flight beat itself (spine #11, "the route Glim swore he knew... does not hold. Wrong junctions. Walls where memory placed passage") is entirely absent from the executed Ch10–11 beats — the clan simply walks from Homewater through the Old Deep into "strata the Underclan had never entered" without incident, confusion, or backtracking. The Tartar is arrived at, not earned. This converts the bible's single most distinctive worldbuilding device (the LOCKED, unresolvable navigation mystery, SS-A39) into an unused asset — the story never uses the tool it was built around.

2. **The reunion-identification mechanism is skipped (Ch07 beats 2–3).** The story jumps from anonymous bureaucratic interrogation of a feral, silent boy straight to "a woman in the doorway" with zero on-page explanation of how the authorities matched this specific catch to Noor's twelve-year-old missing-person case. The bible (§4) specifies Noor "knows him by a scar and a cowlick before she dares believe it" — that recognition scene never appears; recognition in the prose is Glim's (by smell), not Noor's, and nothing shows how *she* was found or convinced to come. This is a load-bearing procedural gap: a woman does not get produced at a feral boy's holding cell door without a chain of causation, and the story has none.

3. **The Ch03 "face in the basin" plant is set up and then abandoned.** Beat 4782 has Glim glimpse an object in the Oarsman's Fare-hoard with "a face... small and pale, with large dark eyes," which he explicitly links to "the face in the fragments" — even though the immediately preceding beat (4781) states flatly that his memory-fragments include "not the face" at all. That's an internal contradiction on its own (a face that doesn't exist is matched to a face he sees), and the object itself — almost certainly Toby's original surrendered "shine," per bible §4/§5, which explicitly says this artifact should pay off "when it is the one thing that can prove who he was" — is never mentioned again. It doesn't surface at the reunion, doesn't factor into Noor's identification of him, and doesn't return at the ending's final Fare (he pays with a *new* surface card Noor gives him, not the original shine). A four-years-carried secret with a bible-mandated payoff path simply evaporates.

4. **The shine's physical placement is also a logic problem, if it's meant to be the original artifact.** The bible states every crossing requires a unique Fare and the hoard accumulates layer on layer ("the more recent offerings layered toward the top"). Given twice-daily crossings by dozens of Underclan members for twelve years, an item surrendered when Glim was four should be buried under thousands of subsequent offerings, not merely "three offerings newer" than the cap in front of it (beat 4780/4782 territory — recheck exact beat 4782). As written, either the object is not the original shine (undermining the intended plant) or the hoard's stated depth-accumulation logic is violated.

5. **Lark's cough is established twice, inconsistently, before and after the disease's stated cause.** Ch01 beat 5 (4827) already shows Lark hiding a cough and Glim pressing her to report it to Knuckle — before Glim has even left for the Surfacing, i.e., before any surface contact has occurred. Lock #2 states the Bright Fever is *only* immunological naivety meeting surface microbes carried down by contact. The Ch10 "first candle sickens, first Brave coughs" beat (spine #10) reuses "Lark is coughing" as the inciting proof of the surface-borne Fever, with no acknowledgment that Lark was already coughing chapters earlier for an unexplained, pre-contact reason. Either this is an accidental continuity slip (the Fever set-up was pattern-matched onto an existing character detail without checking it predates the vector) or it is an intended irony that is never surfaced/explained in the text. As written it reads as a contradiction, not a choice.

6. **A named casualty ("Leaf") is introduced only in the beat that reports her death.** Ch14 beat 1 (4823) lists "Leaf, in the Hollow, when the flood-lights first hit" among the dead. No character named Leaf appears anywhere in Ch1–13. Killing a character the reader has never met produces zero felt weight and undercuts the "All Is Lost" toll the spine calls for (#11: "Someone loved is taken") — the only losses that land emotionally are Slip (built up over three chapters) and Vesh (built up over the whole book); Leaf and "a third whose name he repeated to himself once" are administrative footnotes to a casualty count, not story.

7. **Sorrel's bible-mandated fork is executed as neither option, and her arc goes inert.** §4 explicitly frames Sorrel as carrying unresolved narrative weight: "her body, later, is where the cost becomes unbearable — or she is the one who refuses to flee the Tartar. (Hold which.)" The executed story does neither. She has a late supportive scene in Ch10 (beat 4806) and is briefly present in Ch12 (organizing food stocks) — then disappears from the text entirely. The "hold which" instruction was a promise that one of two costly things would happen to the story's most important peer character; the book delivers a third, unplanned option: nothing. This flattens the emotional stakes of Act 3 and leaves a major character introduced as "the heart" of the piece with no third-act function at all.

8. **Corwin Sallow, the named antagonist, never appears — the "appetite" rung of the ladder is depersonalized.** §4 is explicit that Sallow "collects... keeps trophies... is the surface's appetite with a face." In the executed chapters the Lamplighters are always an anonymous "voice," "footsteps," "a man's voice, level and deliberate" (Ch10 beat 2) — never individuated, named, or given a personal motive or a trophy-collecting beat on-page. The antagonist ladder's rung 3 is functionally interchangeable with rung 5 ("the surface as a whole") because no character carries it. This is a flat-antagonist finding: the bible asks for a face; the book delivers a procedure.

9. **The Daylight Mission's "mercy as catastrophe" — bible's explicitly required "true antagonist... the more devastating of the two" — never does anything devastating on-page.** Its entire on-page presence is: offering medicine (which helps, and which Vesh/Glim voluntarily request), mapping tunnels (a stated fear, never enacted as an on-page raid or forced registration), and one generic "social worker" who leaves a pamphlet (Ch07 beat 5, Noor POV). No registration is forced, no child is taken, no culture-dissolving event occurs attributable to the Mission specifically — the actual cultural/biological damage (Slip's death, Vesh's death, Homewater ransacked) is caused by the Lamplighters' floodlight raids and the disease vector, not by the Mission's "sincere kindness." As executed, the Mission is all threat and no consequence, while the bible requires it to out-damage the hunters. Rung 4 of the antagonist ladder is unbuilt.

10. **Grale's arc skips the required "curdle."** §4 specifies Grale's rightness, once vindicated, should "curdle into something worse... a true believer who would burn the village to save it." In execution, Grale goes directly from cold vindication ("Surface-tainted," Ch10 beat 1) to unqualified heroic self-sacrifice (Ch13 beat 4) with no intervening beat where his correctness turns into actual harmful action (advocating exile of Glim/Noor, sabotaging the alliance, endangering someone to enforce clan purity). The bible asked for an antagonist who is dangerous *because* he's right; the book delivers a rival who is right and then simply dies well. It's a moving death, but it isn't the antagonist the bible designed.

11. **A structural duplication artifact: Ch09 contains the same beat twice verbatim** (database rows 4913 and 4914, "Homewater smelled right..." — near-identical text, minor copyediting differences only). This is a production/pipeline defect (a beat generated twice into the same chapter), not a craft issue, but it currently sits in the live text and pads the chapter with a repeated scene.

12. **Population-scale mismatch undercuts the stakes of the climax.** Bible §3 states the Underclan numbers "perhaps a few hundred across Homewater." The executed prose treats "the Hollow" (Glim's community) as the entire tribe, sized at 42 people before losses and 38–39 after (Ch10 beat 2; Ch14 beat 1) — with no reference anywhere to other Hollows, a wider Underclan political structure, or the broader few-hundred population the canon describes. If 42 people is meant to be *one Hollow among several*, the book never says so, and the ending ("the clan survives, fewer and changed," presented as an existential near-miss for the whole people) reads as though this Hollow *is* the Underclan — which contradicts the population canon, or at minimum leaves the climax's stakes ambiguous (is this 10% of the tribe lost, or a near-extinction event?).

13. **Canon term violation: "a Rider's crawler" (Ch08 beat 2, 4798).** Per project canon (SS-A38), "Rider" as a job/role term was retired; "Exo" (street) / "RFO" (corpo formal) / "Splicer" are current, with "Jockey" as the only surviving slang alternative. The prose's use of "a Rider's crawler... the Rider's own body waited somewhere above" directly reintroduces retired terminology into new canon prose.

14. **The bible's promised SRZR/Halcyon cross-pollination is set up and then unused.** §2 explicitly stages a specific payoff: "Halcyon Combine and OBERON are drilling... Glim... is able to translate this disruption for surface operatives who have the sense to listen. This is the world-truth that enables the SRZR cross-pollination." Ch12's ambiguous "pulse" beneath the Tartar (beat 4814) is the only candidate for this, but the text never has Glim act on it, tell anyone, or connect it to any operative — the promised cross-strand mechanism is entirely inert within this book.

15. **The closing Fare's "mother" line reads as a continuity slip, not intentional ambiguity (Ch14 beat 4, 4826).** "He thought of his mother's hands... He didn't know if she had gone down into the Underclan willingly or by accident or in fear. He didn't know if she had taken a Fare." By this point Glim has spent an entire book establishing, in explicit detail, that Noor (his mother) never went "down into the Underclan" as a lost child — she followed *him* down as an adult eight days after the reunion, an event the reader watched happen on-page (Ch09). The line as written only parses if "mother" here means some other, unestablished woman (an ancestral Underclan founder?), but nothing else in the text supports that reading. It most likely conflates Glim's birth-origin mystery with Noor's now-resolved arrival, which is a factual contradiction with the book's own preceding chapters.

## Verdict

**Not structurally capable of 90+ as written.** The prose execution (voice discipline, sensory grounding, the DEEP register) is strong and is not the ceiling here — the ceiling is that several of the bible's own load-bearing structural commitments were dropped in the beat-to-beat execution, and two of the four escalating antagonists on the ladder never actually threaten anyone. A reviewer working chapter-by-chapter will feel competent craft but will hit real "wait, what happened to—" moments that cap the score well below 90 regardless of sentence-level polish.

The four structural changes that would matter, in priority order:

1. **Plant and pay off the Tartarian navigation maze.** Add the required Act 1–2 setups (a Brave's contradictory account of the deepest descent; an unsourced carved-stone fragment already in Underclan hands; an Engine Guild elder's testimony that depth "stops making sense" past a point) and rewrite the Ch10–11 flight so the clan's route into the Tartar actually fails and reroutes at least once before they arrive. This is the bible's single most distinctive device and it currently does zero narrative work.

2. **Rebuild the antagonist ladder's rungs 3 and 4 so each does damage on-page.** Give Sallow (or a clearly delineated Lamplighter figure) one identifying, personal beat — a trophy taken, a name spoken, a collector's satisfaction shown, not just "a voice." Give the Daylight Mission an actual coercive or culture-dissolving act (an attempted forced registration, a child nearly taken, a real ultimatum enacted rather than merely feared) so it outweighs the hunters as the bible demands, instead of letting the disease (an accident, not a Mission action) carry that antagonist's assigned weight.

3. **Close or cut the two abandoned plants and the identification gap.** Either pay off the Ch03 basin-object (it proves who he is, it appears at the reunion, or Noor produces it) or remove the setup entirely; add the missing scene(s) showing how Noor was actually matched to the boy (scar/cowlick recognition per the bible, a DNA/records process, something causal) instead of jumping straight to her appearance in the doorway; fix the Lark cough continuity so the Fever's first symptom isn't reusing an already-established, pre-contact detail without comment.

4. **Give Sorrel and the casualty list their promised weight.** Execute one branch of the bible's explicit "hold which" fork for Sorrel rather than neither; and either introduce "Leaf" (or whichever secondary casualty) with at least one prior on-page appearance, or replace the death with one of the already-established named Braves so the All-Is-Lost toll is felt rather than reported.

Fixing 1 and 2 alone would likely move this from "well-written but structurally thin at the climax" into 90+ range; 3 and 4 are what would make the second and third reads hold up.
