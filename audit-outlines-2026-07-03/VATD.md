## Structural Findings

1. **The reinstall — the story's own stated capstone beat — is never dramatized on the page in the enabled cut.** Bible §0 calls this "It ends with a reinstall. Not a speech... Explanation to the daughter: 'Change of heart.' Triple joke, never underlined," and Lock #5 says "The reinstall is a procedure, not a speech." But the actual procedure scene — Daria Holme opening the door again, the 26-minute operation, her line "You came back," and Levin's on-page delivery of "Change of heart" — lives entirely in **beat 3915, which is soft-deleted (`IsEnabled=0`)**. The enabled sequence only contains the *decision* to do it (4912: "There's something still on the shelf") and a closing beat (3885) that treats it as already accomplished in a single elided tense-shift, with zero on-page depiction. As currently published, the payoff the entire bible is built around is *told*, not shown — the opposite of the story's own explicit design intent. This is the single biggest gap between what the bible promises and what the enabled text delivers.

2. **Timeline contradiction: Ekow says "Three nights I have been finding that out" (beat 4859), but every other clock reference in the story compresses the entire crisis into roughly one night plus the following dawn.** The loaner clock counts down from 72:00:00 at the shooting to 48:00:00 at the transplant to the wedding "tomorrow" (established same-night, beat 3873) — a span of well under 24 hours. Bible's own framing ("spend 18 hours cleaning it up") confirms this. Ekow's "three nights" line is either a leftover from an earlier draft timeline or an unflagged exaggeration, and it directly undercuts the ticking-clock structure that is this story's entire mechanical spine. Small line, load-bearing contradiction — a reader doing clock math (which the text itself repeatedly invites, via the recurring "71:48:09" / "63 hours" / "58 hours" / "48 hours" callouts) will notice.

3. **The Casimir/Ekow name discrepancy the bible flags as unresolved ("resolve in back-check") is still unresolved in the prose, just disguised as intentional ambiguity.** The entity record and memory call him "Ekow Ato (Ghanaian NGRA vet)"; the bible's character section still headers him "Casimir Mwamba (Congolese)." The prose cleverly never states his birth name outright — it plants a Fante day-name clue ("A man born on a Tuesday, to a woman who named him for the ocean," beat 3916) that points at "Ekow" without confirming it, and has him refuse to correct the Lotus woman who calls him "Casimir." This reads as a deliberate "collar-name vs. true name" device, but it is papering over a genuine, still-open canon identity conflict rather than resolving it. If a future continuity pass takes the prose's ambiguity as the resolution, it will canonize a name ("Casimir") that the entity table disputes.

4. **Ekow's turn (the confrontation, beat 4859) is a single-scene flip.** The "leashed dog / collar" resentment is seeded early (beat 3916) and restated almost verbatim as the persuasion argument in 4859 — the same metaphor recycled rather than a build of escalating grievance across his hunting beats (3917, 3918 show pure competence, no wavering). The bible itself flags this as the risk to watch ("must RING TRUE — delicate balance... no cartoon luck"). It doesn't tip into parody, but the turn rests on one conversation and one repeated metaphor rather than a mounting pattern, which is thinner ground than the bible's own anxiety about this scene suggests it wanted.

5. **The shooting's cause is double-stacked improbability.** Lock #3 requires Levin to fail so Tomas fires; the text gives him *two* independent reasons in the same sentence — Tears-slowed reflex **and** "the holster's retention caught the way it had never once caught" (beat 3872). The drug-lag alone is sufficient and character-caused; the added freak mechanical failure is unnecessary insurance that reads as the author thumbing the scale to guarantee the plot turn, on top of a already-locked, already-earned cause.

6. **The fiancée's discovery — the single image the back-half redesign was built around ("at dawn the fiancée discovers Han Do-yun in bed with a dead girl")** — is never shown. It happens off-page, relayed only through Ekow's remote perception of "a scream" and a Lotus doctor going up (beat 3919). Defensible as noir restraint, but it means the scene the §5c redesign singles out as the mechanism that "sets off Lotus-on-Lotus conflict" is inferred by the reader through a third character's guess, not witnessed or confirmed by anyone in the story who could actually verify it. Combined with Finding 1, this means *both* of the story's structural payoffs (the reinstall, the discovery) are elided rather than dramatized.

7. **IdentiScan's reporting path is never specified, and the gap is load-bearing.** The system identifies Do-yun as Lotus-affiliated fatally fast (Lock #2), on a Carrion-run subroutine — but Carrion HQ never reacts to what should be an extreme corporate liability event (an employee crew shooting a Syndicate heir on a routine collection). The story implicitly treats IdentiScan's output as private to the field agent's HUD, never logged upstream, which is *never stated on the page* and is in tension with later references to auditors flagging discrepancies (4912/3885 — "Carrion's going to flag it," implying the company *does* audit after the fact). If IdentiScan results are auditable after the fact, the absence of any real-time corporate response the night of the shooting is an unexplained gap in the story's own surveillance logic.

8. **Legacy draft contamination is a live risk, not just a hygiene footnote.** 21 of 49 beats in the table are soft-deleted alternates (an entire "Orlan Bek / Devorah Bek" second-shooting subplot, a "Solange" witness-blackmail thread, a duplicate Adaya Bernstein pacemaker scene, and a disabled beat in which Levin has his own financed-kidney backstory that **directly violates** the character lock "NO financed-kidney past — that backstory is struck"). None of this is visible to a reader of the published book, but it sits in the same table any automated review, continuity check, or future editor could pull from without filtering `IsEnabled`. This exact task's own instructions did not filter it.

## Verdict

The enabled, published cut of VATD is close to structurally sound at 90+ — the mechanism (Scarab/loaner/IdentiScan), the character locks (no-flinch Levin, Tomas fires, Bram Osei irony, no-scar transplant, Ekow's full arc) are all honored on the page where I checked them, and the Maisy sequence lands its coincidence-as-owned-tragedy the way the bible intends. But two of the story's own headline payoffs — the reinstall ("Change of heart") and the fiancée's discovery — are currently *told*, not *shown*, in the live beat sequence, which is a real gap against the bible's explicit design ("Not a speech... a procedure"). Given how much weight the bible places on the reinstall specifically as the story's closing joke, this is not a minor prose issue; it's a missing beat.
