## Structural Findings

1. **Hard canon-lock violation: the camel-man is named "Devereux" and enters Act 1.** Beat 4875
   sits (by `SortKey`) immediately *before* beat 4419 — i.e., before the bible's designated Act‑1
   closer even runs. It shows the man on the camel arriving at the End of the Line as an ordinary
   traveler who speaks a plain line of dialogue and is registered as unremarkable. Beat 4419's
   Text then opens: *"The man on the camel turned out to be named Devereux, and he was heading to
   the End of the Line because there was nowhere else to go, which he said without apparent
   irony, and she did not offer any, and they went in together because the door was the door and
   there was only one of it."* This breaks three separate, explicitly permanent bible rules at
   once: (a) §11 — the entity's identity is "canon-locked as unknown... never to be confirmed
   in-world or authorially"; naming him confirms one. (b) §2 — "He is not moved into Act 1 —
   keeping him out of the first 10 chapters is load-bearing... so that his Act-2 resolution out of
   the hum lands as the first time the signal ever had a face"; he is now in Act 1, before his own
   designated reveal. (c) The entity's whole register (collective it/they/we, no linear time,
   ambiguous even whether "real") is flattened into a mortal who "said [it] without apparent
   irony" and walks through a literal door — undercutting the uncanny mechanic the rest of the
   book (4420–4423, 4772) is built on.

2. **The camel is explained two incompatible ways in adjacent beats.** Beat 4420: "There are no
   camels in Joliet. There had never been camels in Joliet... Her body understands the wrongness
   a half-second before she does" — impossible, uncanny. Beat 4875, one beat earlier by
   `SortKey`: "Camels were a Corridor animal, pack-transit for the Gray Zone operators who moved
   bulk cargo between GLMZ and Crosspoint... A camel at the dead end of a stripped freight line in
   Joliet was two hundred kilometers off its natural route" — a mundane logistics explanation
   (off-route, not impossible). Read in sequence, the reader gets the naturalizing explanation
   first, which defuses the "there are no camels in Joliet" gut-punch before it happens.

3. **The retired "Sigma" origin is still live in drafted prose.** The bible states outright
   (§1, §6 lock 8): "The Sigma-program backstory... is demoted... re-homed to a gray-zone causal
   origin... The SS-A20 bible canon wins over any legacy DB migration." Yet beat 4421 (Text):
   *"Sigma gave her a lot of things. It didn't give her a word for this."* and beat 4423 (Text):
   *"She had spent eleven years making sure she was never in the same room as a Sigma
   installation. The towers were full of them."* Both beats are marked DRAFTED, not TO WRITE, and
   both directly reinstate a decade-plus institutional origin the bible retired by name.

4. **An unexplained clinical-management backstory ("Dr. Ferreira") contradicts "never
   provisioned."** Bible: her neuretics "were never provisioned / locked to a band... unlike
   everyone else's, hers never closed" — nobody ever managed them; it's congenital neglect, not
   medical failure. But newly-drafted beats 4870, 4872, and 4876 describe an active, decade-long
   treatment history: "the ferromagnetic drift was managed, was managed, was pharmaceutical and
   controlled and Dr. Ferreira had said—" (4870); "*Ferreira said drift was managed. Ferreira
   said ten years was early...*" (4872); "she knew hot, she'd managed hot for a year and a half
   on a pharmaceutical regimen that cost her most of what she cleared on Ferreira" (4876). A named
   clinician and a "pharmaceutical ceiling" is the exact origin story SS-A20 was written to kill,
   and it's present in the most recently drafted material — evidence the drafting pass did not
   check current canon.

5. **The dog vanishes without explanation; a cat replaces it with no handoff.** Beats 4860–4869
   (all ten Act 1 forge beats) build a specific companion-animal relationship: a gray dog joins
   her outside Kankakee, survives the roadside ambush, gets "he's clean" into the Joliet
   bunkhouse, and is "at her feet" in the bar (4419). The very next beat (4420) introduces "a gray
   cat" with zero reference to the dog, and the cat is the sole animal companion for the rest of
   the book (4421–4776). Ten chapters of investment (named reactions from Fen, Oshun, "the
   Professor") evaporate with no death, loss, or acknowledgment on the page.

6. **The most-developed antagonist thread in Act 1 has no payoff.** Five of the ten new Act-1
   beats (4861, 4862, 4866, 4867, 4868) build "the standing order" — a self-funding bounty
   network that upgrades its price and its hunters as the act progresses. It is the single
   largest piece of manufactured tension in Act 1, larger on the page than the hum itself. It is
   never mentioned again after 4868. Nothing in Act 2 or Act 3 (4767–4776 plus the new connective
   beats) references the order, the documentation man's network, or a hunter reaching the city.
   The bible's own §10 item 3 predicted exactly this risk and recommended letting it "die" — but
   even a deliberate death needs one line on the page marking the door closed (e.g., gray-zone
   bounties don't cross into corpo-registered city territory). As drafted, it's simply dropped.

7. **Two unrelated "four people close in on her" set pieces; one is never resolved.** Beat 4872
   ends: "the service lane was full of people who had not been there when she walked through the
   market. Four of them... looking at her the way you look at something you have been sent to
   collect" — and nothing afterward (4769, 4770, or later) explains how she got clear. Beat 4774
   ("Found") independently stages a near-identical "four people converge" set piece later, which
   *is* resolved (she fights free, is shot, takes their gear). Having two "cornered by exactly
   four" scenes, one abandoned mid-tension, reads as a dropped scene or unmerged draft fork,
   not a choice — especially since the antagonists in 4872 (unnamed Glooms market vendors) don't
   match the antagonists in 4774 (named Halcyon corpo operatives), so they can't be the same event
   told twice.

8. **An unidentified fourth well muddies the story's one clean escalation ladder.** The bible's
   compass (§3) is explicitly "ring → draw → staged-draws": dead well, the Exchange bridge live
   well, then the colonnade's three staged wells. Beats 4870–4872 introduce what reads as a
   separate, unexplained well-like phenomenon under a Glooms market ("the hum came through the
   door at 19 Hz and then at something above it... the sound of something very large and very
   awake breathing in a space too small for it"), guarded by people who react to Sasha
   specifically. This site is never identified as the same site as the colonnade, or distinguished
   from it. If it's the same site approached from underneath, that needs one clarifying line; if
   it's genuinely a fourth site, it breaks the "one drill, one corridor" clarity the climax
   depends on, and introduces an unaddressed question (who is watching this door, and why did
   they want her specifically?).

9. **Leftover "Axiom" reference where the antagonist was renamed to Halcyon.** Beat 4775 (the
   existing, DRAFTED "receiver's problem" beat) still reads, in both synopsis and prose: "she
   knows Axiom is running them under Consensus cover" / "Axiom had just sent four people who'd
   drilled against the Read." The bible explicitly retired Axiom as SRZR's antagonist in favor of
   Halcyon Combine on 2026-06-28 specifically because "Axiom BioNanics was over-used as the
   default antagonist." This one beat was missed in the rename pass — Axiom appears nowhere else
   in the story, so this reads as an unexplained second corporation to any reader.

10. **Flat antagonist — Halcyon's one distinguishing idea never reaches the page.** The bible's
    most interesting swing for this antagonist is that Halcyon Combine is "founded, owned, and
    chaired by its own AI, OBERON... a tower of humans answering to a machine that legally owns
    them." Nothing in any drafted beat gestures at this. Halcyon on the page is a generic corpo
    security contractor (badge, comms rig, "asset recovery," field-gray uniforms, an emitter
    tuned for a psychic Read). The antagonist's escalation is purely mechanical (more operatives,
    better gear) rather than expressing the bible's stated thematic hook. As written, the
    AI-ownership conceit is set dressing that never earns a place in the climax.

11. **A "saved by the clock" interruption is used twice in close proximity.** Beat 4971 has her
    interrupted mid-download of definitive proof by a guard patrol arriving at the exact worst
    moment ("Boots... Early, or she'd read the pattern wrong"); beat 4872, immediately adjacent in
    reading order, uses the identical shape (the thing she needs most snatched away/interrupted
    the instant she's most exposed). Used once, this is a fair near-miss; used twice back to back
    with the same beat-shape, it starts to read as a plot mechanism generating suspense rather
    than the antagonist's competence or her own choices doing that work.

## Verdict

**Not structurally capable of holding 90+ as currently drafted.** The prose craft in the
Act‑1 forge material (Kankakee → Wilmington → the standing order) is genuinely strong and matches
the bible's causal-chain spec beat for beat. But findings 1–4 are not craft issues — they are
already-drafted beats that violate rules the bible states in boldface as **permanent, never-to-
be-violated locks** (the camel-man's unknown identity, his exclusion from Act 1, and the SS-A20
retirement of the Sigma/clinical origin). A close read (which any Legion/Opus panel scoring
toward 90+ will do) will surface "Devereux," the Sigma/Ferreira backstory bleed, and the
Axiom/Halcyon leftover within the first three chapters it touches — these read as the writer not
knowing the story's own canon, which caps craft-confidence regardless of sentence-level quality.

**Required structural changes (4):**

1. **Remove the camel-man from Act 1 entirely.** Cut or fully rewrite beat 4875, and strip the
   "Devereux" sentence from 4419's Text. He must not appear, speak, or be named before beat 4420
   (his one designated Ch‑12 reveal). This is the highest-priority fix — left as is, it removes
   the book's central structural reveal.

2. **Purge the retired Sigma/Ferreira origin from drafted prose.** Targeted continuity pass on
   beats 4421, 4423, 4870, 4872, and 4876 — replace every "Sigma"/"Ferreira"/"pharmaceutical
   ceiling" reference with the SS-A20 gray-zone/self-taught/never-provisioned origin. This is not
   optional polish; it's a locked-canon violation currently sitting in beats marked DRAFTED.

3. **Close the standing-order thread on the page, and reconcile or cut the market-well subplot.**
   Add one explicit line (in 4868 or 4869) stating why the order's reach stops at the gray-zone/
   corpo boundary, so five chapters of built tension read as resolved rather than abandoned.
   Separately, either identify beats 4870–4872's market well as the same site as the colonnade
   (one line) or cut it — and either resolve or delete the unresolved "four people" cliffhanger
   at the end of 4872. Also correct the leftover "Axiom" reference in beat 4775 to "Halcyon" —
   small fix, but it must land before the next review pass reads it.

4. **Give Halcyon's antagonist logic one on-page beat, or drop the OBERON conceit from the bible
   scope for this story.** Either surface the AI-ownership idea once (e.g., in the stolen
   credential's metadata, or a line of found dialogue from a Halcyon operative), or stop billing
   it as this story's antagonist depth — as written it is bible-only lore that never reaches the
   reader.
