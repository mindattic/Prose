## Synopsis

Two small insertions into the existing (round-2) confrontation text, both additive — nothing
already in the scene is removed or rewritten.

1. One line added to the projected-evidence sequence (the "elevator-shaft transfer... exterior
   credential override" paragraph), adding a page built from Beat 17's three Cotter facts.
2. A short exchange inserted immediately before the existing "'This stops,' she said..." paragraph,
   where she names the Cotter reconstruction explicitly as the thing that changes his calculation:
   not proof he sabotaged her, but proof he authors disappearances on paper. His reaction stays
   inside the established doctrine — precision sharpens one further turn on the same wire-under-load
   tell already used at the restaurant and earlier in this same scene — and he declines to engage
   with Cotter's name at all, which reads as its own confirmation without a monologued confession.

## Text (insertion 1 — extends the existing evidence-projection paragraph)

Original (unchanged, quoted for placement):

> She set the secondary kit's projector on the corner of his desk without asking and let it throw
> the first page into the air between them: the elevator-shaft transfer, 800Φ, five hops, his mark

Then one page longer than the rest: a calibration bench sold at a loss through the same holding
chain now leasing this floor, a transit manifest paid for by a credential that had never once
touched the account it named, four incompletes marked against a gray-zone handle called Cotter,
each one dated after he was supposedly already gone. She let that page sit the longest of all.

## Text (insertion 2 — new exchange before "This stops")

Original (unchanged, quoted for placement — the paragraph immediately before this insertion):

> Something in his hands went very still on the desk — not a flinch but its opposite, the
> tightening she'd learned to read off him the way she'd once read it off men in Iowa who couldn't
> tolerate a machine that didn't answer to them on schedule.

**Insert here, before "'This stops,' she said...":**

"That page with Cotter's name on it isn't only about him," she said. "It's about what 'he left'
means, coming from you. I'm not only telling this district you sabotage the people who tell you
no. I'm telling them you rewrite what happened to the ones who disappear — cleanly enough that
nobody thinks to check. It's not a rumor anymore. It's documented, timestamped, and sitting in
the same six channels as everything else on this desk."

Whatever had gone still in his hands wound one turn tighter — the wire pulled past where she'd
watched it hold all night, the pitch of it climbing without once breaking into strain.

"That's a serious inference to build from a bench and a manifest," he said, when he spoke, each
word set down with more care than the sentence needed.

"It's not an inference," she said. "It's sourced. Same as the rest of it."

He didn't say anything else about Cotter. He'd answered everything else she'd set in front of him
tonight — disputed some of it, absorbed the rest, met all of it head-on. This was the one page he
declined to touch, and declining to touch it was the nearest thing to a confession he was going to
give her.

*(The existing paragraph resumes unchanged from here:)*

> "This stops," she said. "The relay work. The client interference. The building. All of it. Not
> because you've decided it's not worth the trouble. Because it is now more trouble than you can
> afford, and I've made sure you know exactly how much, on a channel I'm prepared to keep spending
> until you believe the rest without me having to spend them."

Everything from "This stops" through the end of the beat (his "you understand what I offered you
was real" speech, her "I've seen what your kind of right looks like" response, and the closing
