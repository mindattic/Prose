## Synopsis

Same chain-movement beat — Noor in the chain by accident, her uncanny composure under pressure —
now continuing directly from the maze-failure in the rewritten 4810. At the literal edge of the Old
Deep, the taboo line no living Brave has crossed, Sorrel stops. She will not go further. Rather than
"her body, later, is where the cost becomes unbearable," this executes the bible's second option:
she refuses to flee into the Tartar, choosing instead to hold the threshold against the pursuit
behind them. Glim leaves her there. Nothing is confirmed. The chain moves on one person short.

## Full draft prose

Noor was in the chain. He hadn't planned for this. He had grabbed a hand in the flood-white and it had been hers, and by the time he knew it was hers they were two tunnels past the Hollow and he wasn't turning back.

She moved as well as he had any right to expect. He had taken her hand in the way that meant *I am here, follow me*, and she had followed — not blindly, she was using her other hand on the wall the way he had shown her in the Works, feeling the texture of the surfaces and using them — but with the quality of a person who has made a decision and is implementing it without reservation.

He counted. It was the first thing you did in movement-under-pressure: count the chain. Forty-one Underclan and Noor, the tapped signals passing backward through the human line, each pulse a life accounted for. He kept his feet on the path he knew — and then, for the space of a wrong wall and a hard reversal, on the path he had only half-known — and let the count continue in the back of his mind the way the hum continued, a background certainty he checked against when something felt wrong.

What he noticed, in the particular way the body noticed things it had no language for and held them anyway: Noor did not panic, not even when the chain folded back on itself in the dark and the pace turned briefly to something like fear moving at speed. She had grown up on the surface, where the below was abstract, and she was underground in a chain of people she had known for three months, at the ragged edge of a route that had just failed once already, and her hand stayed steady.

They reached the boundary the training-maps had always stopped short of drawing: the seam where the Old Deep's brick gave way to something older and colder, unmarked except by the specific silence that had lived in every telling of the Tartar-fear since before Glim was born. No living Brave had crossed it. He had been about to be the first.

Sorrel stopped at the seam.

He felt her hand leave the chain before he understood it — a break in the tapped pulse, a gap where forty-one had been forty. He turned, working against the current of bodies still moving behind him, and found her a few feet back, her palm flat against the old stone the way his had been flat against the wrong wall an hour before, and her face, in what candlelight reached it, was not afraid. It was decided.

"I'm not going past this," she said.

"Sorrel—"

"They'll follow the noise before they follow a route they don't have," she said. "Someone has to be the noise, this side of the line, or they walk in behind us with the same lights and find us with nowhere left to run at all." It was true, and he knew it was true, and he knew also that it was not the whole of her reason, because her hand hadn't moved from the stone and her eyes hadn't left the dark beyond it, the dark that had swallowed Marl's certainty three times and given nothing back.

"This isn't the Tartar-fear," he said. "You don't have to—"

"I know what it is." Her voice didn't rise. It was the voice she'd used at the cistern shelf, twelve cycles and a world ago: *you'll be the third.* "I went up. I came back. I know what the choosing feels like from the inside, better than you do yet. This is the same choosing. I'm choosing not to cross this one." She looked at him, finally, and her hand found his in the dark for exactly as long as a held breath. "Go. Count them through. Don't count me — not tonight."

He did not have the four minutes it would have taken to argue her out of it, and he understood, in the same instant, that he would not have won the argument if he'd had an hour.

He went. The chain pulled him forward with it, Noor's hand still in his other one, and behind him the sounds of the Old Deep closed over the place where Sorrel had stood — no cry, no confirmation, only the ordinary silence of a passage with no one left in it to make a sound.

He counted the chain again on the far side, once they were through, the way he counted everything after movement: to confirm what he had. He came up one short, and did not say her name aloud, because saying it would have made it a fact, and he was not ready, yet, to hold a fact that size.
