## Synopsis

Slip dies; his sending-off; the Lamplighters raid a second time mid-ritual, forcing the retreat past
the Old Deep. This is where the audit's central finding lives: the original beat has the chain walk
from the Old Deep into "strata the Underclan had never entered" with zero friction. The rewrite
makes the route Glim leads them on genuinely fail — a wall where his training-route memory placed a
passage, a backtrack under active pursuit, real panic in the chain — before a second route, found
by trial rather than certainty, gets them through. Marl's story (Ch02) surfaces explicitly in
Glim's thinking at the worst of it.

## Full draft prose

They took the medicine. It came too late for Slip and just enough for the others.

Slip had been in Glim's cohort — not a close Brave, not a rival, but a constant: a presence at the thermocline fungus-harvest for six years, the person who could read the spore-load in the thermal gradient by touch and smell in a way that Glim couldn't, the person whose hands checked the frame-mesh for rot while Glim checked the yield numbers, working side by side without speaking because the work didn't need speech and Slip had never been one to use it otherwise. He had not been loud, had not been vivid; he had been reliable in the way the cistern was reliable — present, useful, assumed. The Bright Fever, and the five minutes lost at a grating two levels up, had shown Glim the cost of assuming a person.

Vesh did not speak at Slip's sending-off. The ritual was the Brave-master's, and Knuckle spoke it, flat and correct and without decoration. He said the name, tapped it first in the sign-language of the pipe so the Current would hear it in the way it heard, then spoke it aloud. He said what Slip had known and what Slip had done, in the plain declarative of someone listing a Brave's competencies. He did not say what Slip had meant. That was not the ritual's form, and it would not have helped.

The glow-rat called Tallow was placed in the cistern as the final gesture: a small green-gold light on the black water, moving out toward the center, visible for a long time before the dark took it.

The Lamplighters came while Slip's light was still on the water.

They came the same way they always came — with the floods, from two directions — and this time there was no warning, because Glim had been at the sending-off and not at the tunnel-mouth where the warning would have lived. The white poured through the south junction first, filling the tunnel and then the cavity beyond it, and the first thing it did was erase the green-gold on the black water.

The chain formed anyway, because the drill was in them. Forty-one Underclan and Noor linked in the dark and the white and the noise of the floods arriving, and they went through the tunnel toward the Old Deep, the stratum below Homewater, older than the brick above it, and there was nowhere else left.

The Old Deep held. Glim knew this stretch — route-training territory, every clan child's ground before Brave-status — and the chain moved through it at the pace the chain could sustain, the tapped pressure passing backward, *I have the one behind me*, confirming forty-two lives at a time.

Then the training-route ran out, and Glim led them onto the descent he had made exactly once, alone, in the deepest solitary push of his own preparation for the Surfacing — the furthest down any living Brave besides Marl had gone and come back to describe. He had walked it once. He had walked it certain.

The wall was where the passage should have been.

He stopped so fast that Knuckle's chest struck his shoulder, and the pulse behind them faltered — forty-one signals bunching, uncertain, a chain with its head suddenly still. He put his palm flat against ferrocrete that his memory insisted was open air, ran his hand along a seam that had no business existing, and felt the specific cold sweat of a Brave discovering that the ground his training stood on had a crack in it he had never been taught to check for. *He came to a wall where he said the passage had been,* Vesh's voice said, in his head, unbidden, in the exact cadence of the teaching-chamber. *He tried again, a different way in, and it didn't hold that time either.*

He didn't have time to be Marl.

He turned the chain — a hard reversal, ugly, forty-two people folding back on themselves in a corridor built for one direction, Knuckle's voice barking the recovery-signal so no one panicked into the wrong wall — and took the second branch he'd marked, half-remembered, on that same solitary descent: a route he had considered and rejected as longer, colder, worse footing. It was also, this time, real. The tunnel narrowed and turned twice at angles his memory hadn't recorded and then opened, without warning or fanfare, onto stone that was not ferrocrete and not brick — dressed, fitted, older than either.

Behind them, distant now, the floodlight noise diffused into the tunnels they'd abandoned, chasing a route that no longer existed for anyone, including, for a handful of terrible minutes, Glim.

They went in. There was nowhere else to go, and the way that had almost not opened had opened anyway, and Glim did not yet know if that was the Current, or luck, or simply a boy who had marked a second path once, half-attentive, on a day he'd been thinking about something else entirely.
