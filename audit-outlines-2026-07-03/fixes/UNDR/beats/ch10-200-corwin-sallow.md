## Synopsis

Same raid, same structure — the two-junction floodlight assault on the Hollow, three days after
Glim's return, the retreat chain forming in the white-out. The "one voice, level and deliberate"
is now Corwin Sallow, named and physically present, who takes Leaf — the elder from Ch01 — as a
trophy-catch in the confusion, and kills her, deliberately or through the specific carelessness of
a collector who does not weigh what he holds. Glim does not see the moment itself (he is leading
the chain), which preserves the retreat's forward momentum and the ambiguity of exactly what
happened — but he hears enough, and knows enough of Sallow's voice by the end of it, to carry a
face and a name into the rest of the book.

## Full draft prose

They came on the third day after his return.

The first sign was air. The right-hand tunnel, which came from the direction of the Works, carried a draft that was wrong — not the regular Vent Cog cycle, not the irregular leak of the broken junction, but a forced air-movement, like the Works were breathing wrong. Glim felt it on his arm at the morning-check and stood very still and felt it again.

The draft was carrying something. Not the killing air — the candle was fine. It was carrying the surface: the manufactured scents, the particular not-organic complexity that he had learned to identify in eight days on the surface and had spent the past three days purging from his own clothing and skin. Someone on the surface was in the Works, and they were using surface equipment, and the surface equipment was pushing its smell ahead of it.

He made no sound. He was already in the tunnel mouth, already moving.

He got back to the Hollow in time to tap the warning and then the lights came anyway.

Not from one direction — from two, the south junction and the east Works simultaneously, which meant they had mapped the Hollow's approaches correctly and had used both. The combined white-out was total. He had experienced the flood-lights in the Works when they caught him: the sudden blindness, the panic that lived below the panic. He had been one person in a corridor with his hands on the wall. This was the Hollow. Forty-two people in the Hollow, and the white arrived at all of them at once.

He could hear it happen. The sounds: a sharp intake from Lark, from somewhere near the cistern-mouth; the sound of a child hitting the floor — Slip, who hadn't heard the warning, whose legs simply went; the particular silence of Vesh, who had gone completely still, which was worse than a sound. And underneath all of it, the candles' distress — the glow-rats reacting to the sudden light-flooding, the bioluminescence contracting, the small animal sounds of thirty candles doing the wrong thing at once.

Glim's own candle pressed itself against his hip. He didn't look at the light. He looked at his hands.

His eyes adjusted to white the same way they adjusted to black: imperfectly, slowly, by feel. He could see the shape of the Hollow as a uniform absence of detail rather than dark — the white of everything instead of the dark of everything — and in that white he heard the surface-footsteps enter from both passages at once. Not rushing. Moving with the rhythm of people who have more light than anything in this space and know it, who have done this before and have learned that calm is faster than urgency when you hold the advantage.

He counted the footsteps. Six total. Six surface people in the Hollow.

Then: a voice.

"Corwin," it said, easily, to someone unseen, the tone of a man narrating his own pleasure for an audience he assumed was listening. "Corwin, over here — the old one, by the stone. Look at the hands on her."

He said his own name the way other men said a title. Not loud — the voice of someone who understood sound-discipline in enclosed spaces, which wasn't what Glim had expected from surface hunters, and underneath the discipline, unmistakably, the specific pleasure of a person who has found exactly the thing they came to find.

Glim didn't speak back. He began moving.

In the white-out he navigated by the sounds he knew: Knuckle's breathing, deeper than anyone else's in the Hollow; Sorrel's footfall, lighter on the left than the right since the previous winter's cistern-ledge fall; Noor's voice saying something in surface speech, almost certainly directed at the footsteps entering from the south. He moved toward the chain-configuration and found Knuckle's arm in the white and gripped it in the signal that meant *I have you*.

The chain began to form.

He heard, from behind him and to the left — near the chemical-heat stone, near where Leaf worked every morning of his life — a scuffle that was not the sound of a body moving on its own volition. A grip, a stumble, the particular short cry of someone caught by the wrist and turned faster than her balance allowed. Then a fall, heavier than a fall onto packed earth should sound. Then the specific quiet of a body that has stopped deciding things.

He didn't stop moving. He couldn't. The chain was forming, the grips propagating back from his own arm, and stopping now would cost more than his one pair of hands could buy back. He yelled the direction — not words, the sound they all knew, *this way, follow it, don't stop* — and the chain lurched toward the Old Deep.

Behind them, Sallow's voice said something short and satisfied, the tone of a man closing an account, and did not follow. He had more to do there.

Glim led the chain into the tunnel's dark. He did not yet know, precisely, what he had heard happen to Leaf. He would know, with the rest of the Hollow, by nightfall. He carried the name Corwin the way you carry a stone straight off the heat-stone — with the whole body, until there was somewhere to set it down.
