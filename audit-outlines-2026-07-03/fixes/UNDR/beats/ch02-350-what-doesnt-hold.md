## Synopsis

still carrying the Surfacing-anxiety, asks Vesh what's below the Old Deep. Vesh tells him about

## Full draft prose

They walked a while without speaking. The passage narrowed toward the Warm and Glim matched his pace to the old man's slower one, the way he always did, without either of them remarking on it.

"You asked me once," Glim said, "why the Tartar-fear holds when the air-fear and the flood-fear don't need holding — they hold themselves, because the air kills you and the water does too. I never asked you why the Tartar needed a story on top of the danger."

Vesh's hand found the wall, briefly, the way it did when he was arranging a thought before he spoke it. "Because the danger down there isn't the kind you can teach a child to fear correctly," he said. "Bad air, a child can learn: watch the candle, leave when it sickens. Cold, a child can learn: dress for it, turn back before your hands stop working. But there's a thing about the Tartar that isn't air or cold or flood, and it doesn't teach the same way, and so we gave it a story instead. Stories teach what facts can't carry."

"What thing."

Vesh was quiet long enough that Glim thought he wouldn't answer. Then: "You know the name Marl."

"An old Brave. Before Knuckle's time, I thought."

"Knuckle's time, near enough — they trained together, the two winters before Knuckle took the wrist-grip from his own master. Marl was the best route-holder the Hollow ever raised. Better than Knuckle. Better, they said, than anyone since." Vesh's fingers found the wall again and rested there. "He went down past the Old Deep three times in his life, each time a little further, the way a Brave does when he's testing the edge of what he's allowed. The third time he went further than any of the first two, and he came back three sleeps later than he should have, and he was alive, and he described a place. Vaulted stone. Tile with faces in it. A cold with no source, because the Warm's breath didn't reach it."

"The Tartar."

"He called it that, after. We didn't have the word yet — he made the word, more or less, out of an old surface book-fragment he'd seen once in the Cogs' salvage. He wanted to go back. He wanted to prove it wasn't a fever-dream from bad air and hunger, which is what half the Hollow assumed at the time, myself included." Vesh's voice didn't change, but something in the pacing of it slowed, the way it slowed when the thing being told mattered more than its words let on. "He went back within the season. He used his own route — the one he'd walked out on, that he swore he remembered turn for turn. It didn't hold. He came to a wall where he said the passage had been. He tried again, a different way in, and it didn't hold that time either. He spent the rest of his life going down after it, off and on, and every account he gave of where he'd been disagreed with the last one. Not lies. He believed each one when he told it. The place simply wasn't where he'd left it, or he wasn't the man who'd found it, and by the end he couldn't tell you which."

Glim said nothing. The hum ran under the passage floor, ordinary, unbothered.

"He brought one thing back, the first time," Vesh said. "A piece of stone, carved — a fold of cloth, or a wing, no one has ever agreed which, worn smooth on one edge the way a thing is worn when hands have held it for a very long time before it ever reached his hands. No quarry in the Works cuts stone like it. No Cog has ever recognized the tool-marks. I still have it. I have never found a use for it in the teaching that didn't feel like a lie, so mostly it sits, and mostly no one asks."

"Where is Marl now?"

"Dead a long time, and easier in his mind by the end than at the middle of it, which is the only mercy in the story." Vesh turned his face toward Glim, the old habit. "I tell you this because you're about to go up, and up is the same kind of thing as down, in one way: you'll come back and swear to a route through what you saw, and the swearing will feel certain, and certainty is not the same as the ground holding still under it. Marl trusted his memory of a place more than the place deserved. Don't do what he did. Trust what you can check again in front of you, and hold the rest loosely."

Glim thought of the fragment sitting somewhere in Vesh's keeping, unexplained, waiting. He didn't ask to see it. Some things you carried the way Vesh carried this one — present, not handled, in the dark where things waited until the time was right.

They walked on toward the Warm, and the hum grew stronger, and neither of them said anything else about Marl.
