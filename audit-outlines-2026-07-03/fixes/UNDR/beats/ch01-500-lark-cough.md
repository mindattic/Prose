## Synopsis

Same fungus-harvest scene; same Lark, same cough, same exchange with Glim. Adds a short coda
establishing that Lark did report it, that it was assessed and resolved as ordinary spore-dust
irritation from the harvest — not the Bright Fever, which does not exist in Homewater yet — so
that the identical-sounding cough that opens Ch10 reads as a deliberate echo instead of an
unacknowledged contradiction.

## Full draft prose

The fungus harvest took most of the morning and was the work Glim trusted most.

Trust was specific: he trusted it in the way he trusted the hum from below, not because it gave him something he wanted but because it was honest about what it was. The fungus did not pretend. It grew in the heat-gradient at the base of the Warm's exhaust pipes — a thermocline, Vesh called it, though the word came from some document in the old-deeps library and Glim had never heard it used by anyone else — where the heat rising from the tokamak ring met the cool wet air of the cistern passage and made a band of warmth that was exactly right for growing. You couldn't do this with cold stone alone. It needed the Warm's warmth. That was the particular temperature of fusion exhaust cycling since before anyone could remember.

*First the hum*, Vesh taught. *Then the Word. Then the warmth. Then us.*

The caps grew in clusters on the frame-mesh that the Cogs had helped the Underclan build three generations ago in exchange for something Glim had never been told and had learned not to ask about. The mesh held the growing medium: clay-packed fiber that drank the pipe-drip and held it and let the mycelia thread through it in the dark. When a cap was wrong — too pale, or too red in the gills, which was the color of something that would make you sick — you left it for the compost and took it off the frame so the wrong did not spread.

He moved by smell and touch. The good ones had a faint sweetness when you pressed your thumb into the gill-side and lifted slightly — a clean volatile, not chemical, not the Made Things' smell, which was always a little too complex and a little too wrong. The good smell meant the cap was right. He filled the basket and moved to the next cluster without looking up.

Across the passage, Lark was at the Still.

She had the long-handled rake — the harvesting tool for the algae-bloom that grew in the low pool where the warm-pipe condensation collected, a pool that had been a cistern once and was now something stranger: bioluminescent in deep blue, never warm enough to be comfortable but never cold enough to kill the algae, lit by its own chemistry in a way the candles were not.

She raked with one hand and coughed into the other. A short cough, swallowed quickly, the kind a person used when they were trying not to be heard. Glim watched her check the rake-handle afterward, the way you checked everything in the Hollow that had been near your mouth. She was being careful. She was doing the work. The cough came again and she absorbed it the same way: no pause in the raking, just the interruption and the return, as though the two things weren't connected.

He looked back at the caps.

After a moment, without turning, he said: "You should tell Knuckle."

The raking stopped.

He kept working. A silence from Lark was not the ordinary silence — she was five sleep-seasons from her Surfacing and had the quality of young Braves who had decided that being unnoticed was the same as being safe. It wasn't the same. He had made that calculation himself, at her age, and had been wrong about it in specific and instructive ways.

"I'm fine," she said.

"Yes," he said. "Tell Knuckle anyway."

She told him. He heard about it two days later from Sorrel, in passing, the way you heard about small resolved things in a Hollow this size: Knuckle had sat her by a candle and made her breathe against his palm and pronounced it spore-dust, the ordinary cost of a season spent low over the Still's blue water, and had told her to rest a sleep and drink more than she thought she needed. The cough was gone within two more sleeps. It left nothing behind — no fever, no wrongness in the chest, no candle that so much as flickered near her. It had been exactly the small true thing it looked like: dust, cleared. Glim thought of it once more, weeks later, only because he had asked, and then didn't think of it again.

He went back to the caps. He checked the last cluster: four good, two wrong, one borderline that he set aside for Vesh.

Below him, coming up through the mesh and the clay and the rock, the hum ran its ordinary frequency. The Warm was cycling. The algae were growing. The Hollow was eating, always cycling, nothing going to waste and nothing being wasted.

He took the basket to the chemical-heat stone and set it with the others.

The air was good. The morning was ending. He had work still to do.
