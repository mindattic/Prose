The map went up over Vox's dead gray face, and Rook let Adalemo's name sit on the door where he'd put it, because moving it now would be a lie about what she'd already decided. She gave the room the shape of the thing instead.

"Vault's on the substrate level, which means it's under the bodies, not beside them. We don't go through a wall. We go down through a working floor — clerks at stations, intake on rolling beds, the racks they kept the lights on for humming the whole way." She let the down-route burn its way across the wall, a service ramp threading the old fire core, and watched the crew's eyes follow it down into the part of the building that didn't photograph well. "Boiler, that's your hum. Tell me what it does to your kit when there's four hundred warm deferrals in the concrete instead of four hundred dead drives."

Boiler had been quiet the way he got quiet, which wasn't quiet at all — humming under his breath, thumbs working the resonance kit's calibration wheel, the smile still on but not reaching the part of his face that did the counting. The kit was a flat case the size of a hymnal, open on his knee, and the wheel under his thumb was the only part of him that moved while he thought, a brass detent that ticked in eighths the way he'd tuned it years ago because the eighths felt honest to his hand. He turned it now, slow, and Rook understood she was watching a man listen to a building that wasn't in the room yet.

"Empty concrete sings clean," he said. He thumbed the wheel a notch and the kit answered him with a tone so low it was more pressure than sound, a flat ringing she felt behind her sternum more than heard, the note a wall gives back when there's nothing behind it but more wall. "Like that. I drop a null wedge into a dead floor, the structure tells me where the cavities are, and the return comes back square — a man reading rain off a roof, you can hear the gutters in it, you can hear where the shingles end." He thumbed the wheel two notches the other way, and the tone climbed and went rough at the edges, broke into a faint beating against itself, a sound like the kit was trying to hold a single note and a dozen smaller notes were leaning on it from inside. "Four hundred people in it, the return comes back *wrong*. Every one of them's their own little resonance — augments idling, BCIs filtering the room out so the body can sleep, hearts. They don't sing the note back at me. They sing under it. I'd be reading a choir and calling it a wall." He set the wheel down, and the rough tone died, and the quiet it left behind had a shape now that it hadn't had before. "I can still find you the door. The seam's in there, the choir doesn't move the concrete, only crowds it. But I can't promise the people between you and it don't feel the wedge in their teeth before you're through them. There's a floor on how soft I go, and the floor is them."

*Four years he's kept a manifest dark and I just put it on a wall in front of three crews. The kind man's limit is that he won't lie about the cost, and the kind man just told me the cost is in somebody's teeth.*

Scout came off the wall where she'd been leaning, and the way she came off it told Rook the answer before she said it. "Perimeter doesn't work on a building you go *down* into. There's no outside to hold once you're under the bodies. I ride in. I'm the one who comes back up the working floor with the count if the soft knock turns hard, and I'm the one who's standing wrong if Boiler's wedge wakes the choir." She pulled her gloves on, finger by finger, Reibo slung where she could reach it without looking. "Gerald rides too. I'm not leaving him in the dark on a clock I can close from the inside — if I'm in, he's in, and you all hear him before you hear me. That's the deal or I'm no good to you on the perimeter that doesn't exist."

Rook held still on that a second, because Scout had named the thing the right way and it deserved the second. An Exo left her own body slumped somewhere safe while she went out into the work as nothing the room could touch — and an Exo's whole tradecraft was being the absence, the draft under the door, the count nobody could shoot at. Rook had hired her for that absence. What Scout was doing now, pulling her gloves on one finger at a time so the table had to watch her decide, was the opposite of her own tradecraft. She wasn't asking to bring backup. She was asking to bring a voice the crew would hear *before* they heard her — Gerald first, always, so that there'd be a presence on the channel exactly where Scout's whole skill was to leave one. The boy wasn't insurance. He was the proof that Scout refused to go down into a floor full of people and be the only thing in it without a name. *She's telling me she won't be the absence in there,* Rook thought. *She'll be a woman whose son you hear breathing. That's not a weakness in my perimeter. That's the only honest way down a ramp like that one, and she got there before I did.*

Rook ran it one more time — twenty-one she could prove, fourteen on her word, the forty-first with no QUANTA mark, the man in the door who'd asked to be counted as the one who couldn't be carried twice, and now the Exo who lived in absence asking to be present out loud, with the boy named. She didn't reach for the slate. She set her finger flat on the working floor where the down-route met the racks, a centimeter from where Adalemo's credential opened the only door GLMZ never let rot. "Then here's your clock," she said, level. "Helix audits its own ledger every shift change. Adalemo's credential reads valid until the next audit walks the rack assignments and finds his hand somewhere it filed as gone. That's the storm. We're not racing a guard. We're racing the moment the building reconciles itself and notices the clerk it deferred is standing in its only lit door." She lifted the finger, put it back. "Boiler, you read the choir and find me the seam anyway. Scout, you and Gerald ride the count up. I knock soft. And the second the soft knock costs me Stave, the second crack opens and the first one was never the way in. Everybody hold your frame. We go before the floor remembers us."
