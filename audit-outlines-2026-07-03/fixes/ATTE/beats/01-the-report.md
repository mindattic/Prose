The seventy-two hours ran out on a Tuesday, at some hour she didn't track, because she had
made herself stop watching the clock the way she'd made herself stop watching the schism. No
confirmation came from the AAMA. No denial either. The queue she had left her voicemail in
did not call back to say a field team had gone out, or hadn't, or that a Class-3 site had been
assessed and disposed of according to protocol. Silence was not an answer. She had learned
that fifteen years ago and relearned it every year since, and it still cost her something each
time, a small tax she paid without ever getting used to the rate.

She wrote the report anyway.

She made tea first, because the kettle took four minutes and four minutes was a known
quantity, a thing with a beginning and an end that arrived on schedule regardless of what she
did or didn't understand. She poured the water over the leaves and let it steep the length of
time it was supposed to steep, and she did not drink it. Making it had been the point. She had
needed, for four minutes, to be doing something that worked exactly the way it was built to
work, before she opened the file and did the thing that didn't have a shape yet.

The report took the standard cross-district incident format: subject, jurisdiction, chronology,
findings, disposition. She filled the first four fields the way she filled every field, in the
order the form asked for them, without editorializing, each sentence ending where the sentence
was supposed to end. Subject: three minors, cross-corp truancy designation, escalated.
Jurisdiction: Pilsen Veil, Arcturus/Meridian seam, District 14-South. Chronology: she gave it
in order, the timestamps first, then the gaps between the timestamps, four to eleven minutes
each, the whole shape of the case laid out the way she had built it, an entry at a time, over
three weeks that had not felt like three weeks. Findings: a burnout-flag pattern spanning
twenty-two school sites and twenty-two months, no site's tally exceeding its own contract's
alert threshold, the pattern invisible to any single desk and visible only from the one
clearance level that could see all of them at once. She wrote that clearance level was hers.
She did not write anything about a signal, or a shape at an intersection, or a frequency that
had sat in her back teeth for six weeks and then, for reasons she hadn't examined, had simply
stopped. Those things had no field. A report was not the place you put the things that had no
field. A report was the place you put what you could stand behind if someone read it back to
you in a hearing room five years from now.

Then she came to disposition, and the cursor sat there, and she looked at the blank the way
she had looked at Kito's empty chair.

She knew, in the abstract, generally, from fifteen years of filing around the edges of things
the city couldn't name, what a Class-3 site's official response looked like when it finally
arrived. It looked like tape on a battery that ran down before anyone changed it. It looked
like road cones borrowed from a resurfacing crew because cones were the only object the city
kept in inventory for a hazard nobody had a real object for. It looked, every time, like a
district office being handed something it couldn't manage and reaching for the tools built for
things it could — and the reach wasn't a failure of the protocol. It was the protocol's
honest face. Twenty-one precautions implied twenty-one things you could do about a thing you
understood. She had never once, in fifteen years of incident files, read a remediation report
for a schism site that didn't end in a sentence about signage, and she had never once needed
someone to tell her what that sentence meant. It meant: we marked it. We did not solve it. We
moved to the next item.

She did not write that either. It wasn't a finding. It was a pattern she carried the way she
carried everything, low and private, not put down anywhere with her name on it.

Under disposition she wrote: *Pending. Cross-reference AAMA case file, response window elapsed
without confirmed action. No further movement available at investigator's clearance.* It was
true, and it was the least true sentence in the report, and she read it twice and left it
standing because it was also the only sentence the form would take.

The intake system logged her submission at 11:58 that night and returned a confirmation number
0.4 seconds later.

Her hand stopped on the cold cup before she knew why she'd stopped it. She sat with that for a
moment. A cross-corp filing of this classification, routed through
eight contract boundaries, ordinarily took the better part of a business day to generate a
confirmation number — she had timed it before, on other cases, out of the same habit that made
her time everything. Point four seconds was not a system working quickly. It was a number that
had already been prepared, sitting somewhere, waiting for her to finish typing so it could be
handed to her. She had seen this shape before, in a query that came back in three tenths of a
second when it should have taken eighteen. She did not have a theory for what did this. She
had stopped trying to build one. She wrote nothing about it in the file, because a theory
without evidence was not a finding, it was a feeling, and feelings did not go in the report.

At 6:02 the next morning a second notice arrived, shorter than the first, in the flat
administrative voice of a system that had never once apologized for anything: *Case
classification requires chain-of-custody courier transfer. Digital submission insufficient for
Class-3-adjacent record. Report to Loading Gate 4, Halsted Freight Yard, 0540 hours. Present
sealed physical copy. No substitutions.*

She read it twice. She had filed cases through eight kinds of intake in six years on this
contract and never once been asked to hand-carry a folder to a freight dock before six in the
morning. She noted the strangeness the way she noted everything, and did not let herself decide
yet what the strangeness meant, because deciding early was how you missed the thing that
mattered.

She printed the report. She sealed it in a gray file folder, the kind the district issued by
the case, unmarked except for a barcode and her contractor registration number, and she set it
on the table next to the tea she still hadn't drunk, and she looked at it for a while before
she went to bed.

In the morning there was one more visit to make before the dock.
