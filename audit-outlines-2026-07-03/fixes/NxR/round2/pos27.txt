The diner on the Z1 lakeshore didn't have a name on the sign anymore — it had come down in the atmospheric processor rerouting that took out three blocks of Z1 signage in 2221, and whoever owned the building had decided the absence was cheaper than replacement. The regulars called it the Sojourn anyway, the way a place keeps its name after the sign is gone, because by then the name had stopped being about the building and started being about what you did there, which was sit somewhere with a lake in the window long enough to remember you were a person and not just the last thing you'd finished doing.

The booth faced east. Through the window, Lake Michigan ran its own operation, which it had been running since before any CorpoNation had a name, before the Pulse existed, before anyone had decided the 88th meridian was worth fighting over. The neon from the Z1 transit hub caught the surface of the water and pulled it — the red of the signage bleeding into something closer to copper out there, the white of the maintenance lights dissolving into a line that had no fixed position.

She ran the number again.

*Six.*

Six people now carried a piece of what had been behind that wall — herself, Vox, Lace, Stave, Ohara, Adalemo, each of them holding some fraction of sixty-one names and twenty-one bare columns, none of them holding it in quite the same shape as anyone else. At the diner near the Spine, the morning after Axiom, it had been five who carried the truth about the formula — her, Vox, Lace, Boiler, Scout, the crew and no one outside it, a clean number because the crew was a clean unit and the secret had fit inside it without needing a stranger to hold any part. Six wasn't cleaner. Six had a stranger in it — a man with a shoulder he'd let PEREGRINE's new paper fund instead of fixing right, a man who'd been checking a name off a manifest three months before she'd ever heard PEREGRINE mentioned him, a man she'd shot once, in a storm, on a different lake, and hadn't decided yet whether that was still on the table. The truth this time hadn't fit inside the crew. It had gone out past the edge of who she trusted, into the keeping of someone she couldn't fully account for, and it was staying there whether she liked the arithmetic or not.

*Five was people who agreed to carry a thing together because they trusted each other. Six is people who agreed to carry a thing together because none of us had anywhere else to put it down. I don't know yet if that's worse. I know it's different, and I know I'm the one who's going to have to keep counting it.*

The lake didn't move differently for the additional variable. The neon on the water held its copper line, and then a hauler on the transit hub approach broke the reflection into separate lights — many small versions of the same source, scattered, none of them the original. The hauler passed. The line didn't quite reform. A slight angle in it now, the copper settled differently than before.

Rook picked up the coffee the Sojourn had brought her without being asked — because she had sat here before, because this booth also had its requirements, because apparently she had made herself legible to more places in this city than she'd intended — and drank it. Counted again. Six again.

The slight angle held. The copper line on the water didn't go back the way it had been.
