The neon off the water was Pilsen neon, which meant it had been strung for no one and maintained by the same no one and had outlasted every entity that might have had an opinion about it. The barge sat where Boiler had left it, engine idling at a frequency the water carried better than the air, and the truck was already backed to the dock ramp with its cargo door open and Boiler standing at the hinge, one hand on the frame, watching the lake side the way he watched every side — without urgency, without the pretense that anything he saw would surprise him. He clocked Vox's empty hands in the same half-second Rook had. He didn't move the hand on the frame.

*Sixty-one names, twenty-one bare, and the only thing I carried out is the shape of a man who didn't need to stand where he stood.*

Rook put herself at the top of the ramp without being asked and Vox came down it, the dock planking soft with rot at the center where the weight had been for twenty years, and the water underneath it was the color of the neon it was reflecting — not beautiful, not ugly, just the lake doing what the lake did, which was be there before the neon and after it and entirely indifferent to the difference. Boiler moved aside to let her step up into the cargo bay and she did, and the truck's floor was cold through the soles of her boots and the smell was rubber and machine oil and somewhere under that the iron-cold smell of the corridor that had followed her out on her clothes. She sat down on the wheel well. The four channels ran quiet. She let them.

Ohara was already inside, on the bench along the far wall, Lace beside her with a hand still loose at her elbow out of habit more than need now. She looked up when Vox came in and held her gaze a beat longer than a stranger would have, the two of them reading each other across the cargo bay the way people read each other when they've each spent the same morning finding out what a wall could take out of a person and neither one is going to say so out loud. Ohara didn't ask what had happened behind the wall. Vox didn't offer it. It was, in its way, the most honest exchange either of them had all morning — two women confirming, without a word, that the other one had come back mostly whole, and letting that be enough.

Rook pulled the cargo door down from the outside, and the dock and the neon and the lake went to a seam of light at the bottom, and then that too was gone.
