Vox held the sweep open a beat longer than she needed to, the way she always did after the verdict was already delivered, because the four channels kept talking after the headline stopped mattering and sometimes the talking after was the part worth keeping. Most of it was PEREGRINE's ordinary traffic — tasking orders, a supply manifest for gear Rook didn't recognize, the dry back-and-forth of four professionals coordinating a job. She let it wash past. And then one packet came through on a signature that didn't match the tasking channel at all — a personal credential, logged at 0300, routed nowhere official, querying a single string against the Old Harbor relocation rolls.

She almost let it go. Personnel querying old civic records wasn't PEREGRINE's shape; PEREGRINE queried Ohara's wall, PEREGRINE queried the gate hardware, PEREGRINE did not, as a rule, spend its own operator's clearance at three in the morning cross-referencing a transit manifest two years cold. That was the part that made her stop the packet and read it twice.

The query returned one name. *Sefi Okonkwo.* No hit — the rolls didn't carry a delivery mark for it, same as they didn't for the other twenty of that missing column, though Vox didn't know that shape yet, not consciously, not until the wall gave it up whole. What she knew now was smaller and stranger: someone inside PEREGRINE had gone looking for exactly one person off a list nobody outside the channels remembered existed, had come up empty, and had then done something PEREGRINE's own operational discipline should have made unnecessary — reached into the query log by hand and deleted the record rather than letting the system's own thirty-day purge take it. You didn't delete a thing that was going to clear itself unless you didn't trust the clearing. Unless you wanted no version of you, including the automated one, to have looked.

She brought it to Rook cold, the way she brought everything that didn't have a clean verdict attached. "The credential's Adalemo's," she said. "Not shared, not a shell — his personal log-in, on a query outside anything PEREGRINE's paying him to run."

Rook took a moment with that. She had spent four seconds across a broken safety rail once, in a storm, deciding whether the man on the other end of a negotiated cease-fire meant to keep his half of it, and she had come away from that four seconds with a read that had held ever since: competent, unhurried, the kind of operator who priced a cost before he paid it. She had put a round through the meat of his shoulder that night, close enough to matter and not close enough to finish it, and heard him go into the lake instead of finishing the conversation the way either of them could have. PEREGRINE's new paper hadn't gone toward fixing what she'd broken — she'd heard, secondhand, through the same channels Vox ran now, that the repair job on that shoulder was the kind you got when your employer prioritized new funding over old maintenance, cheap work that clicked when he reached overhead and would keep clicking for the rest of his working life. The rust framing applied to him too. He just wore it under the jacket instead of on the signage.

"He's not sloppy," Rook said. "He knows exactly what the purge cycle does. He deleted it anyway."

"Which means he didn't trust the purge to actually make it not have happened." Vox turned the fragment over once more, looking for a version of it that read as routine and not finding one. "People don't cover a search that clears itself unless the search is the thing they're ashamed of, not the record."

"Or unless he's already decided he's going to need to explain himself to someone who isn't PEREGRINE, someday, and he doesn't want the log making that explanation for him first."

Vox didn't have an answer for that, and didn't try to build one; she filed it the way she filed everything that didn't resolve, in the part of her that kept a running account of things that weren't done being true yet. "I don't like handing you a maybe."

"I don't need a maybe to be a yes," Rook said. "I need it to be a number I have and PEREGRINE doesn't know I have." She looked at the name once more before Vox closed the channel on it — a string of letters attached to a person she would never meet, and a man who'd reached into his own log by hand to make sure no record of having looked for that person survived him. That was the part worth keeping. Not the name. The care he'd taken with it. "Watch for it again. If he goes looking a second time, I want to know before PEREGRINE does."

"He won't leave a second copy," Vox said. "Careful people don't."

"No," Rook said. "But careful people who keep checking anyway are telling you something a clean record can't."

She didn't say what. Vox closed the channel, and the name went into the file with the funding read and the tasking orders and the supply manifest for gear Rook didn't recognize — one line among the morning's traffic, no heavier than the rest of it, filed and left to sit.
