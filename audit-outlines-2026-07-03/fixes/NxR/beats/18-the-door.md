The north corridor had a seal. Old municipal blast door, manual actuator, the kind freight buildings kept from back when a loading-bay fire was a thing that happened and somebody maintained the answer to it. Boiler had flagged it on the walk-in — dead power, live linkage. Throw it by hand and the corridor went from a mouth to a wall. Fourteen meters of stack on the wrong side of it instead of on top of Vox.

Rook moved. She crossed the grate with the gun up, and the actuator housing was where Boiler said it'd be, and the handle was where a handle is, and she got her hand on it.

She keyed the open line at the same time. "Scout. Sealing north. Hold your count on the—"

The channel died mid-word. Not static, not the drop of a bad relay. A clean cut, the kind that only comes from something that had already found the frequency and waited to close it on a sentence that mattered. The open line went to nothing in her ear. Stave's door read live one second and gone the next.

And the actuator moved under her hand.

Not from her. She hadn't thrown it. The dead linkage that hadn't taken a signal since the year it was installed took one now — PEREGRINE's, threaded up through the building's gutted systems while the crew stood over the wall reading it — and the blast door drove itself the wrong way, open on the corridor she'd come to close, the handle wrenching out of her grip so hard it took skin off two knuckles.

The seal was theirs now. They'd had it before she'd touched it. The corridor stayed a mouth.

Rook let go of the dead handle and did not look at the patch. She flexed the raw hand once, closed it, and set her feet on the line the lamps had drawn. Fourteen meters. Some of that number was still hers to spend.
