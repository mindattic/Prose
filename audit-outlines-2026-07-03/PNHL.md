# Structural Audit — Pinhole (PNHL)

## Zero. Precondition finding (read this before the outline)

**The beats currently attached to this node do not implement the current story bible.** The bible
(`docs/nodes/PNHL.md`, revised 2026-07-03) specifies: Iowa → Blur → GLMZ → relay job → **a dress
and a dinner where the Assessor appears in person and she declines him face to face** → a sabotage
campaign (jobs poisoned, contacts pulled, bribes traced) → **a direct confrontation where she walks
in and ends it** → a two-beat Kyle bookend ("Give em hell" at departure and again at the finale).

The 29 beats actually stored on this node tell a different story: a messenger and a phone call make
an offer she declines; her apartment is **broken into and her equipment (including Nit) is
physically stolen**; she tracks the stolen gear with an embedded beacon, does recon, intercepts a
data transfer, and **anonymously dossiers the operation to a rival gray-zone collective, a
competing analysis firm, and the ACS tip line**, who destroy it off-page; Kyle appears exactly once
and says "Lock yours" — the retired pre-revision line named in the bible's own changelog as
superseded.

This matches `docs/USER_STORIES.md` H4b/H4c: *"Beats require full prose rewrite to match new arc...
Standalone review pending new prose."* The 80.7 score was set against this superseded draft. Every
finding below is diagnosed against **this draft as it stands**, because that is what the audit was
asked to score — but the headline conclusion doesn't change: this is not a prose problem, it is the
wrong outline, already known to be wrong, sitting unrewritten.

## Structural Findings

1. **[4886, §3/§9 Lock 8]** Nit is stolen outright — a direct violation of the bible's explicit lock: *"Nit is never stolen. Nobody wants the drone design."* Worse, its recovery is never shown: Beat 14 (the finale) never mentions Nit again. The story's signature instrument disappears from the narrative with no on-page resolution, positive or tragic.

2. **[4398, 4881, all beats]** The Assessor never appears in person anywhere in this draft — only a messenger and a disembodied phone voice. The bible requires him to be "specific and present," introduced at a dinner and concluded at a confrontation. Here he is reduced to an administrative threat with no face, which flattens the "the horror is that he's charming" character design into pure procedure.

3. **[4887, 13, 4885, 14]** There is no antagonist-facing climax. The plan is to leak a dossier to a rival collective, a competitor, and ACS, and let *them* dismantle the operation off-page (dark windows, quiet street). Pixel's own action — building and sending the package — is real, but the actual defeat of the antagonist is never dramatized; we never see him lose, react, or speak again after Beat 4398's phone call. This technically satisfies "she solves it herself" by authorship but reads as legal-remedy-by-proxy, not a won confrontation.

4. **[4888 vs. 12]** Timeline contradiction: Beat 4888 (injection sequence, ~03:55) has her physically opening the recovered primary case and inspecting the drift meter as if it's already in hand. Beat 12 (explicitly titled "04:30") shows her recovering that same case for the first time, later, while evading a returning ACS officer. The case cannot already be open on her workbench at 03:55 and be recovered from the building for the first time at 04:30. This isn't a flashback structure — both are staged as real-time physical action.

5. **[13 vs. 4399]** Missing setup for a stated payoff: Beat 13's dossier narration claims "the ACS entry she'd filed on the stolen equipment had told them exactly what the VacCell was — her own loss report... handed to the Assessor's operation by the city's own bureaucracy." But the only ACS visit on the page, Beat 4399, ends with her explicitly declining to file ("never mind, I think I left it at the station"). The irony the ending leans on — her own bureaucratic caution doomed her — has no beat where she actually files the report it depends on.

6. **[4400, 4401, 4918, 4888]** Competence with no setbacks or cost, repeatedly — the exact register this audit was asked to flag by name:
   - **[4400/4401]** Builds a directional receiver in 90 minutes, gets a clean 4-second location fix, then traces a six-hop shell company instantly through public records. No dead end, no wrong turn.
   - **[4918]** Hacks three separate commercial security systems — a residential-grade shell, an industrial logistics shell, and a "military-grade" surveillance array — in 33 total seconds combined, triggers zero alerts, and restores a repositioned camera to within a quarter-degree of its original angle afterward. Zero near-misses.
   - **[4888]** The one moment framed as a setback (transfer already cached) is solved in 19 seconds by a clean improvisation pulled straight from memory — resolved so fast and cleanly it reads as another competence flex rather than an actual cost.

7. **[4877 → 4400]** Obvious/telegraphed setup: the embedded tracer beacon is introduced with the line "she'd built the signal to be ugly... specifically so it could be found. It was the thing she'd done instead of worrying" — stated almost as a thesis on first mention. When it pays off three chapters later, the reader has already been told exactly what it's for and precisely how it will resolve.

8. **[4401]** Antagonist-pressure logic that's never dramatized: the Tanaka-Arcturus data-sharing agreement (good worldbuilding — explains why she was found without coincidence) sets up a second faction, Arcturus corporate security, racing the Assessor for her on a countdown. Arcturus never appears, intervenes, or complicates anything anywhere in the story. A ticking second clock is introduced purely for exposition and then dropped.

9. **[4396, 4879]** Dropped subplot: Reyes is set up in direct, escalating conflict with Donatella over letting "a stranger" touch the building systems, then shown suspiciously checking Pixel's door at night. He never reappears after that — not during the recovery, not in the finale. A live, personal antagonism thread opens and is simply abandoned.

10. **[4884]** Beat 4884 is entirely empty — no title, no synopsis, no text — a blank slot sitting mid-sequence between the surveillance step (4883) and the stakeout (4885).

11. **[4402 vs. 4918]** Duplicate content: Beat 4402 ("Follow") and the tail of Beat 4918 both independently narrate the same discovery — viewing footage of Nit and the Arcturus-logo schematic on an examination table — without the second pass adding new information or escalation.

12. **[12, 13, 14 vs. 4394–4970]** Data-hygiene flag, not a craft flaw per se, but a likely contributor to score instability: the story's actual final three beats carry Numbers (12/13/14) wildly out of sequence with every other beat (4394–4970), which means any tooling or reviewer relying on Number-order will misread the story's structure — as this audit initially did until the content was cross-checked.

## Verdict

**Not structurally capable of 90+ as written**, and not primarily because of the beat-level issues above — those are fixable polish-adjacent problems. The disqualifying issue is that **this draft is not the sanctioned story**: it violates an explicit narrative lock (Nit stolen), removes the antagonist's only two on-page appearances (dinner, confrontation), and resolves the conflict through offscreen third parties instead of a personal confrontation. No amount of prose tightening raises a score ceiling when the outline itself contradicts its own bible's locks and drops its own climax.

Four structural changes, in priority order:

1. **Rewrite to the canon spine already specified in the bible** (§10, and flagged pending in USER_STORIES H4b): dress + in-person dinner scene where the Assessor is physically present and she declines him to his face → deniable sabotage campaign (poisoned jobs, no-show contacts, traced bribes) → a direct, on-page confrontation scene where she walks in, presents her leverage, and ends it face to face. This single change resolves findings 1, 2, 3, and 7 simultaneously — it's not an additive fix, it's the fix.

2. **Give the antagonist an on-page loss.** Whatever the final arc, the Assessor (or his operation) needs at least one scene where he is present and visibly loses — not implied by dark windows two beats later. A confrontation that happens off-screen cannot score as a climax no matter how well the surrounding beats are written.

3. **Close the Arcturus and Reyes threads or cut their setup.** Either dramatize Arcturus actually showing up to complicate the ending (paying off the ticking-clock logic built in Beat 4401), or strip the data-sharing exposition down to only what's needed to explain the 72-hour deadline. Either resolve Reyes's suspicion on-page or cut his conflict scene back to texture rather than open tension.

4. **Fix the internal continuity gap before any prose polish**: reconcile when the primary case is actually recovered (Beat 4888 vs. Beat 12), add the missing ACS-filing beat the dossier's irony depends on (or remove the claim), fill or delete the empty Beat 4884, and merge the duplicate footage-viewing content (4402/4918).
