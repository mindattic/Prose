## Structural Findings

1. **The story does not end where the bible says it's LOCKED to end.** SS-TEST-4 lock #4 ("Sternum
   touch, SK900... calls the number at 0600. LOCKED through-line closure") and the SS-TEST-5 spine's
   SK862.5 (SWAT finds the empty apartment, service locker, Meridian Cross First Class face-down) and
   SK900 (7-line comm, "CE-0217 closed non-actionable," sternum touch, "the city answered") **do not
   exist anywhere in the database** — I checked full temporal history (`FOR SYSTEM_TIME ALL`) and a
   text search across all Beats for "the city answered," "face-down," and "surgical tray"; nothing
   matches. This isn't a deletion — those beats were never authored. The live story instead ends at
   SortKey 900 (#4105) on Halcyon's 41-page motion to dismiss and a manifest showing the 43 named
   residents being disappeared to an unaddressed compound — an escalating, unresolved institutional
   threat, not the peaceful closure the bible locks. The Meridian Cross that beat #0 deliberately
   places "where he meant it to end up" (a plant, clearly meant for someone to find) is never found
   on-screen by anyone. **This is either a stale bible or a genuinely unfinished ending** — either
   way, the story cannot be scored against its own design doc as written, and a reader reaches a
   cliffhanger, not an ending.

2. **The testimony-delivery method contradicts itself.** Beat [670] (#4106, 0600) has Bear leave the
   three handwritten sheets in a secret drop location via the wall-gap passage — "out of his hands,"
   found by "the one set of hands meant to find them." But beat [700] (#4088, 1301) has him seated in
   the submission room with those same three sheets in front of him on the table, and beats [725]–
   [746.875] have him personally unfolding and reading from each of the three sheets during live
   testimony. He cannot have both mailed the only copy through a smuggling passage at dawn *and*
   personally carried and read from it at the hearing five hours later. Nothing in the text
   establishes a duplicate set. This also creates a motivation hole: the story's whole rationale for
   paper testimony is that "paper held still, paper didn't ping" — untraceable, safe. If he already
   has a secure, secret channel to get the pages into the record, the frontal walk into a surveilled,
   guarded building (two Sunder fights, Declan, Brandt) is unmotivated risk-taking that the text never
   justifies against the safer option it has just shown him using.

3. **The bible's cited SK-numbers for two locked lines don't match where those lines actually live.**
   SS-TEST-4 lock #1 places "We operated on the intelligence we were given" / the dropped clause at
   beat SK746.09375 (the "counting-as-interiority" beat). In the live DB, that line is spoken at
   SortKey 695 (#4122, the pre-hearing lobby confrontation) — a different beat, earlier in the
   sequence. SK746.09375 in the live text is actually Brandt's "forty-three enemy combatants
   deceased" testimony beat. Likewise SS-TEST-5's SK862.5 slot (SWAT/Meridian Cross) is occupied in
   the live DB by the unrelated "Orvenne" thread. The bible and the DB have drifted apart on beat
   identity, not just content — a doctor/digest pass would not catch this because it's a narrative
   mismatch, not a schema one.

4. **Two dangling setups with no in-story payoff.** (a) The "Orvenne" thread at [862.5] — a named,
   real GLMZ faction (`House Orvenne`) is invoked with real dramatic weight ("someone ran the thread
   backward," a name that means "a kind of ruin the Tier system doesn't have a number for") and then
   never mentioned again in the remaining two beats of the story. (b) The blocked sixteen-names beat
   at [748.4375] — Bear is explicitly, dramatically prevented from saying the names of the families he
   has paid for eight years, the emotional center of the entire piece, and the story never returns to
   this defeat: no reaction beat, no alternate route to naming them, no acknowledgment. Given the
   obligation/ledger theme is load-bearing for the whole story, leaving this the single moment it's
   raised and blocked, with zero follow-through, reads as an orphaned setup rather than an intentional
   restraint.

5. **The infiltration stacks an unusual number of "the system was already broken" conveniences.**
   Badge that still works because "nobody had pulled it" [670]; a fire alarm that "believed in an
   alarm that no longer existed" [680]; a wall seam an institution's route analysis never indexed,
   found only via a pet mouse; six fiscal quarters of deferred maintenance on the one gap Bear needs.
   Individually each is thematically on-point (institutional negligence is the story's argument), but
   stacked across three consecutive beats they remove essentially all physical jeopardy from getting
   into the building — every obstacle resolves via bureaucratic decay rather than earned skill or
   real risk. Combined with the "no gauntlet" lock (SS-TEST-4 #9), the infiltration risks reading as
   frictionless rather than "grounded."

6. **Minor: a hard timeline error.** Beat [697.5] (#4124), mid-Fight-2: "There was a room twelve
   hours out with his name on the docket." Every surrounding beat has established the panel as
   minutes away (13 minutes at [680], underway by [700] at 1301, current clock ~1250 in this beat).
   "Twelve hours" cannot be reconciled with the established clock; it reads like a leftover from an
   earlier draft's timeline.

7. **The "institutional justice" ending mechanism is intentional but still functions as authorial
   timing.** SK812.5's causal chain (maintenance flag opens 4 minutes after the submission hits
   the record; nobody escalates it; the decade-overdue NS-7 fails on its own six-plus hours later) is
   well-reasoned and thematically exact — not a random coincidence. But Bear does nothing to cause it,
   learns nothing new to trigger it, and the villain still dies punctually, same day, off-screen, with
   no input from the protagonist beyond having already filed the paperwork hours earlier. It is earned
   *thematically* but not earned *causally* by anything Bear actively does in the final act — worth
   flagging as a residual risk even though the design is locked and the reasoning holds up better than
   a typical deus-ex-machina.

8. **Not a courtroom-drama cliché — but check the "avoids cliché" claim actually holds given finding
   #1.** The bible is right that there's no cross-examination, no surprise witness, no gotcha reveal —
   Bear reads a prepared log, which is a genuine departure from genre convention and works well in the
   live testimony beats [725]–[746.875]. That structural strength is real. It just isn't connected to
   an ending; see finding #1.

## Verdict

**Not structurally sound at 90+ as currently live in the database.** The prose craft in the
individual beats — especially the testimony sequence and the salute/handshake payoff — is genuinely
strong and matches what the bible claims. But the story does not have an ending: it stops on an
escalating, unresolved institutional threat that contradicts the bible's own LOCKED closing beats,
which don't exist in the DB at any point in its history. A score of 91.9 recorded against a node that
has no third act resolution and two live contradictions (testimony delivered two incompatible ways;
Brandt-lobby quote misfiled against its own spine number) suggests either the review evaluated a
different text than what's currently live, or evaluators did not weight ending-completeness and
internal consistency the way the bible's own locks imply they should.

Structural changes needed (no prose-level notes):
1. **Resolve the ending mismatch.** Either author the bible's LOCKED SK862.5/SK900 beats (SWAT finds
   the empty apartment / sternum touch / non-actionable closure) so the story actually delivers the
   closure it claims to be locked to, or formally retire that lock in the bible and let the "Orvenne"
   /dismissal-motion cliffhanger stand as the real ending — but then the story needs its own resolution
   beat, not a hard stop mid-motion with a 14-day deadline hanging.
2. **Reconcile the testimony-delivery contradiction.** Pick one mechanism — either the wall-gap paper
   drop [670] or the in-person hand-delivered testimony [700]–[746.875] — and cut or rewrite the other
   so only one is true.
3. **Either pay off or explicitly cut the sixteen-names-blocked beat [748.4375].** As written it
   raises the story's central emotional stake and answers it with silence.
4. **Re-sync the bible's SK-numbers to the live SortKeys** (or vice versa) so locks reference the beats
   that actually carry the locked content — SK746.09375 and SK862.5 currently point at the wrong scenes.
