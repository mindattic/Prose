## Structural Findings

1. **The story's central reveal contradicts its own binding lock, and the contradiction is never
   resolved.** Bible Lock #1 (`docs/nodes/NxR.md` §7.1) states in bold terms: *"Ohara locked the
   partition herself. The reveal is not Axiom, not PEREGRINE, not a third party. She did it."*
   But Beat 19/20 (#4840, #4903) has Stave explicitly tell Ohara, on the page: *"The framework
   inside this partition is not yours, Dr. Ohara. Someone built this inside you before you locked
   it. They were using your partition structure as a container for something they needed to
   move."* That is a third party, stated outright, using Ohara's body as a "vessel" without her
   knowledge — the exact reveal the lock forbids. Then Beat 21 (#4841) simply drops this thread and
   reveals something unrelated (Ohara's own manifest of rescued Reads) as if the "someone built
   this inside you" research-platform material never happened. The "pre-Atlas research platform,
   2213, used you as a vessel" plant is never explained, never connects to the manifest, and never
   resurfaces in Act Four or in CxC's bible. It is a fully loaded gun (mysterious third party,
   body-as-container language, "what was done to your body") that is never fired. Either this beat
   pair needs to be rewritten so the inner hardware is legibly Ohara's own historical work (honoring
   the lock), or the third-party vessel thread needs an actual payoff — currently it just sits in
   the story contradicting its own bible and confusing the "she did it" theme the whole strand is
   built around.

2. **Adalemo's Act Three turn is asserted, not earned — he has no prior appearance or characterization
   in the strand before the beat where he defects.** He is introduced for the first time in Beat 22
   (#4842), already mid-defection. His motive is never stated by him; it's supplied by Vox's
   internal guess: *"the only man who'd burn his own confirmation to watch me choose is one who
   already knew what twenty-one names in the cold were worth before he ever put his hand on that
   switch."* Nothing earlier in the strand gives Adalemo a reason to already know or care about the
   21 — no earlier scene, no line of dialogue relayed secondhand, nothing. CxC's bible needs
   "Adalemo finishes crossing" (CxC §4/§7) to land as a completed arc, but NxR gives it no starting
   point to complete from. As delivered, his behavior (killing his own crew's reference signal
   mid-procedure to run a private "test" of Vox, then handing the crew a walk-away) also cuts
   against PEREGRINE's own stated urgency (Beat 6: "they've already decided what's behind it is
   worth more cracked than studied") — sabotaging your own operation's data feed to satisfy your
   own curiosity about an operator you've never met is not the behavior of the competent antagonist
   organization the bible promises (MxG Lock #7: "PEREGRINE is competent").

3. **The book drops the genre engine its own bible commits to.** NxR's bible (§1) explicitly locks
   in: *"HEIST register — same crew, same competence-porn core."* MxG delivers on that promise with
   a bridge explosion, a storm-deck firefight, a VTOL hijack. CxC's plan delivers a body-bank
   infiltration and a facility takedown. NxR's actual beats, by contrast, are almost entirely talk,
   waiting, and internal monologue: the "heist" consists of finding four people, negotiating a debt
   in dialogue, prepping a room, and then sitting still for two acts while Vox has an internal
   experience the reader can't see anyone else react to. Boiler (demolitions/heavy weapons) draws a
   floor plan and drives a truck. Scout (drone infiltration specialist) holds a stairwell and sends
   pings. Lace (master social engineer) gets one cover-story line. None of the crew's signature
   competencies are exercised under visible external pressure the way MxG's finale used every
   single one of them. The climax itself (Beat 22) is resolved not by the crew's skill but by the
   antagonist unilaterally choosing mercy — nobody on the crew does anything to earn the exit. This
   is very likely the single biggest reason the book plateaus below MxG/CxC: it is structurally a
   two-hander bottle drama wearing a heist bible.

4. **A major stake is set up and never paid off: whether Ohara survives the procedure intact.**
   Beat 11 (#4836) spends real weight establishing that a prior partition-crack left a person
   "worse than dead... missing the part that knew it was missing anything," and that this
   specific procedure is "survivable, probably." That risk is carried through Act Three
   (Beat 18/19, the reference-tone failure, the wall's misbehavior) as the central physical stake
   for Ohara's body. The story never shows the reader Ohara's condition after the procedure — she
   is present at the end of Beat 19 ("very quiet, very controlled") and then not seen again on the
   page. Beat 23 and 24 (the extraction and the diner) never confirm whether she came through
   whole. For a stake this heavily foreshadowed (with the specific horror-image of "asked a
   question they'd already asked"), leaving it unresolved reads as a dropped thread, not
   intentional ambiguity.

5. **A beat is sequenced out of chronological order in the delivered story, producing an unsignaled
   flashback during the climax.** Interstitial #4902 (SortKey 475) describes the crew's *arrival
   and setup* — "Lace had been in first... two hours before the rest of them," Stave's 32-minute
   calibration, Ohara being seated — and is placed in NodeBeats *after* #4916 (SortKey 462.5,
   PEREGRINE detected) and #4972 (SortKey 468.75, the door fight with Rook's knuckles scraped).
   Read in delivery order, PEREGRINE is already breaching the building and Rook is already injured
   before the story cuts back to "Lace had been in first, as arranged" with no flashback marker.
   This reads as a SortKey assignment error from the beat-expansion process, not an intentional
   non-linear structure — it needs to be re-sorted (or reframed with an explicit time marker) so
   the setup precedes the assault.

6. **Minor continuity/plausibility gaps worth flagging alongside the above:**
   - The partition's creation date is inconsistent: the bible (§3) says Ohara "created [it] eighteen
     months ago"; the delivered dialogue in Beat 5 (#4832) has Ohara say "when we walked it off two
     years ago." Six months of drift on the story's own central clock.
   - Lace's "eleven days vs. nine days" secret (Beat 13/#4837) is built up across two books as
     something that will change "the crew's position on the board" (bible §6, Beat 8 goal), but the
     actual content that surfaces is a two-day estimate discrepancy from a reading taken roughly two
     years prior — underpowered relative to the weight the setup promises, and implausible on its
     own terms (an 18-month decay curve isn't legible to single-day precision from a reading taken
     at its start).
   - The ending's central image — "Six. At the Sojourn it had been five. She had miscounted." —
     never establishes what Rook is counting (money? people? something else), which makes the
     capstone beat the bible calls for ("the number is different this time... what is different is
     not just the amount," §8) illegible on the page. It also appears to use "the Sojourn" as a
     *different, prior* diner, while CxC's bible (§8) refers to "NxR's Sojourn" as if it *is* this
     diner — a naming collision worth resolving before CxC locks its own final-diner callback.

## Verdict

**Not structurally capable of 90+ as written.** The score ceiling isn't a prose problem — it's that
the central mystery contradicts its own lock and is abandoned mid-story (#1), the antagonist's
pivotal choice has no character foundation to stand on (#2), and the book substitutes talk for the
heist-mechanics engine its own bible promises and the other two trilogy entries deliver (#3). Any one
of these caps the ceiling; together they explain the gap to MxG/CxC's 93+.

The four changes that would get it there:

1. **Re-thread the partition reveal so it stops contradicting Lock #1.** Cut or rewrite the "someone
   built this inside you... used you as a vessel" material (Beats 19/20) so the inner hardware is
   legibly Ohara's own prior work — or, if the third-party plant is worth keeping, give it an actual
   payoff (connect it to the manifest, to Stave, or explicitly hand it to CxC as a new seeded
   mystery) instead of dropping it one beat later.

2. **Give Adalemo one real appearance before his Act Three turn**, even a short one in Act One or
   Two (on Vox's channel sweep, in Ohara's briefing, wherever fits) that plants a specific reason he
   might already be tracking the 21 or already souring on PEREGRINE's purchase-order logic. His
   Beat 22 defection needs to pay off something the reader watched, not something Vox guesses about
   him after the fact.

3. **Restore at least one genuine competence-porn set-piece where the crew — not the antagonist's
   mercy — earns the exit.** Give Boiler, Scout, or Lace a skill-based obstacle to physically clear
   under time pressure in Act Three or Four (the sealed blast door, the compromised stairwell, and
   the four-person PEREGRINE stack are all sitting right there, already set up, and currently go
   nowhere because Adalemo simply lets everyone walk).

4. **Land the Ohara-survives-intact beat on the page.** One scene (even short) confirming her
   condition after the procedure closes the strand's most heavily foreshadowed physical stake and
   removes a hanging question the reader is never allowed to resolve.

(Secondary/mechanical: re-sort #4902 ahead of #4916/#4972, and reconcile the 18-months/two-years
partition-age numbers before the next revision pass.)
