## Outline

1. **The Offer** — Z3 hotel bar; Gault presents a five-person Axiom BioNanics extraction, Φ40,000 split five ways (Φ8,000 each); Rook agrees before she finishes deciding to. Establishes the exits-counting habit and the unnamed "Wennick" wound.
2. **Assembly** — Rook recruits Agwu (Lace), Väinämöinen (Boiler), Okafor (Vox), Diallo (Scout) in sequence; each takes the job for a personal reason; Rook — unusually — tells all four that the client routing feels wrong before the job even starts.
3. **Legwork** — Surveillance of Compound 7: the 90-second camera blind (a genuine, documented institutional gap, not an arbitrary one), badge colors, guard rotation, a dishonest-but-predictable log. Rook flags two loose threads: Soraya's abnormally fast, "someone set a priority flag" pod transit, and a contractor team suspended from Axiom's vendor list "seven weeks ago" for no documented reason — she boxes the second one explicitly.
4. **The Plan** — Cheap rented room; the extraction plan is walked through: Lace as ISB inspector, Vox's camera blind, Rook walks Soraya out a side door. "It is simple." Hard no-improvisation rule stated.
5/6. **The Complication / The Counter-Client** — Opens already in a moving van with Ohara aboard; the lobby confrontation (the spine's advertised reversal beat) has already happened off-page. Ohara reveals the extraction was hers to order — she hired the crew first, eleven days before Axiom's cutout reached Gault — that the formula lives only in her neuretics, and that Axiom is closing in on the same Class V military pathway independently. Destination becomes the Lake Platform. Vox reports PEREGRINE flagged the badge exception "before the door finished closing." Crew agrees to continue.
7. **The Pursuit Opens** — Bloom Quarter transit corridor ambush. Boiler drops the close-quarters specialist into a column; a second PEREGRINE shooter fires through the space where Ohara's head was and kills a bystander vendor instead — no hesitation, no line drawn for civilians. Rook freezes for a half-second; the shooter clocks it and doesn't exploit it. Vox reports a "ghost" — a tap on the crew's own digital residue, "bait," pre-deployed. Crew is tailed through the following blocks (4905).
8. **The Bridge** — Boiler reads two PEREGRINE vehicles boxing them onto an elevated freight bridge and detonates a rented resonance driver against a support column the 2024 stress report had already flagged; the section drops with the empty forward PEREGRINE hauler in it (personnel evacuated in time). PEREGRINE goes full analog, burning its own network signature.
9. **The Safe House** — Ohara discloses the full scope: eleven months quietly deleting internal file copies, eight months watching Axiom's filing queue, and that Axiom is roughly fourteen months from an independent Class V military filing. She confirms PEREGRINE already knew about this safe house before the crew arrived. Lace confirms, off five words, that Ohara holds one backup copy of the data. Vox and Rook have the "Wennick" scene — his name said aloud for the first time in three years.
10. **Burned** — The water heater stops ticking (Boiler's tell); PEREGRINE breaches at night. Rook pulls Ohara toward the exit; a PEREGRINE point man grabs her wrist and is put down; Vox shoots the second breacher and takes a graze on the arm. Crew gets out.
11. **Gault** — Station-bar confrontation. Ohara draws a distinction between a lie and a wrong assessment; Rook rejects the distinction as beside the point, then sits down with him anyway.
12. **The Approach** — Bay doors open on a building Lake Michigan storm; the crew's "maintenance VTOL" is already being prepped, no acquisition shown. Scout scouts the platform via crawler: twelve personnel total, four PEREGRINE, eight Cooperative maintenance crew locked in deck 2 labs. Roles assigned; crew launches into the storm (4907).

Boiler downs the close-quarters specialist at the crane base; Lace's voice-spoof of Commander Adalemo redirects a PEREGRINE unit; Rook downs the network operator in a blacked-out deck 2 corridor; a "fourth PEREGRINE member" goes for Ohara and loses her when she independently repositions behind a structural column; Rook and Adalemo fight to a standstill on a failing railing, exchange an extended dialogue about "the line," and he goes into the lake on a survival float; Ohara reaches the terminal and publishes inside the four-minute authentication window; Boiler swings the crane to a firing position on deck 3 and is then told to stand down without firing; Rook runs and jumps onto the departing PEREGRINE VTOL's skid, climbs in, and orders the pilot to land on deck 1 — he complies without visible coercion beyond her presence. ## Structural Findings

1. **Finale roster doesn't add up — a PEREGRINE body is in two places at once (Beat 13).** The bible and Scout's own headcount (Beat 12: "Four PEREGRINE") fix the unit at exactly four people: commander (Adalemo), close-quarters specialist, network/signal operator, driver/pilot. Beat 13 requires five simultaneous actors from that roster: the specialist (downed by Boiler on deck 1), the network operator (downed by Rook in the deck-2 corridor), Adalemo (the railing duel), "the fourth PEREGRINE member" who chases Ohara on deck 3, *and* the VTOL driver/pilot who is explicitly described "holding off deck 1... reading altitude, waiting for Adalemo's extraction call" for the length of the entire firefight. The driver cannot be flying position-hold in the cockpit the whole time and also be on foot on deck 3 chasing Ohara. This is a hard bookkeeping contradiction, not a matter of interpretation.

2. **The bible's own promised money-shot is set up three times and never delivered (Beat 13).** Scout confirms the deck-1 crane is operational; Boiler swings it to maximum extension and gets into a firing position at the deck 3 viewport, exactly matching the finale image the bible itself promises ("Boiler on a crane arm, firing through viewport glass, doing something to the deck that the Lake Platform maintenance team will be discussing for years" — §2; Lock #5). Rook then radios "Stand down. Platform's clear," and Boiler never fires. Lock #5 is technically satisfied by the bridge (Beat 8), but the story spends three separate story-beats of setup on a Platform-specific payoff that fizzles with no page-time acknowledgment that it fizzled.

3. **"Seven weeks" is an orphaned plant (Beat 3).** Rook explicitly flags a contractor team pulled from Axiom's approved-vendor list seven weeks earlier with "no documented incident," writes *seven weeks*, and boxes it — narrative language that signals "this matters." It is never mentioned again in Beats 4–14. Either this needs a payoff (does it explain PEREGRINE's readiness? A prior leak? A second mole?) or it shouldn't be dramatized as a held clue.

4. **Ohara's "one backup copy" undercuts the finale's stakes and is never used (Beat 9).** Lace asks; Ohara confirms one backup exists; nothing more is said. If a backup exists, the entire climax's tension — Ohara must personally authenticate at the terminal inside a four-minute window, or the mission fails (Beat 13) — is not actually a hard fail state. The story never shows the backup being surrendered, destroyed, or otherwise taken off the table, so the audience is left holding a safety net the plot never acknowledges either way.

5. **Payment arithmetic contradicts Lock #3 as currently written (Beats 5/6 vs. 14).** The job is contracted at Φ40,000 split five ways through Gault's Axiom-linked channel (Beat 1). Once the real stakes surface, Ohara promises "a second escrow, structured identically to the first" (Beat 5/6) — i.e., a second Φ40,000. Beat 14 shows exactly one payment landing, Φ8,000/person. The story never states whether the original Axiom-side escrow was ever paid, cancelled, or clawed back, so it's unclear whether the crew's total take is Φ40k or Φ80k. Lock #3 treats the exact figure as thematically load-bearing ("This is the exact amount. It is enough.") — the ambiguity needs to close on the page, not by omission.

6. **The pivotal reversal beat is elided, not dramatized (Beat 5/6).** The spine's own Beat 5 ("Ohara is waiting in the lobby; she hired them first; the data is encoded in her neuretics") is the story's advertised twist. As delivered, the scene opens after the fact, in a moving van, with Ohara already extracted — the confrontation in the lobby and the actual walk-out under the camera blind happen entirely off-page and are only relayed in retrospective dialogue. The story's biggest reversal is told, not shown.

7. **The meticulously staged extraction plan turns out not to matter (Beats 4, 5/6, 7).** Beat 4 spends real craft on an "elegant, minimal-violence" cover (ISB inspector badge, camera blind, guard-rotation timing). Beat 5/6 immediately reveals PEREGRINE flagged the badge exception "before the door finished closing" — they were caught in real time regardless of tradecraft — and Beat 7 escalates this to a pre-installed tap on the crew's own digital residue, explicitly called "bait... pre-deployed." If Axiom was watching Soraya rather than reacting to the crew's cleverness, the Plan beat's competence-porn never gets to pay off dramatically; the crew's skill is narratively inert against this particular threat.

8. **The "pre-deployed bait" claim has no supporting mechanism (Beat 7).** For Axiom to have "pre-deployed" bait against this specific crew's digital signature, they would need advance knowledge of Rook's crew before the job began — but Vox's reconnaissance only starts in Beat 3 (Legwork), the earliest point any Axiom system could plausibly have detected them, and Gault's routing is independently described as "clean, used four times." Rook's conclusion ("too available... a frame and not a fee") is delivered with narrative confidence but the causal chain that would justify it — how Axiom set a trap for a crew it couldn't have known about in advance — is never supplied.

9. **PEREGRINE underperforms its own established competence at both of its two biggest tests (Beats 10, 13), which risks Lock #7.** (a) In Beat 10, a four-person corp-black unit that *bought the safe-house address in advance* — full prior knowledge, time to plan containment — executes a two-body breach, loses one operator, and achieves no capture against a mostly non-combatant crew of five. (b) In Beat 13, Adalemo — introduced as disciplined ex-Special-Operations — spends the platform's most dangerous moment (a failing railing over open water, his subordinates being taken down one by one, the asset already at the terminal) on an extended philosophical negotiation rather than disengaging or pressing an advantage. Lock #7 requires the crew to win by being better, not by PEREGRINE being switched off at the moments the plot needs them to lose.

10. **The "hijack" the bible promises never happens on the page (Beat 12).** The logline (§0) states "the crew hijacks a maintenance VTOL," but Beat 12 opens with Boiler already working the fueling panel of a VTOL that is simply available at an unspecified facility with open bay doors — no acquisition, negotiation, or theft is shown. This is the one major resource in the story that costs nothing to obtain, in a story that otherwise insists every resource (Lace's badge, Vox's licensed agents, Boiler's rented resonance driver) is earned and paid for on the page.

11. **Series-canon terminology break: "Rider" (Beats 9, 12).** The prose repeatedly labels Scout/Diallo's profession "Rider" ("nobody outside the trade understood about Riders," "a Rider never rode without people she trusted," "the softest target... would be the Rider lying defenseless," "You didn't interrupt a Rider who'd ejected"). Series canon (SS-A38, job-name canon) retired "Rider" in favor of Exo (street) / RFO (corporate) / Jockey (alt slang) — and this story's own bible (§5a) defines Scout's profession using exactly those retired-compliant terms and never uses "Rider." The prose contradicts both the trilogy-wide canon lock and its own bible, on the page, for a load-bearing crew role used across two major beats.

12. **A second unresolved procedural clue (Beat 3).** Rook builds a small mystery around Soraya's abnormally fast, apparently protected pod-transit response ("either someone was protecting Soraya, or someone was keeping her on a leash"). Ohara's Beat 5/6 confession covers the escrow, the cutout, and the neuretics encoding, but never explains who set the standing priority flag on her transit account or why. Minor next to the "seven weeks" plant, but it's a second flagged-then-dropped procedural thread in the same beat.

## Verdict

Not structurally sound at a defensible 90+ yet — the prose and voice work are genuinely excellent, but a strict contradiction/cliché pass surfaces objective errors, not just judgment calls: the finale has a hard body-count contradiction (Finding 1), a fully-built-and-abandoned set-piece (Finding 2), two orphaned Chekhov's guns (Findings 3–4), an unresolved payment figure against a binding Lock (Finding 5), and a locked-canon terminology violation (Finding 11). The 93.9 score likely reflects register/prose quality rather than structural integrity — those are different axes, and this story hasn't been checked on the second one until now.

**Structural changes needed (pick 2–4, in priority order):**

1. Fix the finale force-accounting (Finding 1) — either give PEREGRINE a fifth body (and update the bible's "four personnel" fact plus Scout's headcount) or reassign roles so the driver/pilot and the "fourth member" chasing Ohara are the same, non-simultaneous person.
2. Deliver Boiler's crane/viewport payoff on the page, or cut the three-beat setup toward it (Finding 2) — Lock #5 is already satisfied by the bridge, so the setup is pure debt if unpaid.
3. Close or cut the two orphaned plants — "seven weeks" (Finding 3) and the backup copy (Finding 4) — before this story goes through plant-audit.
4. Reconcile the payment total against Lock #3 (Finding 5): state on the page whether the crew collects one Φ40,000 split or two, and what happened to the original Axiom-side escrow.

Additionally (canon compliance, not optional): replace "Rider" with Exo/Jockey/RFO per SS-A38 (Finding 11) before this ships alongside NxR/CxC.
