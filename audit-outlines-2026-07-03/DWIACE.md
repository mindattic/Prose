## Structural Findings

1. **The book's stated Law #0 ("the book opens with Celeste") is violated by the current assembly.**
   The chapter titled "Cel" — the mandated opening — is the *last* chapter a reader reaches (ordinals
   564–569, beats 4052–4055). The reader currently opens on Isaac's office (beat 3392) and does not
   meet Celeste's own POV until 83% through the book (beat 3959). The bible's "Illusion Requirement"
   (§SS-DWIACE-1: "every Celeste chapter must make the audience doubt the investigators' version of
   reality... if the reader isn't at least momentarily believing Jace is real, the story collapses")
   is structurally impossible to achieve when the reader already knows the outcome (Celeste rescued,
   89% Scattered) before ever spending a page inside her POV believing the voice is real.

2. **The single largest set-piece in the book — the Scatter-interrupt countdown (41%→89%) — is cut
   in half by a spurious chapter boundary.** The counter starts inside "Voluntary Recall" (beat 3666,
   41%) and continues uninterrupted through "The Same Cold" (beats 3683–3789, 48%→89%). These are
   the same continuous scene wearing two different chapter titles, neither of which describes what's
   actually happening in it (per bible, "Voluntary Recall" = corpo burial/finale; "The Same Cold" =
   Tamsin reading Sol's death scene). This is very likely a chapter-boundary/IsChapterStart mislabel,
   not an authorial choice — nothing in the prose marks a scene break at 3667.

3. **"The Same Cold" is 249 of 569 beats (44% of the entire book)** and contains at least four
   distinct, tonally different threads under one chapter header: the climax rescue, the Sol
   discovery/aftermath, Voss's Feliksas Burokas informant-recruitment backstory (chronologically
   prior to the raid, placed after it with no flashback signal), and Celeste's home-departure
   sequence (which is Ch1 material). Compare to "The Intake" (1 beat), "The Convergence" (4 beats),
   "No Signal" (6 beats), "The Surfacing" (9 beats) — the pacing weight is wildly non-proportional to
   the bible's per-chapter descriptions, and the imbalance is not "big climax chapter earns extra
   length," it's four different chapters' worth of unrelated material dumped in one bucket.

4. **Canon contradiction on Sol Castellanos's "same want" mirror logic.** Bible §SS-DWIACE-2 states
   Sol is "the same want as Celeste (reach the dead boyfriend)... Sol and Celeste are mirrors" (locked
   narrative rule #4). But the prose (beat ~3800, Tamsin/Teller dialogue: *"You have a name." "Mateo,"
   she says. "A brother, not a boyfriend. Dead before her. She died reaching for him."*) establishes
   Sol's lost person as a **dead brother**, not a boyfriend. This directly undercuts the "same want"
   parallel the reveal pivot depends on — the mirror is explicitly broken by the text that's supposed
   to prove it.

5. **The actual finale is missing, not just misplaced.** The true "Voluntary Recall" corpo-burial
   content (Rennick's resolve not to let Meridian "write the ending") exists as exactly one orphaned
   beat (4968) with a ~600-number gap from its nearest neighbor (4392). Given the pattern established
   by the Celeste child-strand beats (large gaps = content that lives in an unlinked child strand),
   this strongly suggests the majority of the real finale chapter is not currently linked into the
   book node's NodeBeats bridge at all — the book, as scored, may not actually contain a complete
   ending.

6. **Voss's backstory thread (beats 3904–3919, turning Feliksas Burokas) is placed after the raid it
   sets up, with no flashback marker.** A reader hits "he had put the word out... quietly" describing
   how Voss got his under-city intel *after* already reading the raid that used that intel. Either a
   flashback signal is missing or these beats are simply out of Number order relative to the scene
   they causally precede.

7. **"The Intake" chapter is a 1-beat phantom.** The bible describes it as the client-intake chapter
   (EP1); in the current assembly it's a single establishing beat, and the actual intake scene
   (Douglas Hartley, the retainer, Mei-Lin's note) is interleaved as unmarked beats inside "What the
   Room Holds" and "What She Wouldn't Do." If this is an intentional three-thread crosscut (Rennick/
   Douglas conversation + Teller's message analysis + Tamsin's room read, advancing in parallel), it
   isn't visible as such — there's no ordinal signal separating "crosscut technique" from "chapter
   mislabel," and it produces the same downstream symptom as findings #1–#3: chapter titles that
   don't match their content's center of gravity.

8. **Corvin Adaora is never called "Corvin" in the beat text sampled — always "Teller."** Likely an
   intentional in-fiction handle (parallel to Voss/"Analog"), but the strand bible never documents
   "Teller" as Corvin's working alias, which makes this audit (and presumably any future reviewer or
   continuity check) have to reverse-engineer the mapping from dialogue. Minor, but it's a
   documentation gap sitting on top of an otherwise-load-bearing character identity.

## Verdict

**Not structurally capable of holding 90+ as currently assembled.** The 90.6–90.95 score on record
was almost certainly produced by a review that scored chapters/beats for prose quality without
surfacing what a straight cover-to-cover read actually delivers: the mandated opening POV arrives
last, the single biggest set-piece is bisected by a mislabeled chapter break, one chapter swallows
44% of the book across four unrelated threads, and the mirror-reveal the whole B-plot depends on
(Sol = same want as Celeste) is contradicted by name in the dialogue that's supposed to prove it.
None of this is a prose problem — it is beat sequencing, chapter-boundary, and NodeBeats-linkage
work, consistent with the bible's own open item (DWIACE-US-5, "Celeste child strands merged... for
full dual-POV effect").

**Required structural changes, in priority order:**

1. **Re-sequence the book strand** so `cel-019ec965`, `what-she-asked-for-019ec965`, and
   `the-ghost-period-019ec965` are interleaved at their intended positions (roughly chapters 1, 4,
   and 7 of 12), not appended after the climax and (nominal) finale. This is the single highest-value
   fix — everything else is secondary until Celeste's POV opens the book.

2. **Split the false "Voluntary Recall" / "The Same Cold" boundary.** Move the continuous
   status-panel countdown (beats 3666–3789) into one climax chapter with an accurate title (this is
   "The Surfacing," not either of its current two labels). Reserve "Voluntary Recall" exclusively for
   post-case corpo-burial content.

3. **Audit and complete the NodeBeats linkage for the finale.** Beat 4968 is orphaned by a ~600-Number
   gap; confirm whether the real "Voluntary Recall" chapter's beats exist and are simply unlinked, or
   whether the finale needs to be written/expanded. Either way, the book cannot be verified to end
   properly until this is resolved.

4. **Resolve the Sol Castellanos mirror contradiction** (brother "Mateo" vs. bible's "dead boyfriend")
   before any further prose work — decide which is canon and align the reveal-pivot dialogue and the
   bible entry to match, since narrative lock #4 depends on the parallel being real.
