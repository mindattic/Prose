## Structural Findings

1. **The manuscript does not deliver its own locked ending — this is the load-bearing finding.** §7 of the bible locks the finale as: Orison ships the Phase II announcement to bury the 8-second broadcast; Ch26 (now 27) is Nuru declining to show up for her Tuesday 9AM calibration; the book's last beat is "whoever Amara or Seto sees in the lobby at 10:47 — the person who was supposed to show up for their visit and didn't." None of that is in the database. Chapters 25–27 instead open a brand-new, completely unforeshadowed thread — an anonymous caller referencing "three segments that didn't run" and a previously nonexistent location ("the Glooms," a name that appears nowhere else in the 27 chapters or in the bible's World Texture section) — and the book stops with Amara walking out the door to meet him. The two-hander's entire promised payoff (Amara and Seto physically reuniting at the fountain, Tuesday 9AM → 10:47) never happens on the page. The bible's own user-story log claims "MNEMO-US-7 ✅ Act 3 (Ch23–28) written per §7 design," which is not true of what's actually stored. A score of 90.3 attached to a book that doesn't finish its own plot is not a score that will survive a reread that reaches the last page.
2. **The pivotal Act 2 turn is reported, not shown.** The bible calls out "razor payoff (open blade)" as the key image of Ch19 ("The Turn") — Ciro forcing Amara onto leave. In the actual chapter, Ciro doesn't appear; the confrontation (razor open on the desk, the phrase "loss mitigation," Ciro provisioning her access and watching what she touched) is relayed a chapter later as dialogue summary between Amara and Seto ("She told him about the razor open on the desk..."). The most dramatically loaded beat in Act 2 — the moment the "genuinely torn between Ciro and Seto" tension is supposed to snap — happens entirely offscreen.
3. **Ekow Ato's introduction is missing from the manuscript.** Ch11 refers back to "Ekow Ato had given him seven [days] at the Seam crossing" as an already-completed event, complete with remembered detail (Ekow's pause before naming the number). No chapter in the current 1–27 sequence contains that scene. It exists only in the orphaned/superseded draft material (beat "Minimum Footprint," id 4163, part of the 14-beat compressed draft that predates the chapter split and is not attached to the current chapter sequence). A first-time reader meets Ekow only by inference, after the fact, in someone else's recollection — his entire introduction scene was lost in a prior restructuring pass.
4. **A covert, non-consensual, unattended calibration event is introduced and never explained or reused (Ch6).** Amara finds a "Zone 8, October 2224" calibration visit on her own health record that her transit logs prove she never physically attended, with her own certification ID (nonsensically) filed in the technician-of-record field. If Orison can insert fabricated calibration records for visits that never physically happened, that is a materially bigger and scarier capability than "the sub-protocol runs during scheduled in-person visits" (the bible's stated mechanism in §1), and it's never mentioned again or reconciled with how the suppression protocol is described everywhere else (technician Pär, in-chair visits, calibration suites). Either this is a plot hole (the mechanism can't do what Ch6 shows it doing) or it's a bigger horror than the book ever cashes in.
5. **An unexplained real-time surveillance channel is introduced in Ch1 and then quietly dropped.** Amara's own account auto-generates and routes a search query to Orison's partnership desk without her input, discovered the same evening the pattern first crystallizes for her — before Ciro has even been introduced as a character. This is a distinct mechanism from the bleed and from Ciro's later, established methods (proximity-triggered query suspension in Ch17, batch wellness reports, editor-database entry). It's never named, sourced, or connected to the rest of the surveillance apparatus, and it directly undercuts the tension of every later "clean log" sequence (Ch10's staged evidence transfer, Ch13's checkpoint alibi) — if Orison already silently mirrors her searches in real time, "looking routine" shouldn't be sufficient cover, and the story never addresses why it apparently is.
6. **A dangling, unaddressed emotional plant: Seto's "person he never stopped missing" (Ch3, Fragment 22).** The bleed carries Amara a wave of grief for someone unnamed — "the missing worn into something that was no longer sharp but was not gone," a name "held low... worn smooth." Nothing in the character bible establishes a lost partner, parent, or other grief-object for Seto beyond his living sister Nuru, and this fragment is never picked back up, named, or connected to anything later (it isn't Nuru — she's alive, and his feeling for her elsewhere is described as "gravity," not grief-for-loss). This reads as a plant with no payoff: either a costly detail invented for one chapter's texture and abandoned, or a thread that was meant to matter and got lost in restructuring, same as the Ekow scene.
7. **Ciro disappears from the page for the back half of Act 2 despite being the load-bearing antagonist.** After Ch17 ("Off Timing"), Ciro does not appear on-page again in the remaining chapters supplied (18–27) — his forced-leave ultimatum, the razor, and "loss mitigation" line are all relayed after the fact in Ch20. For a character the bible insists the reader must be "genuinely torn between... through Act 2," his sudden absence right when the antagonist pressure should be peaking (paired with Finding 2) hollows out the antagonist function exactly where it needs to be strongest.
8. **Chapters 7–9 are one scene, chopped into three chapter-numbered beats, with almost no plot movement.** All three cover a single continuous newsroom pastry-and-conversation exchange (Guzman talking up a Q4 "access" proposal) with no scene break, no time skip, and no decision or discovery beyond Amara's internal reframing of the word "access." Padding chapter count this way thins Act 1's momentum right where the bible's own scorecard already flags Ch7–9 as the weakest (80.6–82.6, "minor polish"/"emotional register lift needed") — the weakness is structural (nothing happens), not a prose-polish problem, and no amount of line-editing fixes a chapter where the only event is eating a pastry.
9. **Extreme, unexplained chapter-length variance suggests uneven drafting, not intentional pacing.** Finished, fully-dramatized chapters run 10,000–15,000+ characters (Ch1–4, Ch11, Ch13, Ch15); several Act 2/3 chapters that are billed as pivotal ("Grooming," "Pressure and Counter-Pressure," "The Turn," "Contract Delayed") run 1,800–3,100 characters — roughly a fifth the length, with no corresponding drop in stated dramatic importance. "Grooming" in particular is described in the amendment log as having absorbed a merged, meaningful "gathering-warmth → floor-32 provisioning trap" arc from a cut chapter (Second Suitor); what's actually stored is an ~300-word phone call with no floor-32 scene at all. The merge appears to have been recorded in the docs but never actually executed in the prose.
10. **The suppression mechanism's stated diagnostic-evasion property is asserted, never dramatized as a cost or risk to the protagonists.** The bible (§1, "Visible only as absence") and Ch10 both state the sub-protocol reshapes surrounding architecture so drift "no longer registers on a standard diagnostic read" — meaning any character who checks their own status would see "clean." Yet no protagonist is ever shown fearing or checking their own diagnostic (Amara doesn't re-run her own read after learning she's Row 312; Seto never checks his own four-months-past calibration for tampering, despite the bible stating his own protocol ran three years ago "as a formality"). Given both leads are themselves recipients, the absence of any on-page moment where either one turns the diagnostic question on themselves is a missed consequence, not just an omission — it's the single most personally frightening implication of the antagonist's power, sitting unused.

## Verdict

**Not structurally capable of holding 90+ as currently stored.** A score in that range implies a complete, earned story; what's in the database is an unfinished plot wearing a finished chapter count. The single disqualifying issue is Finding 1 — the last three chapters abandon the locked ending and stop on an unresolved new subplot — and no amount of prose polish above it changes that verdict. Recommended structural (not prose-level) changes, in priority order:

1. **Write the actual locked ending.** Replace chapters 25–27 (or extend to new chapters if needed) with the §7 design: Phase II ships publicly; Nuru's Tuesday 9AM non-appearance; the Zone 7 fountain / 10:47 scene. Either deliver this, or if the mystery-caller thread is meant to survive, it needs to be planted at least once earlier in the book and reconciled with (not instead of) the Nuru/fountain payoff — right now it reads as a different, unstarted book grafted onto this one's last pages.
2. **Restore or replace the missing Ekow Ato introduction scene.** The character cannot be established solely by other characters' after-the-fact recollection of an event the reader never saw; either reinstate a version of the orphaned "Minimum Footprint" scene into the live chapter sequence or write a new on-page first meeting.
3. **Move the Ch19 razor/forced-leave confrontation on-page**, in its own scene with Ciro present, rather than leaving it as dialogue-summary a chapter later — this is the antagonist's decisive move and the book currently skips filming it.
4. **Resolve or cut the Ch6 phantom-visit mechanism and the Ch1 real-time-query-interception mechanism.** Pick one consistent account of how surveillance/tampering works (in-person visit + sub-protocol only, per §1) and either bring these two chapters in line with it or promote them to an intentional, addressed escalation of Orison's capability rather than an unexplained one-off.
