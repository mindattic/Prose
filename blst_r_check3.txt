And then Prita, who had not let it go the way the rest of the room had, said the thing Teo had been keeping behind her teeth.

"If her mass is that big," Prita said, slow, working it as she spoke, "then the count Sigrun's been reading us all along was never the real count. We've been voting toward a number with a hole in the middle of it."

The room shifted — not stillness this time but the opposite, a low unsettled current, chairs creaking as people turned to the neighbor they'd come in trusting. Someone near the back said it plainly: if Dagny's abstention could swing the payout past the threshold on the day itself, then every figure Sigrun had put on the wall was provisional, and half the households present had already been treating provisional as settled. Sigrun raised a hand for the room and did not get it back as fast as she had the first meeting. Teo watched the count she'd come to understand come apart in the space of four sentences, and understood that whatever she'd thought she was tracking, she'd been tracking the wrong number — and now so had everyone else, out loud, with two weeks left and no clean figure to steer by.
