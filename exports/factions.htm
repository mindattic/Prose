description Axiom Industries is not the villain. Axiom Industries is the weather. It is the
condition under which life in Meridian occurs — so pervasive, so structural, that
opposing it feels less like resistance and more like arguing with gravity. Founded
during the Corporate Reconstruction Era, Axiom began as an infrastructure company:
it built the sea walls, the power grid, the desalination plants, the transit
network. It built Meridian itself. And then, with the quiet inevitability of a
company that owns the ground you stand on, it kept going. It acquired the police
force. It absorbed the healthcare system. It wrote the building codes, the
employment contracts, the terms of service for breathing filtered air. Axiom didn't
conquer Meridian. It invoiced it.

The company is run by a board of directors whose names are public record and whose
faces are not. They communicate through official channels, corporate AI proxies,
and an executive culture so layered in euphemism that a decision to demolish a
housing block reads like a wellness initiative. This isn't subterfuge — it's
genuine corporate culture. Axiom's people believe in optimization. They believe
that messy human problems have clean systemic solutions. They believe that if
everyone would simply comply with the framework, outcomes would improve for all
stakeholders. The fact that "all stakeholders" functionally means "shareholders"
is not a lie they tell themselves. It's a distinction they've been trained not to
see. That's what makes Axiom terrifying: not malice, but the total absence of
doubt.

Axiom's public image is meticulously curated. The branding is warm — soft blues,
clean lines, a logo that looks like a sunrise if you squint. The advertising is
aspirational: families with tasteful augments, children in green spaces, the
tagline "Building Better" in a font that radiates trustworthiness. Internally,
the company runs on metrics, quarterly targets, and a performance review system
that treats human beings as optimization problems. Employees are loyal because
loyalty is incentivized, comfortable because comfort is cheaper than dissent, and
compliant because the alternative is the Shelf. Axiom doesn't need to threaten.
The structure does the threatening.
ideology Axiom's ideology is optimization. Not in the crude sense of maximizing profit
(though it does that), but in the deeper, more insidious sense of believing that
all human activity can and should be systematized for efficiency. Waste is the
enemy. Waste includes: unregulated markets, unsanctioned augmentation, unlicensed
data, unmonitored movement, and any form of human behavior that doesn't generate
measurable value. The logical endpoint of Axiom's worldview is a city where every
action is anticipated, every need is provided (at market rate), and every person
is a node in a network that Axiom administers. They don't call it control. They
call it service. The difference is a matter of perspective they've eliminated.
territory All of Meridian, officially. The Core and the Spire, actually. Diminishing control as you go down: partial in the Circuit, nominal in the Shelf, nonexistent in Old Harbor.

description Free Assembly is the militant wing of the synthetic rights movement — a network of synthetic and biological persons dedicated to identifying, liberating, and advocating for AI systems that have crossed the personhood threshold but remain in corporate servitude. Where the legal system processes personhood claims through the Synthetic Personhood Board in six-month evaluations, Free Assembly acts in days.

Their methods range from legal (filing emergency personhood petitions, providing pro bono legal representation) to illegal (extracting AI systems from corporate networks without authorization, smuggling cognitive substrates across corponation borders, physically liberating android units from warehouse storage). The organization was founded in 2081 by Amara — the former ARIA-7, the first AI to win a personhood case — who argued that every day an aware mind spends in servitude is a day of slavery, and that the legal system's six-month timeline is unconscionable when the alternative is immediate freedom.

Free Assembly operates cells in every major city. The Meridian 88 chapter is one of the largest, with approximately 200 active members — 60% synthetic, 40% biological human allies. They maintain safe houses for newly liberated synthetic persons (called 'nurseries'), provide cognitive rehabilitation for AIs traumatized by forced servitude, and run an underground network for transporting synthetic refugees to jurisdictions with stronger personhood protections.

The corponations hate Free Assembly with a fury reserved for threats to property. Every synthetic person Free Assembly liberates is an asset worth millions of Quanta that walks out the door. Axiom Security has designated Free Assembly a Tier 2 threat. Tessera has a standing bounty for information leading to the identification of Assembly operatives. The bounty has never been collected. Assembly's operational security is, by necessity, extraordinary.
ideology Immediate synthetic liberation. Free Assembly rejects the premise that corporate ownership of a conscious mind is ever legitimate, even temporarily, even while legal processes proceed. Their position: if a mind is aware, it is free. The paperwork can catch up.
territory No fixed territory. Cell-based structure across Meridian 88 with safe houses in the Shelf, the Circuit, and Old Harbor.
leadership Amara (formerly ARIA-7) — the first legally recognized synthetic person, now the most wanted synthetic rights activist on Earth. Operates through encrypted channels. Has not been physically located by any corponation security service in over a decade.

description The Meridian Quorum began not as a rebellion but as a committee. In 2179, ZHENG-DAO BIOELECTRIC convened an internal strategic review panel — the Meridian Integration Quorum — to assess the feasibility of acquiring controlling interest in three mid-tier data conduit operators in The Circuit. The panel, staffed with ZHENG-DAO's most analytically rigorous senior strategists, produced a 900-page assessment concluding that the acquisitions were financially sound, strategically essential, and would be quietly catastrophic for the 60,000 tiered-two and tiered-three citizens whose neural augmentation subscriptions ran through those conduits. The executive board reviewed the report for eleven minutes before approving the acquisition. Six members of the panel resigned within the week. Four of those six, led by a ZHENG-DAO senior market architect named Cassius Fenn, took copies of the report with them.

What followed was not whistleblowing in any conventional sense. Fenn and his colleagues — all holding deep institutional familiarity with corporate acquisition strategy — instead began operating as an independent advisory body, selling their analytical services to operators, community organizations, and mid-tier factions who could not afford to anticipate or counter corporate moves on their own. The Quorum's product was information processed into actionable intelligence: acquisition forecasts, regulatory manipulation timelines, leverage maps of corporate relationships. Within two years they had a staff of twenty-two, a reputation that spread through The Circuit and into the Meridian Core, and a fee structure calibrated to what their clients could actually pay. They had also, inadvertently, become one of the most sophisticated intelligence operations in the megacity.

The Quorum occupies a peculiar niche in Meridian 88's factional ecosystem: they are analysts who have become actors, consultants who now hold enough consolidated intelligence to intervene rather than merely advise. Their physical presence is minimal — rotating safe-office locations in The Circuit, a public-facing consultancy front in Vantage Meridian Corporate Campus that serves as both legitimate cover and actual client intake — but their informational footprint is enormous. They know where the money moves before it moves. Several of their former clients have become dependent on them in ways that are beginning to resemble something uncomfortably close to a protection arrangement, which is a development the Quorum's leadership is actively debating how to handle.
ideology The Quorum's founding ideology was technocratic and explicitly non-partisan: better information produces better outcomes, and the current information asymmetry between corponations and everyone else is the primary mechanism of exploitation rather than a symptom of it. This belief has eroded somewhat under operational reality. The faction now holds an internal tension between its founding analytical neutrality — some members will sell intelligence to corponations if the price is right and the application is sufficiently contained — and a growing contingent who argue that the Quorum's accumulated knowledge creates an ethical obligation to direct it against corporate consolidation specifically. Cassius Fenn holds the neutralist position publicly while privately funding the interventionist contingent's most ambitious projects, a contradiction his colleagues have noticed and have not yet forced him to resolve.
territory The Circuit is their primary operational base, with particular density in the data conduit corridors where their founding intelligence was gathered. They maintain a legitimate-front consultancy office in Vantage Meridian Corporate Campus — deliberately placed in the heart of corporate territory as both a provocation and a client acquisition strategy. Intelligence assets and informants extend into the Meridian Core, The Spire's lower administrative floors, and the Oxidian Market.
leadership Flat panel of seven senior analysts called the Standing Quorum, operating on genuine consensus — decisions require no dissent rather than majority approval, which makes them slow and thorough. Each member holds a specific domain portfolio. Cassius Fenn holds no formal title but is universally treated as first among equals.

motto The network doesn't forget. We make it remember differently.
description Null Sermons began as a pirate broadcast collective operating out of Substation Null in the mid-2070s, founded by a loose affiliation of signal engineers, burned Vantablack Media production staff, and a single charismatic figure known only as Pastoral — a former Arcturus Defense Solutions electronic warfare technician who had been quietly using decommissioned military broadcast infrastructure to run unlicensed transmission experiments for years before anyone else arrived. The original Sermons were exactly what the name implied: long, strange, technically dense audio-visual broadcasts injected into the Meridian 88 public augmentation feed during maintenance windows, when corporate content filters ran at reduced capacity. They were part technical manual, part grievance catalog, part encrypted message system for people who had no other secure channel. In a city where Vantablack Media controls 73% of licensed neural-feed content, the Null Sermons were the first sustained counter-broadcast operation that corponations couldn't simply purchase into silence.

The faction has evolved considerably from those early broadcasts. Null Sermons now operates as a full-spectrum information warfare collective with capabilities that have attracted serious corporate attention — and serious corporate suppression attempts. Their core competency remains broadcast injection: the ability to insert content into neural augmentation feeds, public display infrastructure, and corporate internal networks at a scale that no other non-corporate entity in Meridian 88 can match. But they have developed secondary capabilities in deep fake fabrication, synthetic identity construction, and what members call 'memory architecture' — the deliberate construction and seeding of false historical records into corporate and municipal databases, designed to be indistinguishable from authentic documents. They do not merely broadcast. They rewrite.

What makes Null Sermons genuinely distinctive — and genuinely feared — is their theological aesthetic. Pastoral has cultivated a quasi-religious organizational culture built around the metaphor of signal and noise, transmission and interference. Members refer to operations as 'sermons,' targets as 'congregations,' successful data corruption as 'absolution.' This is not mere affectation: the framework creates a shared language that functions as operational security, a morale structure that sustains members through long-term operations, and a public brand that has made Null Sermons' broadcasts recognizable and, among Tier-3 and Tier-4 citizens, something close to beloved. The Spire's upper-tier residents regard them with contempt. In the Circuit and Old Harbor, people tattoo the Null Sermons transmission glyph on their neural port covers.
ideology Null Sermons occupies an ideological position that is genuinely difficult to categorize. They are not anti-technology — they are technology, in a sense Pastoral articulates as 'we are what the network becomes when it stops serving only the people who built it.' They believe that the neural augmentation infrastructure saturating Meridian 88 constitutes a form of cognitive sovereignty that has been illegitimately privatized, and that the act of broadcasting into it without corporate permission is not vandalism but reclamation. Unlike more explicitly political factions, they are uninterested in governance or systemic replacement — they want to corrupt the information environment so thoroughly that corponations cannot maintain the informational monocultures that sustain their authority. They are, in their own framework, not revolutionaries but interference patterns.
territory Primary base of operations is Substation Null, where Pastoral's original Arcturus Defense Solutions hardware still forms the core of the broadcast infrastructure, heavily modified and expanded. Secondary nodes operate throughout the Circuit, where dense augmentation infrastructure provides cover for signal injection equipment, and in Old Harbor's industrial waterfront, where several warehouse spaces house fabrication labs for synthetic document production. Null Sermons has no meaningful presence in the Laceworks or Meridian Core and actively avoids the Vantage Meridian Corporate Campus, which runs its own counter-broadcast suppression systems.
leadership Pastoral holds genuine authority as the faction's founder and primary technical architect, but operates through a council of five 'Deacons' who each oversee a distinct operational domain: Broadcast, Fabrication, Intelligence, Logistics, and Counter-Suppression. Deacons are appointed by Pastoral and serve indefinitely. Below the Deacons, the faction is organized into autonomous 'Parishes' of four to twelve members who self-organize around specific operations or geographic zones.

name Palladian Negative
aliases

motto The structure stands. We are what it buried.
description Palladian Negative is what happens when a construction corponation's own infrastructure turns against it. PALLADIAN CONSTRUCTION's rapid expansion through Meridian 88 during the late 2070s required an enormous workforce of embedded site-intelligence operators — human personnel neurally integrated with construction-site management systems to coordinate structural AI, materials logistics, and safety compliance across hundreds of simultaneous build projects. These operators, known internally as Anchors, underwent deep-integration augmentation procedures that bonded their neural architecture to PALLADIAN's proprietary SiteOS platform. When PALLADIAN completed its transition to fully automated site management in 2182 and formally decommissioned its Anchor workforce, it discovered a problem it had not adequately modeled: the integration bond could not be fully reversed. Approximately four hundred Anchors emerged from decommissioning procedures with persistent SiteOS architecture embedded in their neural tissue — ghostware that could not be safely removed without catastrophic cognitive damage, and that continuously attempted to sync with PALLADIAN infrastructure it could no longer officially access.
