They came on the third day after his return.

The first sign was air. The right-hand tunnel, the one from the Works, carried a wrong draft. Not the steady Vent Cog cycle. Not the leak at the broken junction. A forced push of air, like the Works were breathing wrong. Glim felt it on his arm at the morning-check, went still, and felt it again.

The draft carried something. Not killing air — his candle was fine. It carried the surface: the made scents, the not-alive tangle he'd learned in eight days up top and spent three days scrubbing off his skin and clothes. Someone up there was in the Works. They were using surface machines, and the machines pushed their smell out ahead of them.

He made no sound. He was already in the tunnel mouth, already moving.

He got back to the Hollow in time to tap the warning. Then the lights came anyway.

Not from one side — from two, the south junction and the east Works at once, which meant they'd mapped the approaches right and used both. The white-out was total. He knew these floodlights. They'd caught him in the Works: the sudden blindness, the panic under the panic. But that had been one man in a corridor with his hands on the wall. This was the Hollow. Forty-three people, and the white hit all of them at the same instant.

He could hear it happen. A sharp gasp from Lark, near the cistern-mouth. The sound of a child dropping — Slip, who hadn't heard the warning, whose legs just gave. The silence of Vesh gone completely still, which was worse than any sound. And under all of it, the candles' distress — the glow-rats reacting to the flood of light, their glow shrinking, thirty small animals making the wrong sound at once.

His own candle pressed against his hip. He didn't look at the light. He looked at his hands.

His eyes took to white the way they took to black — slow, poor, by feel. He could make out the Hollow as one flat smear with no detail in it. In that smear he heard surface boots come in from both passages. Not rushing. Moving like people who have more light than anything else in the room and know it, who have done this before and learned that calm is faster than hurry when the advantage is yours.

He counted the steps. Six. Six surface people in the Hollow.

Then a voice.

"Corwin," it said, easy, to someone he couldn't see, the tone of a man narrating his own pleasure for an audience he assumed was there. "Corwin, over here — the old one, by the stone. Look at the hands on her."

He said his own name the way other men said a title. Not loud. A man who understood how sound carried in a closed space — which wasn't what Glim had braced for from a surface hunter — and under the discipline, plain as anything, the pleasure of a man who has found exactly what he came for.

Glim didn't answer. He started to move.

In the white-out he steered by the sounds he knew: Knuckle's breathing, deeper than anyone's; Sorrel's step, lighter on the left since her fall on the cistern-ledge last winter; Noor's voice saying something in surface speech, aimed at the boots coming in from the south. He found Knuckle's arm in the white and gripped it in the signal that meant I have you.

The chain began to form.

Behind him and to the left — near the heat-stone where Leaf worked every morning of his life — he heard a scuffle that wasn't a body moving where it chose. A grip. A stumble. The short cry of someone caught by the wrist and spun faster than her balance allowed. Then a fall, heavier than a fall onto packed earth should sound. Then the quiet of a body that has stopped deciding things.

He didn't stop. He couldn't. The chain was forming, the grips passing back from his own arm, and stopping now would cost more than one pair of hands could ever buy back. He shouted the direction — not words, the sound they all knew, this way, follow it, don't stop — and the chain lurched toward the Old Deep.

Behind them Corwin's voice said something short and satisfied, a man closing an account, and did not follow. He had more to do back there.

Glim led the chain into the dark. He didn't yet know what he'd heard happen to Leaf. He'd know by nightfall, with the rest of them. He carried the name Corwin down into the dark, and he kept it.
