The shot went wide.

That was the report that reached the map room before anything else did — before names, before blame. A crossbow bolt meant for Forge Marshal Bremmor Aeshel's throat took a lantern hook instead, six inches high and a lifetime lucky. Kressida Haun finished the bout with a champion's cut across her opponent's guard, unmarked, breathing hard, alive — and dry. The gauge on her hip cell read exactly what she'd known it would read since the sprint to the north gate, and she'd swapped to the spare in the tower stairwell without breaking stride, one hand doing the work while the other kept her blade up, because there was no version of the day that let her sit down and do it properly. Aeshel walked from the tower stair on his own legs. The rope-burn on his wrist was the worst of what anyone did to him today.

Kaeric Dunmoor laid the three papers on the map table in the order he'd rehearsed them.

Forged indenture first, Sennah's name — her old, wrong name — inked across the bottom in a clerk's careful hand.

Sealed letter second, unbroken, Vantor Deil's contract folded inside it like something waiting to bite.

Manifests third, three shipments, three sets of weights that didn't match what left Atrament's docks and what arrived at Pallor's.

"Morrint falsified all three," Kaeric said. "Not once. Three times."

Voren didn't touch the papers yet. He looked at them the way a man looks at a debt he's about to inherit.

Aeshel stood a half-pace behind Kaeric's shoulder, hands loose at his sides, the burn on his wrist gone dark red-brown where the rope had bitten deepest.

"You're not called to answer for anything, Marshal," Voren said, not looking up. "Not today."

Aeshel's jaw tightened once, released. He said nothing.

Kaeric watched Voren's eyes move from the indenture to the letter to the manifests and back, slower each pass, the way a man reads a sentence a second time because the first reading didn't seem possible.

"Where did you get the weights on the Atrament shipments?" Voren asked.

"I signed the receiving copies," Kaeric said. "I kept the originals."

"Why?"

"Because the numbers didn't sit right the first time I saw them."

That was true. It was also not the whole of it, and Voren's stare held a beat too long, like he'd heard the gap between the words and was deciding whether to step into it.

He didn't. Not yet.

"The Long Seat will want all of it," Voren said, and swept the three papers into one stack, one motion, taking them out of Kaeric's hands before Kaeric had decided he was ready to let them go.
