The buzzer sounded once. Rennick's optical overlay flagged the street-level camera: a man, late fifties, silver-haired, the build of someone who used to be athletic and hadn't quite admitted the tense had changed. Behind him, a woman a year or two younger, arms crossed at her sternum. Not cold. Rennick noted the distinction.

"North Shore," he said, to the room.

From the window he could already see the car below it, a private hire idling at the curb with its hazards on, the driver not getting out.
