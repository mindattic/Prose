He didn't wait for them to come back with something. Thirty years had taught him that the firm holding the records is the firm writing the ending, and he wasn't going to let Meridian write this one from a chair in a tower he'd never see the inside of.

The one official thread he had was the clinic. Celeste's cat-ear install had been done somewhere licensed - the genemod left a raw auditory channel, and raw channels came with a clinical intake, a consent packet, a date. He put a call through to the mod-clinic on Ashland that had filed the aftercare notice, and a bored kid picked up on the fourth ring and, for eleven Φ against the agency account, agreed to read him the intake record while his supervisor was on break.

"Hartley, Celeste," the kid said. "Auricula Felis, bilateral. Intake dated -"

The line didn't drop. That would have told him something. Instead the kid stopped talking mid-word, and Rennick heard him say, off-mic, huh, and then, that's - hold on, that was right here.

"It's gone," the kid said, coming back on. "The whole file. It was on my screen. Now there's a banner. Meridian Consulting, legal preservation order, third-party review. I can't even see it now. Sir, I don't - it locked while I was in it."

They hadn't reached the record ahead of him. They'd reached it while he was reading it - some process in a tower watching a Gray Zone account touch a sealed name and closing the door in the half-second it took him to reach through.

He turned the cup a quarter-turn on the desk, and stopped.
