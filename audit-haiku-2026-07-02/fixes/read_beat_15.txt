Rook heard PEREGRINE before Scout's read confirmed it — not a sound, exactly, but a pressure change in the north corridor, the kind that happens when a sealed space develops a new opening and the air hasn't decided what to do about it yet. She registered it in her sternum first. Her hand came off the grating.

*Sixteen meters. Maybe fourteen.*

Scout's voice came in on the open line, clipped and flat: "Contact. North load corridor, four-person stack, moving on a count." A beat. "They're not running."

They weren't running because they didn't need to. Rook straightened from the crouch and turned to Lace, and the look she gave her was not a question — it was a number, and the number was how many seconds they had before a four-person stack in a sealed corridor reduced their exits from three to two and the good one to zero. Lace was already moving toward Boiler, one hand out to pull him off the cabinet, her eyes running the same count. Ohara hadn't moved from her position against the wall, but her face had changed — not fear, still not fear, but the flat recognition of someone watching a thing she'd built get used in the way she'd designed it to be unenterable. The patch behind her was the only warm thing in the room. She didn't look at it. She looked at the north corridor door, and her hands came together in front of her, and her fingers laced, and she stood there holding herself the way a person holds a thing they cannot put down and cannot keep.

*Vox is still in.*

Rook drew and did not look at the patch.
