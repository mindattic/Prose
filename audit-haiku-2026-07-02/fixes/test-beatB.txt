He had one more thing to do before the pages were the whole of it, and it wasn't on any sheet.

He set both hands flat on the table and stood. A big man coming up out of a chair changes the light in a room, and it changed it now; the panel felt him arrive at his full height the way a room feels weather come in off the lake. The recorder was still running. He meant to read the sixteen names into it - the ones he paid on the twenty-first, the ones the file didn't have. Not the forty-three. The file could keep fighting him on the forty-three. The sixteen were his to say, and a man ought to be able to stand in a room built for accountability and say a name out loud.

He drew the breath for the first one.

"The witness will not." Declan Iyengar was on his feet at the side table before the breath became a word - Halcyon Legal, the warm voice from the rented room over the noodle stall, the one that always sounded like a favor while it closed a door. "Any recitation touches on material under active classification review. It has no standing in this record. I'd move to strike it before it's spoken, and I'd ask the chair to instruct the recorder accordingly."

The presiding officer didn't make him work for it. "So moved. The witness will confine himself to filed documentation."

Bear closed his mouth around the name he hadn't said.

He looked at Iyengar a moment - not angry, nothing so useful as angry, just taking the measure of a man who'd come up out of his chair that fast to keep sixteen names off a page. Then he sat. The chair complained under him and he let it. The three pages were still in the record; they hadn't found the form for those yet. But he understood the shape of the morning now. Every time he reached for the human part of it, the institution had a form ready, and the form always got there first.

He put the sixteen names back where he kept them. He had somewhere else to say them.
