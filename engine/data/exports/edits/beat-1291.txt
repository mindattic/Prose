Warpature, the Network calls it — a thin spot, the sediment of the mass quantum-entanglement programs they ran and buried sixty years back, surfacing now wherever it pleases. The maps mark it LATENT in a small gray font and the insurers won't write a line on the block. Kyle's Neuretics tries to map it and gets garbage back: distances that won't hold still, a clock running three directions at once. So for once in his overclocked life he flies on the one instrument the lattice can't sharpen and can't replace — the old animal at the base of the skull that every person still carries under all the hardware. He obeys the rules survivors keep, the ones Vance recited and the ones older than Vance: constant speed, eyes locked on a far streetlamp, don't look at the edges, don't trust a turn — because in here *right* is sometimes *left*, and *left* is sometimes *down*, and the only direction that stays honest is the one you already committed to. "Don't look left," he says. "I already wasn't," Imani says, in a voice gone very small and very old.
