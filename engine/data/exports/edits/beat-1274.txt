"Are you the new one," the small face asks — and Kyle understands the question underneath is a different one. She's eight, maybe nine. Her chest is taped under a flexible biopolymer brace that glows a soft clinical blue through her gown, and she's holding a juice box she hasn't drunk from, the straw still in its wrapper. She is calmer than she has any business being: the flat, careful calm of a kid who's been told that calm is a medical instruction. Kyle steps up into the bay. Driver, dead. Medic in the passenger seat, also not coming back. The gurney tag reads IMANI OKONKWO-LINDQVIST — RECIPIENT, POST-OP, FIRST 6 HOURS, NORTHWESTERN MEMORIAL. Kyle reads it twice. The math does itself: somebody printed this child a new heart tonight, it's six hours old, and it is currently unguarded in the middle of a south-side ditch. He says, "Hi, Imani. I'm Kyle." She says, "I know. My uncle says you're the one who doesn't stop."
