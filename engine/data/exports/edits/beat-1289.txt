Halsted north of the tracks runs over the bones of Cinderfall Energy — eleven thermal plants buried under the street, their surface a grid of steam-vent grates and convection shimmer and asphalt re-laid three times this year because it keeps warping in the heat. "Why's the air doing that," Imani says. "Because the company under us is cooking," Kyle says. "Oh," she says — the *oh* of a kid learning the world has a basement. He doesn't tell her the air is about to do something Cinderfall has nothing to do with. Two headlights have hung off his tail since Pullman: patient, matched to his pace, in no hurry, which is its own kind of bad news. Then his Neuretics flags what's ahead instead, and quits having opinions about the headlights. Two blocks on, the streetlights stop agreeing on where they're standing, and the lattice in his skull — which has had a fast, clean answer for every single thing tonight — goes quiet the exact way Vance said it would.
