He goes in anyway, because the long way costs eleven minutes he's currently bleeding through. The tunnel is cathedral-tall and full of scrolling ads, a slow river of light overhead, and halfway down it a corporate security line has a municipal picket pinned behind an overturned cleaning rig: strikers on one side, the company's hired guns on the other, both too far gone in nine days of grievance to clock a motorcycle until it's the most interesting thing in the room. Kyle thumbs Chorus to the flash-bang load and puts a round into the ceiling above the corporate line. The tunnel goes white. He doesn't get through clean — two seconds of blindness is two seconds of frightened people firing at memory, and one of them is unlucky enough to be lucky: a round furrows the meat across the top of his shoulder, not through, a hot graze that nearly tears Chorus out of his grip and paints the sleeve dark. He keeps the median. He keeps the throttle. A thermal-biometric reader catches his face on the way out and does what Stack policy says to do with an unbilled civilian — lets him pass, opens an invoice. Behind him Imani is watching the rivers of light, not the blood she can't see. "It's beautiful," she says. Kyle gets his breath back around the burn in his shoulder before he answers, so it lands level. "Don't tell the advertisers," he says. "It'll go to their heads."
