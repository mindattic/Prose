At the edge of his vision something moves that shouldn't: a shape with one shadow too many, and the extra shadow arrives a half-beat *before* the shape, like an echo that forgot which way time runs. He doesn't look at it straight. The dark keeps it smeared, and he understands — the way you understand a pressure drop in your teeth — that looking at it directly is not a thing a mind comes home from. Imani's breath snags, his only warning, and he throws the bike *right*, which is the wrong word for the direction it actually goes. The miss should have been a foot. It's less than an inch. He feels it pass like a current turning over in deep water, and a line of cold opens along his ribs under the coat — clean, deep, not bleeding yet, because in here the blood runs half a second behind everything else. Then the far lamp is closer, the throttle holds, and a block on the world remembers its own rules and the pain arrives all at once and warm. And behind him is the thing he's been dreading worse than the aberration: the brace at his kidney starts to chirp, fast and climbing, because Imani's borrowed calm has finally come apart. She's crying now, the real thing, no doctor's voice left in it, her whole small body shaking against his back — *I saw it, I saw it, it knew my name* — and her new heart is sprinting in its six-hour-old cradle exactly the way it was never to be let. This is the version of tonight that kills her, and it isn't a knife or a bike or a Vulture; it's a frightened child's pulse. So Kyle does the one thing the situation allows and talks: low, flat, boring, the voice you'd read a tax form in — about the bike, about the loud part coming up, about anything with edges that stay where you leave them. "You didn't see it," he says, which is true in the only way that counts — nobody keeps it. "By morning you won't be able to find it. That's the one mercy the things like that have." A beat. "Breathe with the engine. In on the low note." The chirping slows. Slows. Steadies. "You lied," she says, wrung out, when she can. "You said don't look left and there wasn't a left." "There wasn't," he agrees. "That was the point." "You're hurt." "I'm fine," he says. It's the first time he's lied to her, and she's too tired to catch it.
