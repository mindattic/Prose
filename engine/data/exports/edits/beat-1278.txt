The first mile is gray zone — Hegewisch sprawl, no sovereign in residence, nobody's law and nobody's lights. Kyle takes Avenue O at eighty, threading two rows of collapsed light poles his Neuretics flags and lines up before his eyes have finished arriving. He doesn't signal. There's no one to signal to except a few crows hunched on a dead wire, who decline to comment. Imani's breathing is a small warm patch against his back, even and slow and worked-at, each breath the same measured size as the last. "You're not scared," he says. "I'm not allowed to be," she says. "The doctor said. Scared makes it pump too hard, and it's new, so. I'm doing the breathing." She's reciting; he can hear the doctor in it, the borrowed grown-up calm laid over a kid like a coat three sizes too big. It isn't bravery. It's a child running the one rule she was handed because it's the only thing in reach. "Then keep doing the breathing," Kyle says. He doesn't tell her how good she is at it. Good is not the thing he wants her to be tonight.
