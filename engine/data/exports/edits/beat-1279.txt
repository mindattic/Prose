The checkpoint is two guards in soft-helmet armor and a third behind blast glass, all wearing the bored faces of people expecting a manifest. Kyle doesn't have a manifest. He has a child, a battery, and a smile. He brakes hard, throws his hands off the bars in the universal not-a-threat, and gets out the only words that matter: "Post-op transit. Lacuna cooler. Northwestern." The left guard reads the brace. The right guard reads the cooler logo. And the one behind the glass — Crucible internal, white coat under the armor, because in a gene-foundry zone the gate scans the person, not the plate; it's the blood they price, not the bike — runs Imani's face against the registry. Whatever it tells him drains the evening out of his expression. He doesn't make a speech. He looks at the guards, then at Kyle, and lifts two fingers, and the barrier sinks. "Have a good night," Kyle says, and is gone before the man decides whether he's allowed to answer.
