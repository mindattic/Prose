He lifts her onto the pillion, sidesaddle, and threads the medical harness through his belt loop so the battery rides over his kidney instead of swinging free. He ties her wrists loosely through the back of his coat so she won't slide off if she sleeps. Her cheek finds the back of his shoulder like it's been there before. "Hold on," he says — a stupid thing to say to a kid whose hands are too small to grip his ribs, but it's what you say. "How fast," she says. "Fast." "Good." Across his back rides Silence: a matte-black carbon-nanotube katana, no edge that catches the light and no trick to it but the hand that holds it. Chorus sits low on his left where it always sits. The kid is the only new weight, and the only one he's doing math about. The bike comes alive under them — eight cylinders' worth of audacity in a city that doesn't deserve them.
