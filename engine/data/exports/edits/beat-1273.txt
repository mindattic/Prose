The shotgun comes up first, which is the contractor's mistake, because it tells Kyle everything about the next half-second and nothing the contractor wanted told. Thirty seconds ago this was a disagreement — an Arcturus man, corporate security of the kind that bills you for the bullet, deciding whether he was going to shoot Kyle tonight. He decided yes. He brought the shotgun to underline the point. Kyle, a man of standards, declines to be underlined: he drops under the blast, feels the pellets shred the air where his chest was, and comes up with Chorus already speaking — the stubby five-chamber revolver-shotgun he keeps low on his left hip. One round. The contractor's trigger hand comes apart at the wrist, and the weapon clatters off to begin a quieter life on the pavement. In the ringing quiet after, Kyle hears the wreck behind him settle: an ambulance that ran the Hegewisch crossroads and lost an argument with a stalled hauler, back doors sprung, one wheel still turning. Inside, in the wreck-light, a small face is watching him. It waves.
