He should turn two blocks west. West is Bridgeport; west is normal. He goes straight, because straight is what the call is for, and at Thirty-Fifth and Halsted the world stops being a place and starts being a condition. The headlight hits something ahead and the something doesn't hit back. His stomach drops a foot without him; the wheels are on the pavement but the pavement is half a second behind where his eyes swear it is. And then the worse thing, the part the warnings never sell hard enough: he *knows* this corner. He has never been here in his life. He knows it the way you know a hallway from a dream you've had since you were small and never once walked on purpose — the grey country behind sleep that everyone visits and nobody admits to in daylight. Behind him Imani makes a sound he has never heard a child make. Not fear. Not pain. *Recognition.*
