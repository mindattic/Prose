The bike is dead and the bay is three blocks off, and three blocks is nothing — he has bled more than this for less. He sheathes Silence, holsters Chorus, and picks her up: harness, battery, the whole small weight of her. When she settles against his chest something old turns over in him without asking permission — a body remembering a night it was small and the arms that didn't come — and he files it where he files everything, which is *later*, which is *never*. He is not going to set her down. That's the whole of it; the reasons can wait in line. Her hand fists in his collar. "You're hurt," she says again, and this time he doesn't bother with the lie. "Yeah." "Tell me about the loud," she says — which is the kindest thing anyone has done for him in a year — so he does, the whole way, one foot in front of the other, the red light of the bay getting bigger.
