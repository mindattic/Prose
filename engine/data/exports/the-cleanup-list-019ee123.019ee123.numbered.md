# The Cleanup List

*Seto found the manifest three days ago. He has not told Amara. Today he tries to move it.*

He had broken into the relay annex on Monday morning. The system had been a holding box, not a relay — eleven months of Cellvault contractor records accumulating in a sealed cabinet on the third floor of a building whose management firmware hadn't been patched since 2219. The document was Batch 44-C: eight hundred and forty-seven names, a calibration schedule running out eighteen months, a classification field reading *Managed remediation. Ongoing. See secondary protocol.* Amara's name had been in the nineteenth row of the Zone 6 block. He had found it at 07:40 on Monday morning and had not told her.

Not because there was nothing to say. Because moving the document required a path, and building the path took three days, and telling her before the path was ready would have changed how she worked inside the Cellvault system without giving her anything she could use.

The transfer protocol he had been building for six weeks was ready. The relay path ran through Seam beacon infrastructure — legacy firmware, off the main monitoring grids, low-traffic channel. He committed to the move, opened the relay path, and watched the handshake sequence initialize.

The timing came back wrong.

He had run this path forty times in testing. The Zone 5/6 beacon registered at 340 milliseconds under normal load. Tonight it came back at 340, then at 1,200 as the transfer sequence opened — a gap that appeared when an additional process was monitoring the channel and hadn't finished loading when the first registration fired. The subsequent beacons came back at consistent intervals. Whatever was watching had found its footing.

He aborted before the first real packet cleared. He was certain enough.

He sat with the three drives. Six weeks of work, three days on Ekow's window — Ekow Ato at the Seam crossing, *walk away from the Orison work, close out the inquiry, seven days,* pausing before "seven" the way a man paused when he'd calculated a number rather than been given one. That had been reasonable. Reasonable was the shape of a warning before the shape changed.

The relay path was burned. He needed a different route, possibly a different decision, and he had delayed three days already. He messaged Amara before he worked out what came next.

*I've had your name for eight days. I held it.*

He didn't explain why. The explanation was true and incomplete and she would know both things when she read it.

Her reply came while he was still looking at the drives. The message started, broke, restarted: *Eight — how did you — okay. How long have you been inside the Zone 7 facilities?* No period at the end. He had never seen her skip punctuation before.

He answered: two years of routes, six facilities, the Tuesday morning window at Z-3.

She came back: *I have a client at Z-3. Quarterly for two years.*

He read the Zone 7 block from memory. The middle of the list: *Eze, Theodora. Zone 7, Facility Z-3. Active.*

He sent her the name.

A pause — longer than the transmission lag. Then: *Yes.*

She knew what it meant. He knew what it meant. The same territory, walked from opposite sides, arriving at the same row on the manifest. He had passed Facility Z-3 on Tuesday mornings for two years without knowing. She had gone inside it for two years without knowing what she was walking into. They had both been moving through the landscape of this thing in opposite directions and the document in his kit bag was the point where the two paths finally met.

His right hand registered something before he caught it — the heel of the palm and three fingers, the specific torque of a calibration probe withdrawing. Her hands, not his. The sensation arrived in the muscles before the mind sorted it, the two-second window before it cleared.

She was somewhere she hadn't finished reading. He did not reach further through.

He looked at the drives. Three days on the clock. The relay path burned. One person inside the system and one in the gray zone, eight hundred and forty-seven names between them, and no way to move the data that hadn't just been closed.

He ate the cold githeri standing at the counter and worked out what came next.
