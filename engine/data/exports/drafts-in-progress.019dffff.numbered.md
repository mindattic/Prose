[Beat 2]
His hands rest on the counter, palms down. The light at Mrs. Chen's stall is the warm-amber kind that makes most things look forgiven, and most things, on most nights, are. Tonight Kyle watches his own wrists catch the light and decline the favor.

[Beat 3]
Pink scar tissue runs around both of them in a clean unbroken bracelet. The line is precise — circumferential, the way a surgeon's stitch is precise — because the AutoDoc that put his hands back where the Lotus Syndicate had taken them was a ten-thousand-Φ machine doing ten-thousand-Φ work, and it had not bothered to soften the seam. The pink is bright against the rest of his skin in the way new tissue is always bright against old, the protein still settling, the perfusion still finding its rhythm. It will fade. The medic at the kiosk had told him so. *Six months. Maybe nine. After that you'll have to remember it's there.* Kyle does not need six months. The bracelets will not stop being there for him whether the pink fades or not, because what is at his wrists is not the scar; the scar is the *receipt*. He turns the right hand over. The line continues across the inside of the wrist, perfect, the way a circle is perfect, and the cargo arm and the chum barrel and the cleaver are all in the line if you know how to read the line, and he knows how to read the line.

[Beat 4]
Then the steam from the pot reaches him.

[Beat 5]
Pork bone, slow-rendered, the long-simmered kind Mrs. Chen runs at this hour because the regulars who come at 0300 are either coming off shift or coming off something worse. The smell is what it always is — fat, marrow, mineral, the chili oil cutting under it — and Kyle has eaten it three times a week for four years and has loved it for at least three of them. Tonight the smell hits the back of his sinus cavity and his body does something it has not done before in this stall: it *flinches*. The hardware routes the response off the conscious channel before he can examine it, but the routing is not fast enough. He registers the flinch. He registers what produced it. The smell is pork. The smell is also exactly the smell that came up out of his own wrists when the Lotus Syndicate's cauterizer band sealed the cleaver-cuts six days ago — fat and lymph and the particular sweet rot-edge of human cooking — and the sinus cavity does not have a channel and the smell is not on a channel and the body knows what it knows.

[Beat 6]

[Beat 7]
*Eat. You're burning down to nothing.*

[Beat 8]

He does not understand the distinction the body has drawn and he does not have the time tonight to figure it out, so he files it where he files things that will matter later, alongside the bracelets and the Lotus debt that has been sitting uncollected for six days and the small not-factory pulse he has felt twice in his peripheral vision since waking up at the AutoDoc kiosk — a status-strip glyph at the corner of his sensorium that is not a status-strip glyph because there is no status strip in this stall, which means the glyph is somewhere else, which means he is the somewhere else.

[Beat 9]
The glyph pulses again, brief, not-factory. He does not look for it because looking for it is not how it shows up. It shows up *peripheral*, the way a watcher in the doorway shows up — present in the room because the room is now a room with a watcher in it, regardless of where you put your eyes. He feels Puppeteer settle. The settling is not a settling-down; it is a settling-in. The E.L.F. is observing through him. Not driving — *observing.* Mrs. Chen wipes the counter. Kyle watches her wipe the counter and Puppeteer watches her wipe the counter and the watching is layered, two perspectives sharing a single optical input, the way two operators share a feed. He is allowed to know this is happening. He does not flinch a second time. The flinch is for the smell of his own cooking; this is a different category of company.

[Beat 10]
*Why her,* Kyle thinks, not at the entity, just thinking. *Why are you watching her.*

[Beat 11]
No answer comes. Puppeteer does not yet have language for unsolicited explanations, or has it and is choosing not to use it, and Kyle is not in a position to ask which. He keeps eating.

[Beat 12]
The stabilizer went in ninety-three minutes ago. Two hours early, which means the hardware skipped again — a half-second gap in the feed, the world strobing out and back like a bad splice. He noticed it on the stairs. Gripped the railing. Kept walking. He has not told anyone.

[Beat 13]

[Beat 14]

Her hands are unaugmented. Kyle counts zero chrome every time without deciding to count. The habit predates awareness. He has been doing it for four years. Bare knuckles, age-mapped skin, a small scar at the base of the left thumb she has never explained. She is the only person in his daily radius with no installed hardware. He finds this either comforting or suspicious, and he is no longer certain which.

[Beat 15]

[Beat 19]

[Beat 31]

He is going to read it differently now. She is going to know he is reading it differently. The relationship has aged a chapter without anyone signing off on the aging, and the next contract is going to arrive in the new register whether he is ready for it or not.
