# Interlude III: Before Something Changes

_An ordinary evening in Pixel's apartment. She's recalibrating a prosthetic arm; he's staying for soup._

[Beat 1]
The arm was a Nakamura-Brecht Series 7, upper-limb replacement, pediatric fitting — made for a twelve-year-old whose family had come to Pixel through the gray-zone referral network that Pixel was not officially part of and had never signed anything to join. Wren had passed the name down the channel — Wren took the surgical work and let the calibration land where calibration landed, which meant Pixel, which meant unpaid, which meant nobody put it in writing. The calibration on the proprioceptive feedback array was off by about eight degrees on the ulnar axis, which meant the kid was overshooting on every rightward reach and compensating with her shoulder in a way that was going to cause problems in six months if not corrected now.

Pixel had been on it for an hour when Kyle arrived with soup.

[Beat 2]
Not his soup. Mrs. Chen's soup. He had two containers and he set them on the kitchen counter and found two bowls and poured the soup and brought one to her workbench and said *eat* and she said *I'm calibrating* and he said *I can see that* and set the bowl on the clean corner of the workbench and went back to the kitchen.

She ate between measurements.

He sat at her kitchen table and ate his soup and watched her work. She had the arm clamped in a jig, wrist-end up, and she was running adjustments with a probe that fed to her tablet and an instrument that looked like a modified tuning fork and was probably not that but functioned similarly. She talked to the arm sometimes when she was calibrating — not to him, to the arm itself, short directives like *there you are* and *no, stay* — and she did this with the same unselfconsciousness that she fixed things generally, without checking whether she was being observed.

[Beat 3]
"Left ulnar deviation," Kyle said.

"I know."

"Eight degrees."

"Seven point six," she said. "Don't optimize my margin of error."

He ate his soup. She recalibrated. The arm held its position and then overshot slightly and she made a sound of concentrated displeasure and went back three steps in the sequence.

"It's the sleeve interface," Kyle said. "The socket fit is wearing on the medial side."

Pixel set the probe down. Picked it up. "Yes."

"New socket or compensation?" he asked.

"Both. New socket takes three weeks, the kid needs to use the arm now. So compensation first, socket when the mold sets."

Kyle nodded. He refilled his soup from the pot on the counter and came back and sat down. The evening had gotten to the point where it was clearly going to be a long evening and neither of them had said so.

[Beat 4]
The thing about Pixel's apartment was that it was the only place Kyle sat at a table without tracking the exits. At some point he'd stopped.

Below the building the Pulse ran a slug toward the hub, nineteen hertz up through the table legs, the hum arriving in his teeth before he registered it as sound. None of that reached this table. Here it was just the building, and the light was just the lamp Pixel kept angled at her work, and the soup was getting cold.

[Beat 5]
"You don't have anywhere to be," she said.

"I might."

"Do you?" she asked.

He looked at the pot. "No."

She set the probe down again, this time all the way, and turned to look at him. Whatever she was going to say, she measured it before she said it, the way she measured calibration corrections — accounting for the error, accounting for what the correction would do to adjacent systems.

She said: "I've been meaning to ask you something."

He waited.

"The arm. When you carry Silence — the weight distribution. Does it shift how you read a grip?"

It was about the arm. He answered about the arm. She listened and asked a follow-up that was still about the arm. He answered. She turned back to the workbench and made a small, precise correction to the ulnar axis setting and the arm moved in its clamp and stopped cleanly, no deviation, and she said *there* in the same quiet tone she used for the arm itself.

[Beat 6]
He stayed for another hour. He washed the bowls. She finished the calibration and ran it twice more to confirm. At some point his left hand was on the edge of the workbench six inches from her hand and he noticed this with no particular plan to address it.

She noticed it at the same time. He could tell she noticed it by the quality of the pause in her work — three seconds, no more, before she picked the probe back up.

Nothing was said.

He left at eleven. She said *tell Mrs. Chen the soup was good* and he said *she knows* and closed the door, and the specific sound of the latch catching was different from every other latch in the building and he'd known that for months and now he'd said the word *months* to himself in the stairwell and made himself not stop on the landing to look back at her door.
