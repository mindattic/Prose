# The Weight of a Receipt

_A handwritten note on courier-receipt paper pulls a burned-out freelancer to a welded-container community on a deteriorating tier, where a corporation offers to "preserve" their home in exchange for owning their stories and the right to evict them. He has until Friday to read the fine print no one else can._

[Beat 1]
Mrs. Chen Wei-Lin's stall smelled like star anise and long-cooked pork.

[Beat 2]
Kyle sat on the third stool from the left, the one with the wobble. He ate slow. The implanted array behind his sternum was quiet.

[Beat 3]
The street outside Tier 7's public promenade moved the way it always moved after shift-change: shoulders down, eyes on the grating, everyone calculating what the next twelve hours would cost.

[Beat 4]
The broth was correct.

[Beat 5]
He heard the bike before he saw it — a pedal-assist Carrion Logistics LLC courier frame, the kind they sold to gig workers on a lease that never ended. The frame was too big for the rider. The rider was maybe eleven.

[Beat 6]
The kid threaded between two Pinnacle Civil maintenance drones and braked hard on the grease-stained tiles outside the stall's awning.

[Beat 7]
"Kyle Corbin-Vister?"

[Beat 8]
Kyle set his chopsticks across the bowl.

[Beat 9]
"Yeah."

[Beat 10]
The kid reached into a canvas bag strapped across her chest and produced a folded square of paper. Carrion receipt paper, the pink-and-grey thermal stock they used for delivery confirmations. On the back, handwriting in blue ink. Real ink. Kyle hadn't seen real ink in two weeks.

[Beat 11]
He took the paper. The handwriting was careful. Someone had taken time with it.

[Beat 12]
*To whoever Mr. Corbin-Vister is — my name is Miriam. I was told you are fair. We have a problem with a company called Tessera and I do not know if I am being helped or eaten. The congregation at the container complex on Tier 4 North, berth 9-through-14. Tonight if you can. — Ayushmaan Horváth sends his regards and says you will understand.*

[Beat 13]
Kyle read it twice. The array warmed a degree. Not threat-heat. Something else. Horváth had once given Kyle a way out of a Pinnacle Civil inquiry and never mentioned it afterward. When Horváth sent regards, it wasn't social.

[Beat 14]
He looked at the kid.

[Beat 15]
"You know Horváth?" he asked.

[Beat 16]
"He's my uncle's neighbor," the kid said. "He paid me thirty chips to deliver that."

[Beat 17]
Kyle folded the receipt and put it in his jacket's inside pocket.

[Beat 18]
"Tell him I'm coming."

[Beat 19]
The kid was already back on the bike.

[Beat 20]
Mrs. Chen refilled his broth without being asked.

[Beat 21]
"Eat," she said. "Tier 4 North is cold."

[Beat 22]
He ate.

[Beat 23]
Tier 4 North was the kind of place Pinnacle Civil put in its heritage reports as *organically developed residential infrastructure* — which meant they had stopped maintaining it in 2198 and started calling the deterioration a feature.

[Beat 24]
The container complex at berths 9-through-14 was six rows of stacked shipping containers, the old Carrion Logistics intermodal type, forty-footers in sun-faded red and green and one oxidized yellow that had been there long enough to become a landmark.

[Beat 25]
Someone had cut doors and windows into them with plasma torches, decades ago, and then someone else had filled those windows with actual glass — salvaged, mismatched, some of it colored. The containers were bolted and welded into a structure six stories tall that leaned slightly south, the way the whole tier leaned south, the way everyone on the tier leaned south after years of walking the same slanted floors.

[Beat 26]
There were window-box gardens on every level. Herbs, mostly. Some small peppers. One box on the fourth container up had a single marigold in it, orange in the cold vapor light.

[Beat 27]
It was the only unbranded object Kyle had seen since leaving Mrs. Chen's.

[Beat 28]
He stood on the grating below and looked up at the complex for ten seconds. The array read the structure: load-bearing welds sound, roof-level has a new antenna cluster — not Carrion, not Pinnacle Civil. Community-built.

[Beat 29]
Someone had strung fiber between the containers and run it down to a junction box that had been painted over so many times the brand name underneath was illegible.

[Beat 30]
Kyle walked to the front entrance. The front entrance was a container door left hinged and decorated with a painted mural: the GLMZ skyline upside down, so the towers hung below and the roots of things reached upward. A child's handprints formed the border.

[Beat 31]
The paint was not corporate paint. It had the slight texture of something mixed from available components.

[Beat 32]
A woman stood in the doorway. She was in her late fifties, small, with grey-streaked hair pulled back. She wore a wool-blend coat — Helix Thermal, the budget line, the one they advertised with the slogan *Warmth Is a Right* while lobbying to restrict residential heat subsidies. The coat had been repaired at the elbow with a different-colored thread.

[Beat 33]
"Mr. Corbin-Vister," she said.

[Beat 34]
"Miriam."

[Beat 35]
"Come in."

[Beat 36]
The interior of the ground-level container was set up as a common room. Low tables, mismatched chairs, a gas burner in the corner with a kettle. A Hyacinth ClearBreath air filter — the industrial model, the one Hyacinth leased to clinics — sat in the opposite corner running on what looked like a spliced Arcturus power line. The filter's display read *AIR QUALITY: ACCEPTABLE* in green. The air outside had been amber.

[Beat 37]
Eight people sat in the chairs and on the floor.

[Beat 38]
Miriam sat across from Kyle and put a Tessera Group brochure on the table between them. The brochure was expensive — matte finish, embossed logo, the kind of print job that cost more than a tier's monthly maintenance budget. The Tessera Group called themselves *a heritage and cultural continuity enterprise*. Their tagline: *We Preserve What Matters.*

[Beat 39]
Kyle picked up the brochure and read the relevant page. Tessera was offering a Heritage Stewardship Agreement. In exchange for filming rights and the right to use the complex's image in *educational and promotional materials in perpetuity*, Tessera would register the complex as a Cultural Heritage Site under GLMZ Corporate Preservation Code Section 14-C. A Cultural Heritage Site could not be demolished for development without a forty-eight-month review process. The current demolition order on the complex was from Arcturus Development Group and was dated six weeks ago.

[Beat 40]
Kyle set the brochure down. He looked at Miriam.

[Beat 41]
"You read the sub-clauses?" he asked.

[Beat 42]
"I read what I could," she said. "There are parts I don't understand."

[Beat 43]
"Do you have the actual contract?" he asked.

[Beat 44]
A man in the back — young, maybe twenty-five, NeoCortex-badged jacket with the badge torn off — produced a tablet and walked it forward. Kyle took it.

[Beat 45]
The contract was forty-one pages. He read fast. The array didn't parse legalese, but it held structure — flagged when a later clause quietly negated an earlier one.

[Beat 46]
He found the first problem on page seven. He found the second on page nineteen. He found the third on page thirty-one and sat with it for a moment.

[Beat 47]
"Okay," he said.

[Beat 48]
The room waited.

[Beat 49]
"Page seven," Kyle said. "Clause 4.2. Tessera gets 'exclusive narrative rights to the history and stories of the complex and its residents.' That means anything anyone here has ever done, any story you've ever told about this place — they own the version they film. You can't tell your own story after this without their approval."

[Beat 50]
Miriam's face did not change.

[Beat 51]
"Page nineteen," Kyle said. "Clause 12.1.

[Beat 52]
"The Heritage Site designation protects the *physical structure* from demolition. It does not protect residency rights. Arcturus can still remove the residents."

[Beat 53]
"They just have to keep the containers standing."

[Beat 54]
Someone in the back said something sharp in a language Kyle didn't catch.

[Beat 55]
"Page thirty-one," Kyle said. "Clause 19.4. If the congregation fails to 'maintain the cultural character of the site as determined by Tessera's heritage committee,' Tessera can withdraw the designation. They define *cultural character*. They define the committee. They can revoke protection any time the complex stops being useful for their content."

[Beat 56]
He set the tablet on the table.

[Beat 57]
"It's not preservation," he said. "It's a lease. You pay with your stories. They keep the right to evict."

[Beat 58]
Miriam was quiet for a long moment.

[Beat 59]
"The Tessera representative," she said, "told us we had until Friday to sign."

[Beat 60]
"It's Wednesday."

[Beat 61]
"Yes."

[Beat 62]
Kyle looked at the brochure again. Then at the room. A woman near the wall was holding a sleeping infant. The man with the torn-off NeoCortex badge had his jaw tight. An older man in the back had his eyes closed, either praying or calculating.

[Beat 63]
"Who's the rep," he said. "Name."

[Beat 64]
"She said her name was Cass Inoue. She works out of Tessera's Tier 9 office."

[Beat 65]
"You have her contact?" he asked.

[Beat 66]
Miriam nodded.

[Beat 67]
"Don't sign anything," Kyle said. "And don't tell her you've talked to me."

[Beat 68]
Tier 9 was the kind of tier where the ambient light was better because the corporations that owned it had decided good light reflected well on them. Tessera's office was in a Hyacinth-managed mixed-use building, which meant Hyacinth collected the rent and Tessera paid it and both of them wrote the transaction off as a *community investment*.

[Beat 69]
The building's lobby had a mural of a GLMZ sunrise. The mural had been painted by an artist Hyacinth had contracted for a flat fee and then retained the copyright of. The artist's name was not on the mural.

[Beat 70]
Kyle arrived at 2200 and found Cass Inoue still at her desk. She was in her thirties, polished, the kind of tired that got concealed by good skincare. Her jacket was Arcturus executive-line. Not Tessera.

[Beat 71]
He noted that.

[Beat 72]
The array warmed. She had augmentation — low-grade Helix social-calibration implant, the kind that read microexpressions and fed you prompts. Sales tool. Negotiation tool. She would know he was reading her and she wouldn't know what he was reading with.

[Beat 73]
"Mr. Corbin-Vister," she said, without looking surprised.

[Beat 74]
Someone had told her to expect him or she was good at not being surprised. He filed both.

[Beat 75]
"Inoue."

[Beat 76]
"Sit down."

[Beat 77]
He sat.

[Beat 78]
"The congregation sent you," she said.

[Beat 79]
"I came to ask a question."

[Beat 80]
"Ask."

[Beat 81]
"Arcturus filed the demolition order," Kyle said. "Tessera is Arcturus's heritage division."

[Beat 82]
He watched her face. The Helix implant would be telling her: *maintain neutral expression, rephrase toward benefit framing.* He watched her decide whether to follow it. She followed it.

[Beat 83]
"Tessera operates independently from—"

[Beat 84]
"They're the same corp," Kyle said.

[Beat 85]
She stopped. The array gave him her tells: a 0.4-second lag before the next breath, a micro-contraction around the left eye. Not anger. Calculation. She was deciding how much he knew and whether it mattered.

[Beat 86]
"The designation is real," she said. "The protection is real."

[Beat 87]
"For the containers," Kyle said. "Not the people."

[Beat 88]
"Residency rights are a separate—"

[Beat 89]
"Separate department," Kyle said.

[Beat 90]
"Same corp," Kyle said. She was quiet.

[Beat 91]
"What do you want?" she asked.

[Beat 92]
"Amended contract," Kyle said. "Three changes. One: narrative rights stay with the congregation."

[Beat 93]
"Tessera gets filming rights, non-exclusive, for seven years. Two: Heritage designation protects residency, not just structure. You add a clause, you get it co-signed by a GLMZ civil registrar, not just your own committee. Three: revocation requires independent review, not Tessera's panel."

[Beat 94]
"The first two I can get approved," she said. "Narrative rights, non-exclusive. Independent review on revocation. I have the authority." She set the tablet down. "The third — residency protection against Arcturus's order — that's outside Tessera's scope. That's a different legal entity."

[Beat 95]
"Then get the first two done by 0600," Kyle said. "In writing."

[Beat 96]
She was quiet. Then she turned her own copy of the contract to the last page. "Clause 23," she said. "Twelve-month renegotiation window. The two amendments hold until they don't." She closed the contract. "Mr. Corbin-Vister. The Pivot. Tier 8 South." She said it the way you confirm an address — not to inform, but to establish. His array ran a read: the Helix implant was prompting her to hold eye contact. She was following the prompt. "You know this won't hold," she said. "They find another mechanism inside a year." Kyle had nothing to dispute in that. "Probably," he said. "But not before Friday."

[Beat 97]
"And the residency order?" she asked.

[Beat 98]
Kyle stood.

[Beat 99]
"That fight is separate," Kyle said. "But if I don't see the two amendments by 0600, I spend the rest of the week making sure every community org on Tiers 3 through 6 knows exactly what the demolition order says and which corporate entity filed it."

[Beat 100]
He put the original brochure on her desk.

[Beat 101]
"You have until Friday," he said. "Same deadline you gave them."

[Beat 102]
He left.

[Beat 103]
He was back at the container complex by 2300. Miriam met him at the painted door.

[Beat 104]
He told her what he'd said and what he'd asked for. She listened without interrupting.

[Beat 105]
When he finished she was quiet for a moment. Her thumb moved slowly across the back of her other hand — the patient motion of someone who had practiced waiting for a long time. Behind her, through the open door, he could hear voices, the kettle, the sound of the Hyacinth filter doing its small work.

[Beat 106]
"Will she do it?" Miriam asked.

[Beat 107]
"She'll amend two of the three," Kyle said. "Narrative rights are yours. Independent review on revocation. You'll have the language by 0600." He looked at her. "Have a civil attorney check the wording before you sign. Legal aid on Tier 5."

[Beat 108]
"And the residency?" Miriam asked.

[Beat 109]
"That fight is with Arcturus directly," Kyle said. "The demolition order. If Tessera sends the amendments, you'll have the stories and the review protection. The residency — that's different. I'll help you find the right people if you want to go that route."

[Beat 110]
Miriam nodded slowly.

[Beat 111]
"What do I owe you?" she asked.

[Beat 112]
Kyle looked at the mural on the door. The upside-down skyline. The handprint border.

[Beat 113]
"Nothing," he said.

[Beat 114]
She started to speak.

[Beat 115]
"Ayushmaan sent his regards," Kyle said. "That's enough."

[Beat 116]
He turned and walked back toward the tier ramp.

[Beat 117]
Behind him the container complex stood the way it had stood for thirty years: unlevel, repaired, lit from inside, leaning south the way everything here leaned south.

[Beat 118]
The marigold in the fourth-level window box was still there.
