# Chapter 11: Across the Hall

## Across the Hall

[Beat 1]
The Pivot — the residential block where three floors of old civic housing share an address with a gray-zone commercial strip on the ground floor, and nobody asks questions about either — sits two blocks past the Arcturus checkpoint that bleeds amber across the wet street all night, the booth that scans you for plate and intent and waves you through if you're nobody worth stopping, and tonight Kyle is gloriously nobody. He leaves the slug-rail hum of the gray zone behind him at the door — that low ferrocrete thrum the Pulse, the waterfront grid's subsonic heartbeat, pushes up through everything this close to the lake — and trades it for the warmer ruin of home.

He makes it up the stairs of The Pivot on a banister and a bad idea, leaving a dark smear on the wall that he'll feel guilty about later. The second floor announces Pixel the way it always does — bass through her door at a volume that should bother the building more than it does, the clatter of something being tested and found wanting. Kyle stands in the hall with his hand pressed to his side, blood dark between his fingers, and he does the math he always does: knock, or bleed quietly and not be a problem.

He knocks. It comes out softer than a knock should.

The music cuts. The door opens on the chain, then all the way, and Pixel takes him in the way she takes in everything — fast, flat, already triaging. Pink hair half out of its updo, grey-blue eyes doing arithmetic on the spread of the stain.

"That had better not be yours," she says.

"It's mostly someone else's."

"Inside."

[Beat 2]
She clears the kitchen table with her forearm — a soldering iron, a half-flayed prosthetic hand, a mug — and none of it lands gently. Tools go to the floor and stay there. She doesn't look at them. She looks at him, and at the brace, and at the boot dragging, and the look on her face is the one she keeps for things that are still fixable if she's fast.

"Shirt," she says.

He gets it half over his head before the wound files a complaint, and she's there, peeling the rest of it off him like a bandage from skin, brisk, no ceremony. Then she stops being brisk.

She has seen Kyle hurt before. She has not, somehow, seen *this* much of him — the whole spread of it under the kitchen light. A long pale seam under his ribs, white with age. A burn that climbs his left shoulder, old and uneven.

"Stop cataloguing me," he says.

"Stop collecting them," she says, and reaches for the kit.

[Beat 3]
She works the way good medics work — talking to keep him present, hands too busy to shake. Antiseptic first. He hisses; she doesn't apologize. She irrigates the gash in his side, and her thumb braces flat against the muscle below it to hold the edges while she threads the suture, and he feels the small precise tugs as she closes him, one and then the next, her breath warm and uneven against his collarbone.

Halfway through she turns him — checking the exit geometry, the way the wound tracks — and her fingers find something that shouldn't be there. A screw head, a machine screw, no more than an inch, worked into the skin just below the shoulder blade. The floor, when the table went. She extracts it without a word and sets it on the edge of the kit, and he watches her not react to it, and something in his chest does a thing it doesn't usually do.

Somewhere in the middle of all of it her eyes have gone bright and overfull, and one of them spills, and the tear runs down and lands in the open wound. He doesn't tell her she's crying. She'd hate that. He just holds still and lets her finish, lets the salt find the raw of him, because there's a tenderness in being cried over that he has not let himself have in longer than he can name.

"You keep doing this," she says, not a question, knotting the last stitch. "Coming back in pieces. Making me put you back."

"You're good at it."

"That's not the part I'm complaining about."

[Beat 4]
She should be done. The wound is closed; the forearm is wrapped; the shoulder is taped. Her job, the named and reasonable part of it, is over. And she doesn't move away.

Her hand is still flat on his stomach where she braced to stitch him, and it stays, and then it doesn't stay still — it moves, slow, down the ridges and over the old burn and along the pale seam under his ribs, reading him the way her eyes read everything, except her eyes are closed now. She traces a scar from end to end like she's deciding whether to forgive it.

This is the part where Kyle pulls away. He always pulls away. There is a reason — fourteen years and every name he's carried and all the ways being found has never once gone clean — and it lives right here, in the half-second before contact becomes something he'd have to mean.

He doesn't pull away.

He puts his hand over hers and presses it flatter to him, and lets her feel that his heart is doing something undignified, and when she looks up — startled, the arithmetic finally failing her — he is already looking back.

[Beat 5]
She kisses him like she's been holding it under her tongue for a year, and he answers it with both hands in the pink wreck of her hair. She climbs into his lap right there on the kitchen chair, careful of the bad side and reckless with all the rest, and he pulls her sweater off over her head and doesn't track where it lands.

In the lower-left of his overlay, his neuretics pings once — a routine proximity flag, the Arcturus checkpoint cycling its three-block sensor radius, the kind of alert he has dismissed without thinking for eleven years. He lets it run to timeout. The overlay clears itself.

The last of the tools go off the table — screws skittering across the concrete and into the dark under the workbench — and neither of them flinches. Her hands find him everywhere they spent the last hour being clinical and aren't being clinical anymore. His mouth finds the freckled line of her throat and she makes a sound that she would deny making.

"Bed," she manages, "is eight feet—"

"Too far," he says, against her skin, and she laughs, breathless, and agrees with her whole body.

[Beat 6]
He stands with her wrapped around him — the side screams and he tells it to wait its turn — and they make it as far as the wall, which is closer and entirely sufficient. Her hand fists in his hair and she pulls him back in. Her fingers drag down his back and find new scars to learn. His hands close over the compact strong shape of her and pull her in tighter, palms moving slow across her ribs, her spine, the warm dip at the small of her back.

She gasps his name into the crook of his neck like it's something she's allowed to have now, and his breath goes ragged and stops sounding measured, and his shoulders drop.

They move together, against the wall and then down onto the cleared floor — his mouth at her collarbone, her hands flat on his ribs — until his hand finds hers on the concrete and holds it.

[Beat 7]
After, the floor is hard and they don't care.

She lies half on his good side, and the apartment ticks and cools around them, and the bass from someone else's unit thumps faintly through the brick. Down where the wall meets the floor, a thin green pulse runs the length of the baseboard seam and goes still, lumen mice settling back into the dark, and neither of them turns to look. His chest goes up and down too fast and then slower. Her pink hair is stuck to her cheek.

She runs a finger along the contours of him — not triage now, not a medic's inventory, just the same practiced palpation she'd use to find what was broken, only this time pressed slow and asking nothing, learning a body she intends to come back to. The burn on his shoulder. The new line of her own stitches in his side, neat as a hem. The dip of his sternum.

He does the same to her, because for once his hands are not on a weapon — the freckles across her collarbone, the small old scar through one eyebrow, the strong unglamorous lines of the forearms that put him back together an hour ago. He traces her like she's a map to somewhere he might actually want to go. His thumb finds the pulse at the base of her throat and just stays there, not counting it, only feeling it move. She lets him. She watches him do it with her grey-blue eyes gone soft, and says nothing, which for Pixel is the loudest thing she owns.

[Beat 8]
"This can't happen again," Kyle says. He's looking at the ceiling when he says it, which is how she knows he doesn't entirely mean it.

Pixel props herself up on one elbow, forearm flat on the concrete, and considers him with the particular patience of a person who has just won an argument the other party doesn't know is over. She doesn't answer. She picks a screw up off the floor and turns it once in the low light. Then she sets it down.

His thumb is still at the base of her throat, where he left it. He doesn't move it.
