# Interlude II: Half a Step

_Kyle comes back from The Interview hollowed out. Late. One shoe. Pixel makes tea. They sit in the near-dark of her kitchen and the thing that has been accumulating gets close enough to touch — and he steps back from it, the way he always steps back._

[Beat 1]
He came up the stairs at two in the morning with one shoe.

The other one was still on the West Town cross-street where Mira's crew had found him, or it was in the gutter where he'd moved out of someone's line, or it was on the roof of the dry goods warehouse where he'd gone to get above the engagement and decided the shoe wasn't worth the time it would take to find it in the dark. He hadn't looked. He'd walked the thirty blocks home in a dress shoe on the right foot and a gray sock on the left and registered the imbalance in his gait the way his hardware always registered things: clinically, without judgment, as data that mattered for some calculations and not others.

Somewhere around the twentieth block the count had come out wrong. A short stretch of Ashland that should have taken nine strides and took eleven, the hum arriving in the ferrocrete a half-second before the recycler exhaust that explained it, the streetlight at a frequency his eye read as slightly off and his neuretics declined to log. He had been here. He had not been here. His hardware filed it the way it filed the shoe — data that mattered for some calculations and not others — and he kept walking, because the alternative was standing on a thin spot at two in the morning to ask it questions it would not answer.

Pixel's light was on under her door.

She opened it before he knocked. She was already moving toward the kitchen, which meant she'd heard him in the stairwell, and the particular asymmetric sound of his footfall had told her enough that she didn't need him to explain.

[Beat 2]
He hadn't looked for the shoe. The job had a shape he hadn't finished reading, and thirty blocks of wet sock had not clarified it. He still didn't have the name of whatever had been hiring through that shell, or a reason for the routing that didn't bother him, and neither of those were problems he was going to solve in a stairwell at two in the morning.

[Beat 3]
She set two cups down. The kettle was already hot.

He sat in the chair at the corner of her kitchen table — not the one that faced the window, not the one that faced the wall, the one that faced the room — and set Cacophony on the table because the holster had been abraded by the evening and the draw angle had shifted two degrees and he would fix it here or he would fix it later but he would fix it. He left Silence on his back because taking Silence off required both hands in a particular sequence and his left hand had a grade-two compression injury from the third engagement and he wasn't going to ask for help with the sequence and she wasn't going to offer.

[Beat 4]
The tea was not a blend Kyle would have chosen. It was something she made when she was tired — he knew this now — something her mother had made, from a region that no longer existed as a political entity, which she'd once described as tasting like an argument about whether you should go back to bed. It tasted like that now. He found it acceptable.

[Beat 5]
They sat. The near-dark of her kitchen at two in the morning had a specific quality — the workbench lamp still on in the other room, a sliver of light from under her bathroom door, the city doing something low and industrial a mile and a quarter away.

His cup was already on the table when he sat down.

[Beat 6]
He also knew, with the same precision that tracked hinge angles and drive distances and the weight of a sword, exactly what he was and what any version of what he was could offer anyone who decided to hold it.

He took the half-step back. He always took the half-step back. Not out of the room and not out of the tea — he set his cup down and looked at the window.

She watched him do it without commenting.

[Beat 7]
They stayed for another forty minutes. He adjusted the holster angle on Cacophony. She read something on her tablet that she didn't tell him about. At a quarter to three he stood and took the cup to her sink and she said *your shoe's probably still on Erie* and he said *probably* and she said *the third drain grate past the loading dock has a shelf* and he said *I'll check* and she said *okay* and he went across the hall.

He did not check. He went to bed with one shoe under the nightstand and the sock still on the other foot.
