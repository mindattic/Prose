# Interlude IV: The Morning

_The morning after Kyle comes home from the AutoDoc with his hands reattached. He wakes before the city. Puppeteer stirs in his NeoCortex, attending to a pigeon on the sill. Pixel's music has been off all night for the first time in years. He opens his right hand. He puts the kettle on. He leaves the door open._

[Beat 1]
He woke before the city.

This was not unusual. What was unusual was that the first thing he did, before checking the time, before listening for the building's sounds, before the NeoCortex ran its startup diagnostics — the first thing he did was open his right hand.

All five fingers. Full extension. Held it.

The reattachment seams were there if you knew where to feel for them — faint lateral ridges at both wrists where the AutoDoc's bonding medium had set overnight, still processing at the cellular interface. His hardware was running the reintegration protocol at a steady twelve percent of available compute, which meant the hand was technically not fully his yet, not in the firmware sense, and the precise grip strength on the right side was at ninety-four percent of the left. He flexed twice to confirm the reading and found that the instrument was correct and that he did not especially care. Close enough for a sword. Close enough for everything else.

[Beat 2]
In the NeoCortex, something stirred.

Not a diagnostic. Not the reintegration protocol. Something else — an attention that was not entirely his own, oriented toward the window sill with the focused interest of an entity that found the pigeon there more interesting than Kyle did.

The pigeon was ordinary. This was the problem. It was a standard GLMZ roof bird, the grayish-brown variety that nested in the gutters of six-story walkups and ate whatever fell from the elevated gardens on the Spine. Kyle had seen this exact bird, or birds indistinguishable from it, every morning of his occupancy of 2F. Puppeteer had been in his NeoCortex for less than twenty-four hours and had already determined that this specific bird was worth sustained observation.

*Interesting,* Kyle thought, in the direction of his own architecture.

*Yes,* the part of the NeoCortex that was not Kyle agreed, and went quiet again.

[Beat 3]
He sat up. The window gave him the pre-dawn GLMZ in its characteristic state: the northern sky slightly lighter than the south, the elevated rail a dark line against the gradient, the maintenance drones doing their circuit checks while the street below was still in the low-traffic window between the last club crowd and the first delivery routes. The substation on the corner wore an Axiom Industrial collar on its main feed, the company thirty years dead and the mounting bracket still carrying current for whoever flew the flag this month — and this month the checkpoint two blocks east had gone Crucible green, which meant the gray zone had moved again and Kyle's own door was on a different side of someone's map than it had been last week. Somewhere in the building's infrastructure the climate system was running its morning adjustment. The smell of the building was concrete and the particular mix of recycled air and something organic from the vertical garden two floors up.

For a moment the light along the elevated rail sat at the wrong frequency — not brighter, not dimmer, just slightly off the color it should have been, the way the block south of the substation took eleven steps to cross when it should have taken nine. The NeoCortex flagged a 19Hz signature under the deep concrete, the hum that arrived a half-second before the Pulse resonance it belonged to. Then the resonance came and the light settled and the math was nine steps again. Kyle noted it, filed it, did not look harder. Some mornings the city was just like that.

Silence was on the nightstand. He hadn't put it there — he'd put it on the side tray of the AutoDoc and not thought about it again until he was under anesthesia, and then Pixel had been there when he came back, and Pixel had brought the sword home, and she had set it on the nightstand and he had not moved it. He looked at it now. It was not looking back. It was a length of matte black meta-alloy with a saya that had been re-lacquered twice in fourteen years and had been on the table in Hua's chamber with someone else's hand on it.

[Beat 4]
He stood. The floor was cold through both feet — both — and he found that this was the kind of data that was worth noting.

Across the wall that was the wall of Unit 2E, there was no music.

Pixel ran music when she slept. He had learned this in the first weeks of their tenancy-overlap, the low bleed of sound through the shared wall that he'd eventually stopped registering as anything except confirmation that she was home and alive. It was the same wall that separated the landing from both their doors. It was always there when he came back to it. He had oriented on it in the trench, in the sluice water, in the AutoDoc with a foreign thing threading its architecture through his NeoCortex — the wall and what was behind it. It had been there and then it hadn't, when the anesthesia took him, and then she'd been in the room when it came back.

No music now. First time in years that he could recall.

[Beat 5]
He put the kettle on.

While it heated he stood at his kitchen window with his right hand open against the glass, checking the seam lines by feel, running the flex protocol twice more. The NeoCortex noted that the pigeon had not moved. Puppeteer's attention, distributed across Kyle's sensor array in a way that felt less like intrusion than like a second pair of eyes he hadn't asked for, seemed to find this equally interesting. Kyle had not yet determined what to do about the E.L.F. in his architecture and was proceeding under the working assumption that the problem could be deferred until his hardware was running at full capacity.

The kettle was a manual pour-over. It was not the kind that whistled. He poured when the temperature read ninety-three on the NeoCortex's thermal overlay, which was approximately the correct temperature for the coffee he made and also the precise temperature at which you stopped counting the seconds.

He made two cups.

[Beat 6]
He left his door open and stood in the kitchen and waited.

The pre-dawn city was doing what it always did — adjusting, recycling, moving freight and maintenance operations through the windows of low surveillance. From 2E, nothing. No creak of the workbench. No sound of bare feet on concrete. He would not knock. He had never knocked — she had always been the one to knock, or to open the door before the knock, and the convention was load-bearing in a way he wouldn't alter without her say-so. He put her cup on the counter in the same spot he always did when he made coffee and she wasn't here.

Puppeteer's attention came off the pigeon and settled, briefly, on the cup.

*I know,* Kyle said to the architecture.

The pigeon departed the sill without ceremony and the morning came in and the cup was there and the door was open and the kettle, already cooling, would stay warm enough for a while yet.
