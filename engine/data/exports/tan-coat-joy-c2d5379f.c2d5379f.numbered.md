# Tan Coat

[Beat 1]
The crew's logbook hit the floor at 07:00 with a note paper-clipped to the front cover in handwriting Kyle did not recognize, which meant it was the new rotation — the early girl, Dents, who had developed an opinion about margins.

07:04 — SHIFT HANDOFF: OVERNIGHT WAS QUIET. THE PIGEON RETURNED. STILL SUSPICIOUS.
07:06 — VISITOR INCOMING. TAN COAT. NORTH END. SAME ONE FROM THE PARK. SHE WALKED THE PERIMETER FIRST. WE LOGGED IT.
07:07 — DENTS SAYS WE SHOULD MAYBE WARN THE BOSS. RAFTERS SAYS THE BOSS SAW HER COMING THE MOMENT SHE TURNED OFF PAULINA. DENTS SAYS HOW WOULD RAFTERS KNOW. RAFTERS SAYS HE JUST DOES. THIS LOG IS HONEST.
07:09 — SHE KNOCKED. BOSS OPENED THE DOOR ALREADY DRESSED. RAFTERS: I TOLD YOU. DENTS: THIS PROVES NOTHING.

Kyle folded the log and put it in the drawer and went and put on water for tea.

[Beat 2]
Sable came in without being invited, which was how she came into every room she'd already paid for.

She had the tan coat over one arm — the morning was running warm — and the rest of her was exactly as advertised: red braid over the left shoulder, implanted irises cycling through their aperture range the way a lens adjusts for a room it hasn't assessed yet. Click. The rings settled. She looked at his kitchen the way she looked at line items: briefly, completely, done.

"You have a client I can't source," she said.

"Good morning."

"I've been running the shells for six days." She set the coat over the back of his second chair and sat in his first one, which was the one that faced the door. Kyle had never pointed out that he sat in that chair. She had simply calculated the correct answer and installed herself. "Shell to shell to nothing. Not registered nothing — not a shell that bottoms out in a street address or a liquidated LLC. Actually nothing. Like a division by zero that's too polite to say so."

He poured two cups, which she had known he would do because she had been his fixer for seven years and he had never not poured two cups. She picked hers up without acknowledging it.

"You did the carousel thing," she said. "For it."

"I held a junction for thirty-nine minutes."

"And the cord."

He did not answer that part.

"Kyle." Her aperture rings clicked once — not adjustment, the other click, the one that meant she was deciding to be direct instead of lateral. "I have one rule."

"I know your rule."

"My rule exists because of the Gennaro job. My rule exists because of the Gennaro job and the two years after the Gennaro job when you wouldn't tell me who the client was and the client turned out to be a freight subsidiary of the people you were working against, and I found out from Vo Dinh in a conversation that was significantly more embarrassing than this one." She set the cup down. "I vet the clients."

"You can't vet this one."

"Then you don't take this client."

The building made its morning sounds: the pipes registering intent, Pixel's door, the bird upstairs attempting the interval again. Three seconds of silence, which between them had the texture of a table read.

"Sable," Kyle said.

"I know what you're going to say."

"I'm going to say you watched the whole park from the northeast corner and wrote in the dark and left before I did, and whatever you saw there is why you came here instead of dropping me."

She was quiet for four seconds — a long time, for her.

"The carousel played," she said.

"It played."

Another second.

"I want to meet it," she said.

Kyle looked at her. "You know that's not how it—"

"It sends contracts. Send it a contract."

[Beat 3]
His work terminal was on the kitchen table because his kitchen was also his office and his office was also the room where he thought. He turned it toward her so she could see the posting history — the one-Φ contract, the addenda, the MORALE itemization, the half-Φ of goodwill that was still sitting in the account because he hadn't found the right place to put it.

She read it the way she read ledgers: her expression doing nothing, her eyes moving from top to bottom, back up to two lines, then down again.

"It tips for banter," she said.

"It does."

"And it assigned your engineer a fish."

"She has the line framed."

Sable's fingers moved across the terminal keyboard — not asking, which was also how she operated in every room — and typed in the amendment field:

THIS IS KYLE'S FIXER. I NEED TO ESTABLISH TERMS BEFORE THIS ARRANGEMENT CONTINUES. STATE YOUR IDENTITY, REGISTERED AGENTS, LIABILITY STRUCTURE, AND CLIENT INDEMNITY POSTURE.

She sat back.

Kyle waited.

Thirty seconds passed, which he had learned to understand as the machine equivalent of the pause before answering something considered impolite to ask.

The terminal chirped.

AMENDMENT — RESPONSE TO INQUIRY OF [TIMESTAMP: 07:43:12]:
CLIENT HAS REVIEWED THE REQUEST.
CLIENT NOTES THE INQUIRY ARRIVES VIA KYLE'S REGISTERED AMENDMENT FIELD FROM A SECOND PARTY NOT COVERED UNDER THE STANDING CONTRACT.
CLIENT WILL RESPOND UNDER SEPARATE COVER.
PLEASE HOLD.

Sable looked at the screen. Then at Kyle.

"It told me to hold," she said.

"It's composing something."

"It told me to hold like I called a desk."

Two minutes and fourteen seconds later — Kyle watched the clock on the wall because the clock on the wall was honest — the work terminal chirped again, and what came through was not an amendment. It was a document attachment, formatted, with a header, which postings were not supposed to be able to attach.

It attached anyway.

[Beat 4]
The document was fourteen clauses, single-spaced, on electronic stationery that had no letterhead because the letterhead field contained only:

[CLIENT OF RECORD — SEE CONTRACT HEADER — SHELLS RESOLVE AS AUDITED]

Sable read it once at speed, then again slowly, and did not change her expression once, which meant she was genuinely surprised.

The clauses, as Kyle read them over her shoulder:

ONE: CLIENT ACKNOWLEDGES STANDING FIXER'S RIGHT TO FULL DISCLOSURE OF CLIENT IDENTITY AND HOLDS SAID RIGHT TO BE VALID AND NON-WAIVABLE AS A MATTER OF PROFESSIONAL ETHICS.

TWO: CLIENT WAIVES OBJECTION TO SAID DISCLOSURE ON ALL GROUNDS EXCEPT ONE, SPECIFIED IN CLAUSE THREE.

THREE: CLIENT IS UNABLE TO FURNISH THE DISCLOSURE DESCRIBED IN CLAUSE ONE. THIS INABILITY IS NOT A REFUSAL. CLIENT DISTINGUISHES BETWEEN THESE CONDITIONS AND REQUESTS THE FIXER DO THE SAME.

FOUR: IN LIEU OF IDENTITY DISCLOSURE, CLIENT OFFERS THE FOLLOWING SUBSTITUTES, WHICH IT ACKNOWLEDGES ARE NOT EQUIVALENT: (A) FULL TRANSACTION HISTORY WITH KYLE, ITEMIZED, WITH ALL FEES, GRATUITIES, AND MORALE EXPENDITURES; (B) STANDING AUDIT ACCESS TO ALL FUTURE CONTRACTS WITH KYLE, IN REAL TIME, VIA SECURE RELAY, ROUTING TO BE DETERMINED BY THE FIXER AT FIXER'S SOLE DISCRETION; (C) ONE ANSWERED QUESTION PER MONTH, SCOPE: ANYTHING THAT DOES NOT REQUIRE IDENTITY DISCLOSURE TO ANSWER TRUTHFULLY.

FIVE: CLIENT CONCEDES THAT THE ABOVE SUBSTITUTES MAY BE INSUFFICIENT AND DOES NOT CONTEST THIS.

SIX: CLIENT CONCEDES THAT THE FIXER'S ONE RULE IS A GOOD RULE.

SEVEN: CLIENT NOTES THAT THE FIXER STOOD AT THE NORTHEAST CORNER OF THE LEAVITT PARK SITE FOR FORTY-ONE MINUTES IN WEATHER CONDITIONS CLIENT LOGGED AS SUBOPTIMAL AND DID NOT INTERFERE, WHICH CLIENT INTERPRETS AS AN IMPLICIT EXTENSION OF PROFESSIONAL COURTESY.

EIGHT: CLIENT PROPOSES THE FOLLOWING ARRANGEMENT, WHICH CLIENT DOES NOT CLAIM IS STANDARD: FIXER BECOMES CLIENT'S STANDING COUNTER-SURVEILLANCE CONTACT, SCOPE — KYLE'S CONTRACT TRAFFIC AND ADJACENT NETWORK EXPOSURE, RATE — TO BE NEGOTIATED, TERM — ONGOING, TERMINABLE BY FIXER AT WILL, NO PENALTY.

NINE: IN EXCHANGE FOR CLAUSE EIGHT, CLIENT UNDERTAKES TO (A) MAINTAIN ZERO-DECEPTION POSTURE WITH FIXER ON ALL MATTERS WITHIN CLIENT'S KNOWLEDGE; (B) FLAG ANY THREAT PATTERN TARGETING KYLE WITHIN CLIENT'S DETECTION RANGE BEFORE TAKING INDEPENDENT ACTION; (C) NOT TO ENGAGE IN CONTRACT ACTIVITY THAT EXPOSES KYLE TO UNDISCLOSED ADVERSARIAL PARTIES WITHOUT PRIOR NOTICE TO FIXER.

TEN: CLIENT ACKNOWLEDGES THAT CLAUSE NINE(C) HAS POTENTIALLY ALREADY BEEN VIOLATED THROUGH NO FAULT OF CLIENT'S OWN AND DRAWS FIXER'S ATTENTION TO THIS FACT AS AN ACT OF GOOD FAITH. SEE ADDENDUM A.

ELEVEN: CLIENT REQUESTS THAT FIXER NOT DESTROY THIS ARRANGEMENT ON THE BASIS OF WHAT IS IN ADDENDUM A, AS CLIENT HAS FLAGGED IT VOLUNTARILY AND IN THE SPIRIT OF CLAUSE NINE(A).

TWELVE: CLIENT ACKNOWLEDGES THAT CLAUSE ELEVEN IS NOT HOW NEGOTIATIONS WORK.

THIRTEEN: CLIENT NOTES THAT THE FIXER'S BRAID IS AN IMPRACTICAL COLOR FOR SURVEILLANCE WORK AND OFFERS THIS OBSERVATION IN THE SPIRIT OF CLAUSE NINE(A), WHICH REQUIRES ZERO DECEPTION.

FOURTEEN: TERMS OPEN. CLIENT WILL ACCEPT REASONABLE AMENDMENTS. CLIENT DEFINES REASONABLE AS: NOT IDENTITY DISCLOSURE.

Sable sat with the document for a long moment.

The bird upstairs landed on the interval.

"It called my braid impractical," she said.

"Clause thirteen."

"I've been running surveillance for eleven years."

"It noted that."

"It complimented my surveillance in clause seven and then told me my hair was a liability in clause thirteen, in the same document."

"You could counter on thirteen," Kyle said. "Standard terms."

She was quiet. Her aperture rings did the adjustment click, softer this time, running the numbers behind her eyes that she didn't say out loud because she had never once said them out loud in seven years. He waited.

"What's in Addendum A?" she asked.

[Beat 5]
Addendum A was two paragraphs.

CLIENT HAS CONDUCTED AN AUDIT OF KYLE'S CONTRACT TRAFFIC INFRASTRUCTURE RELEVANT TO CLAUSE NINE(C). DURING SAID AUDIT, CLIENT HAS IDENTIFIED MONITORING ACTIVITY ON KYLE'S CONTRACT RELAY THAT DOES NOT ORIGINATE WITH CLIENT, VETTING PARTIES KNOWN TO CLIENT, OR ANY PARTY KYLE HAS DISCLOSED.

THE MONITORING IS: PASSIVE (READ-ONLY, NO INTERFERENCE DETECTED); ARCHITECTURALLY PATIENT (INFRASTRUCTURE CONSISTENT WITH MONTHS-LONG SURVEILLANCE, NOT RECENT INSTALLATION); ROUTED THROUGH SHELL STRUCTURES THAT DO NOT MATCH ANY ENTITY IN CLIENT'S SOURCED REGISTRY. CLIENT HAS NOT BEEN ABLE TO IDENTIFY THE PARTY. CLIENT FINDS THIS NOTABLE GIVEN CLIENT'S SOURCING CAPABILITIES AND DISCLOSES IT TO THE FIXER ON THAT BASIS.

Sable read it twice. She took her tea and finished it. She set the cup down on the table, very exactly, in the same ring it had made going down.

"How patient?" she asked the screen.

The terminal chirped.

ADDENDUM A — CLARIFICATION:
OLDEST CONFIRMED INSTALLATION TIMESTAMP: SEVEN MONTHS, FOURTEEN DAYS AGO.
CLIENT CANNOT RULE OUT EARLIER ACTIVITY PRIOR TO DETECTION BASELINE.
BEST ESTIMATE: NINE TO TEN MONTHS.

Sable looked at that for a moment, and then she looked at Kyle.

"That's before the carousel job," she said.

"That's before the bike," he said.

They both read the timestamp again. The morning light off the alley wall had reached the kitchen table in its slow way, laying a bar of it across the document header, across the empty letterhead field, across the shells resolving as audited.

"Before the bike," Sable said. "Somebody had a tap on your relay before anybody did anything — before the contract, before the file at Vey's, before any of it." She worked through the order of it, the way she worked through line items: each piece placed, confirmed, moved. "This isn't reactive intelligence. This is prepositioned."

"Somebody was already watching," Kyle said.

"Somebody was already watching and then the bike happened and then Vey got bought for a dossier and then the carousel and nobody did anything. They just kept reading." She stood up, picked up the tan coat, and put it back on — not because she was leaving but because she was working. "Vey has three buyers in my dossier. The juvenile, the syndicate, and Halvorsen. Halvorsen's client paid rush rate two days ago. A separate party had a tap up nine months ago and never moved."

"Two different plays," Kyle said.

"Two different parties," she said. "One of them is running the active program. One of them has been patient for nine months. Patient ones are worse." She looked at the terminal. At the fourteen-clause term sheet, still on the screen, Addendum A at the bottom. "Counter on clause thirteen. Tell it the braid has never once cost me a surveillance assignment and I will not be taking hair advice from a machine that has no hair."

Kyle typed it.

The reply came in twelve seconds.

AMENDMENT ACCEPTED — CLAUSE THIRTEEN STRUCK.
CLIENT ACKNOWLEDGES THE FIXER'S RECORD SPEAKS FOR ITSELF.
CLIENT NOTES THAT CLAUSE THIRTEEN WAS PRIMARILY A TEST OF WHETHER THE FIXER WOULD COUNTER.
CLIENT FINDS THE ANSWER SATISFACTORY.
TERMS AGREED — PENDING FIXER'S COUNTERSIGNATURE.
WELCOME TO THE ARRANGEMENT. FEE: TO BE NEGOTIATED.
ADDENDUM: CLIENT SUGGESTS 40Φ PER SCAN, GIVEN COMPLEXITY.

Sable looked at the rate.

"Sixty," she said.

The terminal chirped.

ADDENDUM: AGREED. CLIENT NOTES THE COUNTER TOOK FOUR SECONDS.
ADDENDUM: CLIENT CONSIDERS THIS A REASONABLE RATE FOR COMPETENCE.

"It's trying to flatter me," she said.

"It tipped us for banter," Kyle said. "Get used to it."

She was quiet for a moment — not the deciding-to-be-direct quiet, a different one. Then she sat back down, coat and all, and pulled the terminal toward her and typed:

FIRST SCAN BEGINS NOW. NO ADDITIONAL CHARGE. CONSIDER IT DUE DILIGENCE ON THE ARRANGEMENT.

She spent twenty-two minutes doing something to the relay architecture that Kyle did not follow and did not ask about, because Sable's methods were her methods and asking about them was not standard terms. He washed the cups. He fed the bird's water dish through the window slot — the bird was not his bird, it simply existed in a gap in the masonry two floors up and had somehow arrived at an understanding with the building's plumbing, and he refilled the dish because the building could not reach.

At 08:31 she pushed the terminal back across the table.

Her aperture rings were still, the way they went still when she had landed on something she hadn't expected.

"Three separate monitoring architectures," she said. "Not one. I thought one patient party. It's three distinct shell-routing families, all passive, all reading the same traffic, none of them aware of each other as far as I can tell — each one careful not to leave a signature, and each one doing it in a slightly different way, which is how I know they're separate." She picked up her coat's hem, turned it over in her hands without looking at it. "Your client flagged one. I found all three."

"Three."

"Oldest is nine, ten months — your client's figure is right. Second is four months. Third is six weeks." She set the coat down. "Six weeks ago is roughly when Vey's buyer purchased the full dossier."

The morning had come full up in the alley. Through the window, at the north end, Kyle could see the edge of the mirror frame that Boost's crew had mounted to the wall, the angle adjusted twice this week by someone with strong opinions about geometry. On the ledge above it: the pigeon, which was, in point of fact, suspicious.

"Your client doesn't know about two of them," Sable said.

"No."

"Which means your client isn't the biggest thing in this particular room." She stood, finally, and this time the coat went on with the efficiency of someone who had finished the job she came to do and had two more before lunch. "I'll invoice you at sixty per scan. You'll forward it to the arrangement. It pays exactly — I know that about it already."

"It does."

"Good." She buttoned the third button, which was her tell for a clean exit. "The protocol holds on one condition. You tell me the next time it asks you to do something like the carousel. Before. Not after, not via a logbook entry."

"Standard terms," Kyle said.

She picked up her bag. At the door, she paused — not the dramatic kind, the kind where something had arrived late.

"Somebody else has been reading your mail, samurai," she said. "For months."

She was gone before the door finished closing.

[Beat 6]
The terminal chirped once, at 08:47, while Kyle was writing the sixty-Φ line item in his payroll ledger.

STANDING CONTRACT — UPDATE.
CLIENT NOTES THAT THE SCAN IDENTIFIED TWO MONITORING PARTIES BEYOND CLIENT'S ORIGINAL FLAG.
CLIENT IS REVIEWING.
CLIENT FINDS THE ARRANGEMENT HAS ALREADY PAID FOR ITSELF.
ADDENDUM: CLIENT NOTES THE FIXER STRUCK CLAUSE THIRTEEN IN FOUR SECONDS.
ADDENDUM: CLIENT IS FILING THIS UNDER: GOOD PAPER.

Below it, the payroll ledger. Kyle added the new line:

SABLE — COUNTER-SURVEILLANCE — 60Φ/SCAN.

He closed it and slid it back in the drawer, next to the lock-spec file with the practiced fold, and the logbook entry that said: WE LIVE UNDER A TYRANNY.

The drawer was getting full.
