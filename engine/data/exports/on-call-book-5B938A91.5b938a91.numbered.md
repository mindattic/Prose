# On Call

_A logistics operator traces a maintenance escrow to a decommissioned mass driver on the East African coast and discovers what Sparrow actually is. Told in alternating POV: Elias on the ground, Sparrow from orbit._

[Beat 1]
SPARROW / ORBITAL LOG 2226-02-12
Altitude: ~740,000 mi / Orbital period: 14 days / Day 8 of current orbit

The contact window for Meridian 88 opens at 09:34 UTC.

She notes:

Zone 3, GLMZ: a chemical fire, seventeen minutes, gray smoke column consistent with recycled coolant combustion at approximately 340 degrees Celsius. The corridor authority will cite a salvage cooperative. The salvage cooperative will appeal. She has watched this sequence resolve fourteen times. It resolves the same way.

MERIDIAN-7 salvage fleet, 09:58: authorized corridor deviation, thirty-eight meters, to avoid a subsurface debris cluster. She files the deviation under standard protocol.

The color of Lake Michigan at 10:22 UTC: green-gray, index 4.9. This matches her record for 2224-02-12. It does not match 2225-02-12, which ran paler — a particulate shift from the northern smelting corridor that closed in late 2225. She notes the correction. She will check again in 2227 to see if it holds.

Four ascending objects from the sub-lake corridor remain in her catalog: 2189.1 (1.8 tonnes), 2202.1 (2.3 tonnes), 2213.1-3 (1.6 tonnes, three pieces), 2226.1-3 (2.1 tonnes, three pieces). All filed as recovered and disposed by the recovery chain. The corridor is clear. The filings are current.

No calls to her number today.

The contact window closes at 11:47 UTC. She continues her orbit.

Next window: 14 days.

[Beat 2]
The invoice arrives on a Tuesday in February, routed through a claims clearinghouse he has worked with for six years and trusted in the abstract way you trust systems you have never had reason to test.

Orbital debris recovery services rendered, Lot 7 of 9, Zone 4 south corridor, weight class 2.1 tonnes, recovery agent designation SPARROW/ACTIVE, net amount 14,200 Φ, payment terms NET-30.

At the bottom of the page, in the remittance section: a routing code he does not recognize.

He files it and moves on. It is 9:07 in the morning and he has twenty-two more invoices to process before noon.

At 11:54 he opens it again.

---

The routing code leads to a clearinghouse he does not recognize. The clearinghouse leads, via three hop-accounts that each exist for roughly one financial quarter before rolling over, to a maintenance escrow registered to an entity called Equatorial Ground Station Partners LLC, which has no web presence, which carries a registered address at a law firm in Nassau that handles approximately four hundred such entities, which has been disbursing the same monthly maintenance payment — 3,400 Φ, on the fifteenth of every month — since 2158.

He has been in this job for nine years. He processes an average of sixty-four invoices per day. He has never seen a disbursement that old.

At 13:01 he opens it again.

He pulls the maintenance escrow's full disbursement history: sixty-eight years of 3,400 Φ on the fifteenth, no interruptions, no payee adjustments. He checks whether the payee entity still exists. It does — a ground station registration on the East African coast, listed as decommissioned in the orbital asset registry in 2163, still maintaining the escrow, still receiving 3,400 Φ a month from a chain of shell accounts that extend back through reorganizations he cannot fully trace, to an origin that predates the Concordance.

A ground station that closed in 2163 is receiving a maintenance disbursement in 2226. The maintenance disbursement is tied to a recovery invoice he processed this morning. He processed it for a debris recovery job completed in Zone 4 last week.

He eats lunch at his desk at 14:15. He does not taste it.

---

At 15:30, his colleague Siosaia sends him a message asking if he is joining the team call. He says yes and stays at his desk. At 15:47 she sends: you missed the call. At 15:48: everything okay?

He writes back: found something weird in the routing.

She writes: file it as anomalous and move on. Then: we're behind on Zone 9. Then, three minutes later: what kind of weird?

He takes a pill from the flat matte case on his desk, hinge worn smooth, and starts writing the reply. He writes two sentences and deletes them. He writes four more. He sends: maintenance escrow, 2158, still clearing monthly. Ground station that's been decommissioned for sixty years.

She writes back after a longer pause: that's an accounting ghost. Legal will handle it.

He searches the ground station's coordinates in the orbital asset registry: 3.7 degrees S, 39.9 degrees E. He pulls a satellite image. The image shows a flat expanse of East African coast — green scrub, shallow hills rolling to the coast, and in the center of the frame a large industrial structure. A mass driver barrel, forty meters across, aimed at the equatorial sky. Overgrown at the base. Not, as far as he can determine from 800 kilometers up, in disrepair.

He closes the browser. He reopens it.

At 17:00 the work day ends. He does not notice.

---

Later that evening — he is not sure what time, somewhere past midnight — he sits on the balcony for the first time in a month. He does not go out there much. The city from the forty-second floor is a fact he has processed but not fully internalized, which is a distinction he notices only when the city is doing something he cannot categorize. Tonight the lake is fog. Somewhere below and to the north, the Spine's cargo runs are moving without being visible, felt as a low thrumline through the ferrocement railing. He leans on it with his forearms and looks at where the lake should be.

He has been doing this work for nine years. He has processed forty-two Zone 4 recovery jobs in that time. He has never looked at what he was recovering. He knows this about himself — it is a function of throughput, of workload management, of the particular efficiency that comes from not examining things closely. He is good at his job. His closure rate is in the top six percent.

A ground station that has been decommissioned for sixty-three years is receiving maintenance funds. Those maintenance funds are tied to recovery jobs that moved through his queue. He processed one of them this morning in eleven minutes without looking at what was inside it.

He goes back inside. He opens the orbital asset registry again. He starts a spreadsheet.

[Beat 3]

The color of Lake Michigan at 14:58 UTC: green-gray, index 4.7. Slightly below the February reading. She records both values and waits for more data before noting a trend.

At 15:03 UTC, a GLMZ residential exchange calls her number.
At 15:04 UTC, the same exchange calls again.
At 15:09 UTC, the exchange calls a third time.

The third caller asks about a maintenance account.

She does not answer.

She notes: the caller's exchange is consistent with high-floor residential, Spine district, GLMZ. The call originated in zone coordinates placing it in the northern ferrocement corridor. The number is not published. It passes between operators in the Zone 4 recovery chain on an informal basis. Siosaia Tuivaga-Brennan, Cordon Freight, used it once in 2224 for a Zone 4 routing discrepancy. The lot 2226.1-3 recovery was filed by a Cordon Freight operator.

She does not know which one.

The contact window closes at 16:29 UTC. She continues her orbit.

Next window: 14 days.
Note: first contact from the recovery chain.

[Beat 4]
Eight months. He has been on this for eight months.

He knows what the office thinks. He knows because Siosaia told him, in the direct way she delivers information she has decided to stop filtering: Elias, they think you have a problem with this file. They is the team lead and two of the senior operators who process Zone 4 alongside him. They think you keep opening it because you can't let a gap go. A beat. I defended you. Another beat. I think they might be right.

He has a wall in his apartment — not literally a wall, because he does not print things, but the equivalent in his work files: a directory called EQUATORIAL_GROUNDSTATION_PARTNERS with forty-seven subdirectories, each a chain of shell companies he has traced by hand through corporate registry filings, bankruptcy records, and asset transfer documents going back to 2172. He has found the origin account for the maintenance disbursements. It is a holding company incorporated in 2086, decades before the Concordance, predating the GLMZ financial registry itself. He cannot find who owns it. He cannot find anyone who has ever owned it.

The 3,400 Φ clears on the fifteenth of every month. It has cleared for 808 consecutive months. It will clear again in six days.

He told Siosaia about the 808 months at the coffee machine last Tuesday. She looked at him for a moment. She said: Elias. She said it the way a doctor says a name. She said: this is the Papyrus thing.

A man, years ago, couldn't stop being right about a font choice in a movie credit that nobody else cared about. He recorded a video. It achieved a strange immortality. The font was Papyrus. The problem was that he couldn't stop.

You're right about the escrow, Siosaia said. That's the part that's like the Papyrus thing.

He has thought about this. He does not think she is wrong. He also cannot close the file.

---

Today she gave him a number.

She did it at the end of the day, when the office was quieting, when she could have done it loudly and didn't. She walked to his desk, put a small piece of folded paper next to his keyboard, and said: This is a number for a network operator who handles recovery corridor logistics. They're called Sparrow. The number works on alternate weeks. A pause. I used it once, two years ago, for a job in Zone 4. The routing on the invoice looked off and I wanted a second opinion from someone outside Cordon. She looked at the paper, then at him. They know things about the Zone 4 corridor. I thought you might want to ask.

She went back to her desk. She did not say what she found when she asked. He did not ask her.

He called the number at 15:03. Then at 15:04 because the first call dropped before it connected. Then at 15:09 because the second call produced a long silence he couldn't tell from dead air, and he hung up and redialed.

The third call connected. He gave the job number and asked about the maintenance account in the remittance routing.

What came back, fifteen seconds later, was a text: a routing analysis, three paragraphs, the shell company chain he had spent eight months building by hand laid out cleanly in 200 words, with three nodes he had not found, and at the bottom a single line — The escrow is current. The facility is maintained. Then: What do you need from the corridor?

He stared at this for a while.

He wrote back: How do you know the facility is maintained?

The response took longer. When it came back, it was two words: We watch.

He sits at his desk with the phone face-up on the blotter. His queue has forty-six items in it. He has not touched it since 15:03.

He writes in the notepad: From where?
