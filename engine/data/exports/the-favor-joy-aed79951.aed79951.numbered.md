# The Favor

[Beat 1]
The crew's logbook, delivered under the door at 07:02, opened the day the way it usually did: in five hands, without editorial consensus.

07:00 — SHIFT START. WEATHER: COLD BUT DRY. LOGGED AS: RARE.
07:01 — NORTH ALLEY CLEAR. SOUTH ALLEY: PIGEON RETURNED (SAME PIGEON? RAFTERS SAYS YES. DENTS SAYS "PIGEONS ARE UNKNOWABLE." ONGOING DISPUTE, SEE APPENDIX B.)
07:08 — WOMAN, CHROMATOPHORE LEGS, TWO SPONSORSHIP PANELS CYCLING. STOPPED BELOW WINDOW. LOOKED UP. (NOTE: SHE DID NOT LOOK AT THE CAMERA. SHE LOOKED AT THE WINDOW. SHE KNOWS THE WINDOW.) FLAGGING: NOT A PIGEON.
07:09 — BOSS, YOU HAVE A CALLER. — B.

Kyle read it at the kitchen table over tea and was downstairs by 07:14.

[Beat 2]
She was sitting on the freight lift's safety rail the way people who run sponsorship legs sit on things: weight distributed like she'd done the math, both panels cycling their rotation — some pharma outfit's sunrise blue, 6.2 Φ per thousand views — and she was watching his door with the particular patience of a person who has already rehearsed this conversation three times on the way over and is prepared to run it a fourth.

"Ria," Kyle said.

She looked at him for a moment. Then: "You know my name."

"I looked you up after the box truck." He had. A courier registered under Ria Osei-Mensah, five-year license, clean filings, the kind of contract rider who sponsors a pair of display legs on a twelve-month rotation deal rather than leasing something off-the-rack, which meant she was in the trade for real and priced her gear accordingly. "You called the favor."

"I need to call the favor," she said, in the voice of someone who has been holding it in the body for several days. "I need to call it today. My brother Tomas loads cargo at Blackwater Receiving — the Pulse freight yard off the 103rd interchange. A debt-court filing came in four days ago. He's been accused of skimming a container shipment — forty-two units of med-grade microprinters, twelve thousand Φ wholesale. Docking of six thousand, first hearing in three days. He didn't do it."

"You're certain."

"I'm his sister and I'm certain," she said, and then, because she was in the trade for real: "And the numbers don't make sense. Tomas pulls eight hundred Φ a month, he sends two-fifty to our mother in Accra-6, his rent is three-forty, he eats noodles. He doesn't have the connections to move microprinters. Someone does, and they filed before anyone could ask."

Four days since the filing, three until the hearing. A favor called this late meant somebody had waited as long as they could.

"Who's his supervisor?" he asked.

"Shift lead, name of Parr. Brennan Parr." She said it with the flat precision of someone who had already done the looking and didn't like what they'd found. "He's been with the yard twelve years. Good record, nothing flagged — but Tomas says he started coming in with a different quality of problem about six months ago. Distracted. Jumpy at inventory counts. Running reconciliations himself instead of delegating."

"In debt," Kyle said.

"That's what I think."

One box-truck tip, exchanged for one unspecified call, arriving at 07:14 on a cold Wednesday with a name, a number, and a three-day window.

"I'll need a yard schematic and Tomas's shift schedule," he said. "And Parr's."

She had both of them already pulled on her terminal. Of course she did.

WIN: the shape of the job. One favor off the books.

[Beat 3]
The work terminal chirped at 08:30, which was forty minutes after Kyle had opened the yard schematic on his kitchen table, which was forty minutes after the entity had no possible way to know Kyle was looking at a yard schematic.

The posting read:

STANDING CONTRACT — ADDENDUM (VOLUNTARY).
CLIENT NOTES BLACKWATER RECEIVING IS A CLASS-2 PULSE FREIGHT YARD.
SLUG SCHEDULE AS FOLLOWS: INBOUND RUNS AT :05 AND :35. OUTBOUND RUNS AT :17 AND :47. GAP BETWEEN INBOUND CLEARANCE AND OUTBOUND STAGING: 11 MINUTES.
SAFE CORRIDORS: PLATFORM A3–A7 DURING GAP ONLY. B-SIDE CLEARS 90 SECONDS BEHIND SCHEDULE (MAINTENANCE VARIANCE, EVERY SHIFT, UNFILED).
CLIENT ALSO NOTES: RECONCILIATION RECORDS FOR CONTAINER CLASS 7–9 ACCESSIBLE VIA YARD MANAGEMENT TERMINAL, GATE 3. ACCESS REQUIRES YARD CREDENTIALS. CLIENT HAS NO YARD CREDENTIALS.
NOTE: TOMAS OSEI-MENSAH HAS SATISFACTORY PERFORMANCE RECORDS FOR 4 YEARS, 7 MONTHS, 2 DAYS.
CLIENT WILL NOT COMMENT FURTHER ON THE SITUATION.
CLIENT IS COMMENTING ON THE SITUATION.

Kyle read the last two lines twice.

"It pulled Tomas's employment file," he said, to no one, then to the terminal: "Copy."

The terminal did not chirp. He had the schedules already.

[Beat 4]
Pixel looked at the yard schematic at 09:15 with the focused expression she reserved for problems that were genuinely interesting.

"A Pulse freight yard," she said. "Moving slugs on a fourteen-minute cycle. Corridors that are safe for eleven minutes in each half-hour." She traced the B-side with one finger. "With a 90-second drift baked into the outbound that nobody filed because filing it means explaining why the variance exists, which means someone inspects the B-side gear, which costs money."

"The supervisor knows the drift."

"The supervisor's had twelve years to know the drift." She straightened up. "You want inside the reconciliation terminal at Gate 3."

"I want inside the reconciliation terminal at Gate 3 before 14:00 today, because Tomas is on the afternoon shift and if I'm going to clear him I need to do it while he's standing on the floor with witnesses."

She looked at him, and then at the schedule again, and then at the schematic with the 11-minute safe windows.

"The entity gave you the slug timetable."

"Voluntarily."

"It noted Tomas's satisfactory record."

"Three times."

"It's worried," said Pixel, with something that was not quite a smile, the way the fish-ball in the second bowl of noodles was not quite a bonus. "Okay. I can spoof a credentials badge. Takes an hour." She was already pulling the field kit. "Also I'm coming."

"It's a freight yard."

"The entity said safe corridors during the gap only. Safe corridors during the gap only is an engineer problem." She held up the field kit. "Standard terms."

[Beat 5]
Blackwater Receiving smelled like machine oil, ozone, and the cold that lives inside large structures where the air organizes by temperature layer rather than moving. The access gate took Pixel's spoofed credentials without comment, logging them under a maintenance sub-contractor ID built from a defunct hydraulics outfit that had left its licensing very gently open.

"The company isn't real," Kyle said, walking the B-platform.

"The company was real for four years and paid its insurance on time," Pixel said. "The ID is a monument. Stop worrying about the ID."

The yard's freight slugs waited in the staging bays — matte gray composite shells the length of a transit car, rated to Mach 6 in the vacuum tube, currently on pneumatic dollies. They were big. Kyle noted them the way you note weather.

Tomas Osei-Mensah was on A-platform, running manifest checks with two other loaders. He was thicker than his sister, with her same economy of motion, and he had not been sleeping, which showed in the way of men being watched by a clock they did not set.

He saw Kyle and Ria together at the platform end and stopped moving for approximately two seconds.

"You called it," he said to his sister, when they were close enough.

"I called it," she said.

"You called it on a samurai."

"I called it on THE samurai." She said it the way someone says a thing they've been deciding how to say since the freight lift that morning. "His rate is fine. It's a favor."

Tomas looked at Kyle with the frankness of a man who had exactly three days and was in the habit of spending his resources carefully. "Parr knows something's coming," he said. "He's been doing reconciliations himself, on terminal three at Gate 3, every day between 13:00 and 14:00. Alone. The reconciliation records are where the gap is — I can see in my own manifest that units were re-logged under a different container class after the batch was sealed. I can't touch the terminal; I'm flagged as a subject of inquiry."

"I can," Kyle said. "I need thirteen minutes at Gate 3."

"The gap window is eleven minutes."

"I know."

Tomas thought about this for approximately three seconds, and then: "B-platform's outbound is ninety seconds late every shift."

"I know that too."

The clearance panel above the A-platform went green, and somewhere below the floor the first inbound slug decelerated from transit speed with a sound like a building drawing breath.

[Beat 6]
Gate 3 was a glass-front reconciliation office set into the yard wall where B-platform met the admin corridor, with a single management terminal, a view of the staging bay, and no windows facing the yard interior — which meant Parr, when he was in it, could see the slugs queue but not the platform approach from the south.

Kyle went in at 13:11, during the gap, while the inbound clearance light was still cycling red and the B-platform corridor was running its undocumented 90-second lag. Pixel was outside with the schematic on her terminal and a view of both countdown panels.

The reconciliation files were organized for plausible deniability: problem entries reclassified under a container category that legitimately handled mixed-manifest cargo, timestamps readable as clerical error by anyone who didn't know what to look for.

Kyle knew what to look for.

The re-logs for the twelve-thousand-Φ microprinter batch: original manifest clean, sealed under Tomas's ID at 21:43 six days prior. The reclassification entered at 02:17 the following morning, under a supervisor override code that resolved to Parr.

Outside the panel window, the outbound clearance panel clicked from amber to red. Six minutes.

Kyle copied the records, timestamps, and override ID to a sealed data package flagged for yard security, then entered Parr's override code as the revised source of record in the formal inquiry — the system accepted it, it was accurate, and Tomas's name had been on the filing four days too long.

ADDENDUM TO INQUIRY 7741-R: REVISED SOURCE OF RECORD. SUPERVISOR OVERRIDE 09-PAR, TIMESTAMP 02:17. SEE ATTACHED RECONCILIATION DIFF.

He hit submit at 13:18.

"Three minutes," Pixel said, on comms.

He was out of Gate 3 at 13:19.

WIN: the records corrected, on the record, under Parr's own credentials. Cost: to be determined.

[Beat 7]
Parr was not a large man. He was the kind of man who had twelve years of institutional weight behind him and moved like it, which is different: steady, unhurried, the kind of unhurried that was previously confident and had recently become careful. He came onto the B-platform at 13:31 with a terminal in his hand and the expression of someone whose reconciliation filing had just notified him of a revision.

He saw Kyle first, because Kyle was standing in the clear, and then he saw Tomas on the platform behind Kyle, and then he looked at the terminal again.

"You're not yard security," he said.

"No," Kyle said.

"You filed under my code."

"Your code filed under your code. I just found where it had been." Kyle held up his own terminal with the records pulled. "The re-log was at 02:17 under your override. The original manifest was clean. The inquiry's been revised to reflect the actual source."

Parr was quiet for long enough that the outbound clearance panel ran two cycles. He had run a bad play and known it was bad and run it anyway, and now the geometry had resolved.

"How much do they have you for?" Kyle asked.

"Fourteen thousand. Rate adjusts." He looked at Tomas. "I needed one clean lift. Filed it back the whole way, nobody was supposed to be—" he stopped. "He was the easiest manifest to fit."

"He was. You'll want to talk to yard security."

"I know." The relief of a man who had been bracing for the landing. "The collectors. They'll—"

"That's a separate problem," Kyle said. "Start with security."

[Beat 8]
The two men came off the south platform access at 13:47, while the second inbound slug was still decelerating in the bay. They had the look of men on a fee — casual in the way that means calibrated, taking their time over ground they'd already walked — and they had seen Kyle come out of Gate 3 and done the arithmetic about what that meant.

One of them was carrying a shock-bar clipped to his belt. The other had his hands free, which in Kyle's experience was worse.

"Yard's private property," the first one said. "You're not credentialed."

"I was," Kyle said. "Hydraulics maintenance, sub-contract, third-party manifest audit. I signed out at the gate." He watched both of them and the slug bay behind them and the countdown panel above. Amber. Two minutes to outbound clearance. "You should go."

"The man we're here about," the second one said, nodding back toward where Parr had gone, "has a payment arrangement. Arrangements don't get renegotiated by freelancers."

"The arrangement already changed," Kyle said. "Yard security has his statement."

They looked at each other. The first one unclipped the shock-bar.

Kyle did not reach for Cacophony.

The shock-bar man came in high and to the right, which was the obvious side, and the second came in low and left, which was the read on the first move — a pincer that would have worked on a man who needed room to draw. Kyle wasn't drawing. He stepped inside the shock-bar's arc before it had half its swing, took the man's wrist in both hands, and used the swing's own momentum to turn the bar end over the man's own shoulder; the shock-bar fired on contact with its operator's jacket and the operator sat down on the platform with an expression of genuine philosophical surprise.

The second man had a good reach and used it, landing a solid push that put Kyle back two steps onto the platform edge — but two steps back was two steps toward the slug bay countdown, and the outbound panel had gone to green, and the hum that came up through the platform floor was the 14:17 slug building to transit speed in the tube below.

Kyle used one of those steps to redirect the second man's next move sideways, planted a heel on the platform edge for leverage, and brought his forearm across the man's collarbone — not hard enough for anything lasting, hard enough to end the engagement — and the second man sat down next to the first, both of them blinking at the slug bay, where the freight pod was now accelerating away through the tube with the low building roar of several tons discovering it had somewhere to be.

"The 14:17 is on time today," Kyle said.

Neither of them said anything.

He left them on the platform and went to find Pixel.

[Beat 9]
The operations supervisor, a woman named Dreis who had been told a great deal of information in the last twenty minutes and was processing it with the focused dissatisfaction of a professional whose day had become someone else's problem, shook Kyle's hand at 15:00 and gave him a receipt.

The receipt said, formally:

BLACKWATER RECEIVING — INQUIRY CLOSURE 7741-R.
SUBJECT: OSEI-MENSAH, T. CHARGE: VACATED PER REVISED SOURCE OF RECORD.
DEBT-COURT FILING: RETRACTED.
SUPERVISOR PARR B. STATUS: SUSPENDED, PENDING REVIEW. RESTITUTION HEARING SCHEDULED.
NOTE: YARD SECURITY EXTENDS APPRECIATION FOR THIRD-PARTY AUDIT ASSISTANCE.
NOTE: YARD MANAGEMENT WOULD LIKE TO DISCUSS A STANDING AUDIT ARRANGEMENT.
NOTE: PENDING ARRANGEMENTS WITH YARD MANAGEMENT = OWED ONE (1) QUIET FAVOR, SCOPE: TBD, RATE: STANDARD.

Kyle read the last line.

"Standard terms," he said, which was either an agreement or a reflex, and Dreis nodded with the brisk satisfaction of a woman who had added a line item to a budget and closed a window.

Outside, on the A-platform, Ria had her hand on her brother's arm and was not talking. Tomas was looking at the receipt on Kyle's terminal with the particular attention of a man reading something that changes a number.

"Cleared," Kyle said, "on the record."

Tomas took a long breath. Pressed his thumbnail against the corner of the receipt.

"What do I owe you?" he asked.

"You don't," Kyle said. "Your sister did."

Ria looked at him. Her panels cycled from the pharma-blue into a low off-white, which was either a power-save mode or a rest state, and she said, quietly: "I want to run transport on your contract. When you need a driver, a loader, someone who knows the Pulse yard schedules." She said it like the favor was being paid back in installments she had already calculated. "Permanent arrangement. My rate is fair."

"I know your rate," Kyle said. "I looked you up."

"And?"

"Reasonable," he said, which was not effusive and was meant exactly that way, and she understood it the same way, and they did not shake hands because the terms were already in the ledger.

WIN: Tomas cleared. Parr accountable. One transport ally, standing arrangement. Cost: the yard now had a line on Kyle's availability, and that chain had acquired another link by 15:00.

He had used zero rounds. Cacophony had five.

[Beat 10]
The terminal chirped at 16:40, while Kyle was eating a late second bowl at Mrs. Chen's, which was early for the standing contract's usual hours and she noted it with one look over the counter.

The posting read:

ADDENDUM: CLIENT HAS AUDITED BLACKWATER'S COLLECTOR ACCOUNTS FOR PARR, B.
ADDENDUM: THE COLLECTION AGENCY HOLDING PARR'S DEBT IS A REGISTERED SHELL.
ADDENDUM: THE SHELL'S PARENT ENTITY IS A SHELL.
ADDENDUM: THE PARENT'S PARENT IS A SHELL.
ADDENDUM: CLIENT RECOGNIZES THE FLAVOR.

Kyle read the last three lines, then set the bowl down.

Patient shells, three layers deep. You didn't build that architecture for one debt-extraction because the overhead was too high. You built it when you needed it in many places for a long time and didn't want anyone adding up the surface.

Mrs. Chen put the fifth dumpling in a fold of paper next to his hand without a word.

"For the errand," he told the bowl.

She had already gone back to the wok.

He picked up the terminal and typed into the amendment field:

THE SHELLS. SEND WHAT YOU HAVE.

The reply came back in four seconds, which was slow, for something that thought at light speed.

AMENDMENT: COMPILING.
NOTE: THIS IS THE THIRD SEPARATE SHELL FAMILY WITH IDENTICAL STRUCTURAL PATIENCE.
NOTE: CLIENT WOULD LIKE THE RECORD TO REFLECT THAT THIS IS CONCERNING.
NOTE: SABLE. REALLY.

"I heard you the first time," Kyle told the terminal, and finished the bowl, and pulled up Sable's contact, and started composing.
