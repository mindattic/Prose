# Second Suitor

*Amara returns from the Shen crossing. Ciro invites her to the department's end-of-quarter gathering. He is, for one evening, exactly what he could be.*

She came back through the Zone 6 checkpoint at 10:14. The biometric reader cleared her without incident. The guard from the morning was gone; his replacement was a woman who didn't look up.

The drive was in her jacket pocket, wrapped in the small cloth she kept for wiping calibration contacts — the only non-electronic thing in her kit bag. She kept her hand out of the pocket. It had been in the pocket for two hours and she had already established the habit of not reaching for it.

Ciro was waiting for her when she reached her workstation.

Not standing at her workstation. Leaning against the corridor wall ten feet away, which was not unusual for Ciro — he had a way of occupying adjacency rather than confronting proximity, as if he understood that showing up at someone's desk was a declaration, and showing up in the corridor was a question. He was holding two coffees.

"I was passing the machine," he said.

She took the coffee. It was the kind she ordered. She had not told him.

"I wanted to apologize," he said. "For the meeting yesterday. I know it was uncomfortable."

She looked at him. He looked back, which he always did — not with the blank professional surface most Account Liaisons put between themselves and the client, but with something that appeared to be attention. "You handled it well," he said. "Better than I would have."

She thought about the drive in her pocket. The third log entry. The camera alert that had fired while the terminal was still warm.

"I have a request," he said. "The quarterly gathering is tonight. In the atrium. I usually don't go — it's mostly for the cultivation floor — but I thought this year I might, and I thought you might consider coming." A pause that was calibrated to land as a pause but not a pressure. "As a professional courtesy. Neutral territory."

She had attended two quarterly gatherings in three years. She knew what they looked like: the atrium's ceiling lights on a warmer setting, the cultivation floor staff in clusters, the trays of food that Orison imported from somewhere that wasn't the Seams. Managed warmth. The news feeds cycling through exterior weather and whatever was happening in the outer zones this week, the way they always did, making the world outside the arcology look like something that happened to other people.

"All right," she said.

The gathering was in the atrium at seven. Ciro found her within three minutes of her arriving, which meant he had been watching the entrance. He brought her a glass of something that tasted like a fruit she didn't recognize and then he said: "Tell me something about you that's not on your file."

She was looking at the ceiling. The atrium's ceiling was the one place in Zone 6 that used real glass — eight panels, angled against each other, the lights of the building above visible through them like stars. "I keep adding things to a list," she said. "People I've met in my work. I don't know what I'm going to do with it."

"What kind of list."

"Just their names. And something specific. Something they said or did."

He was quiet for a moment. The cultivation floor staff were laughing about something across the atrium. The news feed was cycling through a story about the outer ring's transit expansion. "Why?" he said.

"Because I see them once and then I don't see them again," she said. "And they were real. The list is how I keep it accurate."

He looked at her for a moment that ran a beat longer than professional attention would have required. Then he said: "That's the most honest thing anyone's said to me in this building."

She felt, without wanting to, that he meant it.

Later — when the gathering was thinning and the cultivation floor staff were drifting toward the exit escalators — she tried to ask him, carefully, whether the calibration audit infrastructure logged any system-level events outside the clinical panel. She had been working up to it for an hour. She phrased it as a question about the technical architecture, the kind of question a tech lead might ask.

He set his glass down. Not a sharp movement. A considered one. "That's a little outside the client-liaison scope," he said. He said it gently. He was still looking at her with whatever that attention was.

"I was curious," she said.

"I know," he said. "Come on. I'll walk you to the escalator."

He walked her to the escalator. At the bottom he said: "Thank you for coming." And then: "Add me to the list, if you want."

She didn't answer. He didn't wait for one.

She rode the escalator down. At the bottom she passed through the lobby doors and out into the corridor and stood for a moment with the managed warmth of the arcology behind her and the Seam air just ahead. The drive was still in her pocket, the cloth wrapped around it still damp from two hours of contact. She had not said anything useful. She had learned nothing she didn't already know.

She had, however, placed a question close enough to him that he had heard it. That was something.
