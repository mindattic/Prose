# One Shoe (The Roost) (CODA)

_Kyle at the Roost after the funeral; Femi Kasparov and the lemon tree; the year-of-life cost of the neuretics overclock named; a Vulture prices the Atlas hardware and steps back; the Roost is Axiom in the Office schedule; "his body had been changed before anyone asked."_

[Beat 1]
The Roost was on the third floor of a walk-up on Paulina, behind a laundry operation that had been running since before the overlay architecture came online and had never registered for a feed address. Kyle had come here after jobs that finished clean. After jobs that didn't. After jobs he couldn't yet classify.

Femi Kasparov was in his chair by the window — his right shoe on, his left foot bare on the radiator cover, which was warm but not running, the way it always was in late summer. He did not look up when Kyle came through the door.

The lemon tree was in the corner. It was four feet tall and growing in something that wasn't quite soil, under a lamp that wasn't quite sun. Tended by a timer. Repotted once a year whether it needed it or not. The Roost had the wrong climate for a lemon tree. The tree had adapted. It bore two lemons at any given time — never more, never less, as though it had run the math and decided this was the sustainable rate.

"Chair or wall?" Femi said.

"Wall," Kyle said.

He put his back to the wall between the window and the door. Femi's chair creaked once as he settled. The grow lamp hummed at the frequency of something doing its job.

[Beat 2]
The medic had come through three days ago. Femi reported this the way he reported everything: once, accurate, in sequence.

His left hand had been running three degrees colder than his right for the past six weeks — a compensatory load redistribution the neuretics managed automatically, the kind of adjustment that logged itself without alerting him. He had noticed it. He had not put a clinical word to it until now.

"She left a number," Femi said. He let it sit there for a moment. "Two years at current load. Possibly less, if the rate holds at the rate it's been holding."

Kyle said nothing.

"She said you knew."

"I knew it was a number," Kyle said. "I didn't know which one."

Femi considered this. He looked at the lemon tree. One of the two lemons was starting to color — moving from the pale institutional yellow of new growth toward the deeper yellow of something ready. The other was still green.

"Two years is a number," he said.

"It's a range," Kyle said. "She said possibly less."

The grow lamp hummed. The coloring lemon turned in some imperceptible draft from the window seam.

[Beat 3]
The Vulture had come six weeks earlier. Kyle had not been here. Femi had been.

The man arrived in the early afternoon, dressed for evaluation — document portfolio, handheld scanner, the expression of a professional who had learned to make no expressions. He had been invited in. The Vultures were a service; Femi did not have a policy against services.

The man had scanned the threshold and the room. He had asked, in the register of someone checking a form, whether the occupant of record was present. Femi had told him no.

The man produced a figure. Catalog value of the Atlas hardware installed in the operator logged to this address, market-rate extraction cost deducted: net recovery value. The figure was significant. Femi had listened to it without expression.

The man then produced a secondary figure. Net recovery value after risk-adjustment, accounting for the probability of a recovery that did not go cleanly.

The secondary figure was negative.

The man thanked Femi for his time, put the portfolio back in his coat, and left. He did not leave a card. He did not schedule a follow-up.

"He left the primary figure," Femi said. "Not the secondary one. The primary one was the compliment."

Kyle stood with this. The lemon tree was something that could not grow here and had grown here anyway. Whoever had brought it had not asked it.

[Beat 4]
The document had been filed three weeks after the Vulture. Femi produced it from the drawer in the table — printed on paper, folded once, the crease sharp from waiting.

It was from the Continuity Office. Not addressed to a person. Addressed to the structure, the way a filing is addressed when the addressee is a location rather than an occupant. The language was standard: the property had been scheduled under the District Acquisition program, WEST TOWN WEST. The word they used was *axiom*: the baseline from which continuity-of-ownership was calculated. It had a reference number. It had an effective date.

The contact name was not Mr. Able.

Femi refolded the paper and returned it to the drawer. "Two weeks after the Vulture," he said. "They run independently. They don't share notes."

"I know," Kyle said.

At sixteen, the installation had taken eleven hours under general anesthetic. He had not been consulted in any meaningful sense. The person who would become the operator known as Kyle had not been in the room when the decision was made — what to put inside him, what it would cost per year, what a Vulture would make of it at current market rates. His body had been changed before anyone asked.

The Continuity Office was booking the Roost the same way. Calculating the asset. Filing the baseline. Not asking.

He looked at the lemon tree. The coloring lemon was fully yellow now.

"How long has it been since anyone asked you about the shoe?" he said.

Femi thought about it. "A while," he said.

The grow lamp hummed.
