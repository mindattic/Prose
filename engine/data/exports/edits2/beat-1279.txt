The checkpoint is staffed by two guards in soft-helmet armor and a third behind a blast-shield. They have the bored faces of people who expect a manifest. Kyle does not have a manifest. He has a child and a battery and a smile. He brakes hard, throws his hands off the bars in the universal not-a-threat posture, and shouts, "POST-OP TRANSIT, LACUNA COOLER, NORTHWESTERN," before the guards can finish their reach. Left guard looks at the brace. Right guard looks at the cooler logo. The guard behind the glass — Crucible internal, white coat under the armor — runs Imani's face against a phenotype registry, because in a genomics zone the gate reads the person, not the plate; the bike is nobody here, it's the blood they care about. Whatever the registry tells him drains the color out of his evening. "Let him through," he says. "Phenotype clearance." The barrier sinks. Kyle says, "Have a great evening," and means it.
