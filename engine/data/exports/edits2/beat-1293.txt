Twenty-one minutes. He hits the south edge of the Loop, where municipal law has strong opinions about his speed — except municipal law has been on wildcat strike for nine days and currently has no opinions, which Kyle considers a generous gift. The fast line is through the Wabash Stack: a six-lane service tunnel under the building's whole footprint, badge-locked at both ends. The city's lower floors own one mouth, the corponation owns the upper, and the standing feud between them is the thing the locals call the Lobby War. Nine days of strike has turned it from standing to hot. From the tunnel mouth Kyle can already hear it — the flat clap of small-arms, a crowd-roar underneath. Imani feels him slow and lifts her head off his back. "Is that the loud," she says, and there is a thread of the thin spot still in it, the not-managing-it she only just got back from. "Different loud," Kyle says. "This one's just people." Which is true, and no comfort: people are the one loud you can't out-ride, and the only way to the bay that keeps his clock runs straight through the middle of them.
