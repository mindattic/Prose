He lifts Imani onto the pillion, sidesaddle, threads the medical harness through his belt-loop so the battery rides over his kidney instead of swinging, ties her wrists loosely through the back of his coat so she will not fall off if she sleeps. He feeds Chorus a fresh moon-clip — five new chambers, the old five scattered back across the Hegewisch crossroads — and lets it settle on his left hip. Silence rides across his back where it always rides. Her cheek finds the back of his shoulder like it has been there before. "Hold on," he says, which is a stupid thing to say to a kid whose hands are too small to grip his ribs, but it is what you say. She says, "How fast." He says, "Fast." She says, "Good." The kid is the only new weight, and the only one he is doing math about. The bike starts. Eight cylinders' worth of audacity.
