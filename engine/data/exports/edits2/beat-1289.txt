Halsted north of the tracks runs over Cinderfall Energy's bones — eleven subterranean thermal installations whose surface is a grid of steam-vent grates and convection waves and asphalt re-laid three times this year because it keeps warping. Imani says, "Why is the air doing that." Kyle says, "Because the corporation under us is cooking." She says, "Oh" — the *oh* of a child learning the world has a basement. He does not tell her the air is about to do something Cinderfall has nothing to do with. His Neuretics flags two headlights still hanging back where they have hung since Pullman: patient, matched to his pace, in no hurry, which is its own kind of bad news. Then it flags what is ahead instead. Two blocks on, the streetlights stop agreeing on where they are, and the lattice, which has had an answer for everything tonight, goes quiet in a way it has only gone quiet once before. Weather, Vance had called it.
