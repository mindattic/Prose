The leader brought the answer to men like Kyle and has only been waiting for the range to clear. It is a Gauss rifle — a man-portable rail driver that throws one hypersonic slug in a line so flat it might as well be drawn with a ruler, and does not care in the least what stands behind the target. It is the one weapon the lattice cannot beat, because you cannot dodge a thing already past you; you can only fail to be where it was aimed. He puts it on Kyle's chest and fires. Kyle reads the shot a hundred and twenty milliseconds early and is no longer there — and the slug, finding only the air he left, does not stop. It crosses the crossroads dead straight and punches through the windshield of an ambulance running its lights north, and kills the driver where he sits. Steered by no one now, the ambulance never finishes its turn; it T-bones a stalled hauler with the flat finality of a thing nobody's holding, and the impact throws its back doors wide. Kyle is inside the leader's reach before the man can cycle for a second shot — the pommel, then one clean cut — and the Gauss rifle clatters off to begin a quieter life on the pavement. Then it goes very quiet. Kyle stands in the wreck-light looking at an ambulance he tore open by ducking, and a small face inside it looking back — like the universe went to a great deal of trouble to make the introduction. The small face waves. Kyle waves back.
