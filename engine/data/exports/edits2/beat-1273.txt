They come for Kyle the way Arcturus does when the invoice runs large: not one man but a kill team — six of them, milspec reflex-ware under the skin, peeling out of two matte SUVs across the Hegewisch crossroads with the bored economy of people who do this for salary and dental. The neighborhood reads it before the squad would like. A noodle cart folds itself shut. A kid on a stoop is hauled indoors by the collar. The street empties the way streets down here have learned to empty, fast and without comment, leaving Kyle alone in the middle of it with his bike and his bad options. Running is the first option his Neuretics throws out — six shooters, open ground, no. Neuretics: bio-compute grown around and through the brain, worked by thought, no jack, no blink-clicking, no poking at empty air. Most people run it to read their messages. Kyle's is a military build, and what it does to a gunfight is what a chess engine does to a club player — it reads shoulders and weight and the hundred tells a body leaks before it fires, and hands him the next half-second a hundred and twenty milliseconds before it arrives. The team leader's finger takes up slack; Kyle is already moving when the first burst stitches the air he left. Chorus clears its throat off his left hip — the stubby five-chamber revolver-shotgun, a rubber slug, because the math has not gone lethal yet — and the nearest operator folds around his own sternum and sits down in the road. Four in the cylinder. Five shooters still up.
