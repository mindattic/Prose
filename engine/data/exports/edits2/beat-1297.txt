Streeterville is a forest of glass towers in crowns of advertising, and at their feet the brutalist slab of Northwestern Memorial — and tonight the whole district is wall-to-wall people, the strike spilled north into the one part of the city no one gets to lock down. Which is exactly why it's here. Kyle sees the bay's red light six blocks out, and between him and it, wading through the crowd on six legs with the patience of a thing that has nowhere better to be, is an Arcturus crowd-control unit: a crab the size of a panel van, matte riot-grey, a Bulwark. Arcturus builds everything into a crab eventually — low, omnidirectional, impossible to tip — and a crab is what waits at the end of his night. Kyle has put this exact unit down twice. He knows it's the same one because it slows when its optics catch his face, and recalculates, and comes for him first out of a crowd of a thousand easier problems. A machine can't hold a grudge. Kyle stopped believing that the second time.
