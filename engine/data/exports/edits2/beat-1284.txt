The Ashgrave Synthesis Corridor announces itself with a wall of haze you can taste before you see it. The perimeter lights glow the wrong color — too yellow, too low — the way a refinery glows when it's having a feeling. This is somebody's commute. A shift-change line of workers in cheap half-masks trudges the shoulder, heads down, lunch pails swinging, walking a thing they've made peace with because the alternative is not eating; a stall at the mouth of it sells filter cartridges and, beside them, a hand-painted board keeps a tally — DAYS SINCE — the number low, the way it's always low here. Kyle has a respirator clipped to his jacket; Imani does not. His Neuretics throws the air up as a number he doesn't like and a clock he likes less. He reaches back without slowing, finds her face with his palm, presses his bandana over her mouth. "Breathe shallow. Through this. Not your nose." She nods against his hand. A masked worker on the shoulder lifts two fingers as he passes — not a wave, just the nod one night-shift gives another — and Kyle returns it, then bites his own respirator's mouthpiece, snaps the seal, and rides into a sky the color of old bruises like he was invited.
