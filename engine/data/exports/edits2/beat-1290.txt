He should turn two blocks west. West is Bridgeport, which is normal. He goes straight, because straight is what the call is for, and at 35th and Halsted the world stops being a place. The headlight hits something and the something does not hit back. His stomach drops a foot without him; the wheels are on pavement but the pavement is half a second behind where his eyes insist it is. And then the worse thing, the thing the briefings never sell hard enough: he knows this corner. He has never been here. He knows it the way you know a hallway from a dream you have had your whole life and never once on purpose — the grey country behind sleep that everyone visits and no one admits to. Behind him Imani makes a sound he has not heard a child make. Not fear. Not pain. Recognition.
