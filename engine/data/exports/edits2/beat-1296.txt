River North is a thirty-second wedge of gray zone where the Loop's grid runs out of nerve at the river. The bridges are still there — the old bascule spans that used to crack open for sailboats, their bridgehouses squatting at either end like chess rooks gone to rust — but the river hasn't been managed in eighteen years, the lift machinery seized a decade back, and they sit permanently down and half-drowned, Lower Wacker a black canal beneath them where a road used to be. The old Hancock still stands over all of it, dark to the eightieth floor, its X-braces holding up a hundred stories of nothing and a crown of advertising nobody's paid to switch off in years. People live here anyway, the way people live everywhere, especially the drowned parts: laundry strung bridgehouse to bridgehouse, a man fishing the canal off a fixed span with a hand line and the patience of someone with nowhere to be, a kid working a cooler of something cold along a toll-line of cars that never moves. Imani turns her head to watch the black water slide past. "The street's drowned," she says — not scared, just filing it, one more entry in a night that keeps teaching her the ground is negotiable. "Most of them down here are," Kyle says, and takes the upper deck of the Michigan Avenue span, where Tollgate Systems strung a credit-gate across a public ruin because someone always finds the rent in a thing nobody owns. He does not have a Tollgate badge. He has twelve minutes and an opinion about Tollgate. The barrier is a swinging steel boom, the sort of thing that stops a delivery van; it folds off its hinge without comment, the least dangerous thing to happen to him all night. What he thinks about instead is the camera that reads his face across the span — not the bike, the bike is nobody, it's *him* the system wants — and bills the only identity it can match: a credit account that hasn't existed since 2207, which Kyle built for exactly this kind of evening and then deleted. The invoice will get an automated rejection, the rejection filed, the filing archived, the archive purged in a routine sweep — six weeks and seventeen credits of Tollgate's money to chase a ghost. He laughs, which pulls at the cold line under his coat. "Why are you laughing," Imani says. "Because the most expensive thing I did to them tonight was the paperwork." She says, "Good."
