Schism, the Network calls it. It has been with the city since the mid-2100s — since the experiments with Bose-Einstein condensates and mass quantum-entanglement broke something in the underlying rules and it never fully healed. The thin spots surface where they like and stay as long as they like, and the city stopped treating them as exceptional a long time ago: the weather service charts them the way it charts a storm front, a stain spreading across the forecast under a LATENT flag and an insurer's disclaimer. Kyle's Neuretics tries to map this one and gets back garbage — distances that won't sit still, a clock running three directions at once — and his own engine note reaches him a half-second late and wrong, like it's coming off a street he already left. For once in his overclocked life he's flying on the one instrument the lattice can't improve: the old animal at the base of the skull that everybody still has. He fixes on a streetlamp on the far rim and obeys the only rules survivors keep — constant speed, eyes on the light, don't look at the edges, don't trust a turn, because in here right is sometimes left and left is sometimes down. He says, "Don't look left." Imani says, "I already wasn't."
