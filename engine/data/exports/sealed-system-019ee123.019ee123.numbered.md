# Sealed System

*Seto receives Amara's message. He works out that the bleed is the only channel Orison can't monitor. He reads the manifest deliberately.*

The card was on the table, in his hand before he'd consciously picked it up. The three drives were beside it. He read the message twice: *I need to meet you. In person.*

He checked the transmission metadata. Routed through a Seam relay node in the Zone 6/7 corridor — not the nearest one to her building, but the third one west. She had walked toward Zone 7 before she sent it. He could tell she had been moving when she typed it: the message's composition timestamp was spread across forty seconds for a nine-word message, the cadence of someone composing while walking, stopping, composing again. She had sent it standing still but drafted it in motion.

He opened his relay terminal and brought up the Zone 3 broker's contact address. The broker had been reliable for seven years and always closed his transmissions with the same archaic sign-off — a two-character string that had gone out of common use before Seto started working the gray zones, the kind of thing someone picked up early and never stopped doing. He had noticed the gap this week because the gap meant no sign-off. Six hours of silence in a sequence where the sign-off came every eight to twelve hours without fail. Something had already changed before the gap appeared.

He drafted the transmission request, opened the send dialog, and pushed it through.

The message died at the third hop. The broker's infrastructure returned: *Endpoint unavailable. No route found.* He ran the diagnostic with one hand still flat on the terminal. Every node in the broker's network had gone offline in the past four hours. Not a maintenance gap. The infrastructure was simply not there anymore. Zone 5/6 relay licensing ran under Orison's infrastructure agreements. Four hours was the standard window for a compliance withdrawal to clear. He had talked to Amara the previous afternoon.

He closed the terminal.

The Zone 5/6 junction was dark. The broker was dark. Three adjacent nodes to the junction were dark. Whatever was blocking the path was moving ahead of him, clearing options before he reached them.

He had two days. He had three drives and no path.

He sat back from the terminal and looked at the ceiling.

The bleed ran through carrier-grade sealed architecture — the kind the calibration facilities couldn't instrument, the kind the monitoring infrastructure had never been built to index. It was not a channel anyone had designed. It was not a channel anyone at Orison would think to watch for. He could not send a file through it. What passed between them was not structured data but something without an addressable format: the weight of a thing held in the mind, proprioceptive memory, the felt quality of attention.

He picked up one of the drives.

He could read the manifest.

He could read it slowly, with the attention he gave to something he wanted to carry. If what passed between them was the weight of eight hundred and forty-seven names read for a reason, she would receive it the way she had received the bicycle routes and the calibration probe grip — not data but something older.

He opened the first drive and extracted the manifest file.

He read it from the beginning: each name in the Zone 1 block, the batch code, the facility assignment, the calibration window. One entry, the next, the name registered before moving on. He was not reading for information. He was reading to make it real in the way that names became real when you committed them to memory for a specific reason.

Zone 3. Zone 4. The afternoon light moved across the west-facing wall. He did not get up. Zone 6 — twenty-two entries — and her name in the nineteenth row. He stayed with it longer than the others, not because it was hers, but because it was the one he had been carrying for eight days before she knew.

He continued. Zone 7. Eighty-four entries, six facilities on his routes. He read them the way he rode those routes — knowing the checkpoint locations, the receiving bays, the lobby schedules. He had passed some of these people. He had not known their names. He knew them now.

He read to the end of the document.

The evening shift was starting in the Seam below — the relay beacons cycling through their hourly test, the dinner vendors replacing the midday carts. He did not know if she had received anything. The bleed worked when it worked. He had no way to signal the intent behind the transmission, no way to tell her *this was deliberate.*

What he had sent was whatever weight the manifest carried when you read it with full attention.

He picked up the pre-paid card and composed his reply carefully. *Third relay west. Tomorrow, 09:00. Shen crossing. I'll find you.* The "third relay west" was the only way he had to say *I know where you sent this from* without saying it — and therefore the only way to say *I know what happened to you today.*
