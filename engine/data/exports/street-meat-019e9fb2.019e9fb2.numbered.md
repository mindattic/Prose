# Street Meat

_A freelancer who has refused the False Death Protocol for nineteen years finally takes the contract: a Vulture rig delivers his counterfeit corpse into a sealed corponation lab where the cargo has no name and the decision waits inside._

## Street Meat

[Beat 1]
Kyle changed in the back of the van. The clothes he had ridden over in went into a sealed bag. The clothes he changed into were the kind the Vultures pickup-bodies wore, which was no clothes at all — the morgue intake protocols stripped the bodies for processing and the Vultures had standardized on delivering bodies pre-stripped to save the intake nurses a step. He had brought Silence and Cacophony in a separate sealed case that Stash placed in the rig's lockbox under the floor of the cargo bay. The case would arrive at the morgue intake but would not be processed with the body. The case would, by the operational plan Hua had laid out, be retrievable from the facility's external supply intake on the secondary timeline.

[Beat 2]
He looked at her across the steel prep table. She let the silence stand the way she let most things stand — as something the other person had to decide what to do with.

[Beat 3]
The cortex came fully online at the four-minute mark. He sat up.

[Beat 4]
"You will know what to do with the cargo when you see it." Hua said it level. He read her face. The face read level back. "I am not telling you what to do with it because telling you what to do with it would frame the decision in advance, and the decision is yours."

[Beat 5]
He had been refusing this kind of contract for nineteen years because the cost — surrendering body control to a hibernation routine, losing the discipline's calibration for an unknown duration — didn't have a category in his accounting. He had refused it from Sable's pipeline twice. Hua had been routing clean for six months. This contract was the one the others hadn't been.

[Beat 6]
"False Death Protocol," he said. Not a question.

"Standard rate," she said.

[Beat 7]
His clothes were not on the slab. His clothes had been bagged and labeled at intake and were now in the facility's storage. Silence and Cacophony were, per plan, accessible via the external supply intake on the secondary timeline. He had between four hours and twelve hours to find the cargo and execute the extraction and get to the supply intake and recover the weapons and get out.

[Beat 8]
He came up on a slab in a room he had not previously seen and which his interior catalog began assembling within the first two seconds of consciousness — temperature seventeen Celsius, humidity controlled, the specific cold-storage smell of a Tier 4 corponation morgue intake, the LED overhead at the angle that meant the room had been retrofitted from a different use within the past decade. The body's autonomic functions resumed. The NeoCortex brought him back in the sequence it knew.

[Beat 9]
He took inventory while the cortex resumed. The lights were standard intake fluorescents. The door was to his left. Two other slabs in the room were occupied — one body older than Kyle's by twenty years, one younger, both genuinely out rather than running protocols. He clocked them and moved on. The room was quiet. The room had not yet been visited by an intake nurse since his arrival, which the operational plan had budgeted between four and forty minutes for, and which the NeoCortex placed at about eleven.

[Beat 10]
Three hours before the slab, Kyle set the brief on the steel prep table between them. The smell registered as a choice before it registered as a smell — Mrs. Chen's smell, four blocks away and one floor up, in a kitchen Hua had been operating out of for years and which Mrs. Chen had not mentioned. Hua had picked this room for exactly the resonance it would carry. Kyle let the deliberateness sit where he could find it again. The not-mentioning sat next to it. The eight-line brief, the False Death Protocol, the cargo whose decision was his. "Understood," he said.

[Beat 11]
Stash did the IV insertion himself. The IV carried the protocol's hibernation cocktail — a calibrated mix that, when run through Kyle's NeoCortex's intake-recognition subroutine, produced the signature the facility's morgue intake was looking for. The protocol did not put Kyle into hibernation in the way the corponation medical literature defined hibernation. It put Kyle into a state the NeoCortex had been built to enter on command — a complete partition between the conscious cortex and the body's surface-level autonomic functions, with the autonomic functions calibrated to register as *recently deceased* on every standard scan.

"Thirty seconds, then you go under," Stash said.

The drip started. The thirty seconds did what they did.

[Beat 12]
He read the brief. Eight lines on a piece of paper: no cargo name, no contact list, just the facility and the method. The facility was a sub-tier complex in the northern outskirts — the corponation registry called it *long-term storage*, but Kyle's preliminary read put its security envelope two tiers above anything long-term storage needed. He read the delivery method twice.

The facility had been running an Atlas-program variant since 2218. Kyle's file on it had five names in it and four of them with no current route. He had been looking for a way inside for three years. Hua had just handed him one.

[Beat 13]
Three hours earlier, the kitchen was hot. The wok burner under the right unit was running. The smell — pork bone and fermented bean and chili oil — was close enough to the smell that came out of Mrs. Chen's kitchen that Kyle read the closeness as deliberate. Hua sat across the steel prep table from him with a small folder open between them. She did not introduce the operation. She had introduced herself, six months earlier, through a back-channel that had eventually become formal, and the formality had taken the form of three contracts she had routed to him over the intervening months and which had paid cleanly and which had been clean contracts. This was the fourth. This was the one she had handed to him personally.

[Beat 14]
Stash's hand on his shoulder before he started the drip was the only gentleness Kyle was going to get for the next several hours. It was not, in Stash's framing, gentleness. It was operational reassurance. Stash had not delivered a False Death body in eleven months and he was going to be careful, and he wanted Kyle to know it. Kyle knew it. Kyle had counted Stash's eleven months before he got in the van.

[Beat 15]
"Plus a Vultures consult fee for the delivery." She closed the folder. "Stash will run the transport. You will be on his slab inside three hours of accepting the contract."

[Beat 16]
He did not move. The first rule, the one he had retained from the protocol's documentation nineteen years ago in the way he retained things he did not want to need: do not move until the cortex is back. He held the rule and waited for the cortex.

[Beat 17]
Two hours before the slab, there was the van. The Vultures van that came to pick him up was the standard Vultures rig — matte black, the patch on the driver's door, the cargo bay set up for stretcher work. Stash himself drove. The two technicians in the back were people Kyle had not previously met and whose names Stash did not give him, because the protocol the False Death Protocol depended on required Kyle's intake into the facility's morgue to read as a routine pickup with a standard crew, and standard crews did not introduce themselves to corpses.

[Beat 18]
"Extraction," she said. "Atlas-program continuation lab. Sealed cargo. Out."

"And the cargo."

She looked at him. The folder stayed closed.

He sat with it for four seconds. He had refused contracts like this twice before. He had good reasons for both refusals, and both of those reasons were still true.

"I accept," he said.

[Beat 19]
Hua handed him the contract in the back of a kitchen Kyle had not previously known belonged to her. The kitchen was on the second floor of a noodle-and-soup operation in the corner of a Tier 3 corridor four blocks from Mrs. Chen's stall. Kyle set the geography aside. She had been running operations within shouting distance of Mrs. Chen for at least four years. Mrs. Chen had not mentioned this. Kyle let the not-mentioning sit where he could find it later and opened the folder.

[Beat 20]
Kyle closed his eyes. The hum in his chest — the ambient Network field he had been carrying since the cage, six hours and counting — partitioned along with the rest of his autonomic functions. The partitioning was not silence; it was a different kind of presence, the same way *asleep* was a different kind of presence from *awake* rather than an absence of presence.

Kyle had been under the protocol once before, in 2222, under conditions the Atlas-program engineers had not anticipated. He had come up three hours later in a utility stairwell in a building he had not entered. He had accounted for the missing time. He had not been able to account for the particular way his hands had felt against the railing — familiar, and also not — in a way that had not resolved in the years since. The protocol's worst case was not death. The worst case was the case where the operator going under was a person and the operator coming up was, in some small but discoverable way, not entirely the same person.

[Beat 21]
"You are the only freelancer on the GLMZ corridor whose hardware can run the protocol. Their intake reads dead bodies. Their intake does not read carefully. The protocol gets you inside the facility through the morgue intake. You wake up. You find the cargo. You walk out."

[Beat 22]
The cargo was somewhere in this facility. He had accepted the contract not knowing what it was.

He stood up from the slab. He took stock of his hands — the way he always did now, coming up from the protocol. They felt like his hands. He decided to proceed on that assumption.

He walked to the door. The corridor beyond was lit the same dead fluorescent as the room. Somewhere down its length, a wheeled cart was rolling toward him, and the decision Hua had refused to frame for him was waiting behind whichever door the cart had come from.
