# Sexy Time

_Kyle comes home bleeding; Pixel patches him up, and for once he doesn't pull away._

## Across the Hall

[Beat 1]
He makes it up the stairs of The Pivot on a banister and a bad idea, leaving a brushstroke of himself on the wall that he'll feel guilty about later. The second floor announces Pixel the way it always does — bass through her door at a volume that should bother the building more than it does, the clatter of something being tested and found wanting. Kyle stands in the hall with his hand pressed to his side and the dark coming up between his fingers, and he does the math he always does: knock, or bleed quietly and not be a problem.

He knocks. It comes out softer than a knock should.

The music cuts. The door opens on the chain, then all the way, and Pixel takes him in the way she takes in everything — fast, flat, already triaging. Pink hair half out of its updo, grey-blue eyes doing arithmetic on the spread of the stain.

"That had better not be yours," she says.

"It's mostly someone else's."

"Inside."

[Beat 2]
She clears the kitchen table with her forearm — a soldering iron, a half-flayed prosthetic hand, a mug — and none of it lands gently. Tools go to the floor and stay there. She doesn't look at them. She looks at him, and at the brace, and at the boot dragging, and the look on her face is the one she keeps for things that are still fixable if she's fast.

"Shirt," she says.

He gets it half over his head before the wound files a complaint, and she's there, peeling the rest of it off him like a bandage from skin, brisk, no ceremony. Then she stops being brisk.

She has seen Kyle hurt before. She has not, somehow, seen *this* much of him — the whole topography of it under the kitchen light. The old roadmap of scars: the pale seam under the ribs, the burn that climbs his shoulder, the constellations a body collects when it does what his does for a living. He is built like something that was made on purpose and then put through a war it was not consulted about.

"Stop cataloguing me," he says.

"Stop being interesting," she says, and reaches for the kit.

[Beat 3]
She works the way good medics work — talking to keep him present, hands too busy to shake. Antiseptic first. He hisses; she doesn't apologize. She irrigates the gash in his side, and her thumb braces flat against the muscle below it to hold the edges while she threads the suture, and he feels the small precise tugs as she closes him, one and then the next, her breath warm and uneven against his collarbone because she is bent that close.

Somewhere in the middle of it her eyes have gone bright and overfull, and one of them spills, and the tear runs down and lands in the open wound, and the salt of it stings worse than the needle did. He doesn't tell her she's crying. She'd hate that. He just holds still and lets her finish, lets the salt find the raw of him, because there's a tenderness in being cried over that he has not let himself have in longer than he can name.

"You keep doing this," she says, not a question, knotting the last stitch. "Coming back in pieces. Making me put you back."

"You're good at it."

"That's not the part I'm complaining about."

[Beat 4]
She should be done. The wound is closed; the forearm is wrapped; the shoulder is taped. Her job, the named and reasonable part of it, is over. And she doesn't move away.

Her hand is still flat on his stomach where she braced to stitch him, and it stays, and then it doesn't stay still — it moves, slow, down the ridges and over the old burn and along the pale seam under his ribs, reading him the way her eyes read everything, except her eyes are closed now. She traces a scar from end to end like she's deciding whether to forgive it.

This is the part where Kyle pulls away. He always pulls away. There is a reason — a long, cold, true reason about what he is and what people think they're touching when they touch him — and it lives right here, in the half-second before contact becomes something he'd have to mean.

He doesn't pull away.

He puts his hand over hers and presses it flatter to him, and lets her feel that his heart is doing something undignified, and when she looks up — startled, the arithmetic finally failing her — he is already looking back.

[Beat 5]
She kisses him like she's been holding it under her tongue for a year, and he answers it with both hands in the pink wreck of her hair, and the careful field-medic distance collapses all at once into something with no manners in it. She climbs into his lap right there on the kitchen chair, careful of the bad side and reckless with all the rest, and he pulls her sweater off over her head and sends it somewhere it will never be found.

The last of the tools go off the table — the iron, the spool of solder, a tray of tiny screws that skitter across the concrete and roll into the dark under the workbench — and neither of them so much as flinches. Her hands find him everywhere they spent the last hour being clinical and aren't being clinical anymore. His mouth finds the freckled line of her throat and she makes a sound that she would deny making.

"Bed," she manages, "is eight feet—"

"Too far," he says, against her skin, and she laughed, breathless, and agreed with her whole body.

[Beat 6]
He stands with her wrapped around him — the side screams and he tells it to wait its turn — and they make it as far as the wall, which is closer and entirely sufficient. The kiss doesn't break so much as deepen. Her fingers drag down his back and find new scars to learn. His hands close over the compact strong shape of her and pull her in tighter, palms moving slow across her ribs, her spine, the warm dip at the small of her back.

She gasps his name into the crook of his neck like it's something she's allowed to have now, and his breath goes ragged and stops sounding measured, and his shoulders come down out of the brace they live in, and he gives her everything he usually saves — every flinch, every unguarded sound, the weight of him let all the way down.

They move together, against the wall and then down onto the cleared floor among the scattered screws and the forgotten kit, the two of them tangled and sweat-bright and entirely, finally, undefended — until the want crests and breaks over both of them at once, and his hand finds hers on the concrete and holds it there among the screws.

[Beat 7]
After, the floor is hard and they don't care.

She lies half on his good side, and the apartment ticks and cools around them, and the bass from someone else's unit thumps faintly through the brick. His chest goes up and down too fast and then slower. Her pink hair is stuck to her cheek. There's a screw embedded in his shoulder blade that he'll find tomorrow and not mention.

She runs a finger along the contours of him — not triage now, not a medic's inventory, just the same practiced palpation she'd use to find what was broken, only this time pressed slow and asking nothing, learning a body she intends to come back to. The burn on his shoulder. The new line of her own stitches in his side, neat as a hem. The dip of his sternum.

He does the same to her, because for once his hands are not on a weapon — the freckles across her collarbone, the small old scar through one eyebrow, the strong unglamorous lines of the forearms that put him back together an hour ago. His thumb finds the pulse at the base of her throat and just stays there, not counting it, only feeling it move, which is a thing he has never once let his hands do — rest on something living and leave it whole. She lets him. She watches him do it with her grey-blue eyes gone soft, and says nothing, which for Pixel is the loudest thing she owns.

[Beat 8]
The cooling-down brings the cold logic back, the way it always does for him.

"This can't happen again," Kyle says. He's looking at the ceiling when he says it, which is how she knows he doesn't entirely mean it.

Pixel props herself up on one elbow, pink hair falling across half her face, and considers him with the particular patience of a person who has just won an argument the other party doesn't know is over.

"How about only whenever you get shot?" she asks.

And Kyle lies there on the floor of her workshop, surrounded by the casualties of the evening, and actually considers the implications — the math of it. The corner of his mouth moves before he can stop it.

He doesn't say no.
