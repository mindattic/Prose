# A Restless Mind

_The fixer he has known for two months never comes in person. Tonight she stands in the rain at the noodle stall, hood up, and says his name. Kyle says yes to a shielded room before he knows whose room it is, and on the walk home the catalog finally matches a voice to a face._

## A Restless Mind

[Beat 1]
"Because you are the one this concerns," she said. "The rest tomorrow. In the room. I cannot say more here."

[Beat 2]
He laughed. Quietly. One short exhalation through the nose, the kind that would not have been a laugh in any room but this hallway. The evening had been: agreeing to walk into a shielded room with a woman whose face he had not seen and whose name he did not know, then walking home to find a cup of tea waiting in his neighbor's doorway. The contrast was funny. Nobody else needed to know why.

[Beat 3]
The hood inclined. Not a nod. An acknowledgment. Then she stepped past him into the dark of the block without unlocking the building behind her. The building was where Kyle was going tomorrow, and Kyle was going to go.

[Beat 4]
The noodles were getting cold. Kyle was watching a woman pretend not to watch him.

[Beat 5]
Not loud. Not for Mrs. Chen. For him, across two meters of rain. *"Kyle."* Just the name. Level, unhurried, the tongue she had chosen to say it in the same tongue she used through the cracked terminal at the parts shop three blocks east — the modulated register, the older one, the voice he had been getting contracts in for two months and had not yet built a face for.

[Beat 6]
"You're back early," she said.

[Beat 7]
He set the chopsticks down across the bowl. He did not stand. He did not move his hands away from the counter. The left hand was already where Cacophony would be in eight-tenths of a second; the right hand was already where Silence's draw would begin in three-tenths. He let her see him not move and let the not-moving be the answer to the question she had not asked yet.

[Beat 8]
*Nobody stands still in the rain unless the rain doesn't bother them or the job requires it.*

[Beat 9]
She sipped her tea. He sipped his. The mist outside was settling on the alley wall the way mist settles when the air below is colder than the air above. Kyle had stopped checking the forecast around the same week he had stopped trying to keep up with Pixel's tea cabinet. *Hm.* He drank.

[Beat 10]
He finished the tea. He handed her back the cup, handle-first, the way she handed him his when the convention ran the other direction. She took it. She bowed, a small bow, the bow of a friend acknowledging the closing of a transaction that had no exchange in it. He bowed back, smaller.

[Beat 11]
She tilted her head — a fraction, a direction, nothing more — and Kyle, who now knew exactly who she was and was therefore in a worse strategic position than if he had not known, did the calculation a freelancer does when his fixer of two months shows up at his stall in person and says only his name. *If she wanted me dead, the angle from the doorway would already be open. If she wanted to ambush me, she would not have spoken. She is here for something the cracked terminal cannot carry. She is here because the room she lives in is not safe.* Kyle's chair scraped back. He set the chip on the counter and said it the way he said it every night, low and level: *Standard rate.* Mrs. Chen's hand covered the chip without looking down, which was how she always took money from him when she thought he was about to do something she didn't approve of and had decided not to say so.

[Beat 12]
Pixel did not ask what was funny. Pixel never asked what was funny. The not-asking was the punchline.

[Beat 13]
She did not ask where he had been. He did not tell her about the woman in the hood, or the three blocks east-northeast, or the shielded room he had said yes to without yet knowing whose room it was. He did not tell her about the agreement — same time tomorrow, somewhere with a cage in it. He did not tell her about the way the agreement had landed in his chest at exactly the angle the agreement had been designed to land, and how he had said yes anyway, and how he could not now articulate to himself whether saying yes had been a discipline decision or something the discipline did not have a category for.

[Beat 14]
"Bird's still out," she said. She meant the upstairs neighbor's bird, which was a recurring data point in Pixel's running commentary on the building and which Kyle had never actually heard but which he understood to be a presence in Pixel's life the way the rain was a presence in the city.

"Thanks," he said.

[Beat 15]
Kyle stood at the door for a count of ten. The brass-gasketed lock he could see through the window beside the threshold was the tell he had assumed it would be. He did not test the door. He turned. He walked back the way they had come — east-northeast in reverse, through the recycler vent's hot industrial breath and past the third-block security drone on its standard sweep and past the awnings that had not stopped dripping in the eleven minutes the walk had taken in the other direction and the further eleven it would take him in this one. He did not run the route past the array. He let it sit. He walked it.

[Beat 16]
He followed the woman into the rain. His left hand stayed where Cacophony would be. His right hand stayed where Silence's draw would begin. He did not stop tracking her three-quarter profile for the entire walk. She was already moving, deeper into the walkway's dark, and he fell in six feet behind her — far enough to read her, close enough that she knew he had chosen to come. The katana's weight shifted against his hip with each step, the way it always did on wet ground: a small correction, muscle memory now, Seo's blade finding its balance in him long after Seo had stopped being able to carry it himself. Kyle did not know who she was. He knew the shape of her competence the way you know a locked door by the quality of the lock, and the lock was very good, and she had been standing in the rain for eleven minutes waiting for him specifically, and those two facts together were either a contract or a problem. In his experience they were usually both.

[Beat 17]
The bike was where he had left it, two blocks south of the noodle stall, the plate still the one he had ridden down on, the cover still beaded with rain. He started it. He rode the long way home through tier crossings he did not strictly need to make, because the long way home was where Kyle did most of his thinking, and tonight was a night he had something to think about. He did not know who she was. He knew he had said yes. He knew tomorrow was a shielded room. He knew the discipline would show up because the discipline always showed up. The rest would have to be tomorrow's problem.

[Beat 18]
He kicked the stand down. He locked the wheel hub. He covered the plate. He walked the bike's stillness into the elevator and the elevator into the second floor and the second floor into the hallway, and his step hit the landing across from 2F at the angle he had not announced and Pixel's door opened anyway.

[Beat 19]
He read the wording. *Shielded* did most of the work. The kind of shielding she meant was the kind that cost money she did not casually spend. The hum in his chest, the low electromagnetic signature that lived in the implant's background like a second heartbeat, was suddenly something he was aware of for the first time in years. He had not known it was there because there had been no reason to notice it. There was, now, a reason.

[Beat 20]
"Tomorrow's going to be its own day," he said, eventually.

[Beat 21]
The woman stepped under the awning. The hood came back two centimeters — not all the way, just enough that the streetlight caught the shape of a face and the dark of red hair beneath the rain hood, and Kyle catalogued *attractive* the way the array catalogues anything: as a fact registered without commentary. Tall. Late thirties. Bone structure that had cost her nothing because she had been born with it. He had not seen her face before tonight and he was not entirely sure he was supposed to be seeing it now.

[Beat 22]
He held her gaze through the hood for another beat. He could leave. He could turn around and ride home and pretend the noodle stall had been a noodle stall. He could pretend three blocks east-northeast had been a walk. He could pretend that *Because you are the one this concerns* had not been the sentence it had been, the one that was already living in his sternum the way other people's important sentences lived in their stomachs. He could pretend the shielded room would empty without him in it.

[Beat 23]
"Sang twice this evening. New piece. I think it learned from the radio."

*Mm.* He turned the cup. The bird was part of the building's texture, the way Pixel's tea cabinet was — something he'd stopped trying to catalog and started using as proof that the hallway still existed.

"Yeah?" The word came back from somewhere. He meant it.

[Beat 24]
Eleven minutes. Same foot position. The rain came down at the angle the atmospheric processors preferred — steep, efficient, redirected from somewhere that mattered — and it came off her hood in a clean sheet that said the coat was quality, the kind of quality that didn't announce itself. No chrome. No augmentation profile his implant could resolve. His NeoCortex kept reaching for a signal and returning empty.

[Beat 25]
"Tomorrow," she said. "This address, this time, the room behind this door. Shielded. Bring nothing electronic you can leave behind. Your implant comes with you because your implant cannot be detached, but anything else you carry stays in your apartment. The conversation we are going to have cannot happen in a room that can hear it."

[Beat 26]
She watched him over the rim of her cup. Not with the focus she put on a fragment in his shoulder; with the looser focus she used when she was working on the larger question of whether he was, on a given night, all right. He was, on this night, all right. She read it. She did not need to confirm it. Her shoulders settled by a quarter inch and she sipped her tea.

[Beat 27]
She was gone in three steps. The Gray Zone's bad rain swallowed the tan coat the way the Gray Zone swallowed most things at this hour.

[Beat 28]
"Why me?" he said. Not the question he wanted to ask, but the question the discipline asked for him. She let it stand in the rain.

[Beat 29]
The relay station resolved out of the dark at the end of the block. Wartime construction: the brutalist vocabulary of a city that had once believed in infrastructure as ideology, poured concrete and no ornamentation and a deactivated antenna on the roof that still served its purpose as a story. *It is a relay station. It is empty. There is nothing here worth stopping for.* The kind of building that became invisible through its own explanation. She stopped at the door. She did not produce a key. She did not unlock anything. She turned, instead, with the same composed efficiency she had walked the route with, and looked at him from inside the hood with eyes he could not quite read in the bad light.

[Beat 30]
"They usually are."

[Beat 31]
Pixel did not ask. That was the shape of the convention they had built over four years across this hallway: she did not ask, and he did not tell, and the not-asking and the not-telling were what kept the thing working.

[Beat 32]
He was not going to pretend any of those things. The discipline was the discipline. It showed up.

[Beat 33]
The bike's headlight crossed the alley wall at 22:14 and Kyle killed the engine before he turned in, coasting the last twelve meters on momentum because the engine cutting at the corner had been Seo's lesson and Kyle had not yet found a reason not to do everything Seo had taught him to do. The rain had softened to mist by the time he got to West Town. The towers leaned in the way they did this far up; the canyon between The Pivot and the residential block across from it was just wide enough for a single VTOL to thread through if a VTOL had any business being this far down, which they didn't. The space above the alley was dark. The light came up from the windows, not down from the sky.

[Beat 34]
The match landed in his head before he had time to refuse it. Voice plus face. *Sable.*

[Beat 35]
They stood in their doorways across the hall and drank. The hallway smelled faintly of someone's microwave dinner two doors down and faintly of Pixel's apartment behind her, which always smelled like solder flux and bergamot in proportions that should not have worked and somehow did. The cup in Kyle's hand was the temperature of "drink this slowly," which suited him because he intended to.

[Beat 36]
He had no idea who she was. He had a sense of the way she walked, the way she had timed the drone interval, the way the coat had not soaked through. He had her voice from twelve seconds at the noodle stall — too few words to identify anyone he had ever spoken to before. The hood was the kind of hood that did not come off accidentally. He filed the hood without prying at it.

[Beat 37]
The recycler vent exhaled hot industrial breath across the walkway — paper-dry and chemical-sweet, the smell of the Gray Zone processing its own refuse — and she walked through it without breaking pace. Kyle held his breath for two steps on instinct. Old facility reflex. The security drone hung at the third-block junction, its sensor array cycling through its standard sweep in the standard interval. It did not flag them. She had timed the approach or she had known the interval, and either answer said the same thing about how long she had been thinking about this particular walk. He filed that. He filed everything. The NeoCortex ran hot in the rain, the implant's temperature regulation working harder than it should have been, a low warmth behind his left ear that he had learned to read the way a pilot reads engine noise — not yet a problem, a thing to monitor.

[Beat 38]
"Goodnight, Pixel."

[Beat 39]
Behind him, Mrs. Chen stirred the broth with a long-handled spoon that had lost its lacquer in three places and been re-gripped with electrical tape, the same spoon she had used for the four years he had been eating at this stall, and she said one word into the steam. *Húlijīng.* Low. Mandarin. Not for him. She said it the way a woman who has seen things names them — with the flat certainty of a diagnosis, not a warning. Kyle set his chopsticks down across the bowl. He had heard Mrs. Chen mutter at the rain, at the Circuit boys who walked too close to her signage, at the atmospheric processors when they made the broth steam wrong. He had never heard her name a person.

[Beat 40]
She was in a t-shirt over leggings, no shoes, the hallway's bad amber light catching the rim of two cups she was holding. One in each hand. Not forceps tonight. Cups. The steam was rising off both of them and Kyle could smell something herbal and lemon-bitter and could not name the specific plant because Pixel's tea cabinet was an ongoing research project and Kyle had stopped trying to keep up with the inventory roughly the third month he had lived across the hall from her.

[Beat 41]
He had not been gone long enough to be either early or late. *Early* was the kind of thing Pixel said when she had been listening for his step and the step had arrived and she had to put words on the relief without putting words on the relief. He took the cup she held out. It was hot. The handle was the side without the chip; she had given him the better cup, which was her version of saying something she did not intend to say aloud.

[Beat 42]
"Goodnight, samurai."

[Beat 43]
She closed her door. He unlocked his. He sat on the edge of the bed in the dark, the tea still warm in his chest, and listened for the bird he had never heard. The building was quiet. He listened until the quiet was the answer, then let it be.

[Beat 44]
The mochi cart first. Closed, a chain through the handles, the vendor's stool folded against the wheel. The rain had filled a small depression in the cart's roof and it spilled in a thin line off one corner, perfectly continuous, the kind of detail the atmospheric processors produced without intending to. Kyle's implant catalogued it and moved on. He tracked her coat. The way the hem swung. The fact that she had not once adjusted her stride for the puddles, which meant she knew the route or had walked it enough times that her feet had learned it separately from her attention. *Preparation. Weeks, maybe.*

[Beat 45]
*She has never come in person before. The shape of an ambush.*

[Beat 46]
"Tomorrow," he said.
