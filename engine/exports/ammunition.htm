1.8x12mm Piezoelectric Micro-Flechette Cluster PMC-18#

The PMC-18 is a pre-packaged cluster of 24 piezoelectric-coated steel micro-flechettes loaded in a cylindrical drum cartridge for the SFL-4 'Hornet' platform. Each flechette is a slender steel needle coated in a piezoelectric polymer that generates a localized electrical discharge upon deformation — when the flechette strikes soft tissue and bends or fragments, the coating converts the mechanical energy into a micro-pulse that disrupts local nerve function and causes involuntary muscle contraction around the wound channel.

The cluster deployment mechanism is pneumatic, using compressed gas to propel all 24 flechettes simultaneously in a controlled spread pattern. At close range (under 20 meters), the cluster maintains cohesion, delivering all flechettes into a target area approximately the size of a human torso. Beyond 20 meters, the cluster begins to disperse, reducing per-target density but increasing the probability of hitting multiple targets in a group. The pneumatic propulsion system gives the weapon its characteristically low acoustic signature — the discharge sounds like a sharp exhalation rather than a gunshot.

The drum magazine holds 8 cluster shots (192 flechettes total) and is field-replaceable in approximately 3 seconds. The flechettes themselves are designed to be surgically difficult to remove — the piezoelectric coating bonds to tissue through a combination of barbed micro-texture and the electrical activity, which causes surrounding muscle fibers to contract around the embedded needle. Field medics in Meridian 88 refer to PMC-18 injuries as 'hornet stings' and consider them among the most time-consuming wounds to treat, often requiring sedation and microsurgical extraction.

11.2mm Tungsten-Composite Resonance Slug TCS-11#

The TCS-11 is a purpose-built projectile for the Arcturus Defense Solutions BRC-6 platform, engineered from a sintered tungsten-carbide composite matrix with an internal resonance cavity that amplifies the acoustic pre-charge generated by the carbine's barrel assembly. Upon firing, the slug exits the barrel already vibrating at a frequency matched to the target material profile selected by the operator, converting what would otherwise be a conventional heavy-caliber impact into a resonance-amplified structural failure event.

The slug's composite construction is layered: an outer jacket of hardened tungsten provides penetration mass, while the inner cavity contains a lattice of piezoelectric micro-crystals that sustain the resonance frequency imparted by the weapon's pre-charge cycle. This means the slug continues to vibrate for approximately 0.3 seconds after impact, propagating fracture waves through whatever material it has embedded in. Against personnel in hard armor, the effect is devastating — the slug does not need to fully penetrate the plate, because the resonance propagation shatters the plate from within.

Manufacturing tolerances are extreme. Each slug must be frequency-matched to its intended weapon within 0.02 Hz, which means TCS-11 rounds are sold in weapon-specific lot batches rather than universal boxes. Using a slug calibrated for one BRC-6 in another produces degraded performance at best and barrel fracture at worst. This has created a black market for 'frequency-hacked' slugs with adjustable resonance cavities, though these are notoriously unreliable.

12.7mm Helical-Sleeve Penetrator HSP-127#

The HSP-127 is a magnetically accelerated anti-materiel penetrator designed for the HVP-4 'Corkscrew' railgun platform, featuring a unique helical sleeve that imparts extreme rotational spin to the projectile during electromagnetic acceleration. The penetrator is a tungsten-ceramic composite rod housed in a discarding helical sleeve — as the weapon's 14-stage coil array accelerates the round to 4,200 m/s, the sleeve's helical grooves interact with the electromagnetic field to spin the penetrator at up to 220,000 RPM. The sleeve discards at the muzzle, and the penetrator continues to its target as a hyper-velocity spinning rod.

The extreme spin rate is the HSP-127's defining characteristic. At 220,000 RPM, the penetrator functions less like a bullet and more like a drill bit striking at four times the speed of sound. Against conventional armor, the rotational energy compounds the kinetic penetration — the round does not simply punch through, it bores through, with the spin dispersing material laterally as it advances. This makes the HSP-127 effective against reactive armor systems that are designed to deflect or shatter conventional kinetic penetrators, because the sustained rotational contact defeats the reactive element's ability to redirect the projectile.

The rounds are loaded in 12-round ceramic-sleeve cassettes that protect the delicate helical sleeves from handling damage. Each sleeve must be precisely aligned with the weapon's coil array for proper spin induction, which means the cassette loading mechanism is the weapon's most mechanically complex and failure-prone component. Field reports indicate a 2-3% feed failure rate under combat conditions, typically manifesting as a sleeve misalignment that produces a tumbling, unpredictable shot rather than a clean penetrator release. The weapon's coolant system must also be maintained — 8 rounds of rapid fire saturate the heat sink, requiring a mandatory coolant purge before continued operation.

12.7x55mm Subsonic Caseless Round SCR-127#

The SCR-127 is a heavy subsonic caseless cartridge designed for the SCBR-1 'Quiet Ledger' precision rifle, engineered to deliver maximum terminal effect at subsonic velocities while producing minimal acoustic and thermal signature. The round uses a caseless propellant block — a compressed solid propellant matrix that burns completely on firing, leaving no brass casing to eject. This eliminates the mechanical noise of case extraction and ejection, and removes the forensic evidence of spent casings at the firing position.

The projectile is a heavy tungsten-composite slug with a mass optimized for subsonic flight at 310 m/s. At this velocity, the round produces no supersonic crack, and when combined with the SCBR-1's integrated wet suppressor, the total acoustic signature is reduced to approximately 94 dB — quieter than a car door closing. The trade-off for this stealth is reduced range and a pronounced ballistic arc at distance, requiring skilled compensation from the operator. The round's weight partially compensates for its low velocity, delivering substantial kinetic energy on impact, but it will not defeat modern hard armor at any range.

The caseless design requires careful storage. The propellant block is hygroscopic and degrades when exposed to moisture, which means SCR-127 rounds must be kept in sealed containers until loaded into the weapon's stripper clips. Ringo Corponation sells them in vacuum-sealed packs of 20, and experienced operators treat opened packs as having a 72-hour field life before reliability degrades. The propellant block also makes the rounds thermally sensitive — storage above 45 degrees Celsius risks cook-off, which has produced at least three documented ammunition storage fires in Meridian 88's hotter districts.

12g Ceramic Composite Scatter Load CCS-12#

The CCS-12 is a pre-measured charge of irregular ceramic composite fragments designed for the PSP-2 'Gravel' scatter pistol, packaged in 8-round speed strips for rapid reload. Each load consists of approximately 12 grams of ceramic-metallic composite pellets of deliberately irregular shape and size, propelled by a 45mL compressed CO2 canister. The irregularity is intentional — uniform shot produces predictable wound channels, while the CCS-12's random geometry creates chaotic, unpredictable tissue damage that is difficult to treat surgically.

The ceramic composite material is specifically chosen for its scanner invisibility. The pellets contain no ferromagnetic components and emit no RF signature, making the loaded weapon effectively invisible to standard security scanning systems. This is the PSP-2's primary selling point and the reason the CCS-12 exists as a distinct ammunition type rather than simply using conventional shot. The ceramic also fragments aggressively on bone impact, producing secondary projectile paths that extend the wound channel beyond the initial penetration depth.

The CO2 propulsion system gives the weapon a distinctive acoustic signature — a sharp hiss rather than a bang — and limits effective range to close quarters. The CCS-12 is designed for engagements measured in meters, not tens of meters, and the scatter pattern beyond 4 meters reduces individual pellet energy below reliable incapacitation thresholds. Within its intended range, however, the combination of multiple irregular fragments, ceramic fragmentation on bone contact, and piezoelectric discharge from the weapon's barrel assembly makes the CCS-12 one of the most devastating close-range antipersonnel loads available.

The chemical compound — a proprietary Helix Biosystems formulation — interacts with standard neural interface hardware to amplify the disruption effect, meaning augmented targets experience more severe and longer-lasting symptoms than unaugmented individuals. This counterintuitive relationship between augmentation and vulnerability has made the TNP-2 a favored tool of operators who specifically target heavily augmented personnel.
