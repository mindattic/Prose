# MindAttic — Voice Notes for Bushido Coda Rewrites

Source: Melody Valkyrie — Huntress of Norp (book 6), and by extension the rest of the
Melody Valkyrie / Aurora Roe corpus at `R:\Desktop\EPub\MindAttic\`.

The voice I had been writing (literary cyberpunk in the McCarthy / Gibson register) is
WRONG. Ryan's voice is mock-epic comic-cosmic action with technical confidence — the
narrator is a CHARACTER with opinions, the world is absurdly specific, every grand
sentence earns a punchline, and the gravity comes from the contrast between cosmic
stakes and breakfast smells.

## 30 specific moves catalogued

1. **Mock-portentous opening + undercut.** "Space is as dark as night. But not as dark as
   the souls of those who prey upon the weak. Their thirst can never be quenched. Like
   sea water it is wet and slippery but briny and only good for soup."
2. **Cosmic horror with absurd parentheticals.** "Eighteen (126 dog) years ago..."
3. **Self-deprecating recursion.** "She isn't a very good judge of distance" → later
   "She isn't a very good judge of time."
4. **Adverbs that don't exist.** "'Meow.' it responds cyber-ghastly."
5. **Mundane cosmic detritus.** "extinction and the smell of burnt waffles in its wake."
6. **Narrator has personality** — comments on the hero's judgment, the reader's
   expectations, the cliché being deployed.
7. **Action that decelerates into a list-gag.** "drops to the ground in a full sprint
   and then decelerates to a gallop, then a canter, then a jog, a stroll and finally to
   a halt."
8. **Worldbuilding via absurd specifics.** Porcelain figurines on the rocket ship. The
   gun is a 20-in1 Multi-gun. The repair tool was Doc Nano's. Knee-high rocket boots.
9. **Mock-portentous sentence stutter for effect.** "as soon as The Redacted had had had
   had its fill." Four 'had's. Intentional. Funny because annoying.
10. **Throwaway cosmic shit-talk.** "Looting, pillaging and the occasional dine & dash
    all along the cosmic superhighways."
11. **Chapter titles ARE gags.** "Chapter This many." then "Chapter ((8 x 64) - 12) /
    100" — yes, that's chapter 5. The narrator is doing math jokes.
12. **The Epic Battle Skip.** "The epicness of the battle that ensued is indescribable:
    ... And then it is over. (Told you.)" — refuses to write the climactic fight scene
    and taunts the reader for expecting it.
13. **Parenthetical narrator-asides about deferred worldbuilding.** "(which will be more
    fully explored at a later time)"
14. **Vivid + silly choreography.** "Spinning in midair like an airborne water wheel
    rotating around an invisible axle..."
15. **Colloquial register interrupting technical narration.** "the parts reconfigured
    themselves into a pair of sick uzis."
16. **Domestic suburban simile in deep space.** "She unleashes their deadly spray like
    an unkind child tormenting a younger sibling with a garden hose."
17. **Italic comic-book SFX.** "*Clang!* *Blorp?*" — the "?" after Blorp is the
    move. The sound has its own confusion.
18. **Trope acknowledgment on the page.** "she even has enough time to turn the corner
    of her mouth upward and shake her head disapprovingly before she leaps away."
19. **Standalone confirmation of the obvious.** "It looks ridiculously badass."
20. **Recurring motif as callback.** "abstract sculpture depicting the futility of
    life" used twice for damaged equipment — narrator has favorites.
21. **Sentence cut by interrupting action.** "it is like some sort of abstract
    sculp--" — the form of the prose IS the form of the world.
22. **Direct address to the reader.** "(Yes, it was purple the whole time! Mind. Blown.)"
23. **Narrator's dry rejoinder to genre-cliché dialogue.** Megaphone says "Attention." →
    "As if they could possibly be ignored."
24. **Antagonist names that are both ridiculous and ominous.** *Plastic Adonis.*
25. **Chapter labels.** "Chapter Omega" → "Conclusion" — naming conventions are jokes.
26. **Earned cliché in closer.** "Suffice it to say, it's going to be a bumpy ride."
27. **Cosmic stakes leveraged via mundane physical detail.** "an ill-fated attempt to
    pull out of its corkscrew descent towards the lemon tinged face of Norp."
28. **The hero performing the cool thing while the narrator deflates it.** "She presses
    the back of one of the guns against her forehead meditatively. It looks ridiculously
    badass."
29. **Technical mechanism described with absurd specificity.** Multi-gun shape-shifting
    is given a full physics-confident paragraph and then the result is *sick uzis*.
30. **Mock-epic chapter openers undercut on second sentence.** Sets up grand tone, then
    the second beat is breakfast.

## What this means for Bushido Coda

**Kyle himself stays Kyle.** Clinical. Augment-burdened. Terse. Boy-from-the-Grind.
The character is more serious than Melody and that's fine. **The NARRATOR is where
Ryan's voice lives** — commenting on Kyle's situation with mock-epic gravity and
breakfast-detail undercut.

**Things to push in the rewrites:**

- More narrator asides ("(which will be paid for in chapter 4)" etc.)
- Italic SFX for the action — *Crack-crack-crack-crack.* for the kneecaps, *Clang.* for
  the wrist-cut.
- Sentences that build epic momentum and drop a punchline at the end.
- Specific brand-names and absurd corporate history. The propane burner has a brand and
  a history. The chrome arms are Hydraulic Solutions ARM-9, but maybe ARM-9 has a
  recall history. Carrion Logistics LLC is already great — push it.
- The boss with the literature is the THESIS JOKE — he literally bought training
  against this sword and dies because Kyle is the exception. The narrator should ENJOY
  this. Drop a parenthetical: "(The training had cost him 480,000 QUANTA. The receipt
  was in his coat. The receipt would survive him by an hour and a half.)"
- Chapter section titles can have personality. "Part I — Teeth" stays. "Part II —
  Standard Rate" could become "Part II — Standard Rate (No Substitutions, No Refunds)".
- One or two reader-direct-address moments. The narrator can say "(more on Hua later)"
  about the watcher's report.
- The mundane is where the world LIVES. Mrs. Chen's three-ladle ritual is good. Push:
  her stockpot was bought in 2208 from a man named Volker who had inherited it from his
  mother and was emigrating to the Settled Worlds and could not take it. The stockpot
  has therefore been on three planets. Etc.

**What to KEEP from v3:**

- The Hammurabi teeth-handoff structure
- Kyle's clinical interiority and the two-channel narration
- Mrs. Chen's specific dialogue
- The corundum strop draw mechanism
- The seppa-locked wrist cut
- The standard-rate ritual
- The watcher closer (and the Tocharian phrase — that's the long-con seed)

**What to ADD that wasn't in v3:**

- Sky descriptions ("describe the sky and the lights" — the user specifically asked).
  GLMZ rain at different tiers, the amber sodium of the dock, the VTOL strata, what
  light the kitchen makes through the airshaft.
- Inconsequential things happening around Kyle (a man across the street arguing with a
  ride-share AI about a routing question, a kid trying to sell stolen ramen, a cat
  asleep on the rear cowl of a parked delivery scooter).
- More world-bleed: brand names, corporate histories, throwaway absurd specifics.
- Narrator's voice on top of everything else — mock-epic comic-cosmic register.
