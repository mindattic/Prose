### MODERATE — P-1 | Ch2 Beat 4 vs Ch12 Beat 2 — Marl's stone / Vesh's teaching-marker

**Plant (Ch2 Beat 4):**
> "He brought one thing back, the first time. A piece of stone, carved — a fold of cloth, or a wing, no one has ever agreed which, worn smooth on one edge the way a thing is worn when hands have held it for a very long time before it ever reached his hands. No quarry in the Works cuts stone like it. No Cog has ever recognized the tool-marks. I still have it. I have never found a use for it in the teaching that didn't feel like a lie, so mostly it sits, and mostly no one asks."

**Payoff candidate (Ch12 Beat 2):**
> "When Vesh reached into the fold of his robe... He found what he was looking for and brought it out — the silence-piece, the carved bone teaching-marker, smooth from decades of handling. He held it out toward where Glim sat."
> "Hold it when the hum is absent. The bone remembers."

These descriptions almost certainly refer to the same object — "worn smooth from handling" in both, given in Glim's extremity, with the function "use when the Current is absent." The thematic payoff is elegant: Vesh says in Ch2 he "never found a use for it in the teaching that didn't feel like a lie," and in Ch12 he finds the right use at last. But the material descriptor contradicts: Ch2 says **stone**, Ch12 says **bone**. These are different materials.

**Fix (option A):** Change Ch2 Beat 4 to "a piece of old bone, carved" — or "a piece of something carved, stone or bone, worn smooth." This makes the ambiguity intentional.  
**Fix (option B):** Change Ch12 Beat 2 to "the carved stone teaching-marker" — aligning with the plant.  
**Fix (option C):** When Glim receives the object in Ch12, have him recognize it as Marl's stone with a brief interior beat — this turns the discrepancy into revelation and also confirms the plant-payoff.

### MODERATE — B-1 | Post-Surfacing register drift (Ch9 Beat 1 primarily)

**Rule:** Glim's narration never uses surface vocabulary except as mis-rendering.

Pre-Surfacing, the register is correctly maintained. Glim renders surface things in deep terms:
- Ch5 Beat 3: "a light that was not a candle... fixed... white-blue, with an edge" (for electric light)

> "The street below was lit but quiet in the middle of the night."

"Street" used without mis-rendering, without the "surface word" framing, in Glim's narrative voice. Also in the same beat:
> "The outside of the building was a different texture than the inside"

These are the only clear violations. The post-Surfacing drift is narrow; most surface vocabulary in Chs 8–10 is handled with appropriate mediation. But "street" and "building" appear in narrator voice, unqualified.

**Fix:** Ch9 Beat 1 — change "The street below was lit but quiet" to "The outside corridor below — the surface's through-space — was lit but quiet" or similar. ## Dimension 6 — REGISTER

*Is the DEEP register maintained? Does Glim's narration stay in the Underclan's ontology?*

**Overall: STRONG.** The register is one of the novel's most consistent technical achievements. Across all pre-Surfacing chapters, surface things are rendered in deep terms, and the teaching language is never traduced.

Strongest moments:
- "The lid taken off the world" — consistently employed as literal description, not metaphor (Ch6 Beat 1: "He had held that phrase since childhood... as a figure of speech... He understood now that it was not metaphor.")
- "A light that lives" / "a light that burns" — the theology of candle vs. fixed light treated as practical knowledge discovered through experience
- "The Burning Eye" — deployed across multiple beats, never named as "sun" from Glim's POV

**The one register finding (B-1 above)** is the sole notable exception. All other post-Surfacing vocabulary usage is either marked as borrowed ("he knew the word"), mediated through deep-register framing, or so post-integration that it represents Glim's genuine vocabulary expansion.

### MODERATE — B-2 | Pre-Surfacing register: "floods" in Works capture beat

The first pass (B-1) caught "street" and "building" in Ch9 Beat 1 (post-Surfacing). This finding
covers a harder violation: "floods" used as a surface technical term in Glim's narration *before he
has surfaced at all.*

Works capture beat (UUID F8669353, SortKey 400 of the sixth chapter):
> "Not candles. Not the Cogs' working lamps, which were warm and directed and made sense in the dark
> because they did what the candle did: illuminated a space. These were floods — white, total,
> scouring — and they came from behind him and lit everything in front of him..."

"Floods" is the surface technical term for industrial floodlights. Glim has been in the Works all
his life and on the surface for zero minutes at this point. He cannot have this word. Compare to
how the narration correctly handles the vehicle immediately after: "They put him in a vehicle. He
did not have a word for vehicle. It was an enclosed space that moved without his volition…" — the
vehicle gets the appropriate treatment; the floods do not.

The same term recurs post-Surfacing in the Hollow raid beat (UUID 6C6B2A12):
> "He had experienced the flood-lights in the Works when they caught him: the sudden blindness, the
> panic that lives below the panic."

"flood-lights" in narrator voice. By this point Glim has eight days of surface vocabulary, but
"flood-lights" is a compound technical term rather than a borrowed word he could have absorbed from
Noor or context. The correct register for the Works capture description would follow the model of
the Hollow's attack on the Underclan: "the white they'd used before, the kind that takes the eyes
before you understand it is aimed at you" — or a Glim-specific coinage. "The white that hunts" or
"the aimed white" would serve.

**Fix:** Works capture beat — change "These were floods — white, total, scouring" to a Glim rendering
(e.g., "Not candles. Not the Cogs' working lamps. These were the white that hunts — aimed, total,
scouring"). In the Hollow raid beat, change "flood-lights" to the same term established in the Works
capture ("the white that hunts") or a close variant. Two beats, one term to land consistently.

### MINOR — T-2 | Ch01 Beat 400 — "Sixteen years later" conflicts with implied age

Beat 400.0, Ch01 (UUID 019F00B7-CB90):
> "He had felt it first at his First Hearing, when he was small enough that he had been carried to
> the ceremony in Knuckle's arms… Sixteen years later it was still there."

The standard reading of "sixteen years later" is sixteen years after the First Hearing. The node
bible puts Glim lost at ~4 and the Surfacing happening at ~16, meaning he has been in Homewater for
~12 years. First Hearing happened shortly after Knuckle found him (he was carried to it "in Knuckle's
arms," placing it within a year or two of arrival). If First Hearing was at ~5 and Surfacing is
at ~16, eleven years have elapsed — not sixteen.

A charitable reading treats "sixteen years later" as "at sixteen years of age" rather than "sixteen
years after the First Hearing," which is a possible if uncommon usage. The first-pass timeline audit
accepted the prose as clean on this basis. The ambiguity is worth flagging: if the intended reading
is "he is now sixteen" the phrasing should be adjusted; if the intended reading is "sixteen years
have passed since First Hearing" then Glim is ~21 at Surfacing, which requires the bible's "~16"
to be read loosely.

**Fix (if Surfacing age = ~16):** Change "Sixteen years later it was still there" to "Twelve years
later it was still there." One word. **Fix (if Surfacing age = ~21):** No change; leave "sixteen
years later" and update the bible's "raised from ~5 to ~16" to "raised from ~5 to ~21."
