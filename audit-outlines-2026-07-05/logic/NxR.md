# NxR Logic Sweep — 2026-07-05

## Summary

The story's core machinery is sound: the partition reveal, the six-dimension Vox read, Lace's surfacing, and Adalemo's pivot all land on the page. Two structural issues require attention — Boiler's building never pays off as the physical relocation haven for the twenty-one (it only pays off as the procedure site), and the prose misrepresents how Ohara knew the dead-drop.

---

## Findings

### BLOCKER

- **[SortKey 450–665, Beat 9 through Beat 13]** The building is used as the procedure site only. — The node bible (§4, Lock 8, and the author note under Boiler's entry) is explicit: "The building is the off-grid relocation point for some or all of the twenty-one Reads. Boiler has known this since he moved in and has been making it incrementally more viable for two years without telling anyone why. The payoff in Beat 13: the decision about the twenty-one Reads has a physical solution, and Boiler has been building it." The prose pays off the building as the setting for the partition crack, but never establishes it as a safe house for the survivors. Beat 13 resolves the twenty-one's safety through Vox's reseal and Adalemo's cover — neither of which involves physically relocating anyone to Boiler's building. The task brief also states: "Boiler's building: Load-bearing to resolution. Condemned 6 years, in civic limbo. It must pay off." This payoff is absent. — Minimal fix: In Beat 13, after Adalemo's pivot, add a clause where Rook or Boiler establishes that the building's off-grid status and existing civics gap is the operational solution for the twenty-one — either as an explicit line of dialogue between Rook and Boiler, or as a brief interiority beat from Rook acknowledging the building is the answer Boiler has been building toward. The sentence "he'd been making it incrementally more viable" needs to close on the page.

### MODERATE

- **[SortKey 50, Beat 1]** `"She had set it in 2206... and she had told four people. Three of them were dead. The fourth had signed it. *O.*"` — The prose states that Ohara was one of four people Rook explicitly told about the dead-drop. The bible (node bible §2 and the audit brief's bible key facts) specifies: "Ohara knew Rook's dead-drop because she built a research file on every freelancer before MxG... through methodical pre-job intelligence work. The anomaly is not that she knew. It is the escalation implied by using it directly." The story's meaning depends on Ohara having researched her way into something Rook thought was private — not on Rook having granted her access. The prose version weakens this: if Rook told Ohara directly, there is no privacy violation, no evidence of Ohara's methodical intelligence work, and the sentence "you build it the way you build things you don't want to need but know you will" loses its edge. — Minimal fix: Revise so the four people Rook told are unnamed contacts from 2206 (all dead), and Rook's reaction to the signature conveys that Ohara should not have known the address — she found it. One clause in the interior monologue (e.g., "She hadn't told Ohara — she had told no one with that name, and the tile was nineteen years old and Ohara was younger than the tile") would close the gap.

- **[SortKey 125 and 150, interstitial and Beat 3]** `"The one we left up"` (SortKey 125) and `"I know what we put in"` (SortKey 150). — Both uses of "we" imply Ohara had a collaborator when she sealed the partition. The bible (Lock 1, §3) is unambiguous: "She encoded their names and locations into a cognitive partition in her own neuretics, locked it behind a passphrase she then deliberately forgot." Beat 11 correctly reveals Ohara's hand and Ohara's hand only; Stave says "This is your hand." But the earlier "we" language plants a false inference of a co-author that Beat 11 then contradicts without addressing. — Minimal fix: Replace both instances with first-person. "The one I left up" and "I know what I put in" align with the Beat 11 reveal and with Lock 1.

- **[SortKey 300–400, Beats 6 and 7]** Stave's debt has no line connecting it to the body trade. — The task brief specifies a required CxC clue-plant: "Stave's debt has a line implying connection to the body trade." Beat 6 describes the debt as "not financial" and the work as "the kind they've been trying to stop," but never specifies what the work is or why stopping it requires leaving the clinic circuit for a transformer room in Z2. Beat 7 details Stave's prior failed crack and the resulting bandwidth loss in one person — compelling — but still does not name the body trade. CxC's opening conditions include the crew understanding what The Marrow is. — Minimal fix: In Beat 7, within Stave's account of the prior crack, add one clause establishing that the work Stave has been doing under the debt involves intake processing for a facility that Stave calls by a name that will echo in CxC (e.g., "The jobs I've been running since then come from the same floor that sent her to the table — intake work, they call it, for a facility I've never been in. I don't ask what happens after intake."). This doesn't need to name The Marrow directly; implication is sufficient for the plant.

- **[SortKey 650, Beat 13]** Adalemo arrives with one name (Sefi Okonkwo) rather than two, and the intel he carries does not change the geometry of the Beat 12 decision. — The node bible (§4 author note for Lock 6) specifies: "Adalemo arrives having independently traced two of the twenty-one Reads... The information he brings (PEREGRINE already has two names and Helix is moving on them now) changes the geometry of the Beat 12 decision: the choice is no longer theoretical, and the window is already smaller than the crew thought." In the prose, Vox has already resealed the manifest before Adalemo speaks. The decision is complete; Adalemo confirms rather than forces or complicates it. He mentions only Sefi Okonkwo — one name. The urgency that two people are already in PEREGRINE's hands and Helix is moving on them is absent, which undercuts the CxC entry condition (the account between Adalemo and the crew is not settled because two people are still known to the enemy). — Minimal fix: Have Adalemo arrive before Vox has fully committed to the reseal. He reveals PEREGRINE already has two names and Helix is queuing against them — then Vox makes the decision knowing the choice is no longer abstract. Adalemo's pivot becomes a response to Vox's decision rather than a confirmation of it. Add a second name alongside Sefi Okonkwo to establish the CxC condition.

- **[SortKey 180–215, Beat 4 and its interstitial]** Helix Biosystems is never named in the prose. — The beat spine title for Beat 4 reads "PEREGRINE's new paper and new funder identified." Vox's read confirms "new paper, new funder" and names a shell entity but does not name Helix Biosystems. The trilogy's antagonist structure runs through Helix; CxC's Anneke Oyelowo is Helix's program director. Without the name appearing in NxR, the handoff to CxC has no named throughline. — Minimal fix: In Vox's Beat 4 read or the interstitial following it, have Vox identify the funding chain terminating at Helix Biosystems by name — one sentence is sufficient.

### MINOR

- **[SortKey 450, Beat 9 synopsis row]** The beat synopsis text reads: `"Scout jacks in and goes quiet."` — "No physical jacks" is canon (NSB terminology rules). The actual prose correctly avoids this phrasing — Scout's operation is described as eyes-rolled-white with crawlers. The offending language is in the synopsis/title field of the beat row, not the prose. Update the synopsis to read "Scout locks into the crawlers and goes quiet" or equivalent.

- **[SortKey 300, Beat 6]** The beat spine (and §5 of the node bible) says "Rook pays the debt. Stave owes her." In the prose, Vox pays it with her face while Rook waits outside. Rook feels the cost as the thing she didn't price — that is preserved — but Stave's acknowledgment, if any, runs to Vox rather than to Rook. This slightly diffuses the "Stave owes Rook" dynamic that the node bible names as a condition. Not a story-breaking issue, but tracking: if CxC calls on this debt, the prose currently makes it Vox's debt (her face on the board) rather than Rook's.
