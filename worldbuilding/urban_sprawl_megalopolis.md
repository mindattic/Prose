# Urban Sprawl & Megalopolis Projections (2025-2100)

By 2100, 85% of humanity lives in urban areas (up from 60% today). The era of the megalopolis is not a future concept — it's an emerging present one.

## Key Corridors

### Chicago → Milwaukee (~90 miles)
**Verdict: Almost certain to merge.**

The corridor along I-94 and Lake Michigan is already heavily suburban — Kenosha and Racine are functionally bedroom communities straddling both metro areas. The gap between Chicago's northern suburbs and Milwaukee's southern suburbs is maybe 15-20 miles of semi-rural land. At current sprawl rates, that gap fills well before 2075. The resulting megalopolis absorbs Gary, Hammond, Kenosha, Racine, and Waukegan into one continuous lakeshore urban band stretching nearly 100 miles — a Midwest coastal strip, a freshwater seaboard city.

**Climate wild card:** As the Sun Belt heats up and water becomes scarce, the Great Lakes region becomes one of the most desirable places on Earth to live. Migration pressure alone could densify this corridor faster than any organic sprawl model predicts.

### Columbus, Ohio — Midwest Interior
**Verdict: Columbus becomes a megalopolis anchor, not a merger.**

The geography between Midwest cities is genuinely vast. Columbus to Cleveland is 140 miles, Columbus to Cincinnati is 100 miles. These could form a loosely connected **Ohio Triangle megalopolis** by 2100 — unified by infrastructure and economy more than physical sprawl. Columbus itself is already one of the fastest growing cities in the Midwest. Less "merged city," more "megaregion with three cores."

### New York City → New York State
**Verdict: The most likely full-state absorption in the US.**

The Northeast megalopolis was formed by the gradual coalescence of large, independent metropolitan areas whose outer fringes eventually merged into an extensive urbanized region stretching 800 kilometers. That process is ongoing and accelerating.

NYC's metro area already effectively consumes most of New Jersey, large chunks of Connecticut, and reaches deep into the Hudson Valley. By 2100, continuous development could plausibly run from NYC all the way up the Hudson to Albany, and across Long Island completely. Add the already-merging **Bos-Wash corridor** — Boston through Providence, Hartford, New Haven, New York, Philadelphia, Baltimore, Washington — and you're looking at a single urban band containing roughly 60-70 million people where "New York State" as a discrete geography barely means anything anymore.

## Projected US Megalopolises by 2100

- **Great Lakes Megalopolis** — Chicago through Milwaukee, Detroit, Cleveland, Pittsburgh
- **Northeast Corridor (Bos-Wash)** — Boston to Washington, ~60-70 million people
- **Piedmont Atlantic** — Charlotte through Atlanta to Birmingham
- **Texas Triangle** — Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, Austin forming a continuous loop
- **Pacific Coast** — San Diego to San Francisco already essentially one strip

## Governance Implications

Urban networks increasingly resemble biological systems, with transportation networks reaching into hinterlands like arboreal fractals. The question of where one city ends and another begins becomes unanswerable.

The critical question: whether governments adapt their political boundaries to match, or whether physical megalopolises and political maps just keep drifting further apart. Given the corponation framework, this creates direct tension — **who actually governs a city that spans six states?** Corponations with infrastructure control (transit, broadband, energy, retail) may hold more functional authority over daily life in a megalopolis than any single state government.

## Historical Precedents

Urban planners identified the Northeast megalopolis as early as the 1960s. By 2030, the concept of discrete cities connected by highways had already given way to continuous urban bands connected by infrastructure. The Boston-Atlanta corridor was the first to be formally recognized as a single economic zone — 800 kilometers of unbroken development housing 70 million people. The Great Lakes band followed within a decade.

## Relevance to StreetSamurai

The megalopolis is the natural habitat of the corponation. When a city spans six states, traditional governance fragments — corponations fill the vacuum with proprietary jurisdiction, private infrastructure, and extraterritorial legal continuity. A character's relationship to the sprawl (born in it, displaced by it, employed by its infrastructure, excluded from it) directly shapes their facet weights.

The Great Lakes corridor is particularly relevant: freshwater scarcity makes it a migration magnet, which means competing corponations fighting over the most valuable urban real estate on the continent.
