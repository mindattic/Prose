# CorpoNations 61-80: The Mid-Tier

## Communications, Security, Health, and Culture in the Post-Sovereign Economy

---

The Big Twenty own the skeleton of civilization. The entities ranked twenty-one through sixty own the muscle and the fat. But the corponations profiled here -- sixty-one through eighty -- own something harder to quantify and easier to feel: the texture of daily life. These are the companies that decide what you hear, who protects you, whether your augmented arm works tomorrow morning, and whether the temple your grandmother prayed in still exists or has been converted to a branded experience center.

They are mid-tier by valuation. They are not mid-tier by consequence. A Ringo outage inconveniences billions. A failure at one of these entities destroys the specific, irreplaceable thing that made a particular life bearable. The Big Twenty are infrastructure. These twenty are the reason infrastructure matters.

## COMMUNICATIONS AND MEDIA (61-65)

### 61. Lumenfiber Networks

**Sector:** Regional broadband, last-mile connectivity, mesh infrastructure
**Origin:** Founded in Lagos in 2054 by Adaeze Obiora, a former Jangala Systems engineer who was fired for publishing internal data showing that Jangala deliberately throttled broadband speeds in Sub-Saharan Africa to maintain pricing leverage. Obiora used her severance and a small investment from a Ghanaian pension fund to build a mesh broadband network across the Lagos Expansion Zone using salvaged fiber-optic cable and repurposed Jangala relay hardware. Within three years, Lumenfiber provided faster and more reliable connectivity to 40 million Lagosians than Jangala's own network.
**Valuation:** $86 billion (2098 GSEI assessment)
**Territory:** Lumenfiber holds proprietary jurisdiction over its physical network infrastructure across West Africa, East Africa, and the Indian subcontinent -- approximately 1,200 square kilometers of cable corridors, relay stations, and data processing hubs, fragmented across 14 countries. Not contiguous. More like a nervous system than a nation.
**Key Detail:** Lumenfiber's mesh nodes are designed to be physically installed by untrained residents using a standardized toolkit that fits in a shoebox. The company calls them "seeds." A seed is a waterproof relay unit the size of a brick that bonds to any flat surface with industrial adhesive, draws power from ambient solar film, and meshes automatically with any other seed within 800 meters. In dense urban areas, Lumenfiber pays residents a micro-commission for each seed they plant -- typically 12 CreditScript per month per active node. In the Lagos Expansion Zone alone, over 2.3 million residents earn income as "planters," maintaining the mesh that provides their own connectivity. The network is, in a literal sense, grown by the people who use it. This makes it nearly impossible to shut down from the outside. Jangala has tried three times.
**Relationship to Big 20:** Direct competitor to Jangala Systems (#13) in developing-world broadband markets. Jangala views Lumenfiber as a parasite on its infrastructure investments. Lumenfiber views Jangala as a monopolist that charges famine prices for a human right. Both are correct. Lumenfiber purchases backbone transit capacity from Jangala under a hostile commercial agreement renegotiated annually, during which both companies' legal teams reportedly require security escorts.
**Security Force:** Lumenfiber Community Defense Network (LCDN), approximately 3,200 personnel. Lightly armed. Primarily guards relay stations and cable corridors. Unusual in that roughly 40% of LCDN personnel are part-time community volunteers who receive broadband credits rather than salary. The cheapest security force of any chartered corponation, and one of the most effective, because the communities it operates in have a direct economic stake in protecting the infrastructure.

### 62. Thornfield Media Group

**Sector:** News production, investigative journalism, documentary media, public records archival
**Origin:** Incorporated in London in 2061 by a consortium of former BBC, Reuters, and Al Jazeera journalists who pooled severance packages after all three organizations were acquired and gutted by Ringo's media division between 2058 and 2060. The founders -- seven journalists and two media lawyers -- filed a sovereign charter application on the legal theory that independent journalism constituted a "critical infrastructure service" under the Lagos Framework. The charter was granted in 2063, making Thornfield the only media organization recognized as a sovereign entity. Ringo contested the charter for eleven years before abandoning the litigation.
**Valuation:** $14 billion (2098 GSEI assessment). Thornfield is the least valuable corponation by market capitalization. It is also the only one whose product cannot be replaced by any amount of money.
**Territory:** Thornfield holds proprietary jurisdiction over its editorial offices, server farms, and archival facilities across twelve locations -- approximately 8 square kilometers total. The sovereignty matters not because the territory is large but because it means Thornfield's sources, notes, unpublished materials, and editorial processes are legally immune from corponation subpoena or search. No other entity on Earth can offer a whistleblower that protection.
**Key Detail:** Thornfield operates the Dead Drop -- a physical network of anonymous submission terminals located in the Grind zones of every major megalopolis. Each terminal is a hardened kiosk, roughly the size of a vending machine, with no cameras, no biometric readers, and no network connection. A person walks up, inserts a physical data chip, and walks away. Thornfield couriers collect the chips by hand on randomized schedules. The Dead Drop network is how the Meridian Disclosure documents reached public circulation in 2089 -- a NeoCortex security officer inserted 14,000 pages of internal documentation into a Dead Drop terminal in the Austin Grind at 3:47 AM on a Tuesday. Thornfield published the material eleven days later. The Dead Drops have been firebombed fourteen times by unknown parties. Thornfield rebuilds each one within 72 hours, every time, at the same location.
**Relationship to Big 20:** Adversarial to all twenty, though the intensity varies. Tessera and Ringo have both attempted acquisition (rejected). Sunderland Group has attempted to deny Thornfield insurance coverage (overturned by Thornfield's own tribunal under sovereign immunity). Thornfield survives because destroying it would confirm every story it has ever published, and because its destruction would eliminate the one venue where corponation employees can anonymously report internal wrongdoing -- a release valve that, paradoxically, several Big Twenty security chiefs privately acknowledge reduces the risk of more catastrophic leaks.
**Security Force:** Thornfield Editorial Guard (TEG), approximately 800 personnel. Small, professional, and ferociously dedicated. TEG operators are recruited exclusively from former corponation security personnel who left their employers under adverse circumstances -- whistleblowers, conscience objectors, operatives who refused unlawful orders. They are the only security force in the world staffed entirely by people who have already burned their bridges with the corporate establishment. This makes them either the most trustworthy or the most dangerous private security force on Earth, depending on your perspective.

### 63. Murmur Collective

**Sector:** Social platforms, identity-verified communication, reputation systems
**Origin:** Emerged from the wreckage of the social media industry's final collapse in the late 2050s, when the last legacy platforms -- the residual shells of what had once been Twitter, Facebook, WeChat, and their successors -- were absorbed into Ringo's content ecosystem. A group of former platform engineers, based in Tallinn, Estonia, built Murmur as an alternative: a communication platform where every account was tied to a verified biological identity (retinal scan plus voiceprint), every post carried a provenance chain showing its origin and modification history, and every interaction generated a reputation score visible to all other users. The premise was radical transparency as an antidote to the manipulation economy. It worked, partially. Murmur incorporated as a corponation in 2067.
**Valuation:** $210 billion (2098 GSEI assessment)
**Territory:** Murmur holds proprietary jurisdiction over its server infrastructure in Estonia, Iceland, and orbital data centers leased from Tethys -- approximately 30 square kilometers of physical territory. Its real jurisdiction is digital: Murmur's Terms of Sovereign Participation govern the behavior of 1.9 billion active users across every megalopolis on Earth.
**Key Detail:** Murmur's reputation score -- called a "murmur number" in common usage -- has become a de facto social credit system that Murmur never intended and cannot control. Employers check murmur numbers before hiring. Landlords check them before approving leases. Potential romantic partners check them before agreeing to a date. A murmur number below 40 (on a 100-point scale) effectively marks a person as socially unreliable, and the score is influenced by who interacts with you, not just what you say. Associating with low-murmur individuals drags your own score down. The system was designed to incentivize honest communication. What it incentivizes is social conformity and the abandonment of anyone whose reputation dips. Murmur's founders -- three of the original seven still sit on the board -- are aware of this. They publish annual reports acknowledging the problem. They have not solved it, because solving it would require dismantling the feature that makes Murmur valuable.
**Relationship to Big 20:** Murmur purchases server capacity and bandwidth from Jangala Systems and Tethys. Ringo has attempted to acquire Murmur four times; each attempt was blocked by Murmur's charter, which requires a 90% user referendum for any change of ownership. Ringo's alternative strategy has been to build competing identity-verified platforms, none of which have gained traction because users do not trust Ringo's verification to be independent of Ringo's advertising. This is correct.
**Security Force:** Murmur Integrity Division (MID), approximately 1,400 personnel. Primarily digital security -- anti-manipulation, anti-spoofing, identity verification enforcement. Physical security is contracted to third parties. MID's notable capability is its "provenance forensics" team, which can trace the origin of any piece of digital content that has passed through Murmur's network, including content that was deleted. This capability has been subpoenaed by other corponations over 600 times. Murmur has complied exactly zero times, citing sovereign editorial immunity.

### 64. Polyglot Systems International

**Sector:** Real-time translation, linguistic AI, cultural mediation services, diplomatic interpretation
**Origin:** Founded in Seoul in 2048 by Dr. Ji-Yeon Park, a computational linguist who had spent fifteen years at Zheng-Dao's AI division before concluding that the major corponations' translation systems were designed not to facilitate understanding but to flatten language into a standardized commercial register that made advertising more efficient across markets. Park built Polyglot on the opposite principle: translation that preserved cultural nuance, idiomatic meaning, and emotional register. The company's early product was a BCI-integrated translation layer that could render Korean poetry into Yoruba with the metaphorical structure intact. It was beautiful technology. It was also, immediately, essential to every diplomatic and commercial negotiation conducted between parties who spoke different languages -- which, on an Earth of 20 billion people crammed into megalopolises where a single city block might contain speakers of forty languages, was nearly all of them.
**Valuation:** $340 billion (2098 GSEI assessment)
**Territory:** Polyglot holds proprietary jurisdiction over its translation infrastructure -- server farms, linguistic research campuses, and the Babel Archive (see below) -- across South Korea, Finland, Brazil, and Nigeria. Approximately 45 square kilometers total.
**Key Detail:** The Babel Archive is Polyglot's crown jewel and its moral burden. It is the largest repository of human language data ever assembled: 14,000 languages, including 4,200 that are now extinct, recorded in full linguistic depth -- grammar, phonology, pragmatics, cultural context, oral tradition, everything. The Archive was built through a sixty-year acquisition campaign that included purchasing the complete databases of every university linguistics department that closed during the academic collapse of the 2060s-2070s, hiring the last living speakers of dying languages to record oral histories, and deploying field teams to indigenous communities offering payment for linguistic access. The ethical questions are obvious: Polyglot owns the recorded essence of cultures that no longer exist, and it charges licensing fees to access them. A researcher studying the grammar of a language whose last speaker died in 2071 must pay Polyglot for the privilege. The descendants of those speakers receive nothing. Polyglot's board has discussed reparative frameworks annually for two decades. No framework has been adopted, because the Archive generates $18 billion per year in licensing revenue.
**Relationship to Big 20:** Polyglot's translation layer is embedded in the communication systems of seventeen of the Big Twenty. Ringo's customer service operates through Polyglot. Tessera's neural advertising is culturally localized by Polyglot. Zhongwei's factory floor instructions are rendered into fifty-three languages by Polyglot. This integration makes Polyglot nearly untouchable -- acquiring it would give one corponation control over every other corponation's cross-linguistic communications, which is why the Big Twenty have collectively agreed, through an informal understanding never committed to paper, to leave Polyglot independent.
**Security Force:** Polyglot Linguistic Security Corps (PLSC), approximately 2,800 personnel. Notable for being the most multilingual security force on Earth -- operational fluency in a minimum of six languages is a hiring requirement. PLSC's primary mission is protection of the Babel Archive, which is stored in a hardened underground facility beneath Polyglot's Helsinki campus. The facility is rated to survive a direct nuclear strike. Polyglot takes the Archive's physical security more seriously than some corponations take the security of their entire sovereign territory.

### 65. Switchback Broadcasting Cooperative

**Sector:** Regional mesh broadcasting, pirate-to-legitimate media transition, community content production
**Origin:** Switchback began as a pirate radio network in the Great Lakes Megalopolis Grind zones in 2059, operated by a rotating collective of unlicensed broadcasters who used salvaged equipment to transmit news, music, and community information to populations that Ringo's media ecosystem had abandoned or priced out. The original signal covered a twelve-block radius in the former Detroit Grind. By 2065, the network had expanded through volunteer relay stations to reach an estimated 8 million listeners across the ungoverned zones of the Great Lakes region. In 2069, facing the choice between being shut down by RingoGuard or formalizing, the collective incorporated as a broadcasting cooperative and applied for charter recognition. The charter was granted in 2071, backed by an unlikely coalition of Thornfield Media (which valued independent broadcasting) and Voss-Kleiner (which wanted a content provider for its arcology internal media systems that was not controlled by Ringo).
**Valuation:** $22 billion (2098 GSEI assessment)
**Territory:** Switchback holds proprietary jurisdiction over its broadcast facilities and relay stations across the Great Lakes Megalopolis and the Northeast Corridor -- approximately 15 square kilometers of fragmented infrastructure. Its broadcast footprint, however, reaches an estimated 200 million listeners.
**Key Detail:** Switchback still broadcasts on analog radio frequencies. Not exclusively -- it operates mesh digital channels and BCI-integrated audio streams like every other media company -- but its analog signal has never been discontinued, and it is the only corponation-chartered broadcaster that maintains analog capability. The reason is practical: analog radio requires no BCI, no mesh subscription, no identity verification, and no CreditScript. A person with a hand-crank radio receiver -- the kind Switchback distributes for free through Grind-zone community centers -- can receive Switchback's signal. For the estimated 1.6 billion unaugmented people on Earth, and for augmented people whose BCI firmware has been revoked or corrupted, Switchback's analog signal is the only source of broadcast information that does not require corporate authentication to receive. The signal is Switchback's identity, its mission, and the thing that makes Ringo's media division lose sleep, because you cannot throttle a radio wave with a terms-of-service update.
**Relationship to Big 20:** Switchback has content distribution agreements with Voss-Kleiner (arcology internal media) and a hostile but functional coexistence with Ringo, which has repeatedly attempted to acquire Switchback's broadcast spectrum allocation. Thornfield Media Group provides Switchback with investigative journalism content under a mutual support agreement that both organizations describe as "professional solidarity" and Ringo describes as "a cartel."
**Security Force:** Switchback Broadcast Protection Unit (BPU), approximately 600 personnel. The smallest chartered security force in the world. BPU's primary mission is protecting relay stations from sabotage -- a task that requires more electricians than soldiers. BPU personnel are colloquially known as "tower monkeys" because they spend most of their time climbing relay masts to repair equipment that has been vandalized, weather-damaged, or targeted by jamming attacks. Three BPU personnel have been killed in the line of duty, all during relay station defense against unattributed armed incursions that Switchback attributes to Ringo proxies and Ringo attributes to "criminal elements operating in ungoverned space."

### 66. Ironveil Services Group

**Sector:** Regional private military contracting, zone pacification, infrastructure defense
**Origin:** Founded in Johannesburg in 2052 by Colonel (ret.) Nia Dlamini, a former South African National Defence Force officer who watched the SANDF dissolve into irrelevance as Ringo and NovaChem's security forces assumed de facto military control over South Africa's industrial zones. Dlamini recruited three hundred former SANDF soldiers and offered their services to mid-tier corponations that could not afford to build internal security forces but needed armed protection for facilities in contested territory. The business model was simple: professional soldiers at corporate rates. It worked because Dlamini insisted on one thing the Big Twenty security forces do not offer -- accountability. Ironveil contracts include independent incident review, defined rules of engagement disclosed to the client, and a use-of-force reporting standard modeled on the old Geneva Conventions. This makes Ironveil slower, more expensive, and more legally constrained than its competitors. It also makes Ironveil the only PMC that mid-tier corponations trust to operate on their sovereign territory without fear that the security contractor will become a bigger problem than the threat it was hired to address.
**Valuation:** $120 billion (2098 GSEI assessment)
**Territory:** Ironveil holds proprietary jurisdiction over its training facilities and operational bases across Southern Africa, West Africa, and Southeast Asia -- approximately 600 square kilometers. Its operational footprint, through active contracts, extends across forty countries.
**Key Detail:** Ironveil maintains a publicly accessible use-of-force database called the Ledger. Every discharge of a weapon, every physical restraint, every detention conducted by an Ironveil operative is logged in the Ledger within 24 hours, with a narrative description, the operative's identifier (anonymized but consistent, so patterns can be tracked), and the outcome. The Ledger is published monthly on Ironveil's public network, unredacted. No other security force on Earth -- corponation, national, or freelance -- publishes anything comparable. Ironveil's competitors call it a marketing gimmick. Ironveil's clients call it the reason they pay a 40% premium. The Ledger has been cited in seventeen Thornfield investigations as a baseline for measuring other security forces' conduct. Ironveil has never attempted to suppress a Thornfield story, even unfavorable ones.
**Relationship to Big 20:** Ironveil contracts with Cascadia Agriculture (#17), Lumenfiber, and approximately thirty other mid-tier entities. Arcturus Defense Systems (#5) views Ironveil as a minor competitor and has attempted to acquire it twice. Dlamini refused both times. Ringo has Ironveil on a watch list because Ironveil's accountability standards make Ringo's own security practices look worse by comparison, which they are.
**Security Force:** Ironveil IS its security force. Approximately 18,000 active personnel, organized into company-sized units deployable within 48 hours to any contracted location. Equipment is professional but not cutting-edge -- Ironveil buys surplus from Arcturus and Halberd Aerospace, typically one generation behind the latest systems. Dlamini's philosophy: "A disciplined soldier with last year's rifle is more dangerous than an undisciplined one with tomorrow's."

### 67. Harrow & Locke Recoveries

**Sector:** Bounty hunting, fugitive recovery, skip tracing, exclusion registry enforcement
**Origin:** Incorporated in Dallas in 2066 by former US Marshals Service deputies Marcus Harrow and Selene Locke, who recognized that the collapse of federal law enforcement had created a market for professional fugitive recovery that no government agency could fill. When the CSES exclusion registry system went live in 2071, Harrow & Locke pivoted from traditional bail enforcement to a new and far more lucrative specialty: hunting people who had been excluded from the corporate economy and gone ghost. The contract market was immediate and enormous. Every corponation with an exclusion registry needed someone to find the people who had slipped off the grid -- debtors, whistleblowers, contract breakers, and the occasional genuine criminal -- and Harrow & Locke built the institutional capacity to do it at scale.
**Valuation:** $58 billion (2098 GSEI assessment)
**Territory:** Harrow & Locke holds proprietary jurisdiction over its operational headquarters in Dallas, regional offices in twelve cities, and a network of detention transit facilities colloquially known as "way stations" -- approximately 40 square kilometers total.
**Key Detail:** Harrow & Locke's operatives -- called "locators" -- are the only private personnel authorized under CSES mutual recognition agreements to detain excluded individuals in ungoverned zones and transport them to corponation sovereign territory for tribunal proceedings. The legal framework is a patchwork of bilateral agreements between Harrow & Locke and individual corponations, each specifying which categories of exclusion warrant physical recovery. In practice, locators operate with enormous discretion. A locator tracking a Tier 4 excluded debtor through the Detroit Grind makes judgment calls every hour about use of force, pursuit boundaries, and collateral risk, with no oversight beyond Harrow & Locke's internal review process. The company's recruitment pipeline draws heavily from former corponation security personnel, former military, and -- increasingly -- freelance street operators who demonstrated competence that Harrow & Locke decided to formalize rather than compete against. A nontrivial number of locators are former bounty targets themselves, people who were good enough at evading capture that Harrow & Locke offered them employment instead. The company calls this "talent recognition." The street calls it "turning the foxes into hounds."
**Relationship to Big 20:** Harrow & Locke holds active recovery contracts with Ringo, Voss-Kleiner, Sunderland Group, and eleven other CSES partner entities. The relationship is transactional and mutually uncomfortable. The Big Twenty need Harrow & Locke because their own security forces are optimized for territorial defense, not manhunting in ungoverned zones. Harrow & Locke needs the Big Twenty because they are the source of every contract. Neither side trusts the other. Both sides pay on time.
**Security Force:** Harrow & Locke Recovery Operations Division, approximately 6,400 active locators worldwide, plus 2,200 support and intelligence personnel. Locators operate in teams of two to four, equipped for extended independent operations in ungoverned territory. Standard loadout includes light combat armor, sidearms, restraint systems, biometric scanners linked to the CSES registry, and -- critically -- a portable detention module that can be mounted on any standard vehicle, allowing transport of detained individuals across zone boundaries without requiring a fixed facility.

### 68. Parallax Intelligence Solutions

**Sector:** Corporate intelligence, competitive analysis, strategic information services
**Origin:** Founded in Singapore in 2055 by Dr. Yusuf al-Rashidi, a former Sunderland Group risk analyst who spent twelve years building actuarial models that predicted corponation behavior based on financial data. Al-Rashidi realized that the models he built for Sunderland's insurance division were more valuable as intelligence products than as underwriting tools -- that the ability to predict what a corponation would do next, based on granular analysis of its financial flows, personnel movements, supply chain adjustments, and internal communications patterns, was worth more than any insurance premium. He left Sunderland, took nothing proprietary (he was careful), rebuilt his methodology from first principles, and founded Parallax. The company sells intelligence to corponations about other corponations. It is the closest thing the post-sovereign world has to a civilian intelligence agency.
**Valuation:** $190 billion (2098 GSEI assessment)
**Territory:** Parallax holds proprietary jurisdiction over its analytical facilities in Singapore, Zurich, and Nairobi -- approximately 18 square kilometers of hardened, compartmented office space. Parallax's territory is small because its product is weightless: information, analysis, prediction.
**Key Detail:** Parallax maintains what it calls the "Graph" -- a continuously updated relational map of every known corponation entity, subsidiary, contractor, and affiliate on Earth, including their financial relationships, personnel overlaps, supply chain dependencies, and historical patterns of cooperation and conflict. The Graph contains an estimated 340 million nodes and 2.1 billion edges. It is the single most comprehensive map of corporate power ever assembled, and it exists on air-gapped servers that have never been connected to any external network. Clients do not access the Graph. They submit questions, and Parallax analysts -- all of whom hold Tier 3 or higher augmentation with Parallax-customized analytical firmware -- provide answers derived from the Graph without revealing the underlying data or methodology. The Graph is Parallax's product, its competitive advantage, and the thing that would get every Parallax employee killed if it were ever leaked in full. Parallax's internal security culture reflects this: analysts undergo quarterly loyalty screening, live in Parallax-provided housing within sovereign territory, and are subject to a ten-year non-compete clause enforced by Parallax's own tribunal. The smart ones understand this as a cage. The very smart ones understand it as protection.
**Relationship to Big 20:** Fourteen of the Big Twenty are Parallax clients. Parallax does not disclose which fourteen. The remaining six either maintain internal intelligence capabilities they consider superior (Arcturus, Tessera) or have been declined as clients by Parallax for undisclosed reasons. The industry assumption is that Parallax declines clients whose intelligence requirements would force Parallax to investigate its own existing clients -- a conflict of interest Parallax resolves by refusing the engagement rather than compromising the product.
**Security Force:** Parallax Protective Services (PPS), approximately 2,600 personnel. Small, elite, and paranoid by professional necessity. PPS is organized around information security rather than territorial defense -- its primary mission is preventing data exfiltration, not repelling armed assault. PPS operatives are trained in counter-surveillance, electronic warfare, and the physical security of air-gapped systems. They are not soldiers. They are vault guards with security clearances.

### 69. Greymantle Counter-Intelligence Corp

**Sector:** Counter-espionage, security auditing, mole hunting, loyalty verification
**Origin:** Founded in London in 2063 by Dame Philippa Craine, a former MI6 officer who served during the final decade of British intelligence operations before the Security Service was dissolved and its functions parceled out to Ringo's UK division and Arcturus's European operations. Craine watched her profession absorbed into corporate structures that valued intelligence only as a revenue tool and concluded that the market lacked a dedicated counter-espionage service -- an entity that did not gather intelligence but hunted the people who gathered it on behalf of others. Greymantle finds moles. It identifies compromised personnel. It audits security architectures for vulnerabilities that other security firms miss because those firms built the architecture in the first place. It is the doctor you call when you suspect your other doctor is poisoning you.
**Valuation:** $74 billion (2098 GSEI assessment)
**Territory:** Greymantle holds proprietary jurisdiction over its operational facilities in London, Vienna, and Hong Kong -- approximately 12 square kilometers. Its territory is deliberately modest. Craine's operating philosophy: a counter-espionage firm with a large territorial footprint has a large attack surface.
**Key Detail:** Greymantle's signature service is the "Grey Audit" -- a comprehensive counter-intelligence assessment in which a Greymantle team embeds inside a client corponation for 90 to 180 days, posing as ordinary employees, and attempts to identify every active intelligence penetration, every compromised system, and every disloyal employee within the organization. The results are delivered in a sealed report to the client's CEO personally. The reports are legendary within the corporate security community for their brutal specificity. A Grey Audit of a mid-tier energy corponation in 2091 identified eleven active moles working for four different competing entities, a compromised BCI firmware update channel that had been exfiltrating executive neural data for three years, and the client's own Chief Security Officer as a Parallax Intelligence informant. The CSO was terminated the same day. Greymantle does not conduct Grey Audits on Parallax. This is not a formal policy. It is an informal understanding that both companies maintain because the alternative is a war neither would survive.
**Relationship to Big 20:** Greymantle has conducted Grey Audits for Ringo, Kessler-Dyne, Cascadia Agriculture, NovaChem, and at least six other Big Twenty entities. Arcturus has never engaged Greymantle, publicly stating that its own counter-intelligence capability is superior. Greymantle has never publicly disputed this claim, which within the intelligence community is interpreted as Greymantle being too polite to embarrass a potential future client.
**Security Force:** Greymantle Operations Group (GOG), approximately 1,800 personnel. GOG is unusual in that its operatives are trained primarily in social engineering, surveillance detection, and interrogation rather than combat. A GOG operative's most dangerous weapon is a conversation. Physical security of Greymantle facilities is contracted to Ironveil Services Group, a relationship that both companies describe as mutually beneficial and that the security industry describes as "the paranoid hiring the disciplined."

### 70. Bastion Personal Protection Corp

**Sector:** Executive protection, high-value individual security, extraction services
**Origin:** Founded in Dubai in 2058 by former Secret Service agent Diana Vasquez (no relation to Tessera's CEO) after the dissolution of the US Secret Service's protective operations division in 2056. Vasquez had spent twenty years protecting American presidents and senior officials and watched the profession she loved become irrelevant as the people who needed protection stopped being politicians and started being executives. She relocated to Dubai, recruited forty former protection agents from seven countries, and built Bastion as a bespoke security firm for the individuals that corponation security forces are not designed to protect: the executives themselves. RingoGuard protects Ringo territory. Bastion protects Ringo's CEO when she leaves it.
**Valuation:** $42 billion (2098 GSEI assessment)
**Territory:** Bastion holds proprietary jurisdiction over its training compound in the Dubai Sovereign Zone and regional offices in Singapore, London, Sao Paulo, and the Austin Sovereign Zone -- approximately 25 square kilometers total.
**Key Detail:** Bastion's most exclusive service tier is called "Shadow" -- a 24/7 protection detail in which the client is never aware of their protectors' specific positions, identities, or number. Shadow operatives dress as ordinary civilians, change appearance daily, and maintain a protective perimeter that the client cannot identify even if they look for it. The premise is that a visible bodyguard creates a visible target; a Shadow detail creates an invisible shield. Clients who subscribe to Shadow protection report that the psychological effect is disorienting: the knowledge that you are being watched and protected by people you cannot see produces, in many clients, a paranoia indistinguishable from the threat the protection is meant to neutralize. Two Shadow clients have requested termination of their contracts because they could not tolerate the feeling of being constantly observed by allies they could not locate. Bastion refunded both contracts in full. The company considers this a feature, not a flaw -- a client who cannot tolerate being watched by friends is a client who will notice when they are being watched by enemies.
**Relationship to Big 20:** Bastion holds active protection contracts for senior executives at Ringo, Tessera, Sunderland Group, Voss-Kleiner, and an undisclosed number of other Big Twenty entities. The relationships are discreet by design -- no corponation wants to publicly acknowledge that its own security force is insufficient to protect its leadership. Bastion's client list is the most closely guarded secret in the personal security industry. Leaking it would be a death sentence for the company and, potentially, for its clients.
**Security Force:** Bastion IS its security force. Approximately 4,200 active protection operatives worldwide, plus 1,800 intelligence, logistics, and support personnel. Bastion operatives are among the highest-paid security professionals on Earth -- a senior Shadow operative earns more than most corponation vice presidents. The compensation reflects the job: these are people who are paid to die for someone else's CEO, and the actuarial tables confirm that some of them do.

### 71. Crucible Augmentation Services

**Sector:** Augmentation maintenance, implant repair, firmware servicing, aftermarket modification
**Origin:** Founded in Osaka in 2064 by Kenji Murakami, a biomedical engineer who had spent a decade at Tessera's NovaMind service division and recognized a structural gap in the augmentation economy: the major manufacturers built implants but provided maintenance only through their own service networks, at their own prices, on their own schedules. A Tessera BCI user whose implant malfunctioned had exactly one option -- a Tessera-authorized service center, at Tessera-set prices, with Tessera-controlled firmware. Murakami saw the parallel to the auto repair industry of the twentieth century, where manufacturers tried to lock vehicle owners into dealer-only service networks, and built Crucible as the independent mechanic of the augmentation age.
**Valuation:** $280 billion (2098 GSEI assessment)
**Territory:** Crucible holds proprietary jurisdiction over its service centers, training academies, and parts fabrication facilities across Japan, South Korea, India, Brazil, and the Great Lakes Megalopolis -- approximately 200 square kilometers total.
**Key Detail:** Crucible's technicians -- called "smiths" in company and street parlance alike -- are the only non-manufacturer personnel certified to service augmentation hardware across multiple manufacturer platforms. A Crucible smith can repair a Tessera NovaMind, recalibrate a Zheng-Dao CortexLink, and replace a Helix BioSystems synthetic limb joint in the same afternoon. This cross-platform expertise makes Crucible indispensable and despised in roughly equal measure. The major manufacturers have fought Crucible's right-to-repair charter in every available legal venue for three decades. They have lost every case, because Crucible's charter was granted under the Lagos Framework's "essential services" provision -- the same provision that protects Thornfield -- and because the alternative to independent augmentation repair is a world in which 4 billion augmented humans are permanently locked into single-manufacturer service dependencies. Even the tribunals that favor corponation power recognize that this degree of dependency would make the current system unsustainable. Crucible's Osaka training academy graduates approximately 8,000 new smiths per year. The waiting list for admission is four years long. ### 72. Stillwater Mental Health Systems

### 73. Ouroboros Recovery Networks

Neural stimulation dependency -- the compulsive use of BCI-delivered dopamine protocols, sensory enhancement loops, and cognitive performance amplifiers -- affected an estimated 800 million people by 2065, and no existing treatment framework addressed the unique challenge of treating addiction to a device surgically implanted in the patient's brain. You cannot confiscate a BCI the way you confiscate a bottle. Restrepo built Ouroboros to address the specific, terrible problem of helping people recover from addiction to their own augmentation.
**Valuation:** $130 billion (2098 GSEI assessment)
**Territory:** Ouroboros holds proprietary jurisdiction over its treatment campuses in Colombia, Thailand, New Zealand, and Iceland -- approximately 150 square kilometers, chosen for geographic isolation. This requires active electronic countermeasures: PCG operates signal-jamming equipment, BCI-interference shielding, and real-time monitoring of the electromagnetic environment within every Prism space. Ringo has filed eleven legal challenges to Prism's CortexCast exclusion zone. Prism has won all eleven, citing sovereign territorial authority. PCG's secondary mission is physical security of artists and their work, which has included, on three occasions, the armed defense of Prism studios against corporate agents attempting to seize artwork created by artists whose labor contracts with former employers contained intellectual property clauses that those employers interpreted as covering all creative output in perpetuity. Prism's legal position: art created within sovereign cultural territory is sovereign cultural property. The tribunals have, so far, agreed.
