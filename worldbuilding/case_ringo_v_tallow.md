# Case File: Ringo CorpoNation Security Division v. Marcus "Brick" Tallow

**Incident Classification:** Armed Robbery, Sovereign Territory Violation, External Actor Incursion

## Incident Summary

On the evening of March 14th, Marcus "Brick" Tallow, a known enforcer for the Eastside Vago Kings street gang, entered a RingoFuel Express filling station located at 4400 Industrial Corridor, Detroit Reclamation Zone — a property operating under full Ringo CorpoNation Proprietary Jurisdiction as registered under the entity's Standing Sovereign Charter, Section 4, Article 9.

Tallow produced a firearm, demanded the contents of the register — approximately $2,200 in Ringo CreditScript and $380 in legacy U.S. currency — and struck a RingoFuel employee with the weapon before fleeing on foot into the adjacent municipal zone.

He was apprehended four blocks away by Detroit Metro Police and taken into city custody.

## The Jurisdictional Problem

The moment Tallow walked through the doors of RingoFuel Express, he stepped off Detroit soil — legally speaking — and onto Ringo Sovereign Operational Territory. The CorpoNation's charter, acknowledged under the Detroit Reclamation Zone Economic Partnership Agreement of 2031, explicitly granted Ringo full proprietary jurisdiction over all incidents occurring on its premises.

That means:
- Detroit Metro Police had no primary jurisdiction over the crime scene
- The evidence collected inside the store by city officers was technically gathered without authority
- The assault victim was a Ringo employee, covered under Ringo's internal labor protection statutes — not Michigan tort law
- The stolen CreditScript is not legal tender under U.S. law, creating a valuation dispute

Ringo's Standing Security Force — the RingoGuard — arrived at the scene twelve minutes after the incident and formally disputed custody of the case, citing the Sovereign Premises Violation Act embedded in their operational charter.

## Dual Prosecution Scenario

Tallow now faces two simultaneous proceedings:

### Track 1 — City of Detroit / State of Michigan
**Charges:** Armed robbery, aggravated assault, weapons possession. Prosecuted in Wayne County Circuit Court.
**Maximum exposure:** 15-25 years.

### Track 2 — Ringo CorpoNation Internal Adjudication Tribunal
**Charges under Ringo Internal Code:**
- Sovereign Territory Incursion (Class I)
- Interference with CorpoNation Commerce
- Employee Bodily Harm (Ringo Statute 7.4)
- Theft of Proprietary Currency

**Penalties under Ringo law:**
- Indefinite placement on the Ringo Exclusion Registry — a blacklist that bars the individual from all RingoFuel, RingoMart, RingoPharma, RingoNet, and affiliated partner locations globally
- Civil damages claim of $180,000 filed against Tallow personally, covering lost revenue, employee trauma compensation, and "sovereign dignity reparation"

The Ringo Tribunal does not require a jury. It does not guarantee counsel. Its findings are binding within all Ringo-controlled spaces worldwide.

## Defense Argument

Tallow's public defender, Lisa Okafor, filed a motion arguing that no private entity can hold concurrent criminal jurisdiction over a U.S. citizen on U.S. soil, and that the CorpoNation charter constitutes an unconstitutional delegation of sovereign power to a private actor in violation of the Fourteenth Amendment.

Ringo's legal team — a 400-person internal law division — responded that the charter was voluntarily ratified by the Detroit Reclamation Zone Authority under economic necessity, that Tallow implicitly accepted Ringo jurisdiction the moment he crossed the threshold of a clearly posted sovereign premises, and pointed to three prior federal rulings in which Ringo's territorial claims had been upheld in similar disputes.

## Outcome

The Wayne County case proceeded. Tallow was convicted and sentenced to nine years.

The Ringo Tribunal issued its findings two weeks later in a proceeding Tallow did not attend and was not notified of in advance. He was added to the Exclusion Registry, fined $180,000, and formally classified in Ringo's internal systems as a **Sovereign Threat Actor** — a designation that, under Ringo's data-sharing agreements with seventeen partner CorpoNations, now follows him across a combined 340,000 retail, residential, medical, and transit locations worldwide.

When Tallow is released from prison, he will be unable to buy gas, fill a prescription, board a RingoTransit bus, or access a RingoNet broadband node — in dozens of countries — for the remainder of his natural life.

Detroit Metro Police closed the case.
Ringo called it a successful enforcement action.
