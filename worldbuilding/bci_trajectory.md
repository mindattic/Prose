# Brain-Computer Interface Trajectory (2025-2100)

## Where We Are Now (2026)

Cortex Dynamics is the loudest name in the space. As of September 2025, 12 people with severe paralysis have received Cortex Dynamics implants and are actively using them to control digital and physical tools through thought alone. The N1 implant includes 1,024 electrodes distributed among 64 threads, replacing a small piece of skull bone and connecting to the brain via hair-thin threaded electrodes.

Real outcomes are happening: the first human recipient now controls computers, plays video games, and posts on social media purely through neural signals. A patient with ALS became the third recipient and can now type using only his brain.

On the manufacturing side, Cortex Dynamics plans to begin high-volume production and move to an almost entirely automated surgical procedure in 2026, with device threads passing through the dura without needing to remove it. Their next-gen surgical robot reduced electrode thread insertion time to 1.5 seconds per thread, and the company is working toward LASIK-like procedures that take only minutes.

VascuLink is the quieter but arguably safer competitor. Their "Stentrode" device is inserted through the jugular vein and lodged in a vein near the motor cortex, recording signals through the vessel wall without drilling into the skull at all. In 2025, VascuLink partnered with Apple and NVIDIA and moved toward a pivotal trial that could make it the first commercially scalable implanted BCI.

Other players are advancing too. ParaNeural's Connexus BCI uses a modular 421-electrode array, and in June 2025 a University of Michigan team performed the first-in-human recording with it.

Expanding applications: beyond paralysis and ALS, some companies are now starting to target more widespread conditions like mental health symptoms, and China has seen an explosion of BCI startups entering the field.

## The Road to 2100 (Informed Speculation)

**2030s** — BCIs become a mainstream medical device for paralysis, speech restoration, epilepsy, and Parkinson's. Surgery is outpatient, fast, and mostly robotic. Clinical focus shifts toward communication, mobility, and assistive technology for the broader disabled population.

**2040s-2050s** — Consumer-grade BCIs emerge. Not just for disability — early adopters use them for AR/VR control, hands-free interfaces, and cognitive augmentation. Memory assistance and focus enhancement tools appear. Biocompatible materials allow implants to last decades without degrading.

**2060s-2070s** — Bandwidth between brain and machine becomes high enough to blur the line between thinking and computing. The concept of "exocortex" — offloading memory, processing, or sensory input to an external system — becomes real. The gap between biological and digital cognition narrows.

**2080s-2100** — Deeply speculative territory. If the trajectory holds:
- Brain-to-brain communication via shared neural interfaces becomes possible
- Persistent memory (experiences recorded, reviewed, replayed) is feasible
- Cognitive merging with AI systems — not just using AI as a tool but thinking with it in real time
- The definition of "self" and consciousness becomes a serious philosophical and legal crisis
- Massive inequality risk: augmented vs. unaugmented humans becomes a defining social divide

## Open Questions

The biggest open questions aren't really technical — they're ethical and social:
- Who owns your neural data?
- Can a government subpoena your memories?
- What happens when a corporation can literally advertise inside your head?

From a cybersecurity angle: neural interfaces as attack surfaces, firmware exploits with literal neurological consequences, and identity theft at the level of thought.

## Relevance to StreetSamurai

This trajectory grounds the 6-facet system in real technological progression:
- **WOUND** — what the implant cost you (surgery, dependency, debt, side effects, lost autonomy)
- **IDEAL** — the code you hold about what augmentation should and shouldn't touch
- **ID** — who you were before the interface, the biological self being overwritten
- **SHADOW** — what the implant reveals about you that you didn't want to know (neural data, subconscious patterns, suppressed impulses now readable)
- **MASK** — the curated self you broadcast through the interface, your public neural signature
- **GHOST** — the question of whether you are still in there, or if you're running on borrowed cognition
