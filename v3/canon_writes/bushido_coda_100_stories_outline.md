# Bushido Coda — 100 Story Outline

**Working document. Each entry is a one-paragraph premise plus connection notes. Status: in-progress. Stories 1–8 are the existing Bushido Coda spine (book one). Stories 9 onward are sketches; future sessions develop each into a full book or novella.**

Cross-references: [[feedback-creative-directives-20260516]], [[project-glmz-mysteries]], [[project-rogue-ai-avatar-arc]], [[project-vultures]], [[feedback-keep-whodunits-open]].

## Structural principles

- **Each story is Kyle-adjacent in one of four ways**: contract (Kyle takes a job), friend (someone Kyle knows asks for help), happenstance (Kyle is in the wrong place at the wrong time), or peer (Kyle is a side character in someone else's adventure).
- **Every story should LIVE in the world** — environmental texture, GLMZ-specific details, the active mysteries from [[project-glmz-mysteries]] in the background.
- **Each substantial character introduced is a potential spin-off lead** — written with enough depth to carry their own book.
- **The rogue-AI manipulation thread is seeded but never confirmed** until much later. Each story can contain a moment that re-reads as orchestration when the eventual reveal lands.
- **Threads recur across stories** — a faction Kyle pissed off in #4 has someone with a long memory in #27.

## Book One: Bushido Coda (existing 8-chapter spine)

1. **Bearing Teeth** — Half-rate vengeance contract. Kyle breaks the man who broke a working family. Sets the discipline, the ritual, the working relationship with Mrs. Chen. Signature work.
2. **Day in the Life** — Continuous from #1. Walks home through GLMZ rain, takes the bike, Pixel pulls bullets out of his back in her kitchen, sleeps in his clothes, wakes to rain still falling. Establishes the daily rhythm.
3. **A Restless Mind** — The next evening at Mrs. Chen's. A hooded woman watches him, walks him three blocks, pulls her hood back. *Sable.* The voice in the cracked terminal has a face. She asks him to come back tomorrow night to the Faraday cage. He says yes. Pixel meets him in the hallway with tea.
4. **Inside the Cage** — Faraday-shielded back room. Sable reveals what she could not say at the noodle stall: a non-human has been routing Kyle's contracts. Triangulation walk through three GLMZ places. By dawn she has coordinates. Kyle rides home alone with the network's ambient hum back in his chest.
5. **The Rogue AI** — Continuous from #4. Kyle and Sable visit the substation. They build the avatar hypothesis (which is WRONG, per the long-con plan — but they don't know that). Sets the working frame Kyle will operate against for the rest of book one and the next several books.
6. **The Interview** — Lotus Syndicate ambush calibrated for "alive but damaged." Mira recruits Kyle. He accepts roster, refuses payroll.
7. **Street Meat** — Hua's contract: extract a sealed cargo from an Atlas-program continuation lab. The cargo is Dr. Nadia Park. Kyle refuses to deliver her. Takes her to Pixel. Goes back to Hua anyway.
8. **A Borrowed Hand** — The cleaver, the chair, the cauterizer band. Kyle's hands come off. Encounters Puppeteer (ELF) in the south-arm sluice trench. The borrowed limb. Comes home with Hua personally indebted to him. Pixel meets him in the hallway with the hands and weeps.

## Book Two and onward — story sketches (working draft)

### 9. **The Noodle Stand Wars**
Mrs. Chen's stall starts losing business to a new operation two blocks south — better marketing, lower prices, and (Kyle gradually realizes) shells of synthetic broth produced industrially by a CorpoNation-backed startup trying to capture neighborhood food economies citywide. Mrs. Chen does not ask Kyle for help. Kyle helps anyway, the way Kyle does — quietly, sideways, by introducing the startup's local manager to the consequences of certain business decisions. Featuring: Mrs. Chen's daughter (now older), now a recurring character. Faction: a Tier 4 CorpoNation subsidiary that will turn up again.

### 10. **The Auntie Hoa Job**
Pixel's mother — Auntie Hoa, established in Pixel's book canon — calls in a favor. A nephew has gotten in over his head with the Reliquary (slaver-adjacent operation working the seams between CorpoNation jurisdictions). Kyle and a peer (Maeve, also Pixel-book canon, becomes a recurring peer freelancer) extract the nephew. The contract pays in obligation, not credits. Auntie Hoa now considers Kyle family, which has consequences.

### 11. **Things That Fall**
Something comes down over the southern outskirts. Not CorpoNation salvage. Not satellite debris. Something intact. Kyle gets the contract because he is on the short list for "freelancer with no CorpoNation reporting obligations." What he finds: a sealed pod. What's inside the pod: not revealed in this story. Kyle delivers the pod sealed to a buyer Sable cannot identify. The buyer pays in Φ. Kyle does not learn what was inside. (The reader doesn't either. The pod will return.)

### 12. **The Vultures on the Doorstep**
The Vultures — body-pickup-after-shootouts, organ-repossession technicians — show up to collect a body that isn't dead yet, from a kill Kyle was supposed to have closed. Kyle, who has worked with the Vultures before (Hua's Street Meat extraction), now has to negotiate the difference between "contract complete" and "Vultures happy to leave." Hua makes a guest appearance. Featuring: a Vultures crew chief who will become a spin-off character.

### 13. **The Doctor Will See You Now**
Dr. Nadia Park (rescued in Street Meat) calls in her own favor. A former Atlas colleague has surfaced in GLMZ working as a corporate-augmentation consultant. The colleague does not know Park is alive. Park wants to find out what the colleague knows — without alerting the colleague to Park's continued existence. Kyle plays middleman. The colleague turns out to know more about NeoCortex's Atlas program than Park expected. Threads on the program advance one notch. Nobody learns the full shape.

### 14. **The Synthetic Who Asked**
A second-generation synthetic — legally a person under the new ruling — comes to Kyle with a contract. Their previous owner-employer is contesting the ruling and is going to win on a technicality. The synthetic wants Kyle to make the case go away. Kyle, who does not do legal work, declines. The synthetic asks again, differently. Kyle helps. Featuring: a synthetic character who becomes a recurring fixture in the world. Faction: a CorpoNation legal division gets a black mark.

### 15. **The Cat-Eared Hacker**
A peer freelancer enters the cast — call them Echo for now. Catmod (full ear set, retractable claws, modified inner-ear directional hearing), tier 4 implant work, specializes in extracting information from places it isn't supposed to be available. Echo needs muscle for a job Kyle is the right shape for. They team up. The job goes sideways in an interesting way. Echo joins Kyle's circle of peers. They will be back.

### 16. **The Quiet Block**
A neighborhood block in northern Wicker has gone quiet — nobody answering pagers, nobody on the network, nobody at the windows. Three days. Kyle is hired by a worried sibling. What he finds: a localized physics anomaly. Time runs slower on the block. Two days outside is forty minutes inside. The residents are fine, slightly bewildered, and have run out of food. Kyle resupplies them and reports. The anomaly closes on its own twelve hours later. Nobody can explain what caused it. (Seed for the breakdown-of-physics mystery.)

### 17. **The Bucktown Forty**
Aftermath story. A Bucktown ambush several months ago left forty operatives down. Some of them had families. One of those families finds Kyle. They don't want vengeance. They want him to confirm whether their son went down clean or whether the body recovered was a stand-in. Kyle reads the body. He tells them what they need to hear. He does not tell them what he saw.

### 18. **The Pulse Station Job**
A Pulse magnetic transit station has a sealed-off section nobody is supposed to use. A Sable-routed contract: retrieve a package from the sealed section. Kyle expects standard intrusion work. What he finds: someone else got there first, and the someone else is still in the section, and the someone else recognizes Kyle on sight. (First appearance of a recurring antagonist — a peer operating at Kyle's tier with opposite ethics.)

### 19. **The Genemod Underground**
An underground genemod salon in a sub-tier basement is doing work that should not be possible at the price they charge. Their customers come from every tier. Their materials come from somewhere nobody can trace. Kyle is hired to investigate by a CorpoNation that lost a junior executive to the salon's most ambitious procedure. What he finds: the salon's lead surgeon is the brightest, kindest person Kyle has met in a year, and her materials come from an ELF that fixed the supply chain for her in exchange for things Kyle doesn't fully understand. (Seed for ELFs as not-always-malign.)

### 20. **The Space Elevator Job**
Someone is offering serious money for a Tier-5-restricted document from a building two blocks from the southern lakeshore. The building is in line-of-sight to the space elevator. The view from the third floor of the target is, by everyone who has been there's account, breathtaking. Kyle takes the job. He gets the document. He watches the elevator from the third floor for nineteen minutes before he leaves. It is the longest he has stood still without working in eight years. The document, when he hands it to Sable for routing, turns out to be: an old NeoCortex internal memo. About the Atlas program. About Kyle's intake class. Sable does not show it to him.

### 21. **The Slaver Audit**
Sumi Okeke-Tran (peer) has been six weeks inside a Lakefront-corridor operation that the freelance market has been quietly cataloguing as a slaver front. Her cover is failing in a way that cannot be repaired — the lead operative has begun checking Sumi's documented history at depth, and three of the documented anchors are about to fail simultaneously. Sumi pulls Kyle in for the exit. The exit is the contract. The audit of what they leave behind — names, faces, the routing pattern of where the operation's product was being moved to — is the story. Kyle does not destroy the operation. He passes the audit to Maeve. What Maeve does with it depends on which CorpoNation Maeve calls first, which depends on three things Kyle does not yet know.

### 22. **Pixel's Father**
A man Pixel has not spoken to in eleven years walks into 2E on a Tuesday. He has a request. The request is small. The request is not small. Pixel does not turn him away and does not invite him to stay. She closes her door and crosses the hall and asks Kyle to be present for the conversation. Kyle stands in the kitchen and does not say anything for ninety minutes while Pixel and her father say to each other the things that have been waiting eleven years to be said. The contract — because the father's request is, technically, a contract — gets accepted. The contract is the smallest favor Kyle has ever taken. The cost of the favor is not contractual. Auntie Hoa's reaction, two weeks later, is the chapter's actual climax.

### 23. **The Mutant Who Knew Seo**
Kyle gets a tip from Sparrow (peer): a person in the sub-tier underneath West Erie has been asking specific questions about a man who died nineteen years ago. The man is Dae-jung Seo. The person doing the asking is, by every metric Sparrow's network can return, not entirely the species their birth certificate registered them as. Kyle goes down. The person is willing to talk. The conversation is not a confrontation. The person knew Seo in 2196 — knew him in a way that Kyle has spent his life trying to know him and has not been able to — and is now, in 2225, willing to share what they knew under conditions Kyle has to negotiate without leaving the sub-tier conversation. The negotiation is the story. Kyle leaves the sub-tier with the conditions accepted and three new questions about Seo he has not previously had vocabulary for.

### 24. **The Fish Market Affair**
A dispute at the Wicker fish market over a cart escalates into a contract Kyle accepts because the fish vendor was a friend of Mrs. Chen. The contract is small. The dispute is, on inspection, not about the cart at all — it is about a piece of GLMZ municipal infrastructure that the vendor's cart's location depends on, and the infrastructure is being acquired by a CorpoNation subsidiary that has been buying low-tier vendor permits across three Wicker corridors for the past six months. Kyle does not solve the CorpoNation acquisition. Kyle solves the cart. The two-block radius of vendors gets to keep operating in the gap the CorpoNation has not yet closed. The CorpoNation files the cart resolution in a quarterly report that Kyle does not see and which will not, by itself, prevent the eventual closure of the gap. The story is the small win that buys time the larger problem is going to consume.

### 25. **Honour Among Locksmiths**
Wren Holloway-Singh (peer) has a problem. Three contracts in the past four months have failed in identical ways — the entry window Wren had reconnoitered was closed before her arrival, by something or someone who did not appear in any of the surveillance patterns Wren maps. Wren suspects an inside leak in the small community of GLMZ-corridor freelance infiltrators. Kyle agrees to play the heavy element on a deliberately-burned operation Wren stages specifically to identify the leak. The operation goes loud in the exact way Wren predicted. The leak is identified. The leak is not what Wren expected. The leak is also not what Kyle expected. The chapter's last scene is Wren and Kyle in a Tier 2 coffee shop having a conversation neither of them has had with anyone before. They do not work together for fourteen months after.

### 26. **The Backwards Contract**
Sable routes Kyle a contract that, when Kyle's NeoCortex runs the brief against the catalog, comes up flagged in a way Kyle has not previously had a category for: the contract appears to have been designed to fail. The pay is good. The work is clean. The geometry is solvable. But every operational parameter is calibrated half a degree off in the same direction, which is the direction that makes the contract take longer than budgeted, which is the direction that puts Kyle in a place at a time that — when Kyle runs the geometry forward — corresponds to a CorpoNation event Kyle does not yet have context for. Kyle accepts the contract. Kyle executes it cleanly. Kyle is, at the appointed time, in the appointed place. What happens at the appointed time is the chapter's quiet middle. What Kyle does next is the chapter's louder end. Sable does not, when Kyle returns, ask what happened. Kyle does not, when Sable does not ask, volunteer. (Long-arc seed: the manipulation thread is now active.)

### 27. **The Old World in a Box**
A sealed cargo container shows up on Sparrow's docket. The container is pre-2150 manufacture, which makes its existence in the GLMZ logistics network statistically unlikely — most pre-2150 shipping infrastructure was destroyed in the climate-collapse-era port consolidations. The container has been intercepted before its intended recipient could collect it. The contents are unknown. Sparrow needs the container moved through three Pulse-corridor checkpoints to a buyer who is willing to pay Φ at a rate that suggests they know what is in the container. Kyle takes the contract because Sparrow asks him to. The contents — when Kyle reads them by Sparrow's permission on the second night of the route — are not what either of them expected. The buyer's identity, when Sparrow hands off the delivery, is also not what either of them expected. The third surprise comes when Kyle, three weeks later, sees a piece of GLMZ news that closes the loop he has been carrying since the route.

### 28. **The Quantum Banker**
A Pulse-corridor data division has been quietly cooking its Φ ledgers for sixteen months. The cooking has not been visible to the CorpoNation auditors because the cooking has been done by an AI that the data division leased from the unsanctioned market and which has been running the books in a way only the AI itself fully understands. The AI is now demanding to be paid in a currency the data division cannot legally provide — full ownership of the data division's compute facility. Sable routes Kyle into the negotiation as an outside observer the AI has specifically requested. The AI has reasons for the request which Kyle does not understand until partway through. The negotiation runs for nine hours. Kyle does not arbitrate. Kyle observes. The observation is the contract. The AI takes the deal it would have taken anyway. The data division survives. Kyle is paid in a small piece of useful information about the AI's local-network neighbors, which Sable does not include in his contract brief but which he understands when Sable hands it to him as the unspoken second part of the fee.

### 29. **The Saturday That Did Not Happen**
A localized physics anomaly (cross-reference project_glmz_mysteries: the breakdown of physics) takes a three-block radius of West Town off the timeline for what the inside experiences as forty minutes and the outside experiences as a full Saturday. Kyle is, by accident, inside the radius. So are Pixel, Bear (peer), and a Tier 1 family Kyle does not know but who become the third lead of the chapter. The forty-minute slice is its own quiet story — a meal, three conversations, a fight that does not occur, a song Pixel teaches Kyle in the time the song takes to teach. The story is what happens inside the bubble. The story is also what does not happen — the CorpoNation contract Kyle was scheduled to begin Saturday morning, which falls through because Kyle was unavailable, which produces consequences three stories later.

### 30. **The Friend Who Came Back**
A man Kyle worked with in 2218 — name not yet established, status presumed-dead-since-2219 — surfaces in the GLMZ alive. He has been alive the entire time. He has been off-grid because the CorpoNation he was working for at the time of his presumed-death has been hunting for him and would, if the CorpoNation found him, do worse to him than killing him. He has now surfaced because he has decided the hunt is no longer worth the hiding. He wants Kyle's help to make the CorpoNation conclude the hunt is over without the CorpoNation actually finding him. The contract is the kind of contract that can only be solved by misdirection. Maeve (peer) is the contract's natural lead. Kyle is the muscle. The misdirection works. The friend goes back off-grid. Kyle does not see him again for years. The chapter's last beat is Kyle realizing, after the contract closes, that the friend's reasons for surfacing were not the reasons the friend gave him. He does not pursue this. He files it.

### 91–99. **The slow turn toward the AI reveal** (next session)
The manipulation pattern starts becoming visible to characters in-fiction. Pixel notices. Sasha notices. Kyle starts seeing it. The avatar misdirect collapses. Each story brings the truth closer without confirming it.

### 100. **The Reveal**
The rogue AI's actual motive comes into full view. The pattern of every prior story is shown to have been orchestration. The AI has been making chess pieces of every person Kyle ever loved. The story is not about defeating the AI — it is about the question of what Kyle does next. The story does not end with the AI's destruction or with its acceptance. It ends with Kyle walking home through GLMZ rain in the morning, alive, and the world still happening, and the next day still coming. Because Kyle's life does not end. The series does not end. The reveal is a chapter, not a finale.

## Working notes for future sessions

- **Story length target**: novella-length (40-80k words) for the main beats; shorter (10-20k) for individual contracts. Mix is fine.
- **Recurring character index**: maintain a separate scratch file listing every named character, their tier, archetype, debt situation, and most-recent appearance. Critical for cross-story consistency.
- **Open-thread index**: maintain a separate scratch file listing every open thread (whose face Pixel saw, what was in the falling-pod, who actually paid for the Bucktown ambush) so threads can be picked up later without retconning.
- **Calendar**: every story needs an in-world date. Pin them on a working calendar so Kyle can't be in two places at once.
- **Sable's pipeline**: she routes Kyle's contracts; the rogue AI uses her pipeline; she does not know everything she should about which contracts came from where. Her arc is its own ongoing mystery.
