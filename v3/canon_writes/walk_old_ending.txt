She reached into the tan coat — the coat that had been with her in the rain for eleven minutes and had not soaked through, which told him more about the coat's cost — and produced a key. Brass-gasketed. The gasket was the tell: standard keys didn't need that seal. She turned it in the lock with the practiced efficiency of a person who has performed this exact motion in the dark, and the door opened on hinges that had been recently oiled, which was another kind of answer to a question he hadn't asked yet.

She stepped in. The dark inside was different from the dark outside — denser, absolute, the rain suddenly absent as a sound. She held the door. Kyle read the held door the way he read everything: *she needs you inside, she needs you inside willingly, she needs you to make the choice yourself.* He stepped through. The door closed behind him and the hum in his chest, the low electromagnetic signature that lived in the implant's background like a second heartbeat, went silent. He had not known it was there until it stopped.
