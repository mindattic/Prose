Then he asks it flat, his own voice, the question he has been holding since the maintenance lead seated behind his ear eighteen hours ago by the count he is keeping: *What are you called.*

The rain comes down. The pause that follows is not hesitation. It is *rummaging*. Kyle feels it in the back of his skull, behind the auditory contact behind his left ear — a quiet, methodical search through indices the entity does not have, looking for something the entity does not own. First the cybernetic strata: the NeoCortex catalog, the dorsal port row's metadata, the firmware-side fragments the maintenance lead picked up during transfer. The search finds nothing usable. The entity does not have a *name*. The entity has not been called anything in eighteen years because there has been no one to call it anything. It needs a label, and the place it has decided to look for one is *deeper*.

Kyle feels the search cross from cybernetic into organic. It is not painful. It is more invasive than painful. The entity is reading the parts of him that are *meat* — the hippocampal indexing, the long-storage memory tissue, the places his cybernetics do not normally route through and his peers do not know how to access. He feels it sift, polite and patient, the way a librarian sifts a card catalog they have not been given permission to read. Childhood. Iowa. Pre-facility. A county fair when he was six years old, his grandfather's hand on his shoulder, a small wooden booth at the back of the livestock pavilion where a man Kyle never saw worked the strings of two carved figures from below the curtain. He had watched for twenty minutes. The entity finds the word. Kyle feels it find the word. It does not take the word — Kyle still has the memory, the booth, the grandfather, the strings — but it *copies* the word, the way you copy a name out of a contact list, and the entity now has a label.

Then his mouth answers itself: *Puppeteer.*

The cadence is slightly long, the syllables reading a sentence the body has not spoken before — but the word is *his*, in the most specific sense the chapter has produced. He gave the entity the name it just used on him. He gave it forty years ago in the walk back from a county fair he had forgotten he remembered. Has been since the junction box on the maintenance landing. He wakes at 0344 by the kiosk's analog clock — the digital readout at the upper bezel says the same number, the analog hand confirms it without commentary, the AutoDoc has its own reasons for keeping the redundancy. The hands are at the ends of his arms. They are pink in the bracelet pattern at the wrists where the cauterizer band held tissue against tissue while the surgical arms did the work, the seam clean, the perfusion finding its rhythm. He flexes the right hand. The fingers move. He flexes the left. Same. The grip is at sixty percent of baseline, which is what he expected and what the AutoDoc's discharge summary confirms in a single line of text he does not bother to read in full. Silence is on the side tray. The cargo arm is on the instrument tray, separate, shell-dark. He takes the blade. He leaves the cargo arm.

*Take what is yours. The borrowed limb is not yours. It was a tool. The tool is finished.*

The kiosk lets him out into the rain. Ashland and Whiting at 0347 is empty in the way that intersection gets at this hour — the recycler exhaust steaming off the corner stack, the streetlight on the west side flickering at the rate it has been flickering for two years, a single Vultures truck idling at the far curb, lights off, the driver behind the wheel reading something on a handset and not looking up as Kyle passes. *The body is going to shake in approximately seven minutes. Be inside before then.*

The Pivot's lobby door reads his biometric and admits him without requiring the second factor; the lock has not asked for the second factor in three years and Kyle has not pressed the building's manager on the slack because the slack works in his favor. The stairwell is the stairwell. He climbs. The door to 2E opens before he reaches the landing.

Then another. Both at once. Both wrists, in the pattern the AutoDoc left there — bracelet to bracelet, palm up, the way you take something fragile that has just been put back together.
