The next evening after the long night in. The noodles are getting cold. Kyle is at Mrs. Chen's, two bowls in, watching a woman pretend not to watch him — hooded, face hidden, posture composed, the kind of stillness that costs effort. After a long beat she tilts her head *come* and walks him three blocks east-northeast through GLMZ rain. The towers lean over the street the way they do this far down. VTOL traffic glitters overhead between the corporate plates, somewhere distant and elsewhere. The walk ends in front of a building that looks abandoned and isn't. She turns, finally, and pulls the hood back. *Sable.* The fixer he has known as a voice through a cracked terminal for two months, face-to-face for the first time. They look at each other. She says she has something to show him, but not here. A back room two blocks further, Faraday-shielded, tomorrow at the same time. She wants his answer before she leaves the street, because once they walk into the cage there is no easy way out of the conversation. Kyle says yes. She nods, steps back into the rain, and is gone in the way professionals leave a meeting they came to with a fallback plan. Kyle walks the long way back, stops at his bike, takes the back roads home, fries something in old oil at his stove, eats it standing up. When his step hits the landing across from 2F, Pixel's door opens. She does not have her forceps tonight. She is just there, in the doorway, holding a cup of something hot in each hand like she has been listening for him. *You're back early.* He takes the cup. They stand in their doorways across the hall and drink. Neither asks. He does not tell her about the woman in the hood. Some nights are for the cup of tea and the open door and the not-asking, and tonight is that kind of night.
