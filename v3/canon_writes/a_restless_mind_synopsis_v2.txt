The next evening after the long night in. The noodles are getting cold. Kyle is at Mrs. Chen's, two bowls in, watching a woman pretend not to watch him — hooded, face hidden, posture composed, the kind of stillness that costs effort. After a long beat she tilts her head *come* and walks him three blocks east-northeast through GLMZ rain. The towers lean over the street the way they do this far down. VTOL traffic glitters overhead between the corporate plates, somewhere distant and elsewhere. The walk ends in front of a building that looks abandoned and isn't. She does not unlock the door. Hood stays on. She tells him only what she needs to tell him to make him show up tomorrow: this address, this time, the room behind this door, shielded, bring nothing electronic he can leave behind. He has no idea who she is. He says yes anyway. She is gone in three steps and the Gray Zone's rain takes the tan coat. Kyle walks back to his bike, rides the long way home through tier crossings he does not strictly need to make, because the long way home is where he does most of his thinking. When his step hits the landing across from 2F, Pixel's door opens. She does not have her forceps tonight. She is just there, in the doorway, holding a cup of something hot in each hand like she has been listening for him. She does not ask. He does not tell. They stand in their doorways across the hall and drink. Some nights are for the cup of tea and the open door and the not-asking, and tonight is that kind of night. He retires to 2F and sits on the edge of the bed in the dark before he sleeps, because that is what the discipline asks for after evenings in which he has said yes to things he cannot yet name.
