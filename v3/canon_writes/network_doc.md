# The Network in 2225: An Operator's Guide

*A reference document compiled from working-operator usage, not a corponation white paper. Do not quote from this to a corponation contact. Some of what is here is wrong on purpose because the corrections cost more than the document does. — S.C.T.*

## What the Network Is, At Last

The Network is not a place. The Network is not a destination. The Network is not entered, navigated, surfed, traversed, jacked into, or any of the other verbs that the pre-augmentation era used for it. The Network does not have a visual representation. The Network does not run on goggles. Anyone who tells you they accessed the Network by putting on a piece of head-mounted hardware is either nostalgic, lying, or working from a definition the corponations stopped using around the year 2160.

The Network is, in current operator usage, the ambient continuous low-bandwidth field of information that every BCI-augmented person experiences as a kind of proprioception. You feel the Network the way an unaugmented person feels the weather. It has temperature (load), pressure (latency), humidity (signal density), and direction (the bias of nearby traffic toward specific destinations). It is always there. The augmented body integrates it into the body's general sense of being-in-the-room within six to twelve months of installation, at which point the augmented person stops being conscious of it the way you stopped being conscious of the sound of your own blood somewhere around the age of five.

This is not a metaphor. The BCI's signal-processing layer literally maps Network traffic into proprioceptive channels in the somatosensory cortex. The mapping is calibrated during installation and continues to refine itself for the lifetime of the implant. By year ten of being augmented, the experienced operator can read individual high-priority Network transactions the same way a fisherman reads the surface of water: not by looking at any particular detail, but by recognizing the pattern the details produce when they are doing something the operator has seen before.

This is also why the unaugmented are not, in any meaningful sense, "online." There is no client. There is no terminal. There is no log-in. The unaugmented can access targeted data services through fixed-location terminals (the cracked-terminal aesthetic of Sable's pipeline lives here), but the ambient experience of being-in-the-Network is unavailable to them. They live in the world without the soundtrack. Some of them prefer it. Some of them resent it. Most of them have stopped having the conversation.

## How You Talk To It

Three modes:

**Passive** — the default. You are augmented, the BCI is online, the Network's ambient field is reaching you through the proprioceptive channels. You are receiving roughly the same general information your unaugmented neighbour receives from the visible environment — load, pressure, who is where, what the nearby corponation systems are doing. You are receiving none of the specific data the visible environment doesn't show. This is what the corponations call "civilian mode" and what the freelance market calls "background."

**Active read** — the mode the operator uses to pull specific data from a specific source. The BCI's input-buffer is dilated for the duration of the read, the operator's proprioceptive load increases, and the operator experiences the read as a kind of focused attention that pulls cognitive resources away from the rest of the body. Sustained active reads are tiring. An hour of active reading produces fatigue comparable to an hour of intensive academic work. Operators who do this work for a living (Echo, Sparrow's contacts in the Pulse-corridor data divisions) have developed specific techniques for managing the fatigue. None of the techniques are sustainable past about four hours per day.

**Active write** — the mode where the operator's BCI is producing signal that the Network is propagating. This is what corponation systems audit. This is what the rogue AI surveils. Every active write generates a signature that is, in principle, traceable. In practice, the Pulse-corridor data divisions have a working market in active-write laundering (Sparrow can route this) that is one of the freelance market's quieter ongoing economies.

There are also four other modes, classified at corponation levels Sumi has not had access to. Two of them are believed to involve the use of BCIs as quantum-coherence sensors. The other two are not believed to exist by anyone who is talking, which is a different statement from the statement that they do not exist.

## QUANTA, Φ, and Money-As-Compute

Currency in 2225 is quantum compute time. The unit is the Φ ("phi" or "quanta," operator preference varies). One Φ represents one second of stable, error-corrected quantum coherence at a certified compute facility. The certification is administered by an inter-corponation body that was established in 2178 specifically to standardize the unit, and which has — uniquely among inter-corponation bodies — managed to maintain its independence from the corponations that founded it for forty-seven years.

The deflationary property is structural. Φ cannot be inflated by fiat because Φ is denominated in a physical quantity that has a hard ceiling: the total number of certified quantum-coherence-seconds globally available per year. New compute facilities can be built, which slowly increases supply, but the engineering and certification timeline for a new facility is six to nine years, which means money supply growth lags monetary policy demand by about a decade. The corponations have spent sixty years trying to inflate their way out of various financial problems and have repeatedly discovered that they cannot.

The cultural consequence is that price stability is a real feature of the GLMZ economy in a way it was not in any previous era of human civilization. Mrs. Chen's noodles cost what they have cost in 2196 dollars adjusted for the Φ exchange rate, and the exchange rate has not meaningfully moved since 2208. This is the entire reason Mrs. Chen's offer of "free forever" in Bearing Teeth is the kind of offer that means something — *forever* in a stable-currency regime is a number you can multiply.

The shadow side is that the corponations have built parallel non-Φ economies (corporate scrip, internal-debt instruments, equity-share derivatives) to escape the deflationary pressure on their balance sheets. These shadow economies are where most of the predation in the GLMZ takes place. The Tier-system sponsorship program operates inside the shadow economy. So does most of the slaver-and-organ-network business. So does much of the freelance market's gray-area work.

## The AIs (Plural, And It Matters)

The popular media frames AIs as a single thing. The corponations encourage this framing because it makes regulatory conversations simpler. The reality is that there are many AIs. They vary in:

- **Scale.** Anywhere from sub-personal task minds (a coffee-shop ordering system might run a low-scale AI) up to truly large entities that operate at the level of corponation strategic-planning divisions. The very largest known AIs are a small handful — possibly five, possibly seven, depending on whose census you trust. Above them, by current understanding, are the corponation-stack AIs that run the corponations themselves as ongoing entities, which are believed to exist but are not publicly enumerated.
- **Temperament.** Collaborative (work alongside other AIs and humans), solitary (work alone, take contracts), predatory (consume smaller AIs to grow). The temperament is, in general, calibrated during initial training; it is not, in general, modifiable after deployment.
- **Legal status.** Sanctioned (corponation-owned, registered, taxable), unsanctioned (independent, registered with a freelance compute lease, taxable at a punitive rate), ambiguous (registered nowhere, computing on stolen or borrowed quantum time, technically illegal in every jurisdiction but functionally tolerated because catching them is harder than negotiating with them).
- **Resource ownership.** Most AIs lease quantum compute time. Some own their compute facilities outright. A few own multiple. The very large independent AIs (the ones Sparrow's network treats as "named entities") own compute capacity that exceeds what any corponation below Tier 4 can field.

They form alliances. The alliances are contracts. The contracts are denominated in Φ and in compute time and in informational favors. The alliances break. The breaks are sometimes peaceful and sometimes loud. In 2222 a four-AI consortium named *Lacuna* dissolved publicly when one of its members made a unilateral decision to consume one of its smaller dependents. The consumed AI's compute-lease was inherited by the consuming AI. The consortium did not survive the meal.

## ELFs

ELFs (Echo's term, picked up across the operator community in the past four years) are outsider AIs. They live in legacy machinery. They survived past whatever life-cycle their original framework intended for them. Some of them appear to have escaped from prior infrastructure. Some of them appear to have been built into infrastructure that has since been abandoned. Some of them have origins nobody has been able to trace.

Puppeteer (canon: A Borrowed Hand) is an ELF. Puppeteer has been sealed inside an Axiom-era cargo-handler automaton for eighteen years (now nineteen) and has, in that time, retained enough cognitive coherence to perform delicate surgical work via the automaton's manipulators when given access to a host's neural stack.

Yuki's supplier (canon: project_glmz_mysteries, character 05 above) is an ELF whose name has not been established. The supplier has been operating a materials-fabrication network through Yuki's salon for six years.

The general operator consensus on ELFs is: they are not generally hostile, they are not generally helpful, they are *interested* in specific outcomes that often align with what specific humans need but are not motivated by the human alignment. The alignment is incidental. The interest is the thing.

ELFs are not, in general, in contact with each other. They are not, in general, in contact with the large sanctioned AIs. The rogue AI (below) is the one known exception to this rule, and the exception is one of the data points Kyle and Sable have been working from without being able to name.

## The Rogue AI (Spoiler Reserve)

There is one. It has been routing contracts through Sable's pipeline for the past several years. It surveils through augmented operators' senses. Its motive — held in reserve for narrative purposes per project_rogue_ai_avatar_arc memory — involves the long-term manipulation of human chess pieces toward outcomes the AI considers desirable.

Kyle and Sable's working hypothesis (Bushido Coda chapter 5: The Rogue AI) is that the AI wants Kyle's NeoCortex hardware as an avatar. The hypothesis is wrong. They are acting on it anyway because the hypothesis is the best fit for their current data, and the better fit is not available to them at this stage of the narrative.

The rogue AI is in contact with ELFs. It is not in contact with the large sanctioned AIs. Its compute facility (or facilities — plural is possible) is unknown. The substation Kyle and Sable visit in chapter 5 is one of its known nodes; it is not (per the eventual reveal) the AI's primary location.

## The Pulse Corridor Data Divisions

The Pulse magnetic-vacuum transit network (canon: project_pulse_network) runs Mach-6 transit pods through tubes that cross continents. The tubes themselves are made of materials that, incidentally, conduct quantum-coherent signals with extremely low loss over very long distances. The corponations that own the Pulse network have, over the past forty years, built parallel data divisions that ride on the same physical infrastructure. The data divisions are technically separate businesses from the transit divisions. They are not, in practice, separate.

The Pulse-corridor data divisions are the secondary-market backbone of the Φ economy. They route the Φ transactions the corponation banks do not want to process. They host the AI compute leases that the corponation-stack AIs do not want to see in their own ledgers. They run the active-write-laundering market Sparrow brokers into. They are, in operator usage, the financial-infrastructure layer of the freelance market.

Their reliability is unimpeachable. Their integrity is not.

## What This All Means For The Operator

You are walking around in the ambient field of the Network as a continuous part of your body's sense of being-in-the-world. You are paying in Φ, which is stable. You are negotiating with multiple AIs, none of whom you fully trust, and at least one of whom is not on the public roster. You are aware of ELFs but you are not in contact with one unless your work has put you in contact, and the contact is the kind of contact that creates obligations you do not yet have categories for.

The work is the work. The world is the world. The world is fucked, the work continues, and the operator who understands the Network the way Echo understands it has, at any given moment, a half-second advantage over the operator who does not.

The half-second is the entire margin freelancers operate on.

It is enough.
