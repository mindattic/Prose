The brass gasket seated with a sound like a breath held and then released. Kyle stood in the dark and let the silence locate itself in his body. It was in his sternum. Below his sternum. A place he had stopped attending to the way you stop attending to the sound of your own blood moving — it was always there until it was not, and now it was not, and the absence had a shape. Eleven years. He had been sixteen when they finished the installation and he had not been quiet inside since.

*So. That.*

He found the equipment rack by feel — the old kind, bolted to the floor, steel, the bolts themselves worn smooth from decades of hands checking them. He sat on its edge. He did not reach for the katana. He sat with his hands on his knees and waited for whatever came next.

The hood came down.

Red hair. Loose braid, one strand against her cheekbone that had never stayed. The ocular implants — black, machined, the aperture rings that made a small sound when she shifted focus, the sound he had stopped noticing through two months of cracked-terminal audio and had just now, in this room, in this silence, identified as *her* — adjusted once as she looked at him. He had talked to this voice every Tuesday and Friday for two months. He had built a contract file, a behavioral profile, a trust index. He had built two centimeters of a face under streetlight, and the rest arrived now and fit itself over the partial like a key into a lock she had already shown him. He did not move. The cage held.

She watched him have it. The implants did not adjust again. Then: "If it is not looking through your eyes, it is interpreting your senses." The voice was the same. Level, unhurried, the voice that had given him eleven operational briefings in the two months since she picked him up. "Either way — the moment you recognize me, it knows we are meeting. So I needed you to read me as a stranger." A pause that was not hesitation. "I needed you to choose to follow someone you did not know." Kyle looked at the floor. The concrete was old, marked with the ghost-outlines of equipment that had stood here for decades and been removed. He thought about eleven years of the harmonic he had catalogued as ambient, as background, as *self.* He thought about what it meant that it was not self. He thought about whether it had been keeping him running or simply watching him run, and understood that he did not know, and understood further that this was a thing he should have asked a long time ago and had not asked because the asking had its own kind of answer. "I do not like," Sable said, "being some non-human's puppet." Flat. The way she said *the risk premium is already factored in.* A thing stated, not performed. She let it sit. Then: "I can triangulate its physical position. Three locations. Three readings of surveillance traffic against your implants. I have placed the nodes. I need you outside this room behaving exactly as you behave." Kyle looked up. The ocular implants were still. The aperture locked, which he now understood meant she had moved into a register where every word cost something. "I need you," she said, "to not change."

He considered what it meant to agree. The hum had been there for eleven years and he had not known it was there, which meant it had been with him in every contract, every room, every choice he had believed was his. Agreeing to help locate the thing was not a neutral act. He knew that. He sat with it for three seconds, which was longer than he usually needed, which was its own kind of answer. "All right," he said.
