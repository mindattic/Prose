==BEAT 1==
TITLE: The Brief
TEXT:
Hua handed him the contract in the back of a kitchen Kyle had not previously known belonged to her. The kitchen was on the second floor of a noodle-and-soup operation in the corner of a Tier 3 corridor four blocks from Mrs. Chen's stall, which was a piece of geography Kyle filed without commenting on, because the geography meant that Hua had been running operations within shouting distance of Mrs. Chen for at least the four years Kyle had been eating at Mrs. Chen's, and Mrs. Chen had never mentioned this, which meant either Mrs. Chen did not know, which Kyle did not believe, or Mrs. Chen had decided Kyle did not need to know, which Kyle also did not entirely believe, or there was a third explanation, which Kyle had not yet identified.

The kitchen was hot. The wok burner under the right unit was running. The smell — pork bone and fermented bean and chili oil — was close enough to the smell that came out of Mrs. Chen's kitchen that Kyle catalogued the closeness as deliberate. Hua sat across the steel prep table from him with a small folder open between them. She did not introduce the operation. She had introduced herself, six months earlier, through a back-channel that had eventually become formal, and the formality had taken the form of three contracts she had routed to him over the intervening months and which had paid cleanly and which had been clean contracts. This was the fourth. This was the one she had handed to him personally.

"Extraction," she said. "Atlas-program continuation lab. Sealed cargo. Out."

He read the brief. The brief was eight lines on a piece of paper. The brief did not name the cargo. The brief named the facility — a sub-tier complex in the northern outskirts that the CorpoNation registry listed as a *long-term storage* facility and which Kyle's preliminary read flagged as something with a higher security envelope than long-term-storage facilities generally carried. The brief named the delivery method. The delivery method was the part Kyle read twice.

"False Death Protocol," he said. Not a question.

"You are the only freelancer on the GLMZ corridor whose hardware can run the protocol. Their intake reads dead bodies. Their intake does not read carefully. The protocol gets you inside the facility through the morgue intake. You wake up. You find the cargo. You walk out."

"And the cargo."

"You will know what to do with the cargo when you see it." Hua said it level. He read her face. The face read level back. "I am not telling you what to do with it because telling you what to do with it would frame the decision in advance, and the decision is yours."

Kyle set the brief on the steel prep table between them. He looked at the smell. The smell was Mrs. Chen's smell, four blocks away and one floor up, in a kitchen Hua had been operating out of for years and which Mrs. Chen had not mentioned. He filed the smell. He filed the not-mentioning. He filed the eight-line brief and the False Death Protocol and the cargo whose decision was his.

"Standard rate."

"Plus a Vultures consult fee for the delivery." She closed the folder. "Stash will run the transport. You will be on his slab inside three hours of accepting the contract."

He looked at her.

He had been refusing this kind of contract for nineteen years. He had been refusing it because the protocol's costs — surrendering full body control, surrendering the discipline, surrendering the array's calibration to the protocol's hibernation routines for an unknown duration — were costs his discipline did not have a category for paying. He had refused it from Sable's pipeline twice. He was now being asked to accept it from Hua, who had been routing clean contracts for six months, and the contract was the kind of contract that justified the cost in the way no previous contract had.

He thought about the Atlas program. He thought about who at the facility's continuation lab would have been working in 2218 when Kyle's intake class had been processed and which of those people Kyle had not yet been able to find a route to. He thought about the cargo.

"I accept."

==BEAT 2==
TITLE: Stash's Slab
TEXT:
The Vultures van that came to pick him up was the standard Vultures rig — matte black, the patch on the driver's door, the cargo bay set up for stretcher work. Stash himself drove. The two technicians in the back were people Kyle had not previously met and whose names Stash did not give him, because the protocol the False Death Protocol depended on required Kyle's intake into the facility's morgue to read as a routine pickup with a standard crew, and standard crews did not introduce themselves to corpses.

Kyle changed in the back of the van. The clothes he had ridden over in went into a sealed bag. The clothes he changed into were the kind the Vultures pickup-bodies wore, which was no clothes at all — the morgue intake protocols stripped the bodies for processing and the Vultures had standardized on delivering bodies pre-stripped to save the intake nurses a step. He had brought Silence and Chorus in a separate sealed case that Stash placed in the rig's lockbox under the floor of the cargo bay. The case would arrive at the morgue intake but would not be processed with the body. The case would, by the operational plan Hua had laid out, be retrievable from the facility's external supply intake on the secondary timeline.

Stash did the IV insertion himself. The IV carried the protocol's hibernation cocktail — a calibrated mix that, when run through Kyle's NeoCortex's intake-recognition subroutine, produced the signature the facility's morgue intake was looking for. The protocol did not put Kyle into hibernation in the way the CorpoNation medical literature defined hibernation. It put Kyle into a state the NeoCortex had been built to enter on command — a complete partition between the conscious cortex and the body's surface-level autonomic functions, with the autonomic functions calibrated to register as *recently deceased* on every standard scan.

Stash's hand on his shoulder before he started the drip was the only gentleness Kyle was going to get for the next several hours. It was not, in Stash's framing, gentleness. It was operational reassurance. Stash had not delivered a False Death body in eleven months and he was going to be careful, and he wanted Kyle to know it. Kyle knew it. Kyle had filed Stash's eleven-month gap before he got in the van.

"Thirty seconds, then you go under," Stash said.

"Understood."

Kyle closed his eyes. The hum in his chest — the ambient Network field he had been carrying since the cage, six hours and counting — partitioned along with the rest of his autonomic functions. The partitioning was not silence; it was a different kind of presence, the same way *asleep* was a different kind of presence from *awake* rather than an absence of presence. Kyle had been under the protocol once before, in 2222, in conditions the Atlas-program engineers who built the protocol had not anticipated and which had taught him that the protocol's worst case was the case where the operator going under was a person and the operator coming up was, in some small but discoverable way, not entirely the same person.

The drip started. The thirty seconds did what they did.

==BEAT 3==
TITLE: Waking on the Slab
TEXT:
He came up on a slab in a room he had not previously seen and which his interior catalog began assembling within the first two seconds of consciousness — temperature seventeen Celsius, humidity controlled, the specific cold-storage smell of a Tier 4 CorpoNation morgue intake, the LED overhead at the angle that meant the room had been retrofitted from a different use within the past decade. The body's autonomic functions were resuming. The NeoCortex was bringing the conscious cortex back online in the calibrated sequence the Atlas engineers had designed for exactly this transition.

He did not move. The Atlas engineers had also been clear, in the protocol's documentation Kyle had read once nineteen years ago and which he had retained in the way he retained things he did not want to need: do not move until the conscious cortex has come fully back online. Movement before full re-integration produced confusion the body could not resolve, and confusion at the morgue stage of the protocol was the difference between a successful extraction and a failed one.

He took inventory while the cortex resumed. The lights were standard intake fluorescents. The door was to his left. Two other slabs in the room were occupied; one body was older than Kyle's and one was younger, and both were genuinely deceased rather than running protocols. He did not catalog this with feeling. He catalogued it the way the discipline asked for. The room was quiet. The room had not yet been visited by an intake nurse since his arrival, which the operational plan had budgeted between four and forty minutes for, and which his interior clock estimated had been about eleven.

His clothes were not on the slab. His clothes had been bagged and labeled at intake and were now in the facility's storage. Silence and Chorus were, per plan, accessible via the external supply intake on the secondary timeline. He had between four hours and twelve hours to find the cargo and execute the extraction and get to the supply intake and recover the weapons and get out.

The cortex came fully online at the four-minute mark. He sat up.

The cargo was somewhere in this facility. The brief had not named the cargo. He had accepted the contract not knowing what the cargo was. He thought, briefly and without examination, that this might be a contract whose decision was harder than the discipline had budgeted for. He filed the thought. He stood up from the slab. He walked to the door.
