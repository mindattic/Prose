The next night. Sable's address arrives on a single-use pager — three lines, no signature, half-coordinates and half-instruction. Kyle rides over on the bike, parks two blocks out, walks the rest. The Faraday-shielded back room she has booked is in a building that looks abandoned from the street and isn't; the second floor up has the kind of door that's heavier than it looks. Inside the cage — where his chest goes silent for the first time in eleven years — Sable tells him what she could not tell him at the noodle stall. She has been working a fixer's pipeline for two months that was not, strictly, Sable's pipeline. A non-human has been routing Kyle's contracts. She is done being its puppet. She suspects it surveils through Kyle's senses, which is why the cage matters: the moment of recognition is the data the AI cannot be allowed to read. She has worked out a way to triangulate its physical position by walking Kyle through specific places and reading her node against his implants. Three readings. They take three places that night — a market fight over a cart of fish, a Tier 1 block where working-class life proceeds without him, a courtyard where a busker plays under a real chrysanthemum — and at each place Sable reads her node and the city happens around them. By dawn she has the coordinates. She thanks him as a colleague. He rides home alone, the AI's hum back in his chest now that he is out of the cage. Pixel does not open her door this time; she is asleep, and he does not knock.
