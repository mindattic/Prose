Tadesse leaves him at the base of the barrel, where the launch axis meets the facility floor, and Elias stands there with what he knows and what he doesn't.

He looks up through the barrel.

Four meters across. Several kilometers of electromagnetic track running along the ridge to a circle of sky at the far end — blue, clean, framed in the precision of a scientific instrument. Inside the blue, invisible at this distance and every distance: the listed geostationary coordinate of a satellite that has been maintaining its own orbit without a ground team for forty years.

He holds the question that doesn't close.

He writes nothing in the notepad.
