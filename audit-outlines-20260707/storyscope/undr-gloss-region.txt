He thought, as the candle's small light found the basin's rim and lost it again in the vessel's rocking, of the object he had seen there once — the flat thing on a cord, the small pale face with the dark eyes that pulled light in instead of giving it back. He had carried it four years before the Surfacing and twelve since, unexplained, present, never handled. He understood it now, the way a thing becomes plain only after every other possibility is used up: it had not been a stranger's face, and it had not been a memory his mind had shaped to fit a longing. It had been his own — a child's transit-ident, the shine a mother clips to a boy so the surface will always know whose he is, surrendered at four years old to a rowing thing that took what was offered and asked no questions about whether the giving was understood. The Oarsman did not return what it was given. The law did not allow the same kind of object to pay twice, which meant his shine had been resting in that hoard, unchanged, since before he had words for anything, and would go on resting there long after he was done crossing this water himself. He would never hold it again. He did not need to. He understood, now, exactly what a four-year-old had given away to come home, and exactly what it had cost, and that the cost had been paid in full a very long time ago.
