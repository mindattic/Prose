He'd run the form. The form was better than what he'd been taught. That seemed like an answer, though not the kind that closed a file.

The form was better because he'd fixed it in the gap. Saito had built him — made the decision, laid the foundation, drilled three hundred Tuesday repetitions into a hand that would never stop moving — and then the cost of that building arrived slowly, over years, in alleys and jobs and lateral drag on cuts that no archive had predicted. Between the choice and the cost, Kyle had lived. That was where the adjustment happened. That was where everything happened, if he was honest about it: after choices that weren't his and before consequences that hadn't finished arriving, in the gap Saito had named and then inadvertently given him. He'd been moving there his whole life without knowing it had a name. The form was just the clearest proof.

He kept walking.

The city made its particular northside sounds — a freight slug pulling under the street, the low hum of a cooling tower three blocks east, something avian and foolish in the gap between two buildings.
