Kaeric put the paper inside his coat, against his chest, where the cold of it would have to fight through wool before it reached anything.

Voren had already turned to the next stack on the table, as if the Long Seat's chair had already forgotten there'd been anyone standing in front of it.

The lens over the doorway kept humming, unbothered, the way it had hummed through Aeshel's silence and his own.

He walked toward the door because the alternative was staying, and staying meant more weighing of what a name was worth against what it cost to keep clean.

Aeshel fell in beside him without being asked, and Kaeric heard the man's boots on the stone a half-step behind his own, uneven, favoring the leg that had done the running two nights ago.

Neither of them spoke until the door was shut behind them and the corridor's cold took the place of the room's close, waxed air.

The axe between his shoulder blades had ridden there through worse rooms than that one — through Morrint's hall, through the fair's bracket announcements.

Here, walking away from it, it felt like dead iron, a shape on his back that hadn't done a single hour of work today that mattered.

Sennah doesn't know yet, Aeshel said, quiet, not looking at him.

No, Kaeric said.

You going to tell her the ledgers part? The trial that isn't happening?

I'm going to tell her she's free, Kaeric said. The rest of it she can ask me for when she's ready to hear it.

*That's a coward's kindness and I know it. I'm doing it anyway.*

Aeshel didn't answer that. The man had learned the same trick, keeping the worst of a thing folded small enough to carry alone.

The corridor's torches threw their light in long unsteady lines across the flagstones, and somewhere below them a bell rang the change of watch, flat and final.

Kaeric's hand found the paper again through the wool, checking it the way another man might check a wound to see if it was still bleeding.

It wasn't. It had gone still, folded, done.

He walked out into the yard, into air that smelled of coal-smoke and wet stone, and did not look back at the door.
