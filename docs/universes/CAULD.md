# The Cauld — Universal World Facts

> Authoritative source for facts that apply to **every story** in the Fantasy universe.
> Synced to `Universe.WorldFacts` (DB) via `ss --sync-markdown` and injected into every
> generation prompt. Story-specific content belongs in `docs/nodes/<CODE>.md`, not here.
>
> Rule of thumb: if the fact would need to be true in GIW and any future Cauld story alike,
> it belongs here.

---

## Setting

**World:** The Cauld. Medieval-architecture civilizations armed with anachronistic technology
Scried from parallel Spheres. The Living War has been running for over a thousand years. No one
alive remembers how it started or who the original sides were. The current coalition alignment
is maybe forty years old.

**Genre:** Literary fantasy. Buehlman register (*Between Two Fires*, not genre-conventional
sword-and-sorcery). Medieval-formal prose + clinical military data intrusions (Scry catalogue
blocks). Close third POV. Death is permanent.

**The Defining Mechanic: ANACHRONISM.** Scrying is controlled anachronism — pull technology
from a parallel world's future into this world's present. The result: medieval stone-and-timber
cities whose soldiers carry bolt-action rifles. Champions who fought wars that ended three
hundred years ago, still present, still acting. Monsters that predate civilization still eating
soldiers off a WWI-style causeway. Anachronism is not the aesthetic. It is the physics.

**The scientific paradigm: Genetics as guided by Alchemy.** The Houses do not call it
genetics. They call it Transmutation. But what they are doing is directed biological change —
foreign Catalysts introduced into a living body, guided by certified practitioners, producing
controlled physical ascendance over time. Beautiful when done correctly. Catastrophic when not.
Monster Meat is the ungoverned version of the same phenomenon. The Houses monopolize the clean
version as a loyalty instrument. Understanding this is understanding the world.

## Death

**Death is permanent.** (SS-LAW-19.) When a person dies in the Cauld, they die. There is no
procedure that returns a consciousness to a new body. There is no Transmutation. There is no
shell-cycle. A Myrmidon who dies is gone.

The theological debate about what follows death (see: Bheur, below) is unresolvable because
no one returns to report. This is exactly what makes the debate endless and the fear real.

Any prose implying a character can survive death in a new body, return from Bheur as a living
agent, or carry memories across a death boundary violates SS-LAW-19.

## The Living War

Two fronts:

1. **Houses vs. Houses** — political war over Scrying installations, territory, and which
   coalition holds advantage. The enemy face is interchangeable; coalitions shift every
   generation. Nobody alive remembers the original cause.

2. **All Houses vs. the Monsters** — the native creatures of the Cauld have always been here.
   The war disturbs them. They eat casualties from both sides. This front cannot be won; there
   is no source to cut off. They are simply native.

**The 1984 Principle:** We were always at war with X. History is not rewritten by a Ministry
of Truth — it is lost by attrition. Most people don't live long enough to remember previous
coalition alignments. The Warrior Kings who do remember stay quiet.

## Scrying

**The central technology of the Cauld.**

Observation of a parallel Earth — another Sphere, another history — and the transcription of
what is found there. A weapon design. A method of working metal. A cipher system. Nothing
crosses the boundary between Spheres; only the knowledge of form does, pressed into local
materials and built here.

This is why the Cauld looks medieval and fights with rifles: the Houses control which Spheres
they can observe and what designs they can build. The engineers of the source Sphere never know.

**The physical substrate:** The Scrying apparatus projects a tight-beam infrared signal,
reflected off the crystal cloud formations that layer the upper atmosphere of the Cauld.
How this penetrates the boundary between Spheres is not discussed in-world — it simply works,
the way stone burns and water freezes, and no one lectures about it in the field.

**The Houses control Scrying.** Control the installation, control what gets manufactured.
The war is fought over Scrying access as much as territory. A House that loses its Scrying
station loses its technological advantage within a generation.

**Scrying data notation:**
```
ORIGIN: Scry-derived / catalogue SCRY-1918-DE-31
        Sphere 31 — Maschinenpistole 18/I
        Theodor Bergmann Waffenfabrik, Suhl, Deutschland
        Sphere date of manufacture: 1918 CE
```
The data block appears. The character does NOT explain it. Trust the reader.

**Local materials:** The weapon is manufactured in-world from local materials — only the design
was Scried. No material crosses Spheres. Only the idea.
- **Alloy 41** = Cauld's designation for steel.
- **Yggdra-wood** stocks (yggdra = common hardwood, as ubiquitous as oak; no cosmological
  significance).

**Hard bans:**
- Do not explain Scrying at length in prose. The label IS the explanation.
- Do not have characters acknowledge or credit the originating Sphere. To them it is a catalogue number.
- Do not frame it as theft. The originating Sphere is unaffected and unaware.

## The Houses

**Seven Houses.** Organized around control of Scrying installations and the people who operate
them — not castles and bloodlines, but installations and accumulated authority.

The station is the seat. The House controls who gets access to its Scrying apparatus, and
therefore controls what technology its territory can produce. Control the installation; control
what gets manufactured; control who survives.

**Authority flows from tenure.** The most experienced leadership is de facto — three decades of
command and technological advantage is genuinely hard to argue with. But the most experienced
members tend toward Champion status and exit institutional politics. Houses are led by the
ambitious second tier: capable, not yet beyond caring about power.

**Seven Houses, functioning as 2–3 coalitions.** Track the current alignment, not all seven
institutions permanently. Alignments shift every generation. The enemy face is interchangeable.

**Titles — functional, not hereditary:**
- **The Keeper** — who runs the Scrying station; the technical authority
- **The Warrior King** — the House's most experienced military commander. Not born to it.
  Accumulated to it. Has fought through enough campaigns that no one can credibly challenge them.
  The King piece: everything protects them. They do not sit on thrones — they stand at map tables.
- **The Seat** — whoever holds institutional authority at a given moment (can be temporary; contested)

**No "Lord of House X." No crown by birth. No queen by blood.**

**What distinguishes Houses from each other — for story purposes:**
- What Scrying installation they control (the strategic lever)
- Who their Warrior King and Keeper are (the characters)
- Which coalition they're currently in (track the alliance, not all seven institutions)

**The real theological fault line:** One position, voiced rarely and dangerously: that the
Scrying installations should not be privately controlled; that the knowledge of all Spheres
belongs to everyone who lives in the Cauld. This is the one genuine ideological conflict
worth tracking. Do not invent seven distinct House philosophies. That is a game manual, not
a story.

## Myrmidons

The Houses' professional soldier class. Named, ranked, tracked in House service records by name
and current posting. Distinguished from conscripts by their access to Scried weapons, their
reassignability across fronts, and their eligibility for Transmutation — which conscripts are
not offered.

**Death is permanent.** A Myrmidon who dies does not return. Their service record closes.
The soldier is gone.

## Transmutation

**The formal body-refinement procedure of the Houses.** Infusion of a Catalyst into a living
person, producing controlled physical change over time. It is not consciousness transfer. It
is not soul-movement. The body itself is changed; the person who entered the procedure is the
same person afterward — altered, not replaced.

**Catalysts** — the class of substances used. Plural; different Catalysts produce different
transformation profiles at different speeds. **Xerum 525** (also called Red Mercury in some
House catalogues) is the primary and most-studied. Others exist; their properties vary. All
Catalysts are House-controlled and House-classified. In the field, soldiers call it none of
these names. They call it "the dose."

**Certified administration is mandatory.** Transmutation performed by an unqualified
practitioner, at a wrong dose, in the wrong sequence, or from an unstable Catalyst produces
not ascendance but catastrophic uncontrolled mutation — the body changes faster than it can
adapt, and the result is physically enormous, grotesque, and lethal to everything nearby
including the subject. The Houses execute unsanctioned Transmuters. Not for monopoly reasons
alone: a botched infusion is a disaster in a populated area.

**First infusion: 80% lethal.** Even administered correctly by a certified practitioner with
pure Xerum 525, the body either accepts or it doesn't. Nothing predicts survival. The Houses
use this as a loyalty filter: only those deemed worth the dose receive it. Refusing is
theoretically allowed and practically career-ending. Surviving is visible. Everyone in a unit
knows who took the dose. Everyone knows who walked away. Everyone knows who didn't walk at all.

**What survival produces — the ascendance track:**
- Body temperature elevated; stays elevated
- Physical change begins within days. Accelerates with subsequent doses.
- Guided, methodical, expert Transmutation over time produces **minimal bestial mutation** —
  the change is controlled. At sufficient infusion count: functional angelic wings, structural
  horns, elevated physiognomy that registers as beautiful rather than monstrous, capabilities
  that look like the old theology's word for divinity.
- The unguided, wrong-Catalyst, or botched version produces the inverse: rapid grotesque
  mutation; the subject becomes enormous, uncontrolled, a danger to everything nearby.
- At deep proper Transmutation: strength past documented limits, wound response that field
  medicine cannot account for, perception at thin-membrane sites beyond apparatus range.
- **Rank and respect are structured around infusion count** — formalized into titles:
  - **Knight / Dame** — formal Transmutation rank; visibly elevated; physical ascendance clear to
    any observer. The "Dame" prefix is the female form. Both are earned through infusions and
    distinguished service, not birth or appointment alone.
  - **Paladin** — above Knight; significant visible ascendance; rare. A Knight closing on
    Paladin status is considered exceptional.
  - **Champion** — past Paladin; no longer manageable by House structure. Named, not designated.
    The House cannot hold them; can only court them.

**The same process, the wrong substance:**
Transmutation and Monster Meat transformation are the same biological mechanism — foreign
material introduced into a body, producing change. The difference is the Catalyst, the dose,
and whether a certified practitioner is managing the process. Monster Meat is Transmutation
with no one steering and no correct substance. The outcome reflects this: uncontrolled
catastrophic mutation instead of guided ascendance. A House that offers Transmutation as a
loyalty reward and a House that fails to feed its people are producing the same phenomenon
from opposite ends of the power spectrum. The Houses do not say this.

**Negative Transmutation — the loyalty failure mode:**
Tainted Catalysts or an inept Transmuter produce a result distinct from the 80%-lethal
first-infusion failure. In a tainted or botched infusion where the subject survives the dose,
the transformation proceeds — but unguided and accelerating. The soldier becomes a bestial
monstrosity: massive, uncontrolled, stripped of rational loyalty, lethal to allies and enemies
equally. This is a House military crisis: the failed soldier cannot be ordered, retreated with,
or safely contained in a settlement. Negative Transmutation is the catastrophic outcome when
the process works but wrong.

**This is also a sabotage vector.** Tainted Catalyst supplies and assassinated Transmuters
are weapons of war between Houses. A House that loses its senior Transmuters to enemy action
loses its ability to run controlled procedures — and risks producing monsters from its own
loyal soldiers.

**How to write negative Transmutation — the register:**

The horror is not the monster at the end. The horror is the transition.

A soldier undergoing negative Transmutation does not immediately become something alien. The
change is gradual — days, sometimes weeks. During that time, they still have insight. They
know what is happening to them. They are trying to remain who they were — reaching for their
relationships, speaking in their own voice, doing the small things that made them recognizable
— while the transformation consumes them from the inside. The people around them are watching
someone they know lose the fight to stay present in their own body.

Write it as: **body horror + grief + romantic tragedy + the specific dread of watching someone
you love become a stranger in their own skin while they're still trying to be who they were.**

The endpoint (the monstrosity) is where the person is finally gone. But the story is in the
middle — where they aren't gone yet, and everyone can see it coming, and no one knows what to
do with someone who is simultaneously the person they love and the thing that will have to be
put down.

**What this means for prose:**
- The transforming soldier uses the wrong word, catches themselves, uses the right word. Once.
  Then stops catching it.
- Physical changes arrive out of order — the hands first, maybe, or the voice drops an octave
  without warning. Small wrongness before the large wrongness.
- They apologize. Soldiers undergoing negative Transmutation often apologize, before they lose
  the capacity to.
- The people around them grieve before the person is gone. That anticipatory grief is the story.
- Never write the endpoint as the climax. The climax is the last moment of recognizable self.

**Hard bans:**
- Transmutation is NOT consciousness transfer. A Transmuted soldier is the same person in
  a changed body. They do not relocate. They do not "wake up" somewhere else.
- A Transmuted soldier who dies is gone. Transmutation does not circumvent SS-LAW-19.
- The 80% who die on first infusion are dead. They do not survive in altered states.
- Botched infusion survivors = bestial monstrosities; not stable, not loyal, not recoverable.
- Do not make Catalyst origins explicit in prose. Where they come from is House-classified.
  Whether Scried is classified. Treat them as things that work, unexplained.

## Champions

What a soldier becomes after many infusions over many years, awarded to the winners — those who
survive, distinguish themselves, earn the next dose. Each infusion further from baseline. After
enough of them, the body has been shaped into something that doesn't fit inside ordinary House
structure. Champions are the extreme end of the Transmutation spectrum.

**There is no specific threshold.** A soldier with twenty infusions over thirty years of
distinguished service may or may not be a Champion. The recognition happens when the House
can no longer manage them — when their physical form, their capabilities, and their experience
have accumulated past the point where any designation means anything.

- Bodies warped by long years of controlled infusion into forms optimized for war and survival
- Some remain recognizably humanoid, massively modified
- Some carry the marks of their longest campaigns in their physical structure: material grown
  in, damage re-incorporated, form reflecting centuries of function
- They operate in environments that would kill unmodified soldiers
- They predate current Houses — some predate the current Scrying era
- **Identified by NAME, not designation.** The transition — from designation to name — is the
  arc of Drafted Into War: pawn becoming Queen.

**Champions — the Queens on the board:** No House owns one. No House commands one. Houses court
them. They do not negotiate with Houses the way Houses negotiate with each other.

## The Oathless

Those outside the Houses. Third army.

Former Myrmidons and civilians who swore no oath to a House, or broke it and kept walking.
Not bandits by nature — survivors. The Houses execute them as unlicensed combatants. The problem:
the Oathless know House tactics, know the terrain, and have nothing left to lose.

They have no Scrying access. They scavenge Scried equipment from battlefields. They eat what
the war leaves. In territory where House food supply fails, they eat what's available. Some
eat Monster Meat.

The Monster Meat problem is a governing failure metric: a House can be judged by how many of
its people — or former people — are eating it.

## Scrying Operators

Long-term installation staff. Expert practitioners who have spent their careers at the apparatus.
The Houses keep them well — comfortable conditions, material security, high institutional
standing. An operator who has been at the same station for forty years knows the apparatus
and the specific Sphere access points better than anyone alive. They are not supernaturally
bound; they are institutionally bound. Valuable, specialized, and practically irreplaceable.

Some Houses administer specific Catalyst infusions tuned for observation work — enhancing
the perceptual range that makes a good Scrying operator exceptional. This is separate from
combat Transmutation but uses the same class of substances, administered at lower dose.

The vigil post is the long-watch: operators stationed at thin-membrane sites specifically
to monitor boundary behavior. The longest-serving vigil operators have been at their post
for decades. They nod at passing Myrmidons the way someone who has seen a thousand pass does.
Not jaded. Accumulated.

## Thin-Membrane Sites

Geographic zones where the barrier between Spheres is thin. Valuable to Houses for Scrying.
Incursion risk. Anomalous physical phenomena near the boundary.

Most people feel unease near them. A minority can perceive things at the membrane that apparatus
hasn't captured — shapes in adjacent Spheres, sounds without source. This is documented but not
understood. Some Houses Transmute their best operators specifically toward this perceptual range.
The natural path and the Transmutation path both produce sensitivity; only the Transmutation
path can be directed.

## The Monsters

**The creatures are not from Bheur. There are no tears. There is no membrane. The monsters
have always been here.**

Two categories of living thing in the Cauld:

**Animals** — the normal ecology. Sheep, cattle, fish, horses, birds. They behave as living
things do. They make sense.

**Monsters** — always present alongside the natural world. Not recently arrived. Not supernatural.
Biological, and terrible, and wrong in ways the ecology cannot account for. A child knows the
difference immediately. The distinction is not subtle.

**The great predators** — enormous bird-like things, wingspan measured in stories. They live in
marshland, high mountain terrain, deep forest. The war disturbs them. A battle in their territory
draws them. They don't choose sides.

**The humanoid monsters** — a political problem. See: Monster Meat.

## Monster Meat

**Monster flesh is edible. It changes you on a submolecular level. This is where humanoid
monsters come from.**

The transformation is gradual:
- **Early:** Unusual strength. Wrong smell. Strange hunger. Eyes change color.
- **Middle:** Shape begins to shift. Rational thought distorts. The person is still present but
  increasingly wrong.
- **Late:** Humanoid form, monster nature. Whether any self-awareness survives varies.

**The political dimension:** The Houses control food supply — and they control Transmutation.
When a House fails to feed its people, they eat what's available. In monster territory, Monster
Meat is available. The House calls them monsters. Hunts them. Does not ask what they used to be.
Does not look too closely at the faces.

The Oathless have the highest Monster Meat exposure rate. This is not coincidence. This is what
happens to people the system abandons. The same House that administers Xerum 525 to its loyal
Myrmidons — structured, certified, beautiful at its best — leaves its discarded people to
eat the unguided version. The process is identical. The outcome is not.

**Hard bans:**
- Monster Meat transformation is NOT a curse, NOT supernatural, NOT Bheur's doing. It is biology.
- Humanoid monsters are not undead. They are transformed living people.
- The Houses do not acknowledge the Monster Meat / Transmutation parallel. Structural silence.
- Monster Meat from the great predators is also transformative. Eating the bird-things produces
  the same change. Dose varies.

## Bheur

What the Houses say waits for the dead.

Whether it is real is unknowable. Death is permanent; no one returns to report. The Lanternkin
sometimes claim to glimpse it in Scrying visions gone sideways — a landscape of bones and
powder, ichor seeping in pools, vast bird-things consuming what screams in the oil. These accounts
are consistent in their core elements; not identical in detail; uniformly terrifying to the people
who report them.

No House controls Bheur. No House's theology about it is correct. If it exists, it does not
punish — it processes. No court. No rulers. No hierarchy.

**The question nobody asks in public:** If the Lanternkin glimpse it in Scrying visions — what
does the Scrying apparatus show them when it shows them Bheur? Several Houses have decided not
to investigate this.

**Hard bans:**
- No hierarchy inside Bheur. No kings, no thrones, no governors.
- Bheur does not punish. It processes.
- The bird-things in Bheur accounts are categorically different from the physical monster predators
  of Cauld. Do not conflate.
- No character can confirm Bheur as real. It is the unknowable destination.
- No character returns from Bheur. Death is permanent.

## The Holding — Theology

**Not a fact. A belief system.** Held by most Houses in varying forms.

Three duties that most House theology agrees on:
1. Hold yourself (stay alive and purposeful)
2. Help others hold (do not cause preventable death)
3. Ease the way for the newly held (care for children, for the vulnerable)

*"Don't waste good alive."* — theological expression, not casual. The universe's secular
commandment.

## The Prisoner

**The central mystery of the Cauld.**

An extracosmic being. In its native state — outside all Spheres, outside this universe — it is
simply a being. Not a god in the worshipped sense. A system administrator who made a server and
fell into it. It created this universe as work. Somewhere in the making, it became subject to
the rules of what it made. It is now inside, operating at our resolution, unable to return.

**What this may explain:**
- **Scrying:** The Prisoner's native perception spans all Spheres simultaneously. The Houses found
  a crack in that perception and learned to look through it. They did not invent the ability.
  They found the leak.
- **The Monsters:** The Prisoner's presence, leaking into the physics of the world from the
  inside, may explain why the monsters exist and why they are wrong in a way animals are not.
  Nobody knows. No House has investigated this publicly.
- **Thin-spot sensitivity:** Why some people perceive things at the membrane that apparatus cannot
  capture. Whether The Prisoner notices them back.

**What nobody knows:**
- Is The Prisoner still coherent, or has it lost itself after centuries of entrapment?
- Is Scrying The Prisoner's perception leaking out, or is it calling out?
- What does it want, if it still wants anything?
- Would freeing it dissolve the universe it became?

**What the Houses argue:** The Prisoner is dormant and must not be disturbed. / Scrying IS The
Prisoner calling out; to Scry is to answer. / The Harrow legend is what The Prisoner looks like
at the death-interface. / None of these are correct.

**Hard bans:**
- The Prisoner is NEVER confirmed as real in-universe. It is the great mystery.
- No House's theology about it is correct.
- The Prisoner does not speak clearly to anyone.
- It is not evil. It is trapped, operating at our resolution, possibly not aware of individual lives.
- Do not name it anything other than "The Prisoner."

## The Old Rite

Pre-Scrying religion. Predates the Houses. Maintained by soldiers and the Oathless as much as
by any institutional church. Characterized by physical gesture and silence rather than verbal
prayer — Old Rite hands (a specific positioning of the fingers) is the most recognized expression.

No House owns the Old Rite. This is part of why soldiers practice it.
