# The Great Record of the Cauld
## Being a Chronicle of the Seven Houses, Their Champions, Their Wars, and the World They Made

> This document records the history of the Cauld from the First Migration (Year 1) through Year 1700.
> Current story events take place after Year 1700. This history is the substrate all story arcs inherit.
> It is written as the Cauld's own archivists write — not neutral, not complete. The gaps are part of the record.
> Authoritative world facts remain in `docs/universes/CAULD.md`. This document expands the Long Record section.

---

## Preface by the Vierna Archive, Third Revision

What follows is not the truth. It is the record, which is different.

Every House keeps its own chronicle. The chronicle of Fjorheim is written in the language of oaths and service tallies; the chronicle of Lacerta is written in the language of assets and liabilities; the chronicle of Pallor is written in the language of what was watched. When you assemble them, you do not get the truth of what happened. You get seven versions of the truth, each of them internally consistent, each of them protecting something.

The Vierna Archive has assembled this document from all seven chronicle traditions, from the Liturgy's partial records, and from the Oathless oral histories that no House has ever thought worth preserving. We have noted contradictions. We have not resolved them.

Three truths we can guarantee: the Founding Champions existed. The Relic weapons exist. The living war is real.

Everything else is interpretation. You may have it.

— Filed under the seal of the Archive Keeper, Coline d'Archive, Year 1698

## PART I: THE MIGRATION AGE (Year 1 — Year 283)

### The Southlands

Before the Cauld was the Cauld, it was not empty. It is important to say this first. The chronicles of every House manage, in their different ways, to suggest that the seven founding cultures arrived on unoccupied land, that the continent waited for them as a body waits for breath. This is not accurate. The Cauld had people when the migrants came. What became of those people is the oldest gap in the record.

The Southlands are the desert states south of the Cauld's current boundary — an ocean of sand and limestone plateau that had, at the time of the First Migration, been pressing against the agricultural margin for three generations. The climate had shifted. The wells had moved. The families that had maintained the great southern oasis-cities for five hundred years watched the sand advance and made a calculation: go north, through the wild corridor, or remain.

Many remained. The Cauld records only those who left.

### The Three Waves

**The First Wave (Year 1 — Year 87)** was not an exodus. It was the slow bleed of families and small groups who crossed through the desert margin, following the river corridors that ran north through what would become Calyx territory. These were farmers, primarily. They carried seed stock and draft animals. They crossed the sand in three to five generations of slow northward pressure, arriving in the Cauld's eastern plains as strangers who did not know they were entering anything in particular.

The plains were rich. The soil was the deep dark loam of a continent whose rivers had been depositing for millennia. The first-wave migrants settled there and built the agricultural culture that would become House Calyx. They had, at this point, no knowledge of the membrane sites. They built in the plains because the plains could sustain them, and the membrane's first effect on them was simply disappearance: people vanished near the low hills of the eastern plain's interior, and no one yet knew why.

**The Second Wave (Year 88 — Year 193)** came faster, driven by a catastrophic drought that killed the remaining oasis-cities in the Southlands in a single decade. These migrants crossed in larger groups and carried cultural institutions with them — religious traditions, a legal tradition based on written contract, a strong scribal class, and metallurgical techniques developed in the Southlands' volcanic highlands.

The second wave dispersed more widely. Some groups stayed in the eastern plains and merged with first-wave settlements. Others continued west along the river valleys, reaching the central forests and the broad agricultural basin that would become Vierna territory. Others moved north along the coast to the fjord country. The metallurgists, following the volcanic ridge geography they recognized from home, settled in the central forest's western highlands and established the first permanent smelting operations at what would become Atrament territory. The scribal class, predictably, settled wherever the trade routes crossed — in the basin between the forest and the coast, the geographic center of what would become the Cauld.

**The Third Wave (Year 194 — Year 283)** was the last and most diverse. It included groups from both the deep Southlands and from the coastal fringe — people who had moved along the southern coast and arrived by sea at the Cauld's western cliffs and southern peninsula. The cliff-coast arrivals founded what would become Lacerta territory; the southern-peninsula arrivals, with the warmest and most temperate land and the oldest written tradition of the Southlands cultures, settled the limestone ridge country and the inland sea coast that would become Ocipheus.

By Year 283, the seven geographic zones of the Cauld's future Houses were occupied by distinct cultural groups with distinct origins, distinct technologies, and distinct relationships to the membrane sites none of them yet understood.

### The First Contact with the Membrane

The membrane sites made themselves known differently to each culture.

The eastern plains people (proto-Calyx) experienced the membrane primarily underground: crops growing wrong in certain fields, water tasting of minerals that did not match the local geology, cattle found with wrong-colored blood in the low hills. They developed a tradition of "root-readers" — people who could detect membrane activity by physical contact with the soil — before they had any conceptual framework for what they were detecting.

The central forest people (proto-Atrament) found the membrane at the volcanic ridge when their smelting operations moved into the geothermally active ground. Metal behaved differently there. Iron that should have been brittle was not. They attributed this to the god of the forge. They continued using the ground.

The basin scribes (proto-Vierna) encountered the membrane through documents. Records made in the basin center were, at certain times of year, more accurate than they should have been — catching errors the scribe had not noticed, preserving detail the scribe had not intended to write. The scribal tradition noted this and did not explain it. They called the phenomenon "the Truthing Season" and scheduled important records for it.

The southern peninsula people (proto-Ocipheus) were the first to observe the membrane deliberately. Their culture, which had already developed a tradition of systematic observation in the Southlands — weather-watching, star-cataloguing, the recording of seasonal tide behavior — applied the same tools to the sites where people vanished. They built the first observation platform at the site that would become Ocipheus Station in Year 212. It produced no conclusions for forty years.

## PART II: THE SETTLING AGE (Year 283 — Year 512)

### The Seven Founding Champions

Each House knows what its founding champion was. No two Houses agree on exactly what founding means.

---

#### Septimia Vigile, Founder of Ocipheus (Year 279 — Year 397)

The oldest founding legend is the simplest. Septimia was an observer. She was thirty years old when she took the post at what is now Ocipheus Station; she was then a young woman from the coastal scholarly tradition, with no particular credential except willingness. The post involved sitting on the observation platform and recording what she saw. What she saw, for the first three years, was nothing.

This is in the record. Three years of "nothing observed" in her hand, with the date and the weather conditions and the quality of the light, recorded daily with the same care a person uses when they expect the nothing to eventually become something.

In Year 312, the record changes. Not dramatically. One line: *the boundary moved today. Not the air above it. The boundary itself.* The next three years of records show the boundary moving, by increments, in patterns she began to predict. By Year 318 she could tell you what the membrane would do at any given apparatus output and weather combination, to a precision that no one else in the Cauld could approach.

She did not call herself the House's founder. She called herself an observer. When others came to learn from her, she taught them what she had learned. When they asked what the installation was called, she said: Ocipheus Station, the name of the location. When they asked what the House was called, she said: the House is what you make of it. By the time she died at one hundred eighteen — an age no natural person reaches, a length of life that has never been explained — the institution built around her records was the most sophisticated membrane-observing body in the Cauld, and her personal archive filled eleven rooms.

The Memory Eye — *Oculus Memoriae* — was found in the deepest layer of the installation's geological foundation in Year 314, during excavation work Septimia had ordered to find the physical source of the membrane's ground-level distortion. It is a lens, twenty centimeters across, ground from a crystal that no mineralogical survey of the local geology can account for. When you look through it, you see the location as it was at some previous time. Which time is not controlled by the operator. What it shows is what it chooses. This is either a property of the material or a behavior that cannot yet be described as a property. Septimia's record calls it "the site's memory, externalized." She carried it for the remaining eighty years of her life. It has lived in the Ocipheus Station archive since, except for a period of forty years (Year 1088–1128) when it was confiscated by House Atrament as a spoil of the Ridge War and eventually returned under the Treaty of the Three Terms.

#### Zsombor of the Root, Founder of Calyx (Year 281 — Year 378)

The eastern plains people had been dying of a famine for six years when Zsombor of the Root walked into the low hills' worst field and did not come back.

This is, more or less, the whole legend. He was a root-reader — trained in the plains-people's tradition of soil-contact membrane detection. He had told the community's council that the failed fields were not drought failures. The membrane ran under them in a way that was drawing the soil's vital capacity elsewhere. He had no solution. He said he would look for one.

He was gone for twenty-one days. When he came back, the fields were producing. Not abundantly — they were not miracle-fields. But producing, at a rate that would sustain the community through the season. He said: *the membrane goes down here, not through. I followed it down. I found what the soil was missing and told the soil where to find it.*

He could not explain this in greater detail. He carried, when he returned, a war-scythe. The blade was forged from the first iron ore ever taken from the eastern plains — he could not have carried that ore into the hills and worked it in twenty-one days, and the blade was worked with a skill nobody in the community could match. The handle was wrapped in something that was not leather, with a grain and weight that nobody could identify. He said: *it came from down. From where the membrane goes when it goes underground.* No one pressed him further. The famine was ending.

The scythe — *Gyökér*, the Root — has been held by the Seat of Calyx since Year 1200, when the last Warrior King to actually carry it in battle died at the Battle of Blind Teeth without using it. It is now ceremonial. The Seat holds it at the harvest rite. The harvest rite produces, annually, the most accurate agricultural forecast in the Cauld.

Nobody has studied how.

#### Ignamund Unburnt, Founder of Atrament (Year 298 — Year 401)

In Year 334, the smelting settlement in the volcanic ridge western highlands lost three furnace-workers to what the settlement's records describe as "the ground's fire." The workers had gone too deep into the geothermally active ground with their equipment, and the ground had responded in a way that killed them before they could retreat.

Ignamund was the senior furnace-worker at the time. He was thirty-six, a second-generation migrant whose parents had brought the metallurgical tradition from the Southlands' volcanic highlands, which meant he had more experience than anyone else in the settlement with ground that killed people. He said he would go down and determine what had happened.

He took nothing with him. He left at dawn. He returned three days later from a direction nobody had seen him go, carrying a sword.

The sword — *Die Ewige Flamme*, the Eternal Flame — is warm to the touch. Not hot. Warm. The warmth does not diminish with distance from the Forge Hearth; it is the sword's own heat, not borrowed. The blade is of an alloy that no metallurgical analysis has ever fully characterized: the elements are local but the crystalline structure is not something local smelting produces. Ignamund said he had smelted it inside the Forge Hearth, using the membrane's own output as heat source. This is the first documented use of the membrane site at the Forge Hearth for deliberate manufacture rather than observation.

He went back in three more times in the following decade. Each time he came out unchanged. Each time he produced something: a set of tools, a method for high-temperature Catalyst handling, once a document in a script none of his contemporaries could read that was translated only in Year 891 by a Vierna archivist who recognized the Southlands' old administrative language. The document described a process for refining Catalyst material at temperatures above open-flame capacity. Atrament has used this process ever since.

Ignamund died in the Forge Hearth in Year 401. He went in during a high-output session, with no stated reason. The apparatus was running at its full thermal output. He walked past the boundary markers. No one stopped him. They did not stop him because he had walked past those markers before. He did not come back.

His service record was left open for forty years. The Atrament tradition holds that keeping it open was correct procedure; the man was last known to be inside an active installation, not confirmed dead. When it was finally closed in Year 441, the closing notation read: *location: the Forge Hearth, active session. Status: not recovered.* This is the only Atrament service record with a non-standard closing notation. All others say simply: *died in service.*

#### Arnault d'Archival, Founder of Vierna (Year 302 — Year 399)

Arnault was not a warrior. He was the oldest surviving story of a different kind of founding: the institution-builder.

The basin scribal tradition had been providing record-keeping services to all the other proto-House cultures for two generations by the time Arnault was born. They were the scribes of the pre-Compact era — trusted by everyone because they had no weapons, controlled no site, owned no contested territory. Their usefulness depended entirely on not threatening anyone, which meant they had developed, over two generations, a very precise understanding of how to be useful without being dangerous.

Arnault inherited this tradition at twenty-two and immediately expanded it. He personally negotiated the first agreements between the Calyx grain culture and the Atrament metal culture — the trade arrangements that would define both institutions for the next century. He did not fight. He wrote. Every agreement he produced was internally airtight: no ambiguities that could be exploited, no edge cases left unaddressed, no language that could mean two things. He said once, in a letter that survives: *a document that can be read two ways is not a document. It is a loaded knife set on the table.*

The Stylus — *Le Stylet de Vérité*, the Truth Stylus — is a writing implement. It is approximately twenty centimeters, constructed from a material that is metallic in weight but organic in warmth, pointed at one end and capped at the other. Any record made with it is accurate: the scribe who uses it finds, afterward, that they have written what was true rather than what they intended to write, to a degree that is not always comfortable. Arnault carried it from approximately Year 330 until his death, and used it for every significant Compact document. The first Compact document — the eleven-line agreement between three Basin powers that became the template for the entire structure — was written in his hand, with the Stylus, in Year 643.

The Stylus was lost in Year 891 during the sack of a minor Vierna installation by Fjorheim forces. It was recovered in Year 1203 from a Lacerta intelligence depot where it had apparently been sitting unidentified for three hundred years, its records having been mistranslated as forgeries. It was lost again in Year 1409 when the Warrior King Marguerite the Locked deliberately concealed it because of what it produced when she used it to write a diplomatic document she had intended to fill with useful ambiguities. What it actually produced is sealed in the Vierna archive's restricted section. The Stylus was recovered again in Year 1441. It currently resides in the Deep Archive.

The Vierna founding legend, unlike the others, includes no feat of arms. Arnault's monument is not a weapon. It is the Compact itself — the eleven lines that ended the Blood Century and became the political structure the Cauld has operated within since. His name is at the bottom in his own hand. The Stylus confirmed the signature. Nobody has ever disputed it.

#### Ulfheiðr Ironflood, Founder of Fjorheim (Year 321 — Year 418)

The fjord people came to the Cauld in the third wave and arrived already organized: they came from a coastal maritime culture with mandatory military service, oath-taking traditions, and the institutional memory of sea-raid as a primary strategic instrument. They needed, in the Cauld, only a site and a use for their existing competence.

The membrane site in the glacial plateau above the fjord coast was the site that found them, not the other way around. Ulfheiðr — then a sea-captain in the third wave's maritime contingent, thirty years old, twenty years of ocean behind her — was camped with her crew on the inland plateau when the site opened without apparatus, without ceremony, without any preparation she had made or requested. She described it later as: *the sky thinned and something came through it, and I was not afraid, and that was when I understood that fear would have been the correct response.*

What came through, or what she perceived coming through, she never recorded in recoverable detail. What she did afterward was systematic: she mapped the site, measured its seasonal activity, documented the conditions under which it opened without apparatus. This took two years. At the end of those two years, she built the first Muster Hall on the plateau's inland face and posted the terms: any person who could cross the sea in under three days and survive the plateau's winter for a single season could come in and learn what she knew. This was the founding of Fjorheim's conscription tradition — not compulsion, not coercion, but a standard that the culture around her was already built to meet.

The Relic — *Kaldvinn*, Cold-Surrender — is a longspear with a head of black iron that has never corroded in one hundred years of salt air and fjord winter. The wood is a species not native to the fjord country. Ulfheiðr claimed she took it from the glacial lake on the plateau's eastern margin, from under the ice, on a day when the membrane site was active. Her crew's record says she dove at dawn and surfaced at dusk and the lake was three hundred meters deep. This is impossible. The record says she did it.

Ulfheiðr died at the Battle of the First Shore in Year 341, defending the fjord coast's northern approach against a proto-House incursion from the south. She took three spear-wounds and continued fighting. She died facing outward. Her service record says: *fell at the First Shore. Kaldvinn recovered from the field.* The spear has been passed to each Fjorheim Warrior King in a ceremony since. The ceremony involves crossing the glacial lake. Most do it in boats. The tradition holds that the truly worthy do it as Ulfheiðr did. No one has. The tradition also holds that this is expected.

#### Brennid of Three Bloods, Founder of Pallor (Year 341 — Year 440)

The island was three cultures before it was one. The northern highland people, with their cold-climate pastoral tradition and their oral histories going back before the Migration. The central lowland people, who arrived in the second wave from the basin and brought their scribal skills north across the channel. The southern maritime people, who had already lost one mainland membrane site to a proto-Atrament advance before retreating to the island, and who carried the operational knowledge of a richer apparatus than the island's installation should possess.

They had been contesting the island's central upland for sixty years when Brennid was born. He was born to a highland mother, a lowland father, and raised by a southern maritime family that adopted him at seven after his parents were killed in a lowland-highland skirmish. He was, constitutionally, all three of the island's cultures and politically affiliated with none of them.

The founding legend goes: he challenged each of the three regional champions to single combat. He told each that if he lost, they should consider their respective grudge settled, because a man with all three bloods losing to any one side meant that side had won by the only measure that mattered. Each champion agreed, partly because the offer was philosophically interesting and partly because none of them expected a thirty-five-year-old man with no house to be a serious fight.

He lost all three combats. He was not killed in any of them. Each champion landed the decisive blow — and the decisive blow did not kill him. This is not documented as miraculous in the Pallor chronicle. The Pallor chronicle documents it as: *he did not die. He received the blow and stood up. This was observed by all parties.* Whether this is accurate, embellished, or metaphorical, the political result was real: all three champions swore to him as a neutral body, none of them swore to each other, and the neutral body became the institutional foundation of House Pallor.

The shield — *Caer Gwyn*, the White Keep — was the object that accumulated those three decisive blows. Each champion struck it once: one dent from a highland maul, one notch from a lowland blade, one crack from a maritime boarding-axe. The crack was repaired with silver. The silver is visible. The notch and the dent remain. The chronicle's official reading of this is that a House built on concession is stronger than a House built on victory, because concession can absorb more damage than pride.

Brennid established the channel-watch tradition before he died: a permanent observation post on the island's southern coast, watching the narrow water between Pallor and the mainland. He said: *the channel is the house's only wall. A wall you stop watching is no longer a wall.* This observation post has been continuously staffed since Year 343. No enemy has crossed the channel without the watch knowing first.

#### The Three Corsairs, Founders of Lacerta (Year 348 — Year 453)

Lacerta has three founding champions. Every other House has one. Lacerta's chronicles present this not as an embarrassment of riches but as the essential character of the institution: born from an irresolvable three-way standoff, built on the agreement to stop fighting only because no one party could win, maintained by the awareness that any of the three founding lines could, at any moment, betray the other two, and hasn't yet.

**Iago Salanova** arrived on the western cliff coast in Year 348 from the mainland, driving a ship that his own crew could not fully account for — the records he kept were meticulous enough to log the wind and weather for every day at sea, but the route he had taken from his point of origin (the Southlands' coastal fringe, a harbor that no longer exists) was longer than the calendar allowed. He claimed to have found the cliff-top membrane site on the first day after landing and to have operated it immediately. No one trained in membrane operation had previously operated a site on first contact. Iago had no formal training. He said: *it was obvious. You stand in the right place and it opens. The hard part is knowing where the right place is.*

**Marisol Tethys-Corda** arrived the same year, from a different direction: she came from the Southlands' western desert coast, following the ocean's edge north. Her ship was crewed by people from at least four different Southlands cultures; she herself had no single origin she acknowledged. She arrived at the cliff site to find Iago already there. They fought briefly and inconclusively, and then discovered that the site behaved differently when both of them were in it simultaneously than when either was alone. This was worth more than the site held by either one separately.

**Tethys the Wrecker** arrived in Year 360, from the ocean's west — a direction that implies either an origin on the far side of the great western ocean (unconfirmed, no such civilization documented) or a route that took longer than a normal lifetime. She arrived with no crew, on a ship that was not built from any local timber, and immediately went to the cliff site without asking where it was. She said, when Iago challenged her: *I could smell it from the water.* She could not explain this further.

The nine nights they sat together at the same fire — before they walked into the site together on the tenth night — are the most documented nine nights in Lacerta's early history, precisely because no one knows what was said. Iago, who kept records of everything, kept no record of those nine nights. Marisol, who was not a record-keeper, produced the only account of that period, and it is written in a shorthand that was hers alone and has never been fully decoded. Tethys left no records at all.

What they walked out of the site with — the agreement, the three matched blades, the shared understanding of how the installation operated — was binding enough to hold for a hundred years before the founding generation died out. The three blades, *Las Tres Navajas*, are matched knives of a white metal that no smith has been able to identify or replicate. They tarnish in the presence of deceit, according to the Lacerta chronicle. They are maintained and tested regularly. The chronicle does not record what they have been found near when they tarnished.

## PART III: THE BLOOD CENTURY (Year 512 — Year 643)

### How the War Began

No one alive when the Blood Century ended could say how it started. This is not rhetorical. The Liturgy's earliest surviving chronicle — which predates the Blood Century, written in what was then the Liturgy's function as a clerical and record-keeping body — describes the onset as a cascade of local disputes over membrane access rights that began resolving themselves through force before any diplomatic mechanism had been established to resolve them otherwise.

The trigger, as best the archive can reconstruct: in Year 512, a proto-Fjorheim military unit crossed the glacial plateau's southern margin and occupied a position within operational range of a proto-Atrament secondary site. The occupation was not an attack. It was a position. The proto-Atrament reading was that a position that controlled an approach to a site was a hostile act. They responded as if it were hostile. The proto-Fjorheim response to their response was hostile.

Eight major confederations. A century of war.

### The Named Battles

**The Battle of Glas Tor (Year 591).** The largest single engagement of the Blood Century, fought on the highland plateau between what was becoming Fjorheim and what was becoming Atrament. Fjorheim's Warrior King Harvar Longreach led 2,400 Myrmidons against an Atrament advance force moving north along the volcanic ridge's western face. The battle lasted four days.

On the third day, a Bheur Incursion Predator — the creature the records call only "the Maw of Glas Tor," a cave-dwelling predator of the ridge country with a wingspan that later estimates placed at twenty-two meters — came down from the ridge country and began taking casualties from both sides without preference. The Atrament force broke first. The Fjorheim forces, holding the highland position, did not break, but they did not pursue. Harvar Longreach died on the fourth day of a wound taken on the first day that he had not disclosed to his officers. He bled out at the map table. His service record closes at Glas Tor.

The Maw itself was killed in Year 622 by a combined Fjorheim-Calyx hunting party that had been tracking it for thirty years. It had killed an estimated 1,800 soldiers across the Blood Century, on both sides of every engagement it attended. The kill was executed by Calyx longspear-fighters using a technique Zsombor's tradition had developed for ground-membrane predators: approach from the underground angle, where the creature's senses do not cover. They came up through the root system of the plateau's edge. The beast did not see them coming.

**The Siege of the Archive (Year 618 — Year 621).** Atrament's push west into Vierna's basin territory, driven by the logic that the scribal institution was the strategic throat of the Compact-era negotiation: control the Archive, control what agreements get recorded and enforced. The siege lasted three years.

The Archive did not fall. It has never fallen. This is because the Archive is built over the membrane site, and the site cannot be shut down from outside; the proto-Vierna defenders had simply retreated to it and kept the apparatus running. An active site in a besieged building produces phenomena that an attacking force without practitioner training cannot navigate. The Atrament forces spent three years on the perimeter watching their own scouts disappear when they approached the inner walls and occasionally reappear, changed, with no memory of the interval. They withdrew in Year 621.

**The Channel Trial (Year 704).** Technically after the Blood Century, but arising directly from it: Atrament's first attempt to project force across the channel to Pallor's island. This was a strategic calculation — Pallor had held the island throughout the Blood Century and used it as a resupply and recovery ground. Taking the island would cut Fjorheim's southern rear. The naval force assembled by Atrament in Year 704 was the largest fleet the Cauld had seen.

It failed. Not in the crossing — the ships reached the channel — but in the landing. Pallor's Warrior King Caradoc Long-Eye had been watching the fleet assemble for six months from the island's watch-post. He had six months of preparation. The Atrament ships that reached the shore found a prepared beach, and the Atrament forces that landed found defenders who had read every approach in advance. The fleet withdrew. Two-thirds of the ships made it back.

### The Three Darkened Sites

During the Blood Century, three membrane sites went dark. Their locations are known. They have not been reactivated.

**The Orvaine Site** went dark in Year 589, when proto-Orvaine's entire senior practitioner lineage was killed in a single ambush by an unidentified raiding party. The ambush was carried out with unusual tactical precision — twelve people killed in twelve separate locations on the same night, none of them together, none of them aware they were under threat. Who organized it is not recorded. The site's apparatus is still present. The site has not been operated since.

**The Cresset Northern Site** went dark in Year 623, when the practitioner family holding it defected to the eastern borderland cultures. They crossed the Wilderness Wall — the impenetrable eastern forest boundary — and did not return. Whether they are still alive, or what culture they joined, is the eastern question the Cauld does not discuss publicly.

**The Unnamed Southern Site** — which the records call only "the third site," or sometimes "the Void Site" — went dark in Year 577. No one knows what happened. It was operational in Year 576. In Year 578, an inspection sent by the proto-Liturgy found the apparatus intact, the site empty, and the surrounding ground producing an effect that no one in the inspection party could describe and from which two of the twelve inspectors did not recover. The effect has diminished with time. The site now produces, at its lowest margin, a persistent low-level disturbance that is classified by the Liturgy as "minimum stable output." Shutting it down produces larger effects. It has been left running.

## PART IV: THE AGE OF THE COMPACT (Year 643 — Year 890)

### The Eleven Lines

Year 643. Arnault d'Archival, at age 341 — an age that cannot be explained by any biological mechanism in the record — writes eleven lines. The document is witnessed by representatives of three Basin powers who have been at war in various configurations for thirty years. It says, in the official translation:

*We will stop raiding each other's sites for two seasons. If the two seasons pass without incident we will meet again.*

The rest of it is logistics: where to meet, who to send, what constitutes a raid for purposes of the agreement. It is not a peace treaty. Arnault was explicit that it was not a peace treaty. He said: *A peace treaty requires both parties to agree on what peace is. We don't agree on that. This is an operational pause. Write the agreement you can keep, not the agreement you wish you could keep.*

The operational pause held. They met again. Over the next forty years, through seventeen subsequent meetings, the operational pause became something more: not peace, but a structure for managing conflict that had become more valuable than winning the conflict would have been.

This is the Compact. The Liturgy, which Arnault's tradition had been seeding into its clerical function for two generations, formalized as the keeper of the agreements. By Year 700, the Liturgy was administering ceremonies at every major membrane site, maintaining records in the Deep Archive, and providing the neutrality that allowed Houses to negotiate without pre-declared trust.

### The Formation of the Seven Houses

The seven Houses formalized in the century between Year 643 and Year 743. Not at once. In sequence, as each proto-House institution became large enough, stable enough, and sufficiently integrated with the Liturgy's administrative apparatus to function as a named House rather than a regional confederation.

**Calyx** formalized first (Year 643), as the agricultural institution was the most structurally necessary: without Calyx's food supply, the other institutions could not sustain long campaigns or large populations. Vierna wrote their charter. Atrament objected. Vierna's charter-writing clause was unambiguous. Atrament was overruled. The wound opened. It has not closed.

**Ocipheus** formalized in Year 651. Septimia Vigile had died in Year 397; her successors had maintained her observation tradition continuously. By Year 651, the institution had more documented Sphere catalogue entries than all other proto-Houses combined. Their formalization was the quietest: they simply continued what they had been doing and added the House name to the header of their documents.

**Atrament** formalized in Year 661. The Forge Hearth, by this point, was the Cauld's primary Catalyst refining facility. The Liturgy required a formal institutional structure to continue the Catalyst supply agreements. Atrament formalized under that pressure.

**Vierna** formalized in Year 670. They had been the scribes. Now they were the scribes who had written the document that made them a House. They were aware of the recursion. The Stylus was used to sign the charter. The Stylus had been writing what was true for thirty years. What it wrote in the charter was: *House Vierna, custodians of the record, guarantors of the agreement, holders of what the others cannot afford to hold for themselves.* Nobody changed it.

**Pallor** formalized last among the mainland-adjacent Houses, in Year 701, when all three regional island leaderships jointly signed a petition to the Liturgy. Vierna brokered it. Vierna brokered most things that required patience. Pallor's charter specified one unusual clause: the House's Warrior King was to be chosen not by the House's senior military structure but by the watch. The watch captain at the southern shore observation post cast the deciding vote when succession was contested. This has never been changed.

**Fjorheim** formalized in Year 697. Their charter required the most internal negotiation, because the military structure of the Muster tradition had to be adapted to the Compact's administrative requirements without losing the operational flexibility that made Fjorheim valuable. The compromise: the Muster tradition remained intact; the Compact received formal acknowledgment of Fjorheim's military services as exchangeable for Catalyst supply preference. This is still the operative agreement.

**Lacerta** formalized in Year 743, last of all. The three founding lines had spent a century arguing about what formalization meant when you were founded by three people who had explicitly refused to give any one of them primacy. The solution was elegant: Lacerta's charter has no single author. Each of the three founding-line signatures appears in a triangular arrangement at the bottom, none above the others. The Liturgy accepted this. The Vierna archivist who received the document noted in the filing margin: *ingenious or intractable. Possibly both.*

### The Great Marriages of the Compact Age

The Houses discovered, in the century after formalization, that military alliance was expensive and marriage was cheap. A daughter sent to another House's administrative structure built relationships that survived individual Warrior Kings; a son sent to serve in another House's installation learned the membrane protocols of a facility his own House would like to know more about. It was done from both motives simultaneously, by people who were aware of both motives and did not consider this dishonest.

**Year 712 — Atrament's Hildegard to a Lacerta captain.** Hildegard Atrament was the first female Warrior King in that House's tradition, holding the Eternal Flame for forty years. Her political marriage to a Lacerta corsair-captain (name not preserved in the Atrament record, preserved in the Lacerta record as "Rodrigo the Untrusted") lasted six years and dissolved when Rodrigo's intelligence activities on behalf of Lacerta within Atrament's administrative structure were confirmed. The dissolution was handled as a trade dispute to avoid the military implications of describing it accurately. Hildegard held the Eternal Flame throughout. The Lacerta record notes that Rodrigo brought back more information than any Lacerta operative in Atrament since. The Atrament record notes that Hildegard knew, and allowed it, and received something in return that the record does not specify.

**Year 877 — Calyx's Erzsébet sends daughter Ágnes to Atrament.** Ágnes Calyx-Atrament is the most complicated diplomatic marriage in the record. She was sent east at age twenty-six, married into the Atrament administrative structure, and over the next forty years became Atrament's Warrior King — the commanding military authority of a House she had not been born into, fighting campaigns against the border territories that included Calyx's eastern farmland. She is recorded in the Atrament chronicle as one of the finest Warrior Kings the House has produced. The Calyx chronicle does not record her at all. She died in Year 943 holding Atrament's contested river-border position against a joint Calyx-Vierna incursion. The position held. She died facing outward.

**Year 892 — Svala Fjorheim to Arnault IV of Vierna.** The marriage that ended the Vine Country War (Year 889-892) and produced the Northern Shore Treaty. Arnault IV was the Vierna Keeper; Svala was the eldest daughter of the Fjorheim Warrior King Ragnarr the Quiet. The Northern Shore Treaty, which she is credited as the primary negotiator of (her husband Arnault IV handled the documentation), established the first formal arrangement for Fjorheim military traffic through Vierna's northern coast territory. This treaty, seventy years later, became the legal foundation for the Gyldir Shore Accord.

**Year 901 — Osian Pallor to Vierna as the Borrowed Keeper.** Not a marriage but a form of diplomatic pledge that functions similarly: Pallor's eldest son, trained in the island's practitioner tradition, was sent to Vierna's installation as their Keeper for a term of twenty years. The "borrowing" was reciprocal — a Vierna practitioner family relocated to Pallor in exchange. The arrangement is unprecedented and has never been replicated: the only time in the Cauld's history that a House's installation was administered by an operator from another House. The Borrowed Keeper produced more Sphere catalogue documentation than any Vierna Keeper before or since; a practitioner from a different tradition, observing the same membrane, sees different things.

### The Fallen Houses in the Compact Age

**House Meridian** declined across this entire period, from its peak wealth in Year 700 through the contractor replacement (Year 780–840) to its final dissolution in Year 880. The efficiency calculation proved correct and then fatal: the contracted institution ran the site better, then became the institution with the knowledge, then renegotiated from a position of monopoly. A bad harvest year in Year 873 broke the fee. The contractors changed sides. Meridian's site went dark for twenty years before Vierna brought it back online using Sinter's archived protocols. Vierna now administers it.

**House Sinter** ended itself in Year 1089 — though the process began in Year 1079, when their practitioners first attempted to extend the apparatus's fixed-coordinate access. Eleven days of experimental operation. The membrane at Sinter's ridge site did not return to baseline. The zone around the installation has been producing its persistent low-level wrongness since. The Sinter practitioners who survived dispersed to Vierna and Atrament; Vierna's record of the experiment is more complete than the Liturgy's official account, and Vierna has made the institutional decision to keep it that way.

## PART V: THE LONG PEACE (Year 890 — Year 1143)

### The Great Feast Culture

The century after the Compact's consolidation is what the Houses' own chronicles call the Long Peace. This is not accurate. There were wars. There were always wars. What the Long Peace actually refers to is the period when the Compact's framework held well enough that no war escalated to existential scale — no House was destroyed, no membrane site was taken permanently, no population was wiped. By the standards of the Blood Century, this was peace.

The feast culture arose because surplus existed for the first time in the Cauld's history. Calyx's agricultural output, with the Catalyst-enhanced soil work that Zsombor's tradition had developed, was producing more food than the Cauld could eat. Atrament's Forge Hearth was producing Catalyst supply faster than the military demand could absorb. The Liturgy had more practitioners than it needed to administer the current war levels.

The Houses responded to surplus with the traditional instruments: elaborate ceremony, public display of what had been accumulated, and the sending of sons and daughters to distant Houses as a demonstration that the House could afford to give away its most valuable people.

The Feast of the Seven Houses — first convened in Year 912, the year of Svala Fjorheim's marriage to Arnault IV — became an annual institution that lasted until Year 1139. Every House sent a delegation. Every House provided one course of a communal meal. The meal's composition was, always, a detailed political signal: what you brought said what you had, what you were willing to share, and what you were keeping back. The scribes of Vierna documented every Feast's menu from Year 912 through Year 1139. This is, in the opinion of the current Archive Keeper, the most information-dense single document in the Deep Archive.

### The Whitening Plague (Year 412 — Year 419)

This plague strikes the record before the Long Peace technically begins, but its effects shaped the Long Peace's character. The Whitening — so called because its initial presentation was a whitening of the mucous membranes — was a respiratory plague that killed humans preferentially in order of their Catalyst load. The more infusions a person had received, the faster the disease progressed.

This is why it killed Ignamund Unburnt. He had not been inside an active session, as the Forge Hearth's conventional record suggests; he had been in the early stages of the Whitening for six months and had told no one. The walk into the Forge Hearth in Year 401 was thirty-eight years after the Whitening, when Ignamund was one hundred and three years old and had been Catalyst-loaded from his founding decade. The Whitening had been killing people slowly since Year 412. He walked into the Forge Hearth rather than allow the institutional record to show him dying of a plague.

The Atrament chronicle does not record this interpretation. The interpretation appears in the Vierna archive, in a notation made by a physician who treated Whitening cases in both Atrament and Vierna territory and recognized, in the Forge Hearth's behavior at the time of Ignamund's disappearance, the pattern she had seen in terminal cases.

**Effect on the Blood Century's end:** The Whitening killed practitioners at a disproportionate rate. This accelerated the Blood Century's resolution; the most experienced operators on all sides were dying before the war had resolved. The Compact's timing — Year 643, thirty years after the Whitening's end — reflects a generation of institutional rebuilding before the Houses were strong enough to negotiate from positions worth negotiating from.

### The Red Winter Famine (Year 1021 — Year 1023)

The wine country burns in Year 1021. A late-summer heat wave — the hottest in recorded Cauld history — kills Vierna's harvest and drives the grain failure three seasons east into Calyx. The Red Winter is named for the color of the failed harvest, which rots on the vine rather than dying cleanly.

What the famine produces: the first large-scale documented movement of displaced persons between Houses. Calyx's eastern farming populations, unable to feed themselves, move west into Atrament territory, where the volcanic-ridge agriculture is not affected by the eastern drought. Atrament accepts them as labor. The population movement lasts three years and leaves permanent demographic marks: Atrament's eastern borderland communities develop a mixed Calyx-Atrament cultural character that persists to the current era.

The diplomatic consequence: Calyx's Erzsébet of the Plains (Year 821–912), who had spent her career building the Calyx-Atrament alliance through her daughter Ágnes, watches the Red Winter demonstrate exactly how much Calyx's agricultural survival depends on Atrament's geographic stability. The alliance deepens. Calyx begins paying Atrament in food-supply guarantees rather than in military service pledges. The arrangement survives the Red Winter and becomes the standard Calyx-Atrament covenant for the next three hundred years.

### The Grey Serpent (Year 1102 — Year 1141)

The creature called the Grey Serpent occupied the channel between Vierna's northern coast and Pallor's southern shore for forty years. It was not one of the Bheur Incursion Predators — it was an ocean creature, native to the deep channel water, that had been disturbed by the increased shipping traffic of the Long Peace era. At full extension, it measured perhaps sixty meters. It was not aggressive, precisely. It simply occupied the water and responded to ships that entered its territory by responding.

The response killed eleven ships in forty years. This is a low rate for something sixty meters long. The historians who have studied the Grey Serpent have noted that it seemed to be learning the channel traffic patterns — the ships that were lost after Year 1120 were taken in configurations that suggested the creature had adapted to shipping avoidance behavior.

Both Pallor and Vierna independently organized hunting expeditions during the forty years. None of them connected, because neither House shared information about their attempts with the other, and the creature was large enough and the channel wide enough that both Houses' expeditions could search for years without finding each other.

In Year 1138, a Pallor junior knight named Brennid Shield-Boy — named in the tradition of the founder, without relation — was crossing the channel with a supply convoy when the Grey Serpent took his ship. His survival account is the most detailed record of the creature's behavior in the archive. He describes, from inside the creature, a passage of approximately twelve hours before cutting through the stomach wall. He killed the creature from inside it. He emerged with a Pallor emergency knife and the creature's own spine-bone, which he carried back as evidence. The Grey Serpent was not seen again.

Brennid Shield-Boy's account of twelve hours inside a sixty-meter ocean creature without dying is not biologically explicable. He did not volunteer an explanation. His service record notes: *distinguished conduct in the channel engagement of Year 1138.* He did not receive a Catalyst infusion. He said, when asked, that he had already had one, and that this was not the story of a Catalyst survivor. He never gave another account of the twelve hours.

## PART VI: THE AGE OF FRACTURE (Year 1143 — Year 1389)

### How the Living War Began in Earnest

The Compact's framework had been managing conflict for four hundred years. In Year 1143, the framework failed for the first time at existential scale — not because anyone decided it had failed, but because the accumulated pressures of four centuries of managed conflict had reached a point where the management cost more than the conflict.

The precipitating event: House Sinter's experimental collapse in Year 1089 had produced a membrane disturbance that was slowly expanding. By Year 1143, the disturbance radius had tripled. The Sphere access that three Houses had been dependent on from Sinter's territory was no longer reliable. The coalition alignment that had been stable for forty years re-formed around the question of who would control the Sinter site's remaining viable output. Three Houses wanted it. None of them wanted the other two to have it. The Compact had no mechanism for resolving this.

The first engagement of the formal Living War is recorded as Year 1143, at a membrane installation in Atrament's southern territory. By Year 1200, all seven Houses were committed in one or another coalition configuration.

### The Channel War and the Gyldir Shore Accord (Year 1284 — Year 1302)

The Channel War is the conflict that produced the Cauld's most important and most unusual military arrangement.

In Year 1256, Ocipheus's Warrior King Ferrante di Vigile — son of a long practitioner line, technically gifted, militarily frustrated — concluded that the Living War could not be won from the southern peninsula. Ocipheus Station had the deepest Sphere access in the Cauld and the oldest practitioner tradition; what it did not have was a second front. Atrament was pressing from the north. Calyx, Atrament's food supplier, was feeding the war machine that was degrading Ocipheus's territorial control. Ferrante's calculation: if Pallor could be taken, Fjorheim's northern naval access would be cut, Calyx's food supply routes to Atrament would be exposed, and the northern coalition would collapse.

Taking Pallor required crossing the channel. Crossing the channel from Ocipheus territory directly — sailing north up the inland sea, through the straits, north up the western coast, into the channel — took six months in favorable conditions. Vierna's northern coastline was two days' sailing from Pallor's southern shore.

Ferrante sent a delegation to Vierna's Keeper in Year 1262 with an offer. Vierna declined. Ferrante sent a better offer. Vierna declined again, but this time provided a counter-proposal.

Vierna's condition for allowing Ocipheus military transit through its northern coastal waters was not simply payment. It was oversight. Any Ocipheus fleet moving through Vierna's waters would be accompanied by escorts who could guarantee the fleet did not raid Vierna towns, did not take Vierna shipping, did not make opportunistic use of Vierna's northern coastline for anything outside the agreed transit.

The escorts Vierna specified were Giants.

The Giants — Champions so fully transformed through decades of Catalyst infusion that they stood nine feet tall, lean and immovable as ridge stone, bound to no House oath because no House could hold them — were the Cauld's most reliable guarantors precisely because no House owned them. Fjorheim knew where most of them were. Fjorheim's tradition of transformation had produced more Giants than any other House. The Giants did not serve Fjorheim. They sold their services.

Thibault Iron-Pen, Vierna's Keeper in Year 1284, negotiated the three-way arrangement that became the **Gyldir Shore Accord** (so named for the northern shore village where the signing took place):

- Ocipheus pays Vierna a crossing fee of forty Catalyst-weight units per ship, per transit
- Vierna contracts one Giant per twelve ships from Fjorheim's network, at the rate of twenty Catalyst-weight units per Giant per campaign
- The Giants ride the Ocipheus ships as route-guarantors; they have kill authority over any Ocipheus crew member who deviates from the agreed transit corridor
- Vierna receives the crossing fee before any ship leaves; if the fee is unpaid, no transit
- Fjorheim receives its cut from Vierna, not from Ocipheus; Ocipheus has no direct financial relationship with Fjorheim

Clause 7, the accord's most-contested provision: *The escort shall not be refused regardless of the routing House's conduct against third parties; the crossing is commercial, not covenantal.* This means that even if Ocipheus is at war with Vierna, the crossing is available as long as the fee is paid. Even if Fjorheim and Ocipheus are fighting on the same front, the Giants perform the escort. The commercial relationship is outside the military alignment.

In Year 1284, Benedikta of the Ridge launched the Channel War. Twenty-three ships transited Vierna's northern coast, each accompanied by one Giant from Fjorheim's pool, each paying forty Catalyst-weight units at the Gyldir wharf before departure. The fleet arrived at Pallor's southern shore in two days. The island had been watching. Pallor's Warrior King Rhiannon of the Shield had been watching the Gyldir negotiations for four years. The island was ready.

The Channel War lasted eighteen years. Ocipheus landed forces four times. Three of the four landings were repelled. The fourth held a position on the island's southern coast for three years before being driven off in Year 1302 by a combined Pallor-Fjorheim counterattack. The counterattack included Giants who had escorted the Ocipheus transit and then, the transit complete, hired themselves to Pallor for the defense. Clause 7 covered this too.

The Channel War ended in stalemate. The Accord remained in force.

### The Twice-Stalker

The Void-Crow — the enormous bird that has appeared at every major battle since Year 512 — was present at the Battle of the Ridge in Year 1043. It was present at the First Crossing of Year 1284. The same creature, if it is the same creature, would be over seven hundred years old. The Cauld's record has catalogued it twenty-three times. No one has killed it. The Ocipheus vigil operators, on long watches at the station, call it the Twice-Stalker — *il testimone*, the witness. The name is in their private vocabulary, not the official record. They say it comes to battles the way other predators come to food. They say it does not eat. They say it watches.

The one operator who filed an official report on the Void-Crow, in Year 1291, described it as: *wingspan consistent with a Bheur Incursion Predator, approximately eighteen to twenty-two meters. Behavior: observation only. It circled the engagement at altitude throughout. No wing-beat sound at distances under 300 meters. No wind-disturbance evidence of the expected mass at the altitude. Eyes: visible from 300 meters.* The report was filed. The House administration classified it under "anomalous fauna observation." It is in the archive. Nobody has added to it.

### The Plague of Forgetting (Year 1351 — Year 1371)

The Plague of Forgetting is the event in Cauld history most likely to have been a weapon.

Its symptoms: progressive degradation of autobiographical memory, beginning with recent events and moving backward over weeks. A person experiencing the Plague would forget the most recent year first, then the one before it, then the one before that. The degradation was not uniform — some memories were spared, others lost in the middle of apparent stability. The most reliable indicator of terminal stage was the moment when the affected person could no longer recall their own name.

It killed record-keepers at three times the rate of the general population. This is the statistic that suggests engineering: record-keepers are not biologically distinct from anyone else; there is no natural mechanism that would select them preferentially. The selection pattern implies a targeting mechanism. The targeting mechanism implies a designer. The designer has never been identified.

Lacerta denies any knowledge of the Plague of Forgetting's origin. Their denial is in the record. The Night of Unwritten Hours — Year 1361, when the Plague peaked — was the period when three Houses' record-keeping went dark: no entries in Atrament's service record between Year 1358 and Year 1369, a gap in Calyx's agricultural survey spanning Year 1355 to Year 1364, and a significant loss in Vierna's diplomatic correspondence archive from Year 1352 to Year 1368. The Deep Archive's restricted section contains reconstructed versions of some of this missing material. Restricted. The Archive Keeper has not released it.

The Plague ended in Year 1371 without any treatment or intervention being credited. It simply stopped. This too is in the record.

### The Great Hollow Famine (Year 1380 — Year 1395)

The Great Hollow is the worst famine in the Cauld's documented history. It was not caused by a single event but by the intersection of three concurrent failures: a membrane disturbance in Calyx's eastern territory that disrupted the root-system's ability to channel soil productivity (the same mechanism Zsombor's tradition had managed, now running in reverse); a military campaign that had pulled Calyx's labor force away from the fields for two consecutive harvests; and a Lacerta-sourced Catalyst supply problem that contaminated the enhanced-fertility preparations the Calyx root-readers used for the worst soils.

The Great Hollow killed approximately one-third of Calyx's population and significant percentages in Vierna, Atrament, and parts of Fjorheim. The monster incursion rate in Calyx territory during the Hollow was the highest ever recorded: the Thornwood Herd — seventeen linked creatures that moved as a coordinated unit through the eastern farmland — killed four hundred Myrmidons and displaced an estimated sixty thousand civilians before being driven back into the monster corridor in Year 1382. Two of the seventeen creatures that went into the corridor did not return to the corridor's normal fauna pattern. What they became is in the record only as an absence: they are not in subsequent surveys.

Calyx's Warrior King during the Hollow was Imre the Underfed, who held the position for thirty-seven years (Year 1381–1418) through what the Calyx chronicle calls "the years the ground refused." He was not a military commander by nature or training. He was an agricultural administrator who had been elevated by the watch when the prior Warrior King died in Year 1381. He managed the Hollow through distribution rather than military means: he coordinated with Fjorheim for emergency food imports, with Atrament for equipment loans, and with the Liturgy for a Catalyst supply guarantee that stabilized the root-reader system within five years. When the Hollow ended, Calyx was smaller, weaker, and more deeply in the debt of its neighbors than at any prior point. Imre spent the remaining decades of his tenure paying those debts through careful grain diplomacy.

## PART VII: THE WAR OF INHERITANCE (Year 1389 — Year 1612)

### The Coalition Cycles

The War of Inheritance is not a single war. It is the two centuries during which the Cauld's Houses cycled through coalition configurations faster than institutional memory could track them. The House that was your ally in Year 1400 was your enemy in Year 1430 and your ally again in Year 1461, and neither of you had fundamentally changed what you were.

**Year 1401 — The Accord Violation.** Ocipheus's Corrado the Furious — a Warrior King whose name describes his administrative style accurately — violated the Gyldir Shore Accord in Year 1401. His specific violation: he expelled the Fjorheim Giants from two ships mid-transit and continued the crossing without escort, having decided that the Giants were intelligence assets for Vierna rather than neutral escorts. He was not wrong about this. He was wrong about the leverage he had to act on it.

Vierna closed the crossing. The mechanism was simple: the Gyldir wharf's payment administration refused to process Ocipheus transit fees. No payment processed, no transit. No transit, no second front. Corrado's campaigns against Pallor from that point were conducted via the six-month southern route, at enormous expense, with significantly reduced force. He died in Year 1398 having retaken none of the territory lost after the crossing was closed.

**Year 1441 — The Twice-Crossing.** Fiamma Vigile, Ocipheus's next Warrior King worth naming, rebuilt the Vierna relationship over twenty years by the simple method of not violating it: she paid the fee, she accepted the Giants, she did not question the escort's intelligence function. In Year 1441, she made a state visit to Vierna's Deep Archive — the first Ocipheus Warrior King to do so — and signed a revised Accord that doubled the fee in exchange for a guarantee that the crossing would remain available regardless of Vierna's coalition alignment. This arrangement is now standard. The Accord's current terms, at Year 1700, remain Fiamma's doubled fee and her guarantee clause.

### The Notable Champions of the War of Inheritance

The War of Inheritance produced more Champions than any period since the Blood Century. Champions — the extreme end of Transmutation, bodies changed by many infusions into forms no House can designate or hold — had been rare in the Long Peace era, as the rate of distinguished service sufficiently high to earn infusion was lower in a managed conflict. The War of Inheritance's escalation rate produced the combat-distinguished at unprecedented speed.

**Gunnvor Ironflood** of Fjorheim (Year 1134–1221) is the first of the War of Inheritance's Champions with a complete record. Gunnvor was six feet, then seven, then eight over a career spanning thirty campaigns. The Kaldvinn — Ulfheiðr's founding spear — was in her hand for forty years. She did not survive her encounter with it: in Year 1221, she attempted the founding ritual, the glacial lake dive, as a seventy-year-old Champion who had received seventeen infusions. She dove at dawn. She did not surface. The lake is three hundred meters deep, as it was when Ulfheiðr dove it. Kaldvinn was found, the following spring, in the shallows on the lake's eastern margin. It was recovered. Gunnvor was not.

**The Question of Pallor's Long Seat.** The current Warrior King of Pallor — known only as "the Long Seat" in current political discourse, refusing by tradition to give any name to the other Houses — took the position in Year 1589 and has held it for over one hundred years. This is not impossible; a Warrior King with enough Catalyst infusions can outlast normal human aging by significant margins, and a Warrior King of Pallor who has been using the island's practitioner tradition for that long could be... whatever they are now. The other Houses have three theories about what the Long Seat is waiting for. None of the theories are documented. The Long Seat has not offered clarification.

### The Battle of Three Waters (Year 1567)

The largest engagement of the War of Inheritance involved all seven Houses in direct or proxy engagement around the junction of the three rivers at the center of Atrament-Calyx-Vierna territorial margins. The battle lasted forty-three days. It resolved nothing: no territory changed hands, no installation was taken, no alliance shifted. Seventeen thousand Myrmidons died. The monster incursion rate in Calyx territory spiked for two years after as the battlefield's Catalyst residue — from the high volume of infused Myrmidons killed in the engagement — created an unguided Gifted matter dispersal event across the river valley.

What the Battle of Three Waters produced, administratively, was the Great Exhaustion: a Compact-level agreement that the coalition framework required recalibration. The Compact of the Three Waters (Year 1569) established the current practice of coalition alignment reviews every forty years, at which point all Houses formally acknowledge the current arrangement and identify whether they remain in it or are reconfiguring.

### The Sending of Three Daughters (Year 1567)

In the same year as the Battle of Three Waters — an act of diplomatic optimism so disconnected from its context as to seem almost deliberate — Fjorheim, Vierna, and Calyx simultaneously arranged for each House to send one daughter to the other two Houses.

The three marriages:
- Fjorheim sends Áslaug Ironflood to Vierna; Vierna sends Orlane d'Archive to Calyx; Calyx sends Borbála Root-daughter to Fjorheim
- All three marriages were intended to signal mutual commitment across the three-House network
- Áslaug Ironflood died in Vierna in Year 1571, of the Whitening's recurrence, at age thirty-four
- Orlane d'Archive was expelled from Calyx in Year 1574 when the Calyx Seat died and the succession went to a faction hostile to the Vierna arrangement
- Borbála Root-daughter remained in Fjorheim for the rest of her life (she died in Year 1619) but produced no children and built no political alliance; she is remembered in Fjorheim's chronicle as "the Root-daughter who planted nothing"

The failure of all three marriages within a decade of the Battle of Three Waters is presented by the Vierna archive as evidence for the position that diplomatic marriage functions on institutional trust, not on the individuals who are sent. If the institutions are not committed, the people do not matter.

### The Whitethroat Campaign (Year 1634)

Fjorheim's largest conscription operation in the War of Inheritance era. The Muster Chamber ran at near-continuous output for fourteen months, bringing through the membrane a force supplement that the Fjorheim chronicles record only as "the Whitethroat intake." The origin Sphere of the Whitethroat conscripts is not documented. What is documented is that the intake numbered approximately 3,400 soldiers, that the oath was administered before sufficient language acquisition in all but two hundred cases, and that the Warrior King who oversaw the intake — Malvina Ironflood, last holder of Kaldvinn before its loss at sea — wrote in her private record (not the official chronicle; this is from a private document recovered by the Vierna archive in Year 1690): *they looked at the sky when they arrived the way you look at a sky you have never seen before. I have seen this before. I know what it means. The record will not say it.*

The record does not say it. The private note does. This is what the Vierna archive means when it says the gaps in the record are part of the record.

## PART VIII: THE GREY DECADES (Year 1612 — Year 1700)

### The Exhaustion

The Houses are not, in Year 1700, at peace. The Living War has been running for over five hundred years from its formal Year 1143 start. What the Grey Decades represent is exhaustion — not the end of the war, but the end of the war's capacity to surprise anyone.

Every House has lost membrane installations. Every House has lost Champions. Every House has sent sons and daughters to other Houses and watched some of them not come back. The Compact's framework has been strained to near-breaking and has held, after a fashion, because the alternative to the framework is the Blood Century, and the institutional memory of the Blood Century is still in the records.

The four remaining Fallen Houses confirm in this period. House Venn was absorbed into Ocipheus in Year 1651; the Venn practitioner tradition vanished into Ocipheus's institutional culture, leaving behind only the word *vigil operators* use for the membrane on night watches when the administration is not present. House Orvaine was formally absorbed by the Silence in Year 1680; the Orvaine site is now the only active installation in the Cauld administered directly by the Liturgy rather than a named House.

### The Current Coalition Alignment

The coalition alignment that has been in place for approximately forty years from Year 1700 represents a reconfiguration produced by the Three Waters Compact's forty-year review cycle. It has two anchor Houses (Atrament and Ocipheus), three in the opposition bloc (Fjorheim, Vierna, Lacerta), and two formally unaligned (Calyx and Pallor).

The unaligned Houses are the balance point. Both blocs court them. Neither bloc has offered enough to shift either one. Calyx's Seat is calculating the food-supply leverage in both directions. Pallor's Long Seat is waiting. What they are waiting for is not known.

The Gyldir Shore Accord continues in force: Ocipheus transits Vierna's coast to access Pallor's southern approaches, pays the doubled fee Fiamma established in Year 1441, and accepts the Fjorheim Giant escorts that Vierna contracts. The Giant escorts report, probably, to Vierna. Possibly also to Fjorheim. The Accord's Clause 7 — commercial, not covenantal — keeps this arrangement surviving every coalition shift. Money is more durable than allegiance.

## APPENDIX II: CHRONICLE OF DIPLOMATIC MARRIAGES

These are the marriages the Houses used to build, test, and sometimes destroy their alliances. The list is not complete — the archive holds approximately 340 documented cross-House marriages across the 1700-year record. These are the ones that mattered most.

**Year 399** — Héloïse Vierna to Rémi-Elder of Calyx's founding family. Bound Calyx's record-keeping to Vierna's tradition. First cross-House marriage in the archive.

**Year 532** — Sigrid Fjorheim to an Atrament installation Keeper. Sigrid died childless at the Battle of Glas Tor, fighting on the Atrament side against Fjorheim. The Atrament chronicle records her heroism. The Fjorheim chronicle records her as a casualty of the engagement, without noting which side she was on.

**Year 712** — Hildegard Atrament to Rodrigo Lacerta. Six years, dissolved. See main text.

**Year 877** — Ágnes Calyx to Atrament. Ágnes became Atrament's Warrior King and fought against Calyx. See main text.

**Year 892** — Svala Fjorheim to Arnault IV Vierna. Ended the Vine Country War; produced the Northern Shore Treaty; the template for the Gyldir Shore Accord. See main text.

**Year 901** — Osian Pallor to Vierna (the Borrowed Keeper). Not a marriage. A diplomatic pledge with similar binding force. See main text.

**Year 1001** — Consuela Lacerta to an Ocipheus administrative family. The Lacerta intelligence archive's record of this marriage is detailed and clinical. The Ocipheus record is warm and describes a genuine alliance. Both records are accurate.

**Year 1089** — Ragnheiðr of Calyx origin, raised in Atrament from age eleven, fought for Atrament against Calyx in Year 1143. He held the Atrament river-border position for six months in Year 1148. He wrote, in a letter to his Calyx birth-family that was intercepted and is now in the Vierna archive: *I do not know which country I am from. I know which house I hold. For now that is enough.*

**Year 1231** — Morwenna of Pallor's daughter sent to Vierna as part of a failed peace negotiation. The daughter (name withheld by Pallor; she took her mother's House by choice not blood-right) became Vierna's Keeper's administrative secretary for thirty years. The Vierna record describes her as the best administrative secretary in the archive's history. The Pallor record does not mention her.

**Year 1302** — Þóra Fjorheim's brother Leifur sent to Lacerta in a marriage arrangement. The marriage lasted; Leifur Fjorheim-Lacerta died in Year 1378 in Lacerta, never having returned north. His children are the only documented Fjorheim-Lacerta bloodline in the record. Their descendants have been Lacerta's most effective operators in Fjorheim territory for two centuries.

**Year 1400** — Szilvia of Calyx sent to Fjorheim. She became Fjorheim's Warrior King after her husband's death in Year 1439. She returned to Calyx in Year 1456 when the succession required her. She held Calyx's position for twenty-two years. She is the only person in the record to have been Warrior King of two Houses.

**Year 1567** — The Three Daughters. See main text. All three failed.

## APPENDIX III: THE NAMED BEASTS

The Cauld's native creatures predate civilization and will outlast it. These are the ones the Houses have seen clearly enough to name.

**The Maw of Glas Tor** (Years 512–622). Cave predator, volcanic ridge. Wingspan twenty-two meters. Wingspan-to-body ratio inconsistent with standard Bheur Incursion Predator taxonomy; possible separate species. Killed 1,800 Myrmidons across 110 years. Killed by combined Fjorheim-Calyx hunting party in Year 622 using underground approach technique.

**The Grey Serpent** (Years 1102–1141). Channel creature. Ocean-dwelling. Sixty meters estimated. Forty-year channel territory. Non-aggressive in the conventional sense: it killed ships that entered its territory, not people on shore. Killed by Pallor's Brennid Shield-Boy from inside the creature's stomach, in Year 1138.

**The Void-Crow** (Year 512–present). The witnessing bird. Twenty-two meters wingspan. Present at twenty-three documented major engagements across seven centuries. Does not eat. Does not sleep, as far as any observer has established. Cannot be killed, as far as the record can determine — there are two documented attempts, both in Year 1567 at the Battle of Three Waters, both resulting in injuries to the Myrmidons who made them and no apparent effect on the creature. Ocipheus vigil operators call it *il testimone*. The Liturgy has no classification for it. It is in the record. It is not explained.

**The Thornwood Herd** (Year 1380–1382). Seventeen linked creatures, eastern farmland. Coordinated movement as a unit; individual size of a large draft animal, but linked locomotion implying shared perception. Killed 400 Myrmidons. Driven into monster corridor in Year 1382. Two did not return to corridor patterns. What they became is not in the subsequent fauna surveys.

**The Twice-Stalker** (Years 1043 and 1284). The same enormous bird — or an identical bird — appearing at the Battle of the Ridge in Year 1043 and the First Crossing of Year 1284. Two hundred forty-one years separate these appearances. If it is the same creature, it is the second oldest individually documented creature in the record after the Void-Crow, and its appearances at two of the most strategically significant moments in Cauld history are either coincidence or something the record does not have a framework for.

**The Ashgrave Corridor Predators** (ongoing). Unnamed individually. The Sphere outside the catalogue sequence that Ocipheus's vigil tradition has identified as the Ashgrave — a location-specific access point reachable only from coordinates in the ridge country — occasionally sends through fauna that no Sphere catalogue entry explains. The fauna are catalogued individually by Ocipheus's practitioners and destroyed as quickly as possible. The catalogue numbers go up. The corridor does not close.

## APPENDIX IV: THE MAJOR TREATIES

**Year 643 — The Eleven Lines (the First Compact)**
Three Basin parties. Non-aggression for two seasons. The document that became everything. Arnault d'Archival's hand. The Truth Stylus confirmed the signatures.

**Year 700 — The Muster Accord**
Between the Liturgy and House Fjorheim. Formalizes the conscription capability as a Compact-permitted practice, with restrictions on intake rate and a requirement that the origin Sphere not be documented. The restriction has been honored. The practice has exceeded the intake rate restrictions in every period of sustained conflict.

**Year 843 — The Refinery Covenant**
Between the Liturgy and House Atrament. Establishes Atrament as the exclusive processor of Liturgy-supplied Catalyst material. The Covenant's terms do not specify where the Catalyst comes from. The Liturgy did not offer to specify. Atrament did not ask.

**Year 892 — The Northern Shore Treaty**
Between Fjorheim and Vierna; negotiated primarily by Svala Fjorheim-Vierna. Established formal right of military transit through Vierna's northern coastal waters for Fjorheim military traffic; defined the fee structure; established the escort tradition for the first time in an official document. The Giants are not named in the treaty — they are referred to as "certified House Champions serving in a commercial escort capacity." This language was Svala's. Arnault IV wrote it down. The Truth Stylus was present. The document says what it says.

**Year 1284 — The Gyldir Shore Accord**
Between Ocipheus and Vierna, with Fjorheim as co-signing guarantor of the escort provision. See main text. The key clause — Clause 7, commercial not covenantal — is the operative foundation of the current Ocipheus-Pallor military relationship. As long as Ocipheus pays and the Giants are available, the crossing exists. It has existed through four coalition shifts, two changes of Warrior King on all parties' sides, and one Accord violation (Year 1401) that was resolved by closing the crossing and reopening it forty years later at double the fee.

**Year 1441 — The Fiamma Amendment to the Gyldir Shore Accord**
Between Ocipheus and Vierna. Doubles the crossing fee from forty to eighty Catalyst-weight units per ship. Adds the guarantee clause: the crossing is available regardless of Vierna's coalition alignment, including alignment against Ocipheus. This means that if Vierna and Ocipheus are fighting each other, Ocipheus may still pay to use Vierna's coast to approach Pallor. The fee goes to Vierna. Vierna finds this reasonable. Ocipheus finds it degrading and has paid it continuously since Year 1441 because the alternative is the six-month southern route.

**Year 1569 — The Three Waters Compact**
All seven Houses. Established the forty-year coalition alignment review cycle. Required formal acknowledgment of current coalition structure at each review. Did not establish any mechanism for enforcing the acknowledged structure. This is the Compact's current operating document.

## APPENDIX V: THE GREAT FEASTS — MENU AS POLITICAL RECORD

*Selected entries from the Feast of the Seven Houses, Year 912 to Year 1139, as documented by the Vierna Archive*

**Year 912 — The First Feast** (Vierna hosts): All seven Houses present. Calyx brings the grain and prepares the bread; Fjorheim brings preserved fish from the northern sea; Atrament brings ironwork table settings as contribution; Ocipheus brings olives and the pressed oil of the southern peninsula; Vierna provides the wine and records everything; Pallor brings salted mutton from the island's interior; Lacerta provides salt and the three corsair-tradition knife-preserved fish preparations. The wine is from Vierna's surviving stock from the red-winter famine's bad year — a bitter vintage that everyone drinks in full nonetheless.

**Year 1021 — The Dry Summer Feast** (held despite the ongoing famine, in Atrament): No wine. No grain bread. Calyx contributes nothing; they have nothing. Fjorheim brings double fish. Atrament, hosting, serves the iron-broth — a mineral water from the volcanic-ridge springs, heated over the Forge Hearth until it is safe to drink, with yggdra-bark flavoring. Every House drinks it. Nobody mentions that they are drinking groundwater because the food supply is gone. This is the feast that the archive's current Keeper calls "the most diplomatic meal in the Cauld's history."

**Year 1139 — The Last Feast** (Lacerta hosts): All seven Houses present. The feast proceeds normally through six courses. The seventh course — traditionally provided by the hosting House as a gesture of abundance — is served by Lacerta as a single item: one salt-blade knife, one per guest, with no food attached. The Las Tres Navajas are on the table. All three are tarnishing. The feast ends without the traditional toast. The Feast of the Seven Houses is not formally discontinued after Year 1139. It simply does not convene in Year 1140, or since.
