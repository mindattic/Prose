# The Genealogies of Jesus — two family trees that do not match

The New Testament gives exactly two genealogies for Jesus. They agree that his legal line runs
through Joseph and through King David. They disagree on almost everything else: the name of
Joseph's father, which of David's sons the line descends from, and how many generations separate
David from Jesus. Both Gospels that supply a genealogy also state, in the same breath, that
Joseph was not Jesus's biological father — a genealogy through a man neither author calls the
biological parent is itself the first thing worth sitting with before comparing the two lists.

## 1. The Scriptural Claim

- **Matthew 1:1–17** — three sets of fourteen generations: Abraham to David (14), David to the
  Babylonian exile (14), the exile to Christ (14) — a structure Matthew states explicitly
  (1:17). Descends through **Solomon**, David's son by Bathsheba. Names Joseph's father as
  **Jacob** (1:16). Unusually for an ancient genealogy, it names five women: Tamar, Rahab, Ruth,
  "the wife of Uriah" (Bathsheba, unnamed directly), and Mary — each attached to a
  morally complicated story (deception, foreign origin, scandal), a pattern too deliberate to be
  incidental to Matthew's purpose.
- **Luke 3:23–38** — runs the opposite direction (Jesus back to Adam, and finally "the son of
  God"), with no stated numerical structure. Descends through **Nathan**, a different son of
  David (2 Samuel 5:14 lists Nathan among David's sons born in Jerusalem). Names Joseph's father
  as **Heli** (3:23). Between David and Jesus, Luke lists roughly twice as many names as Matthew
  does for the same span.
- Luke 3:23 is explicit about whose line this is: "Jesus ... being the son (as was supposed) of
  Joseph, the son of Heli" — the text names Joseph, not Mary, as Heli's descendant.
- Both texts state Joseph was not the biological father: Matthew 1:18–25 and Luke 1:34–35 both
  describe a virginal conception. Neither Gospel offers a genealogy through Mary.

## 2. The Spectrum of Scholarship

- **Traditional / confessional — the levirate-marriage harmonization:** the earliest attempt on
  record. Julius Africanus (early 3rd century), in a letter to Aristides preserved by Eusebius
  (*Ecclesiastical History* 1.7), proposed that Matthan (Solomon's line) and Melchi (Nathan's
  line) married the same woman, Estha, at different times: Matthan fathered Jacob, then died;
  Estha married Melchi and bore Heli; Heli died without a son, and by the Jewish law of levirate
  marriage (a childless widow's brother-in-law fathering an heir in the dead man's name) his
  half-brother Jacob fathered Joseph as Heli's legal son. On this reading, Matthew gives Joseph's
  biological line and Luke his legal one, and the two genealogies were never meant to compete.
  Africanus claimed this account came from surviving relatives of Jesus (the *desposynoi*) who
  preserved family records — a claim with no independent way to verify.
- **Traditional / confessional — the "Luke is Mary's genealogy" harmonization:** a later,
  considerably more popular explanation (developed from the Renaissance onward, not attested in
  antiquity), holding that Luke actually records Mary's ancestry and that "son ... of Heli" means
  Joseph was Heli's **son-in-law**. This has no support in Luke's actual wording, which names
  Joseph as the one descended from Heli, not as married into that line.
- **Mainstream historical-critical** (Raymond E. Brown's *The Birth of the Messiah* is the
  standard critical treatment of both infancy narratives): both genealogies are **independent
  theological compositions**, not preserved genealogical records, each serving its author's own
  argument — Matthew's tightly patterned 3×14 structure links Jesus to Abraham and David for a
  Jewish audience; Luke's unpatterned, much longer list reaches back to Adam to make Jesus's
  significance universal rather than exclusively Israelite. On this view, Matthew and Luke wrote
  without knowledge of each other's genealogy, so the "contradiction" was never adjudicated by
  either author and the later harmonizations (Africanus's included) are retrospective attempts to
  solve a problem the original writers did not know they had created.
- **Skeptical / minimalist:** goes further, treating the claim of Davidic descent itself as a
  theological necessity imposed after the fact (messianic expectation tied David's line to the
  promised king — 2 Samuel 7:12–16, Micah 5:2) rather than a preserved family memory, particularly
  since both authors independently deny Joseph is the biological link the genealogy is
  supposedly tracing.

## 3. The Independent Record

- No Roman, Jewish, or other administrative genealogical record survives, from any period, for
  Joseph's family specifically, or for any claimed continuously-tracked private Davidic lineage
  after the Babylonian exile. Pre-exilic royal genealogies for the kings of Judah are preserved
  in the Hebrew Bible itself (e.g., 1 Chronicles 3), which is not an independent source.
  Josephus (*Life* 1) claims access to public priestly-line genealogical archives for his own
  family, showing such record-keeping existed for priestly families in his era — but no
  comparable surviving archive is ever cited for a non-priestly Davidic family line.
- Africanus's claim to have consulted surviving relatives of Jesus is reported only by Eusebius,
  a full century later, and cannot be independently checked against any other source.

## 4. The Gap Table

| Attested (independent sources / physical evidence) | Assumed (plausible, widely repeated, rests on scripture alone or inference) | Legendary Accretion (added later, for a traceable reason) |
|---|---|---|
| Two genealogies exist in the canonical text, disagreeing on Joseph's father's name and the Davidic son of descent (Matthew 1:16, Luke 3:23) | That either list reflects a continuously tracked family record rather than a theologically constructed literary genealogy | Africanus's levirate-marriage harmonization (3rd century) — a specific, detailed reconciliation with no independent corroboration beyond his own say-so |
| Matthew's 3×14 structure is an internal literary feature, verifiable simply by counting the names as given | That 1st-century Judean families of Joseph's presumed social standing kept multi-century private genealogical records at all | The popular "Luke = Mary's genealogy" reading — textually unsupported by Luke's own wording, which names Joseph, not Mary, as the descendant |
| Both Gospels independently deny Joseph is the biological father (Matthew 1:18–25; Luke 1:34–35) | That a genealogy through a stated non-biological father was intended by either author as a claim of biological descent rather than legal/dynastic standing | — |

## 5. Open Questions

- Whether either genealogy preserves any authentic transmitted family information at all, versus
  being wholly theological composition, is not resolved and may not be resolvable — no
  independent record exists to check against.
- Why two independent authors would each construct a genealogy establishing Davidic descent
  through a father both explicitly deny is the biological parent is a genuine internal tension in
  the text, not a question external evidence can settle.
- Whether Africanus's claimed source (interviews with Jesus's surviving relatives) reflects real
  transmitted family history or is itself a 3rd-century literary device to close an
  already-noticed gap is unknown.
