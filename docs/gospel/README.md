# Gospel (GSPL) — History vs. Heritage

## What this is

**Gospel (GSPL)** is StreetSamurai's 3rd Universe (registered in `dbo.Universe`, slug `gspl`,
alongside GLMZ and SCRY) — a real, DB-backed universe using the same `Nodes`/`Beats`/
`BeatNodes` schema, sharing the prose-generation pipeline. It differs from GLMZ/SCRY only in
subject matter (the real world, not an invented one) and in an added craft discipline: every
factual claim in finished prose must be citation-grounded — see
[`../GSPL.md`](../GSPL.md) for the Universe-layer craft rules this enforces (the DCM hierarchy's
tier-2 layer, same role `GLMZ.md`/`SCRY.md` play for their universes).

The research documents in this directory (this file's Topics/campaign sections below) are the
**grounding material** GSPL prose is written from — the same role a `NodeBible` or entity doc
plays for fiction — not the finished, reader-facing deliverable. A finished GSPL beat is prose,
citation-numbered per `docs/GSPL.md` §1a; the Scriptural-Claim/Spectrum/Gap-Table documents here
are the research substrate underneath it.

**The subject:** a topic-by-topic reexamination of the Abrahamic scriptural record against the
independent historical, archaeological, and documentary record — asking, for any given claim,
"scripture says X; what does the non-scriptural evidence actually show?"

## The mission statement (the George Washington test)

A biography of George Washington that never mentions his dentures were carved from hippopotamus
ivory and human/animal teeth (not wood — that part's also a myth) isn't lying, but it's telling
**heritage**, not **history**. Heritage is the version of the past a community keeps because it's
useful to believe; history is what the surviving evidence actually supports, including the
unflattering, the uncertain, and the frankly unknown parts.

**Gospel's purpose is not to debunk scripture and not to prove it.** It is to supply the missing
context required to read scripture *accurately* — as an ancient text produced by real communities
with real interests, describing (and sometimes inventing, compressing, or retrojecting) claims
about real places and people who left an independent evidentiary trail of their own, or didn't.

## Method: the spectrum, not a verdict

Every topic document lays out a **spectrum from the scriptural claim to the most skeptical
academic position, with every serious position in between** — not a single adjudicated
conclusion. Concretely:

1. **The Scriptural Claim** — what the text(s) actually say, cited precisely (book:chapter:verse),
   including internal disagreements between passages/books before any outside source is consulted.
2. **The Spectrum of Scholarship** — named positions across the range:
   - **Traditional/confessional** — the claim taken at or near face value, per mainstream
     denominational scholarship.
   - **Mainstream historical-critical** — the position held by the working majority of
     non-devotional biblical historians and archaeologists (regardless of their personal faith
     or lack of it) — this is the academy's actual center of gravity, not a "middle ground"
     invented for balance.
   - **Minimalist / skeptical** — named schools and scholars who doubt more of the traditional
     claim than the mainstream does, with their actual evidentiary basis (not caricatured).
   - Fringe positions (on either end) are labeled as fringe when they are fringe. "Spectrum"
     does not mean false balance between a consensus and a discredited outlier.
3. **The Independent Record** — what non-scriptural evidence exists: archaeology, epigraphy,
   contemporary non-Christian/non-Jewish sources, administrative and diplomatic records,
   material culture. Dated and sourced.
4. **The Gap Table** — the load-bearing section. Three columns: **Attested** (multiple
   independent sources or physical evidence), **Assumed** (plausible, widely repeated, but
   resting on scripture alone or on inference), and **Legendary Accretion** (later tradition,
   often centuries later, added for a traceable theological or political reason — the
   "dentures" category).
5. **Open Questions** — what genuinely isn't known, stated as such rather than papered over.
6. **Sources** — bibliography in **APA 7th-edition format**, with a bias toward citing primary
   evidence and naming real scholars/schools rather than anonymous "some historians say."
   Scripture and other ancient primary texts with universally recognized numbering (chapter:verse,
   book.chapter.section) are cited in-line by that numbering rather than as reference-list
   entries, per standard APA practice for classical/religious works; modern editions, translations,
   and secondary scholarship get full reference-list entries. **Do not invent a page range,
   publisher, or year for a citation that has not been verified** — an unverified but
   plausible-looking citation is a worse failure mode for this project than a gap flagged as
   unverified, since the entire point of the Gap Table is to be honest about what is and isn't
   actually known.

## The New Testament Claims Campaign

The topics above were the initial examples. The standing goal is now **every verse of the New
Testament, in canonical order** — checkable claims get full topic-doc treatment, theological/
moral content gets explicitly flagged as out-of-method rather than silently skipped. This is
tracked as its own campaign, since it spans many future sessions: see
[`nt-claims/README.md`](nt-claims/README.md) for the method, the per-book status table, and the
chapter-by-chapter ledgers (Matthew's is drafted for chapters 1–2; the rest of Matthew and all
26 remaining books are queued).

## A note on scope and neutrality

This track deliberately does not adjudicate theological truth claims (whether God acted in
history is not a historical question any evidence can settle). It restricts itself to
questions evidence *can* speak to: did this place have this border, did this person leave a
documentary trace, does the archaeological layer match the narrative's claimed date. Readers
of any faith position or none should be able to use these documents, because they report the
state of the evidence rather than argue a conclusion about belief.
