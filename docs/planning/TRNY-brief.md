## 1. Series Position {#SS-BRIEF-TRNY-§1}

**Universe:** SCRY / The Entos

**Story type:** Standalone Entos novel — independent story, not part of a series chapter sequence

**Book(s) this story serves:** None as a required prerequisite. Feeds the Entos universe (Three
Points as a recurring location; the unaffiliated Pallor Champion first named in `docs/universes/ENTOS.md`
§ Pallor territory gets his first on-page appearance; Kressida Haun gets a pre-fusion appearance)

**In-universe timing:** **One year before VIGL.** Year 1370 (58 AoV). This is load-bearing: Kressida
Haun's VIGL entry state is explicitly "Atrament battle-rig pilot, active service, Schmalburg
garrison; rig intact; no existential crisis yet — she's a functioning soldier." TRNY must be set
before the Schmalburg raid where a Fjorheim anti-armor round fuses her permanently to BR-7. In
TRNY she is bonded to her rig the way any battle-rig pilot is bonded to their machine —
professionally, operationally — but separable. She still has a body that is only sometimes
inside a machine. VIGL is where that stops being true. Do not write TRNY events that Kressida
would later have to have forgotten or that contradict her VIGL introduction.

## 2. Arc Contribution {#SS-BRIEF-TRNY-§2}

Entos-universe threads this story advances:

- **[x] Three Points as a living place, not a map label.** ENTOS.md establishes Three Points as
  neutral truce ground where Verlaine, Atrament, and Pallor interests converge and no House may
  station military forces. This story is the first to actually walk the reader through it during
  its signature event — the triennial truce tournament — rather than describing it from outside.
- **[x] The unaffiliated Pallor Champion.** ENTOS.md plants a Champion "affiliated with Pallor but
  not sworn to it," present at Three Points for eleven years, sharing an unnamed watch with the
  Warrior King. This story gives him a name (Thallor Meraq) and his first on-page behavior — still
  without resolving what he's waiting for. That stays a plant for a future book.
- **[x] Kressida Haun, pre-fusion.** Establishes who she was before VIGL's cost fell on her:
  competent, unsentimental, already carrying the House's arithmetic-of-loss worldview that her
  VIGL-era quotes show fully formed — but with a body she can still leave the machine bay in.
- **[x] House Atrament's internal fault line.** ENTOS.md notes Atrament's power depends on never
  asking where the Catalyst really comes from. This story dramatizes a different but adjacent
  fault line: an internal power struggle over who controls the Refinery Seat, exploited during
  the one three-day window a year when Atrament's guard is institutionally required to be down.
- **[ ] None** — not applicable

## 8. Thematic Complement {#SS-BRIEF-TRNY-§8}

**Theme:** The truce is the only honest thing about the war — three days where the Houses agree
to stop pretending they aren't all still counting each other's strength, and the one place enemies
get to be people first.

**Register:** Adventure-picaresque, warmer and funnier than VIGL/M101's war-weight — closer to
*A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms* (lowborn-but-honorable fighter stumbling into a court plot he
can't walk away from) crossed with the fairground-thief chaos of Disney's *Robin Hood*. Still
Entos: the violence is real, the Pit fights are ugly, the indenture market is grim under the
bunting. But this book gets to breathe and laugh in a way the war books don't, because for three
days, in-world, nobody is required to be at war.

**Adjacent stories in the same universe:** VIGL (war-weight, cosmic horror, institutional
complicity), M101 (Verlaine desertion, war logistics), LLSS (frontier horror, ambivalent monster
morality)

**How it complements them:** Every other produced Entos story is written from inside the war.
TRNY is written from inside the one structure Entos built to survive not being at war for 72
hours a year. It's the closest thing the universe has to a slice-of-life — fair food, gambling,
drinking, a found-family duel-team, class tension between a Warrior-culture berserker and an
industrial House's soldier — and the plot exists specifically to show that even the truce isn't
safe, because institutions don't take three days off from their own rot just because the armies do.

**What would be duplicated if this story didn't exist:** Nothing — no other produced Entos story
is set at Three Points, during the truce, or gives Thallor Meraq or Kressida's pre-fusion self any
page time.

## 9. Structural Blueprint Seed {#SS-BRIEF-TRNY-§9}

**Resolution mode:** Mixed — external success (Aeshel lives, Morrint exposed, Kaeric's sister freed)
with real cost and no clean bow (Morrint's fate is institutional and unglamorous; Meraq's mystery
is untouched; Kaeric's new visibility is as much danger as reward)

**Moral polarity:** Ambivalent default, with one clean exception — Morrint's plot to kill Aeshel
during a truce built specifically so the Houses don't have to keep killing each other is written
as a real betrayal, not a grey one. Everything else (Corrik's trade, Zennia's theft, the indenture
market, the Houses' mutual distrust) stays ambivalent.

**Ending style:** Avalanche — the assassination attempt lands inside the championship bout's
public chaos, not on a schedule anyone chose; Kaeric and Kressida act from who they've become
over three days, not from a plan they had time to finish making.

**Escalation curve:**
- Ch 1–3: Arrival, the draw, Round 1 vs. mercenaries — low stakes, fairground texture, Zennia's
  theft-of-the-week established as comic engine
- Ch 4–6: Round 2 vs. Corrik's chained Sinterspawn in the Pit (first real violence, first real
  fear) → Zennia unknowingly lifts Morrint's assassination ledger → suspicion without proof
- Ch 7–9: Round 3 vs. a rising elite pairing (cost: Brigg hurt, the team nearly broken) →
  cornering Zennia → the evidence is read and understood
- Ch 10–12: Nowhere safe to report it (Morrint IS Atrament's security command) → Meraq's cryptic
  nudge toward the Warrior King instead → the assassin identified as the final bracket's opponent
- Ch 13–14: The Duel-team Final and the assassination attempt collide in public → aftermath →
  the truce ends on schedule and the war resumes in three days, as promised

**Event-type palette:** Tournament combat / heist-comedy chase / political suspicion / found-family
bonding / public confrontation

**Subplot thread:** Zennia Vrask's thief-of-everything fairground spree — thematically parallel
to the main plot's institutional theft (Morrint stealing the Refinery Seat, the indenture market
stealing years from the Oathless) via one honest, small-scale thief who steals from everyone
without pretending it's anything but what it is

**Form device:** The tournament bracket itself as structural spine — each round is a chapter-unit
escalation with its own stakes, and the final round's public spectacle is the frame the
assassination attempt hides inside

**Intertextual anchors:**
1. *A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms* (George R. R. Martin, "The Hedge Knight") — lowborn,
   honorable fighter at a tournament stumbles into a plot that isn't his to walk away from
2. *Robin Hood* (1973, Disney) — fairground/festival thief chaos, warm rogue energy, animal-fable
   lightness without losing the theft's real stakes
3. *Gladiator* (2000) — tournament-as-political-instrument, arena combat with real institutional
   weight behind it
4. *The Hunger Games* — escalating bracketed combat rounds as both spectacle and political
   instrument, crowd as a character
