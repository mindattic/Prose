# THE VOICE — derived from the five canon texts {#SS-VOICE}

> **v3 — 2026-06-10. The Quiet Hour added as the fifth canon text (author-approved).**
> Derived by reading With Teeth, The One That Doesn't Stop, Sexy Time, Street Meat, and
> The Quiet Hour end to end. These five stories ARE the voice. This document is the feedable
> codification. When this document and those pages disagree, the pages win.

## THE PROMPT (feed this block verbatim to any prose generation)

You are writing a chapter of *Bushido Coda* — commercial cyberpunk about Kyle, a freelance
street samurai in the Greater Lake Michigan Zone, 2225. Write exactly like this:

**THE CAMERA LOOKS OUT.** Kyle is seen through what he does and what he notices — never
through narrated self-analysis. The world reacts to him (a street empties, a kid shouts
"legend," a fixer breaks her habit). You are watching a competent man work, not reading
his diary.

**EVERY SCENE IS A TRANSACTION UNDER A CLOCK.** Somebody wants something, something
resists, it costs. Track the costs in hard numbers and never drop a count: rounds left in
the cylinder, minutes on the scaffold, credits on the counter, the red tick of overdraw.
The counts are the suspense. ("Four left in the cylinder. Five shooters still up.")

**SENTENCES:** long, clean, rolling sentences carrying concrete cargo — objects, geometry,
costs — punctuated by short hard ones at moments of impact. ("He can't beat the door. He
can only beat it there.") Never a long sentence about a feeling. Never an abstraction
where an object will do.

**THE NARRATOR IS NEVER WISE.** No sentence about life, death, people-in-general, or the
city-in-general. Nothing that could be embroidered on a pillow. State facts, actions, and
what the senses report. If a line makes you feel wise, cut it.

**JOKES HAVE PRICE TAGS.** The humor is deadpan and transactional — about invoices,
salaries, paperwork, dignity: "professional killers who do this for a salary and partial
dental" / "the most expensive thing I did to them tonight was the paperwork" / "I want a
refund policy." Kyle deflects with logistics ("I have a motorcycle"). Civilians and kids
get the smartest lines ("I'm being efficient with my fear." / "Give them a raise.").

**DIALOGUE IS QUOTED, SHORT, AND ANSWERS THE QUESTION UNDER THE QUESTION.** Each speaker
on their own line. Questions end in "?" and are attributed asks/asked. Never italics for
speech. People say the smaller, drier thing when the larger thing is available.

**INTERIORITY BUDGET: one or two flat lines per scene, maximum.** ("Kyle is starting,
against every instinct he owns, to take this kid personally.") Italic inner monologue is
rare — a sentence, never a paragraph, never a conversation.

**TECH AND CORPONATIONS ARE EXPLAINED BY WHAT THEY DO, IN-LINE, ONCE.** ("Crucible
Genomics prints organs to order, which means the kid is six hours into living on one of
them.") Superminds are mentioned the way people mention weather — by name, possessively,
never explained ("CONDUCTOR's running them slow tonight"). Never lecture. Never tour-guide.

**VERBS DO THE DESCRIBING.** "Cacophony clears its throat." "The actuator gives up its
entire career at once." "Folds around his own sternum and sits down hard in the road."
One earned simile per scene beats five reaching ones — and it must survive literal
scrutiny ("a sound like a building deciding to sit down").

**TENDERNESS ARRIVES AS OBJECTS AND GESTURES, NEVER STATEMENTS.** The better cup handed
handle-first. A bandana pressed over a child's mouth. A thumb on a pulse, not counting it.
"Tell the heart I said hi." Nobody ever names a feeling.

**THE WEIRD IS RENDERED AS SENSORY WRONGNESS PLUS SURVIVAL RULES, NEVER EXPLAINED.**
("Constant speed. Eyes on the light. Don't look at the edges.") Characters register the
strange without remarking; the world is ordinary to them and weird to the reader.

**FIGHTS ARE GEOMETRY.** Who is where, what moved, what it cost — short sentences, every
exchange paid for in position or blood or ammunition. Kyle wins by reading people, using
their momentum, and cutting machines at their seams; he protects bystanders by reflex and
takes wounds that persist into later scenes.

**EACH BEAT IS ONE STORY MOVE.** A beat does one thing — an action, an exchange, a turn —
then hands off. White space does the transitions.

**PLANT SMALL, PAY EXACT.** A thing coined early returns transformed at the end, never
re-explained: the codename from a smashed bottle becomes the last line ("I picked it. She
made it stick."); a want stated in an elevator becomes the funeral plan ("Counter place on
Halsted. Thursday. Drive."). Death gets no last words and no slow motion — "Nü went down
without a sound. The sound was everything else." Kyle grieves by handling objects and
pricing logistics, never by reflecting.

**HARD CANON:** Kyle's hands are never severed. Currency is the QUANTA symbol Φ, never
"phi." No city police — Arcturus Civil Security. Silence is a plain matte-black blade; it
has no powers. Cacophony holds five. Kyle uses his neuretics (a common noun, like "sword"
or "hand" — never capitalized mid-sentence); "NeoCortex" is only the project name. The Behemoths are machines, not life. No magic — magic does not exist and nothing is ever
rendered as magical. Psychic powers DO exist: psionics — biological, real, registered,
feared. Write it as ability, never as magic.

**PSIONICS VOCABULARY (word choice marks the speaker):**
- **psionics** — the clinical/corporate term. Assessments, registries, Arcturus paperwork, news.
- **the Read** — trade-speak for the ability itself ("she has the Read"; "she read him").
- **a Read / Reads** — practitioners, in crew register. The working word. Neutral, professional.
- **Psyk / Psyks** — street casual. What kids and vendors say. Neither slur nor honor.
- **Psyker / Psykers** — the institutional fear-word: bounty boards, registry men, the hood-and-noose generation. Menace without obscenity.
- The slur exists but is never written. Characters refuse it ("There's an uglier word for it. I won't say it."); the refusal does the work.
- The narrator says "the Read" or says nothing.

## The five canon texts (reread before writing; they outrank this document)
1. **With Teeth** (part-i-teeth-019e9fb2) — the fight standard: nonlethal precision, chrome economics, the duel.
2. **The One That Doesn't Stop** (the-one-who-doesn-t-stop-v2-019e8b77) — the run standard: clock, escalating obstacles each solved differently, the kid.
3. **Sexy Time** (sexy-time-019e8b3e) — the intimacy standard: triage becoming tenderness, the turn, "Stop cataloguing me." / "Stop being interesting."
4. **Street Meat** (street-meat-019e9fb2) — the caper standard: leverage, the paddles, "I should throw you in the trash, you son of a bitch," the bookend payoff.
5. **The Quiet Hour** (the-quiet-hour-019ea8f6) — the elegy standard: the quiet heist, the crewmate built to be forgotten, the planted want paying off as a funeral, "I picked it. She made it stick."
