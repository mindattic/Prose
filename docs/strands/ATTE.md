# STRAND BIBLE — Attendance (ATTE) {#SS-ATTE}

> **Purpose.** The dry spine of Yemina Fola's investigation: three missing children, three neuretics
> burnout flags, one quiet pattern the system logged as truancy. What happens, not how it reads.
> When prose and spine disagree, fix one in the same change. See [[../BIBLE]] for universe laws.

## 0. What this story IS {#SS-ATTE-0}

A procedural investigation story. A cross-district truancy investigator follows a bureaucratic
anomaly — three children logged as unexcused absences — and discovers that the city's most
ordinary-sounding problem is a cover for something the city can't officially acknowledge.

It ends on institutional ambiguity, not triumph. Yemina files a report. The report goes somewhere.
She doesn't learn where.

**This is NOT:**
- A Kyle story. Kyle does not appear.
- A conspiracy thriller. The scale is deliberate small: three children, three flags, one
  investigator with a gray contractor-issue car.
- A rescue story. Whether the children are recoverable when Yemina finds the pattern is a
  separate question from solving the investigation.
- A CorpoNation villain story. The antagonist is the system's architecture — not a person.

## 1. The central object {#SS-ATTE-1}

**The folder.** Nadia brings it to Bear (a secondary contact) at the dock gate at 0540. He holds
it for four minutes without opening it. Then he puts it in his locker. The folder is not what
matters; what matters is that someone hand-delivered it to a man who wasn't the investigator —
which means the case has already left the official channel.

Yemina's version: the school roster. Three names. Three timestamps. The timestamps don't match
the burnout flag timestamps by 4–11 minutes each.

The gap is the whole investigation. Four to eleven minutes is enough time to move a child.

## 2. The wound (background, never stated on the page) {#SS-ATTE-2}

The Pilsen Veil cross-district contract is a consolation posting. Yemina was on a major-case
team before; she was moved when she filed a report that implicated a CorpoNation sub-contractor
in a different cover-up that was "resolved administratively." The investigation she's running
now is low-prestige. Nobody expects her to find anything.

This is why she finds it. She has nothing left to protect career-wise. She is the investigator
who already filed the one report that cost her the interesting cases, so she will file another.

**Rule: her history never surfaces in this story.** It is the engine, not the content. Her
methodical, unsentimental pace is its effect. She has learned that careful documentation is the
only lever she controls.

## 3. Character rules for this story {#SS-ATTE-3}

**Yemina Fola (POV):**
- Cross-district truancy investigator on an Arcturus district contract.
- Drives a gray contractor-issue car that has "stopped being argued with."
- Her competence is archival. She is not a detective; she is a bureaucrat who learned to
  read bureaucracy as a weapon.
- She doesn't rush. She doesn't emote on the page. The prose shows what she observes,
  in the order she observes it, without editorial comment.
- She has seen every institutional evasion and files each one correctly.

**The school android (Aldiss-Mwangi Community Learning Center):**
- A 2190s reception design: built to read as warm and approachable by 2190s standards.
- What 2190s-era warmth looks like from 2225 is its own uncanny texture.
- Not a character — a bureaucratic interface. Treat it accordingly.

**Bear (supporting; secondary contact):**
- The one who receives Nadia's folder at the dock gate.
- Does not open it for four minutes. This is the whole scene.
- Does not appear again in the story. He is a relay point.

**Nadia:**
- The contact who hand-delivers the folder.
- Carries it pressed against her chest like she is keeping it from a wind that doesn't exist.
- She does not explain why she brought it to Bear instead of Yemina directly. She leaves when asked.

## 4. Narrative locks {#SS-ATTE-4}

1. **The gap is four to eleven minutes.** The flag timestamps and the departure timestamps
   don't align. This is the whole case. Do not add a more dramatic inciting discovery.
2. **Yemina is the system's best reader, not its opponent.** She doesn't fight the
   bureaucracy. She uses it. The report she files is correct and complete.
3. **The children are not recovered on the page.** Their fate is the open question the
   report creates. Whether an answer ever comes is not this story's problem.
4. **Bear doesn't open the folder for four minutes.** This beat is locked. He sits with it,
   then puts it away without reading it. That's the scene.
5. **The contractor-issue car stays gray.** It is not redeemed. It doesn't become significant.
   It is the texture of a consolation posting.

## 5. Story structure {#SS-ATTE-5}

44 beats across 4 chapters. The investigation proceeds:

### Chapter 1 — The Car and the School
Yemina drives the contractor car through the Pilsen Veil. The Aldiss-Mwangi Community
Learning Center: four stories of poured concrete, each repair cycle a geological deposit.
The 2190s-era android at the reception desk. The roster. Three names.

### Chapter 2 — The Flags
The neuretics burnout flags. Each one filed to a different sub-authority: one to the
district medical registry, one to the CorpoNation that manufactured the hardware, one
to the school's own records system. Nobody filed to all three. Nobody had reason to look
across all three. Yemina does.

### Chapter 3 — The Gap
Four to eleven minutes. The timestamps are verifiably inconsistent. She doesn't speculate
about what filled them. She documents the inconsistency in three parallel filings.

### Chapter 4 — The Folder
Nadia at the dock gate. Bear with the folder. Four minutes. The locker. Yemina's report
goes in. The story ends where the investigation hands off to something she cannot follow.

## 6. Register and voice {#SS-ATTE-6}

Close-third, Yemina's POV. The city is observed the way a filing system is observed:
accurately, without sentiment, with attention to what doesn't fit.

**What her voice notices:**
- Institutional age. The building that "stopped being argued with." The android whose
  warmth is calibrated to a decade that is now ancient.
- Timestamps. She reads time the way a forensic accountant reads ledgers.
- What is filed where, and to whom, and what is therefore invisible to anyone who only
  looks in one place.

**What her voice does NOT do:**
- Express frustration. She has already used up frustration. What remains is method.
- Marvel at the city. It is her jurisdiction. It is the object of her work.
- Tell the reader what the facts mean. They mean what they mean. The reader can add.

**Tone:** Administrative horror is the register. Not the cyberpunk spectacle register —
the quieter one. The one where a child is logged as an unexcused absence and the system
moves on to the next item. Yemina's competence makes this visible, not dramatic.
