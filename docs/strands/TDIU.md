# STRAND BIBLE — The Door Is Unlocked (TDIU) {#SS-TDIU}

> **Purpose.** The dry spine of Pixel's origin story: how she got from Iowa to Unit 2E of The Pivot
> and became the person who leaves her door unlocked. What happens, not how it reads. When prose and
> spine disagree, fix one in the same change. See [[../BIBLE]] for universe laws;
> [[../books/bushido-coda-strands-bible]] for Pixel's role in BCODA.

## 0. What this story IS {#SS-TDIU-0}

A coming-of-age story. A country girl rides the Pulse from Iowa into GLMZ and learns three things
in order: (1) the city is faster than she is; (2) the institutions that look like help are the
danger; (3) she can solve her own problems.

It ends on a choice, not a triumph. She leaves her door unlocked because she has decided to — not
because she doesn't know better. She knows better now.

The final image is her neighbor across the hall with something wrapped in cloth under his arm. She
asks if it's a sword. He doesn't answer. She goes inside. She leaves the door unlocked.

**This is NOT:**
- A Kyle story. He appears in one beat. He says nothing.
- A chosen-one story. She solves a small, concrete problem with technical skill.
- A violence story. No weapons drawn; no fights. The resolution is procedural.
- A romance setup. The final beat is curiosity, not longing.

## 1. The central symbol {#SS-TDIU-1}

**Her mother's boots.** Big black work boots, one size too large. She's been padding the toes with
folded paper since she took them out of her mother's closet. She wears them anyway — not because
they fit but because she has decided she will grow into them.

**The arc of the boots:** In Beat 1 the toes are padded. In Beat 13 she notices she hasn't
re-padded them after unpacking. She looks at the boots. She leaves them as they are.

The boots are never explained to anyone in the story. They don't need to be.

## 2. The wound (background, never stated on the page) {#SS-TDIU-2}

Her mother had a licensed SNT bridge — the kind with a warranty and a hotline number. It failed
at month eight of integration. There was no one to report it to that wasn't owned by the same
CorpoNation that sold the bridge. Her mother died in Iowa while the licensed industry's automated
response system generated case number RX-2217-44091 and escalated it to a team that responded
in eleven business days.

Pixel was nineteen. She inherited the tools, the boots, and the understanding that the only
hardware she can trust is hardware she can put her hands on.

This is why she came to GLMZ: unlicensed neuretics calibration is where the real work is. The
licensed industry calls it gray-market. She calls it the only market where the work actually works.

**Rule: the wound never surfaces in this story.** It is the engine, not the content. The ACS
encounter in Beat 6 echoes it — another institution generating paperwork while missing the point —
but the echo is never named.

## 3. Character rules for this story {#SS-TDIU-3}

**Pixel (pre-handle; she's Vera here, but the name is never used on the page):**
- **Her handle "Pixel" is GLMZ-acquired.** She did not arrive with it. It is not used in this
  story. She arrives as no one in the city knows her yet.
- **Her unlicensed hand-enhancement was done in Pilsen, age 19** — after she arrived, not before.
  She is unsentimental about it. It does not appear in this story.
- She is 19. She moves like someone who grew up handling livestock — economical, present, with
  good instincts for when something's wrong.
- She's not naive. She is *new.* There is a difference. She's been studying GLMZ for two years.
  She knows the theory. She doesn't know the texture yet.
- Her competence is quiet and total. She doesn't narrate what she's doing. The prose shows hands,
  decisions, results.
- She doesn't ask for help a second time. Beat 6 is the first and last time she goes to anyone else.
- She is allowed to be scared. Once. In the stairwell after ACS. It passes.

**The ACS officer:**
- Professional, pleasant, efficient. Not a villain. He is a system doing what systems do.
- He is not personally threatening her. He is generating a file. This is exactly the point.
- Never name him.

**Kyle:**
- One beat. Hallway. Carries Silence wrapped.
- He looks at her. He does not speak. He goes inside.
- Do not give him interiority. He is observed, not rendered.

## 4. Narrative locks {#SS-TDIU-4}

1. **She solves it herself.** No one helps her recover the kit. Not Kyle, not a neighbor, not a
   friend. The resolution is entirely hers.
2. **ACS is not evil — it is unhelpful in a structural way.** The officer is not corrupt. The
   institution does not serve her interest because her interest is not its mandate. This is worse
   than corruption; it is accurate.
3. **No weapons, no violence.** The city has violence in it; it doesn't touch her in this story.
   The resolution is an RF broadcast and an unlocked door.
4. **The boots are never explained.** Symbol, not metaphor. Never state what they mean.
5. **Kyle says one thing.** The question "Is that a sword?" goes unanswered. Instead, from behind
   his closed door: *"Lock yours."* He doesn't explain himself. She considers it. She doesn't.
   The unlocked door is her choice made *against* his advice — deliberate defiance, not symbol.
6. **The door is left unlocked deliberately.** She knows the city now. Locking the door would be
   the scared choice. Leaving it unlocked is the chosen one.

## 5. The 14-beat authorial spine {#SS-TDIU-5}

Beats are events. Not prose.

### Beat 1 — Cedar Rapids Pulse Station, 05:47 {#SS-TDIU-5-B1}
She boards the Pulse for GLMZ. Inventory: one bag (clothes), her mother's primary hardware kit
in a hard case, secondary kit in a shoulder pouch, the boots she's wearing, a scrap of paper
with a name and an address. The boots' toes are padded with folded paper. She doesn't look back
at Iowa.

### Beat 2 — Arrival, GLMZ Pulse Terminal {#SS-TDIU-5-B2}
The vertical scale hits her before the sound does. She navigates by the transit map she memorized.
Moves like she knows where she's going. A kid tries to bump her; she catches the wrist without
thinking — farm reflexes — and releases it without a word. The kid reads her differently now. She
keeps moving.

### Beat 3 — The Pivot, Unit 2E {#SS-TDIU-5-B3}
She pays first and last. Functional unit. Adequate. Hers. She notices the latch on 2D — across
the hall — is worn; someone opens it without the full mechanism, just pressure and push. She
doesn't know why she notices this. She sets down the kit and sits on the floor.

### Beat 4 — West Town Street Market {#SS-TDIU-5-B4}
Out for supplies: calibration fluid, a thermal coupling, food for three days. She's reading the
geography by walking it — price codes, sight lines, who's watching whom, where the gray zones
feel different. She buys what she needs. She is careful with the case.

### Beat 5 — The Incident {#SS-TDIU-5-B5}
Classic distraction. Someone asks for help reading a transit map. A second person passes close.
She registers it a beat too late. When she turns, the primary case is gone. Both people are gone.
She is in the middle of the street with her secondary pouch, her boots, and nothing else. She
stands still for three seconds. Then she walks.

### Beat 6 — Arcturus Civil Security {#SS-TDIU-5-B6}
The office is clean and professional. The officer pulls up the form. CorpoNation affiliation?
Freelance. Stolen hardware — neuretics calibration equipment? She says yes. He slows. Licensed?
Working on it. He asks what spec of hardware, what kind of calibration. She watches his hands —
he's not taking a complaint, he's building a file. She says: never mind, I think I left it at
the station. He says he's noted the inquiry. She leaves. In the stairwell she stands in the
manufactured air and recalibrates everything she thought she knew about cities.

### Beat 7 — The Room, 14:00 {#SS-TDIU-5-B7}
Back in 2E. She sits on the floor with the secondary kit open. She runs the options: returning
to Iowa (listed, crossed off), buying replacement tools (she can't; eighteen years of calibration
history in that hardware, irreplaceable), the tracer. She built a low-frequency handshake signal
into the kit's chassis during the last overhaul. It's for calibration sync. It's also a tracer,
if she's in range.

### Beat 8 — Signal Trace {#SS-TDIU-5-B8}
She builds a receiver from the secondary kit in under two hours. Music in one ear — a habit, not
a preference; it occupies the background half of her brain while the hardware half works. The
signal is there. 2.3 km. It hasn't moved in four hours.

### Beat 9 — Follow {#SS-TDIU-5-B9}
She walks to it. Gray zone between territories. She reads the seam markers, the unspoken codes.
Makes herself unremarkable — pace, posture, eyes. A block with three businesses that have no
signs. She finds the signal in the one that looks like a repair shop and isn't.

### Beat 10 — Observation {#SS-TDIU-5-B10}
An hour across the street. Two people visible. Her case is there — she can see the corner of it
under a table. She counts: entry, exit through the back, one potential blind spot. She goes home.

### Beat 11 — The Intervention, 03:00 {#SS-TDIU-5-B11}
She's modeled ACS's RF sweep protocol since the office visit — irregular cadence, publicly
visible on a signal monitor. At 3 AM she sends a burst: her kit's ID broadcasting "unregistered
neuretics hardware, no licensing affiliation" on the ACS inquiry frequency. Accurate location tag.
She goes downstairs and buys coffee from the 24-hour counter. She watches the street.

### Beat 12 — 04:30 {#SS-TDIU-5-B12}
She walks back. Lights off. Door unlocked — they left fast. She walks in, picks up the case.
Checks it in 90 seconds. Her hands know the case from the inside. Everything is there. She leaves.

### Beat 13 — The Setup {#SS-TDIU-5-B13}
She unpacks. The primary kit goes on the window ledge that will become her workbench. Secondary
kit wired in. Music through speakers this time, real music, the kind that takes up half the shelf
she doesn't have yet. The room begins to be hers.

She's sitting on the floor in her mother's boots when she notices: she hasn't re-padded the toes.
She looks at the boots. She leaves them as they are.

### Beat 14 — The Hallway {#SS-TDIU-5-B14}
She comes out to leave the boots by the door — she does this at home in Iowa, the boots stay at
the threshold. The man from 2D is in the hallway. She's seen him twice before, quick impressions.
This time he's carrying something long wrapped in cloth under his arm. She looks at the shape of
it. She says: "Is that a sword?"

He looks at her. The pause is longer than it should be. He goes inside. She goes inside.

She leaves the door unlocked.

## 6. Register and voice {#SS-TDIU-6}

This story is close-third, Pixel's POV. Not Kyle's, not omniscient. She is the camera.

**What her voice notices:**
- Hardware before people. She reads a room for its technical state before its social state.
- Calibration data. The worn latch on 2D. The ACS officer's keyboard cadence. The sweep signal
  pattern. These are not metaphors. They are observations.
- Music as a throughline. What she's playing when. Never named aloud — but the prose registers it.

**What her voice does NOT do:**
- Explain her own emotion. She acts; the reader infers.
- Refer to her mother's death. It is the wound; it doesn't bleed on the page here.
- Marvel at GLMZ. She's been studying it for two years. She's catching up between theory and
  experience, not experiencing wonder.

**Tone:** The New Cyberpunk register applies — administrative horror is mundane, the strange leaks
through seams, money is the language. But the register here runs *warmer* than BCODA because the
protagonist is 19 and we are watching her become herself. The warmth is not softness. It is the
warmth of competence finding its footing.
