# STRAND BIBLE — The Door Is Unlocked (TDIU) {#SS-TDIU}

> **Purpose.** The complete spine of Pixel's origin story: how she got from Iowa to Unit 2E of The
> Pivot and became the person who leaves her door unlocked. When prose and spine disagree, fix one
> in the same change. See [[../BIBLE]] for universe laws; [[../books/bushido-coda-strands-bible]]
> for Pixel's role in BCODA.

## 0. What this story IS {#SS-TDIU-0}

A coming-of-age story. A country girl rides the Pulse from Iowa into GLMZ and learns three things
in order: (1) the city is faster than she is; (2) the institutions that look like help are the
danger; (3) she can solve her own problems.

It ends on a choice, not a triumph. She leaves her door unlocked because she has decided to — not
because she doesn't know better. She knows better now.

The final image is her neighbor across the hall carrying something wrapped in cloth. She asks if
it's a sword. He doesn't answer. She goes inside. She leaves the door unlocked.

**This is NOT:**
- A Kyle story. He appears in one beat. He says nothing meaningful.
- A chosen-one story. She solves a small, concrete problem with technical skill.
- A violence story. No weapons drawn; no fights. The resolution is procedural.
- A romance setup. The final beat is curiosity, not longing.
- A story where Bear appears. Bear is not in this story.
- A story where corporate AIs pursue her. No Docent, no corp-entity antagonist.

## 1. The central symbol {#SS-TDIU-1}

**Her mother's boots.** Big black work boots, one size too large. She's been padding the toes with
folded paper since she took them out of her mother's closet. She wears them anyway — not because
they fit but because she has decided she will grow into them.

**The arc of the boots:** In Beat 1 the toes are padded. In Beat 14 she notices she hasn't
re-padded them after unpacking. She looks at the boots. She leaves them as they are.

The boots are never explained to anyone in the story. They don't need to be.

## 2. The wound (background, never stated on the page) {#SS-TDIU-2}

Her mother had a licensed SNT bridge — the kind with a warranty and a hotline number. It failed
at month eight of integration. There was no one to report it to that wasn't owned by the same
CorpoNation that sold the bridge. Her mother died in Iowa while the licensed industry's automated
response system generated case number RX-2217-44091 and escalated it to a team that responded
in eleven business days.

She was nineteen. She inherited the tools, the boots, and the understanding that the only
hardware she can trust is hardware she can put her hands on.

This is why she came to GLMZ: unlicensed neuretics calibration is where the real work is. The
licensed industry calls it gray-market. She calls it the only market where the work actually works.

**Rule: the wound never surfaces in this story.** It is the engine, not the content. The ACS
encounter echoes it — another institution generating paperwork while missing the point — but the
echo is never named.

## 3. The VacCell Drone {#SS-TDIU-3}

She built it herself in her mother's barn over the winter after the harvest. It is:
- A vacuum-cell buoyancy unit: a rigid shell maintaining partial vacuum for positive lift
- Two pairs of dragonfly wings (rear pair slightly longer than front) for thrust and maneuvering
- A camera eye mounted beneath for downward surveillance
- Charged off a standard power line; handshakes with her primary kit's tracer signal every four seconds

**VacCell physics tie to aeroblocs:** An aerobloc is built on the same vacuum buoyancy principle —
a rigid shell, partial vacuum, positive lift — scaled up from the size of a hand drone to the size
of a city block. When she sees the drifting aerobloc through the Pulse window during The Blur
transit, she recognizes the physics immediately. Same principle. Factor of ten thousand in scale.
Both of them, in the air.

**The VacCell in the story:**
- Beat 1 (Cedar Rapids): She launches it for a final orbit of the platform before boarding
- Beat 9 (The Room): She identifies it as her scouting tool for the recovery plan
- Beat 11 (Launch): She sends it as an Exo scout — piloting remotely through GLMZ streets
- Beat 12 (Follow): It navigates to the signal location and hovers outside the lit window
- Beat 13 (Observation): Its camera confirms the case location and room layout

The VacCell is her Exo rig. Flying it remotely is the Exo part of her skillset.

## 4. Her Handle: "Pixel" {#SS-TDIU-4}

**Her handle "Pixel" is GLMZ-acquired.** She did not arrive with it. She arrives as no one in the
city knows her yet. The handle is given to her in Beat 4 (The Pivot, Unit 2E) by a watching
neighbor when she performs a pixel-precise calibration fix on the building's climate controller
firmware. It sticks to her even though she doesn't choose it.

Her internal name (never used on the page) is Vera.

## 5. Her Three-Class Archetype {#SS-TDIU-5}

She is a third Slicer, a third Exo operator, a third Channeler — a full-stack Engineer of the
unlicensed variety.

| Class | What it looks like in this story |
|---|---|
| **Slicer** | Cracks the building management terminal's proprietary service menu (Beat 4); models ACS's RF sweep protocol and broadcasts the intervention signal (Beat 14) |
| **Exo** | Pilots the VacCell remotely through GLMZ streets and hovers it outside the target building (Beats 11–13) |
| **Engineer** | Diagnoses and corrects the building climate controller's temperature calibration offset (Beat 4); builds the signal receiver from the secondary kit in two hours (Beat 10) |

Her competence is demonstrated through hands and results, never narrated.

## 6. The Blur and the Aerobloc {#SS-TDIU-6}

**The Blur** is the transitional zone between GLMZ and anywhere else — a geographic and
jurisdictional liminal space where corponation coverage thins and atmospheric management loosens.
Riding the Pulse from Iowa, you pass through it.

In Beat 2 she sees a **drifting aerobloc** through the Pulse window. An aerobloc is a city block
(four to six stories, infrastructure included) built on vacuum-cell buoyancy for voluntary elevation
above the GLMZ surface grid. This one is **unmoored** — its tethers failed or cut — and it drifts
freely on thermal currents through The Blur. It is not in distress; it is simply going where the
thermals take it.

**What she sees:** Four or five stories, a corner unit, a parking structure on the north end with
cars in it, all of it in the air at around three hundred meters, traveling laterally at a speed
wrong for anything that large. The windows catching odd Blur light. The whole block unhurried,
unmoored, going.

**What she recognizes:** Same vacuum buoyancy principle as the VacCell she built. Same rigid-shell
physics, scaled by a factor she doesn't want to calculate because the number would be too large
to carry meaning. She built hers in a barn in Iowa. Someone built that one with nine thousand
engineers and a fabrication facility the size of a town. Both are in the air.

She files it. She keeps watching the window. She does not expect to feel anything about it and
she is wrong.

## 7. Character Rules {#SS-TDIU-7}

**Pixel (Vera, never named on the page):**
- She is 19. She moves like someone who grew up handling livestock — economical, present, with
  good instincts for when something's wrong.
- She's not naive. She is *new.* There is a difference. She's been studying GLMZ for two years.
  She knows the theory. She doesn't know the texture yet.
- Her competence is quiet and total. She doesn't narrate what she's doing. The prose shows hands,
  decisions, results.
- She doesn't ask for help a second time. The ACS visit is the first and last time she goes to
  anyone else. After that, she works it herself.
- She is allowed to be scared. Once. In the stairwell after ACS. It passes.
- Her voice notices hardware before people. She reads a room's technical state before its social
  state. Music is a throughline — unnamed, always present during work.

**The ACS officer:**
- Professional, pleasant, efficient. Not a villain. He is a system doing what systems do.
- He is not personally threatening her. He is generating a file. This is exactly the point.
- Never name him.

**The building neighbor who names her:**
- One moment. She names the girl "Pixel" for the calibration fix, not the drone. The precision.
- She is not a character with interiority. She names Pixel and is gone.

**Kyle:**
- One beat. Hallway. Carries Silence wrapped in cloth.
- He looks at her. The pause is longer than it should be. He goes inside.
- From behind the closed door: "Lock yours."
- Do not give him interiority. He is observed, not rendered.
- He says two words through a closed door. Nothing else.

## 8. Narrative Locks {#SS-TDIU-8}

1. **She solves it herself.** No one helps her recover the kit. Not Kyle, not a neighbor, not a
   friend. The resolution is entirely hers.
2. **ACS is not evil — it is unhelpful in a structural way.** The officer is not corrupt. The
   institution does not serve her interest because her interest is not its mandate. This is worse
   than corruption; it is accurate.
3. **No weapons, no violence.** The city has violence in it; it doesn't touch her in this story.
   The resolution is an RF broadcast and an unlocked door.
4. **The boots are never explained.** Symbol, not metaphor. Never state what they mean.
5. **The door is left unlocked deliberately.** She knows the city now. Locking the door would be
   the scared choice. Leaving it unlocked is the chosen one.
6. **Kyle's advice is rejected.** She considers it and decides against it. This is agency, not
   recklessness.
7. **Bear does not appear in this story.**
8. **No corporate entity pursues her.** No Docent AI, no town car, no corporate response to her
   ACS visit. The file sits in the system. Nothing comes of it in this story.

## 9. The 14-beat authorial spine {#SS-TDIU-9}

The story has 14 named beats and interstitial connective beats between them.

### Beat 1 — Cedar Rapids Pulse Station, 05:47
She boards the Pulse for GLMZ. She launches the VacCell for one last look at Iowa from above.
She calls it back. She packs it. She boards with: hard case (primary kit), soft case (VacCell,
homemade), shoulder pouch (secondary kit), her mother's boots (toes padded with folded paper),
a scrap of paper with a name and an address. She does not look back.

### Beat 2 — Through the Blur
On the Pulse car. She passes through The Blur. Through the window: a drifting aerobloc, unmoored,
floating on thermal currents three hundred meters up. Cars in the parking structure. Windows
catching the Blur's strange light. She recognizes the physics — same as the VacCell, scaled
enormously. She watches until it vanishes. She does not expect to feel anything. She is wrong.

### Beat 3 — GLMZ Pulse Terminal
The vertical scale hits her before the sound does. She navigates by the transit map she memorized.
A pickpocket attempts her jacket pocket — she catches the wrist by farm reflex, releases without a
word. She keeps moving. She has a heading.

### Beat 4 — The Pivot, Unit 2E (Adventure + Name)
The Pivot's second-floor climate system is down and 2E can't be rented until it's fixed. She asks
to look at it. She deploys the VacCell through the ductwork to scout the fault (Exo), cracks the
building management terminal's proprietary service menu (Slicer), finds and corrects a temperature
calibration offset in the climate controller's firmware (Engineer). Done in under two hours. She
gets the room. As she carries her things up, a woman in the hall watches the VacCell and says:
"I'll call you Pixel." She doesn't like it. It is already true.

### Beat 5 — First Night
She sets up. Learns the building sounds: the pump cycling every forty minutes, the specific creak
of the second-floor hall, the climate system she fixed running right. Notes 2D — active at odd
hours, exits and returns without pattern. Sleeps eventually.

### Beat 6 — West Town Street Market
Out for supplies: calibration fluid, a thermal coupling, food for three days. The VacCell stays in
the room — too conspicuous. She reads the market by walking it. The calibration fluid kid knows
she's in the Pivot before she tells him. His reader isn't a reader.

### Beat 7 — The Incident
Classic distraction. Someone asks for help reading a transit map. A second person passes close.
She registers it one beat too late. When she turns, the primary case is gone. Both people are
gone. She stands still for three seconds. Then she walks.

### Beat 8 — Arcturus Civil Security
The office is clean and professional. The officer pulls up the form. She watches his fingers —
he's not taking a complaint, he's building a file on her. She says: never mind, I think I left
it at the station. He notes the inquiry. She leaves. In the stairwell she stands in the managed
air and recalibrates everything she thought she knew about institutions.

### Beat 9 — The Room, 14:00
Back in 2E. She runs the options: Iowa (crossed off), replace tools (can't — eighteen years of
calibration history, irreplaceable). The tracer she built into the kit's chassis. The VacCell.
She starts building the receiver.

### Beat 10 — Signal Trace
Receiver built in two hours. Signal found: 1.4 miles, hasn't moved in four hours. She prepares
the VacCell for an urban scout run — wing calibration for street-level turbulence, night
filtering. She finds an eleven-minute ACS sweep gap in the public schedule.

### Beat 11 — VacCell Scout (Exo)
She sends the VacCell north in the sweep gap. Pilot it by camera feed from her room — full Exo
mode, navigating through GLMZ street canyons at low altitude. The VacCell hovers outside the
lit window of the target building. Through the camera: she can see the corner of her case under
the table.

### Beat 12 — Observation
She herself does a midday observation pass: one hour across the street with a hot drink. Two
people, their cycling pattern, the room layout — table, shelving, curtained rear exit. The case
under the table, latch to the wall: catalogued, not opened. She goes home. The VacCell confirms.

### Beat 13 — The Intervention, 03:00
She broadcasts: the kit's chassis ID on the ACS inquiry frequency with accurate location tag.
Twelve seconds. Then she goes downstairs for coffee from the 24-hour counter. She watches the
ACS unit arrive, the two people exit from the rear. She waits for ACS to clear.

### Beat 14 — 04:30
She walks back. Lights off, door unlocked — they left fast. She walks in, picks up the case,
checks it in 90 seconds. Everything there. She leaves.

### Beat 15 — The Setup
She unpacks. Primary kit on the window ledge — her workbench now. Secondary wired in. Music on
real speakers. The room begins to be hers. She is in her mother's boots. She notices she hasn't
re-padded the toes. She looks at them. She leaves them as they are.

### Beat 16 — The Hallway
She comes out to set the boots at the threshold — work boots stay at the door, a rule she carried
from Iowa. Kyle is in the hallway. He carries something long, wrapped in cloth, both forearms.
She looks at the shape. She asks: "Is that a sword?" He looks at her. The pause is too long. He
goes inside. Through the closed door: "Lock yours." She considers it. She goes inside. She leaves
the door unlocked.

## 10. Register and Voice {#SS-TDIU-10}

This story is close-third, Pixel's POV. Not Kyle's, not omniscient. She is the camera.

**What her voice notices:**
- Hardware before people. She reads a room for its technical state before its social state.
- Calibration data. The worn latch on 2D. The ACS officer's keyboard cadence. The sweep signal
  pattern. The VacCell's charge percentage. These are not metaphors. They are observations.
- Music as a throughline. What she's playing when. Never named aloud — but the prose registers it.
- Physical sensations translated into technical language: fear is "the neck going cold before
  the thought catches up."

**What her voice does NOT do:**
- Explain her own emotion. She acts; the reader infers.
- Refer to her mother's death. It is the wound; it doesn't bleed on the page here.
- Marvel at GLMZ. She's been studying it for two years. She's catching up between theory and
  experience, not experiencing wonder.

**Tone:** The New Cyberpunk register applies — administrative horror is mundane, the strange leaks
through seams, money is the language. But the register here runs *warmer* than BCODA because the
protagonist is 19 and we are watching her become herself. The warmth is not softness. It is the
warmth of competence finding its footing.

**Sentence rhythm:** Shorter than BCODA. Accessible. Anglo-Saxon vocabulary preferred. The
character is direct; her prose should be direct.
