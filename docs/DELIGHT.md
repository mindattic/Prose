# DELIGHT — What Readers Love {#SS-DELIGHT}

> **Scope: all universes, all stories. The positive companion to CRAFT.md.**
> CRAFT.md is the floor (the DON'Ts — what pushes readers *out*). DELIGHT.md is the ceiling
> (the DOs — what pulls readers *toward* a 4.75+ beat). Narrower scope still wins on conflict
> (Register > StoryBible > Universe > Base/CRAFT > DELIGHT); DELIGHT is a craft layer, not a
> canon layer. Reach for it when a beat is merely *correct* and you want it *loved*.

**Evidence base.** Every rule below is reverse-engineered from data, not taste. Two corpora:
the **99 highest-scored beats** in the GLMZ review history (reader mean ≥ 4.75 / 5 across ≥ 12
ballots) and **114 praise ballots** (story scored ≥ 88 / 100), mined for the rare places where
readers name what *worked* rather than what to fix. Each rule cites the loved beats it was
extracted from and, where a reader said it out loud, the ballot language. Nothing here is
generic writing advice; if a claim's evidence is thin, it says so.

**Who owns the top decile.** Two stories dominate the 99: **DWIACE** (~40 beats) and **BCODA**
(~21). Everything the top does that the low-scorers don't is concentrated in what those two
stories' beats *do* — see §14. The point of this doc is to make that reproducible everywhere.

---

## 1. Open on a sensory fact that is already a clue {#SS-DELIGHT-1}

**The loved beats almost never open on setup. They open on one concrete perception that a
competent mind is already reading — the body knowing before the mind names it.** The reader is
inside a perceiver, moving, from the first line.

- *"She hears the door before she sees the woman."* — [TLC b4]
- *"The smell reached him first. Turned earth and warm metal... Wes lay still in bed and knew
  the machine had been working in the night."* — [ICFI b1]
- *"It comes from the front-left corner... It arrives the way warmth arrives — before the sound,
  the room going from cold to not-cold in the half-second before her ears turn it into a word."*
  — [DWIACE b5]

**Why it lands:** a cold open on a perceived clue makes the reader a co-detective on line one,
and buys forward momentum before any exposition is spent.

---

## 2. Let a mind read a system and find the seam — show reasoning as physical evidence {#SS-DELIGHT-2}

**The single most-loved move in the whole corpus is competence resolving ambiguity into fact.**
Not deduction announced — deduction *shown* through objects: which way a buckle faces, how a
furrow angles, what a pronoun costs. The reader watches a mind move through a system and catch it.

- *"A right-handed man threading a belt in distress threads the buckle to the right... A left-side
  buckle meant someone else had fastened it."* — [DWIACE b249]
- *"A boy eight months dead does not develop that kind of precision along the way."* — [DWIACE b191]

Reader ballots name this exactly: *"a mind moving through a system and finding the seams"*
(DWIACE / Jessica); *"the cold-instrument precision Gibson uses when Case is running a trace"*
(DWIACE / Archie).

**Why it lands:** readers reward the pleasure of watching a professional turn a room into a
verdict. It is the beat DWIACE built its top decile on.

---

## 3. Give one involuntary body-truth the character can't fake or stop {#SS-DELIGHT-3}

**The most trusted signal of real feeling in the data is the body betraying the mind — one
physical response that fires ahead of, and against, the character's will.** DWIACE's cat-ears are
the corpus's clearest engine of this, but it is a universal move (a jaw pressed molar-to-molar,
a hand flat on a bench).

- *"Her cat ears had flattened all the way back... folded down flat the way an animal folds them
  against a thing it can't fight."* — [DWIACE b494]
- *"His back teeth came together at thirty-one. He didn't decide to; he found them pressed."*
  — [TEST b9]

Ballots single it out: *"the ears as involuntary emotional telemetry"*; *"a smart piece of
biological characterization"* (DWIACE / Archie).

**Why it lands:** one involuntary, physically-specific response is worth a paragraph of named
emotion — it is the body under oath. (This is CRAFT's "one felt response per scene," rendered as
a reproducible move.)

---

## 4. Pay the cost on-page and keep it as a number {#SS-DELIGHT-4}

**Loved beats do not gesture at consequence; they price it, exactly, and never soften the figure.**
An unforgiven number carries more moral weight than any adjective, and readers return to it.

- *"Forty-three. He pays on the twenty-first."* — [TEST b9]
- *"Category C is the smallest: sixty-seven subjects... The cost per subject in Category C is F43.
  The same number she keeps in the petty cash box."* — [TLC b22]

**Why it lands:** a specific, refused number is the genre's cleanest instrument of stakes —
"the human body as infrastructure, the record as resistance" (TLC / Dalary). The character who
keeps the count is the character readers trust.

---

## 5. In a two-hander, offer a hard truth flat — and let it cost the speaker {#SS-DELIGHT-5}

**The relationship-shift two-hander is the second-largest cluster in the 99, and it works when
the truth is delivered without softening, at a price to the one delivering it.** Gentle is how you
let someone keep not hearing you.

- *"When she spoke, she didn't make it gentle. Gentle was how you let a person keep not hearing
  you. 'She's right, Cel.'... 'And I'm telling you the same thing for free.'"* — [DWIACE b168]
- *"'You were the only one who kept paying on the twenty-first.'"* — [TEST b8]

Ballots: *"relational work... drawn with the patience of someone who actually knows what it costs
to work beside a person you can't fully trust"* (VATD / Linda).

**Why it lands:** interpersonal work is the secret-sauce lever in every two-hander; a truth that
costs the teller is the version readers feel. Never sand the edge off it.

---

## 6. Spend the load-bearing image once, hard, then walk away {#SS-DELIGHT-6}

**Loved prose deploys its best image a single time and trusts it. The corpus's most repeated
complaint is the opposite — a good image counted three or four times until it goes numb.**

- *"the arm resting against the dead hull's collar joint. It nudged... like checking a door that
  won't open."* — [ICFI b1]
- *"The brass gasket seated with a sound like a held breath let go."* — [BCODA b262]

The negative case is loud in the ballots: *"The fig tree gets counted four separate times. I got
it the first time"* (DWIACE / Archie); *"the drawer metaphor has been used four times... it is no
longer atmospheric, it is a verbal tic"* (BCODA / Gracelynn).

**Why it lands:** an image spent once is a strike; spent twice it's furniture. Scarcity is the
whole of its power.

---

## 7. End on the act, not the gloss {#SS-DELIGHT-7}

**The loved beats land on a concrete object or action and stop. They cut the interpretive
sentence that tells the reader what to feel.** Sometimes the last line is a single name.

- *"He wrote one line that was not a fact and would never go in evidence: 'It never knew I was
  there.' He underlined it once... and closed the notebook."* — [DWIACE b448]
- The four-minute confrontation with a forty-year architect ends on: *"Yim Seul-ki."* — [IxS b32]

This is the most frequent single note in the improvement lists: *"Cut the final sentence... the
explanatory gloss undercuts it"* (LDGR / Margaret), repeated across DWIACE, BLST, SRZR, VATD.

**Why it lands:** readers want to arrive at the meaning themselves. The gloss is the author
losing their nerve one sentence before the end.

---

## 8. Put the competence in the body, not the monologue {#SS-DELIGHT-8}

**The loved action beats are verbs-first, physics-honest, and use each skill precisely — and they
carry their theme in the choreography rather than in commentary.** BCODA owns this cluster.

- *"Wrist blade on a backhand line, a feint off the elbow that turned into the knee — three edges
  inside a second and a half."* — [BCODA b21]
- *"Cut everything; cross nothing. That was the whole of his code, and the array held the map so
  his hand could keep to it."* — [BCODA b12]

Ballot: *"the corridor fight uses the 'after the choice, before the cost' interval as actual
fight choreography rather than just a motto... you put it in the body, not the monologue"*
(BCODA / Raven).

**Why it lands:** action that demonstrates the theme reads as consequence; action that pauses to
explain it reads as spectacle. Earn the philosophy in the movement.

---

## 9. Build a reversal that recontextualizes without contradicting {#SS-DELIGHT-9}

**The loved revelations rearrange everything already shown without making any of it false — and
the wrongness is often *felt* a half-beat before it's named.** The reader re-reads the past
instead of distrusting the writer.

- *"'I did not decide to help the thread. I decided not to stop it.'... It landed with the weight
  of something that rearranges what came before it."* — [IxS b37]
- *"The word waiting carried something the word missing would not have. Waiting meant he'd spent
  something... And a thing spent wants paying back."* — [DWIACE b302]
- *"From the lake. She had told no one in this city that... every word had been handed to him."*
  — [DWIACE b173]

Ballot: *"reframes the whole operation without contradicting anything established, which is
exactly what a third-act pivot should do"* (IxS / Saint).

**Why it lands:** the best surprise deepens the record rather than voiding it — and a seam the
reader feels before they can name it is a pleasure, so let the body register it before the prose
explains it.

## 10. Let mundane heroism win — someone just does the work and holds {#SS-DELIGHT-10}

**A recurring high-scorer: a character wins not by force but by doing the paperwork, keeping the
record, refusing the offer, standing where the weight has to be borne.** The genre rarely bothers;
readers love it when it does.

- *"nobody was ever going to be able to say the Continuity Office had bought him anything... He ate
  the second one."* — [BCODA b412]
- *"It is the only thing I took off that perimeter that was true and mine to keep, and I have kept
  it true, and I am putting it where it belongs."* — [TEST b27]
- Wen sets his jigs on the vote table: *"A number on a wall and a jig on a table aren't the same
  kind of true."* — [BLST b13]

Ballot: *"somebody just does the paperwork and wins. I respect that enormously. It's the right
kind of mundane heroism"* (BCODA / Genesis).

**Why it lands:** quiet competence against a system is the rarest beat in the genre and one of the
most loved. Let the win be earned in procedure, not muscle.

## 11. Stay present to witness the thing no one else will {#SS-DELIGHT-11}

**Several of the highest grace-notes are a character choosing to bear witness at cost — to be the
one who knows what a death, a grief, or a machine's mourning actually was.** No sentiment; just
presence.

- *"She stayed at the edge of the read and witnessed it, because that was the whole of what she had
  to offer the girl: to be the one who knew what this had actually been."* — [DWIACE b368]
- *"He stood at the edge of the clearing and watched one Behemoth mourn another."* — [ICFI b2]

**Why it lands:** witnessing converts horror and grief into meaning without ever naming the
feeling — the reader supplies the weight. It is how the corpus does earned tenderness.

## 12. Give each narrator a distinct rhythm — sameness is the wall {#SS-DELIGHT-12}

**The loved beats are not one house voice; they span radically different sentence-shapes, and the
variety is itself a driver.** The corpus-wide complaint is monotony — one cadence across every
POV — and the data shows readers scoring *against* it and *for* its opposite.

Evidence is the spread across the 99 itself:
- Stash — clipped surgical present tense: *"Stash runs a code for eight minutes."* — [TLC b6]
- Bear — deadpan military ledger: *"He held it... He counted forty-three distinct voices."* — [TEST b9]
- Corvin — cold analytic, long controlled clauses: *"The pronoun wasn't a slip. It was the first
  word in the message that mattered."* — [DWIACE b191]
- Wes — pastoral, plain, ground-level: *"Corn, tower, corn. They'd been there longer than he had."*
  — [ICFI b1]

Ballots confirm both signs. **Praise:** *"the cadences are varied, the writer knows when to cut
and when to let a sentence breathe"* (RTR / Olivia). **Penalty when absent:** *"the rhythm gets
predictable: large physical observation, metaphorical extension, moral accounting... by Beat 16 I
could feel the cadence coming before the sentence arrived"* (TEST / Melissa); *"their speech
patterns are largely similar, making them less memorable"* (LDGR).

**Why it lands:** distinct per-narrator rhythm is scored, not just noticed. Write a Stash chapter
in Stash's clipped tense and a Bear chapter in Bear's deadpan; monotone is the thing every panel
names. (This is the positive form of the anti-monotony directive: voice is the character — SS-A46.)

## 13. Anchor the uncanny in the bureaucratic — horror through correct bookkeeping {#SS-DELIGHT-13}

**The loved reveals make the monstrous *ordinary*: a predator that pays out in stolen compute, a
protection racket printed as a benefits package, an invoice that prices a threat before it
exists.** The mundane frame makes the horror both believable and colder.

- *"Nobody in that chain loses. The thing feeds... Carrion books the parts... Now eleven people are
  inventory, and the books balance."* — [DWIACE b321]
- *"'It's a protection racket wearing a benefits package. You wrote down everyone I love in order
  of leverage and showed it to me over noodles.'"* — [BCODA b412]

Ballot: *"the invoice as prophecy, the bureaucratic form as the most frightening object in the
room... the horror arriving through correct bookkeeping. That's the genre working at its best"*
(SPRW / Esther).

**Why it lands:** treating the monstrous as routine paperwork is the specific cyberpunk pleasure
the top ballots reach for by name. The flat ledger is scarier than the scream.

## 14. Vary the moves — a palette, not a stamp {#SS-DELIGHT-14}

**These thirteen moves are a palette to distribute across a story, never a template to hit every
beat with. Applied uniformly they recreate the exact monotony CRAFT.md §8 bans — just a more
sophisticated version of it.** §6 (spend the image once) and §12 (distinct rhythm) apply to the
*moves themselves*, not only to the base prose.

Proven on SPRW (2026-07-20): a delight-rewrite that ended nearly every beat on §7's hard stop and
framed every deduction as §2/§4's ledger split readers exactly in half — one cluster loved it
(86.8), the other (50.4) named the *same* "fragment, colon, list, gut-punch" cadence and the
"ledger / column / field" framing as a **new tic**. The moves had become a stamp. The lift was real
but capped by the sameness of its own devices.

- **Never run the same move-shape twice running.** If beat N ends on a kept number (§4), beat N+1
  ends some other way.
- **No single framing metaphor** (ledger, invoice, column, arithmetic, the-books-balance) may carry
  more than two or three beats in a whole story. Vary *how* a mind reasons.
- **Vary the ending.** Some beats end on the act (§7); others on a line of dialogue, an unresolved
  question, a plain image, a beat of quiet. A gut-punch every time is no gut-punch.
- **Let beats breathe.** Not every beat needs a loved move. *Density* of loved beats is the goal;
  *uniformity* of loved moves is the trap.

**Why it lands:** variety is itself the delight. The top-decile stories deploy a *different* move
each time; the flat ones find one move and repeat it. Distribution beats repetition — always.

## Using this doc

DELIGHT is a **generation-time craft layer**, not a checklist to satisfy per beat. No beat does all
thirteen. Reach for the two or three that fit the beat's job: a forensic beat wants §1/§2/§7; a
two-hander wants §5/§3/§7; a setpiece wants §8/§6; a reveal wants §9/§13; a closing grace-note
wants §11/§7/§6. The through-line under all of them: **concrete over abstract, once over twice,
the act over the gloss, and a different rhythm for every voice.**
