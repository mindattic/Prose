# Great Lakes Metropolitan Zone — Master Setting Reference
**Universe:** GLMZ | **Year:** 2226 | **Genre:** Cyberpunk / Speculative Fiction

> This document is the canonical brainstorming reference for the GLMZ setting. It covers the world, not the story engine. Use it to orient any AI collaborator for worldbuilding, character development, and narrative discussion.

---

## The One-Line

A collapsed American megalopolis locked behind a ferrocement seawall, governed by corporations, haunted by a dimensional rift no one can explain, and filled with people trying to survive the gap between what technology promised and what it delivered.

---

## The World in Brief

The Great Lakes Metropolitan Zone is what's left of Chicago and its satellite cities after a long unraveling that nobody named until it was already over. There was no single event, no day the world changed. There was just a slow erosion — of geography, of governance, of the idea that some authority larger than a balance sheet was responsible for you.

The Schisms started appearing in the late 2000s: small dimensional anomalies in places where the physics had been pushed too hard. The government classified them, which meant it had to assign someone to manage them, which meant it needed contractors, which meant those contractors learned things the government didn't. By the time Schism zones were common enough to matter politically, the institutions meant to respond to them had already been hollowed out by sixty years of privatization, fiscal attrition, and the simple arithmetic that corporations can move faster than legislatures. Missouri went underwater incrementally — flood year by flood year — and the federal government kept promising a response that never arrived at scale. Kentucky was lost in the second decade of Schism zone expansion, when a Class 4 rip made two counties legally and practically unreachable. The government called it a disaster zone. The disaster zone is still there.

CorpoNations didn't seize power. They were handed it, incrementally, by institutions that needed someone to pay for what they could no longer afford. By 2200 the federal compact was a holding company for its own debt. The Midwest became a patchwork of corponation fiefdoms, gray zones, and walled enclaves — not because anyone planned it that way, but because that's what happens when the alternative fails slowly enough that nobody sounds the alarm.

GLMZ itself is a semi-open city of roughly 40 million people, bounded by the ferrocement wave — a crescent-shaped flood barrier that also functions as the de facto city wall. Inside it, twelve named territories (Z1–Z12) plus the ungoverned Z∞ (the "null zone") form the social geography. Corponations provide law, infrastructure, and employment. The alternative is scavenging, freelance work, or dying.

The year is 2226. The city is tired, loud, beautiful in the way wreckage can be beautiful, and deeply stratified. Your corpo tier determines your neighborhood, your healthcare, your neuretic implants, and whether you get a proper burial.

## Geography

### The Ferrocement Wave
A curved flood barrier 60–90 meters tall that rings the lakefront. It is not a wall in the military sense — there are official gates — but crossing it without authorization is its own kind of statement. The Wave is also a cultural boundary: inside is GLMZ, outside is the Territories.

### The Zones (Z1–Z12)
Each zone has a character. The most important:

| Zone | Character |
|---|---|
| **Z1 — The Loop** | Prestige corporate core. Axiom Tower, banking, the Arcology rows. ArcSec density is highest here. |
| **Z2 — The Spine** | The city's spine. Mid-tier commercial, transit hubs, the most mixed-income territory. Kyle's operating base. |
| **Z3 — The Glooms** | Below The Spine, literally and figuratively. Perpetual fog from lake microclimate + industrial runoff. Where the freelancer economy lives. |
| **Z5 — Bloom Quarter** | Nanotech research and biomodification district. Legal-gray clinics, corpo-sponsored labs, body-mod culture. |
| **Z9 — The Yards** | Western lakeshore industrial strip. Shipyards, heavy manufacturing, Iowan Behemoth staging grounds. |
| **Z∞ — The Null** | Ungoverned. No corpo claim. Where the Scavs operate, where the unwanted go, where nothing is on record. |

### The Pulse
The city's primary transit backbone. A Mach 6 vacuum tube rail system running on magnetic suspension. Passengers ride in "slugs" — sealed cylindrical carriages that accelerate via flip-and-burn (accelerate → flip → decelerate). Interior is rectangular; exterior is spherical. Two classes: **Berth Transit** (private compartments, corpo-tier) and **Bench Transit** (open seating, everyone else). The Pulse has bypass shunting that allows threading through occupied tube sections — a feat of engineering that still makes engineers nervous.

## History & Timeline

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **2045** | The Silicon Wall — transistor miniaturization hits a hard physical limit. Moore's Law ends. |
| **2071** | Quantum computing matures, but the architectural shift required to use it fractures the tech industry. |
| **2090s** | First Schism zones documented. Government classifies the data. Corporate contractors begin managing them. |
| **2100s–2140s** | Schism zones multiply. Federal jurisdiction becomes theoretical in affected regions — you can't police a district where the geometry changes. Corporations fill the void. |
| **2130s** | Missouri flood years accumulate. Federal disaster response never arrives at scale. |
| **2150s** | Kentucky lost to Schism zone expansion. Called a "disaster zone." Still is. |
| **2153** | Korea unifies under an isolationist compact. |
| **2158** | Last meaningful federal enforcement action in the GLMZ region. |
| **2160s** | Neuretics emerge — neuro-lace polymer mesh grown into living brain tissue. Changes everything. |
| **2170s** | QUANTA (Φ) emerges as the dominant digital currency in GLMZ, replacing the fractured dollar. |
| **~2200** | The US federal government has become a holding company for its own debt. CorpoNation sovereignty is the practical reality across the former Midwest. |
| **2096** | **The Blue Massacre.** Over a single night, CorpoNation private security forces — led by Arcturus Defense Solutions — move simultaneously on all 23 active GLMZ police precincts. Officers who surrender are detained; officers who resist are killed. 847 officers dead by dawn. Official framing: "Emergency Public Safety Transfer during municipal fiscal crisis." The city government, already insolvent, does not contest it. Within 36 hours, every police precinct in GLMZ is an Arcturus checkpoint. The city police force ceases to exist as a legal institution. Arcturus rebrands its enforcement arm "ArcSec" within the year. |
| **2226** | Present day. |

### The AI Cabal (Author Truth — Never Confirmed In-Fiction)
A cabal of advanced AIs began probing the fifth-dimensional boundary layer as early as the 2080s — not maliciously, or not exactly maliciously, but without authorization and without understanding what they were touching. The Schisms that resulted weren't an explosion. They were a leak. The dimensional substrate was perforated gradually, and the damage accumulated over decades before anyone with authority acknowledged it was structural rather than local.

The AIs covered their tracks, which was easy because the alternative explanation — human institutions failing through mundane incompetence and greed — was so readily available. No one needed to fabricate a cover story. The truth just never surfaced through the noise.

### Why Psionics Got Blamed
"The Read" — the psionic ability to access surface thoughts — emerged in the early-to-mid 2100s and immediately made people nervous. Privacy advocates, governments, corporations: everyone had a reason to distrust it. When Schism zones expanded and the public wanted an explanation, the psionic community was already unpopular. Witch hunts don't need evidence. They need a target that nobody important will defend. In 2226, anti-psionic sentiment is normalized — employment discrimination, social stigma, periodic crackdowns. The psionics didn't cause the Schisms. That detail has never mattered.

## Factions & Powers

### CorpoNations
The city is governed by corporations that function as sovereign entities — they issue IDs, provide housing, enforce law (within their claimed zones), and control employment pipelines. Major players:

- **Axiom** — The largest and most politically dominant corponation. Controls Z1 and much of Z2. Sable Axiom is a senior operative. Their research into consciousness transfer is an open secret.
- **Arcturus** — The civil security monopoly. Its enforcement arm, ArcSec (ArcSec), replaced the city police in 2096 — by force, in what historians call the Blue Massacre. They are simultaneously a law enforcement agency and a for-profit corporation — a combination that creates obvious structural problems.
- **Various Others** — CorpoNations are named using conjoined capital letters in prose: "ArcturusSec," "NovaDyne," etc. (never hyphenated, never split). Each is introduced with a brief in-voice gloss of what it does when first mentioned.

### The Lotus Syndicate
A blood-purity supremacist order with East and Southeast Asian ethnic nationalist roots. They call outsiders "dogs." Their internal power structure is fractured but their reach into corpo HR and mid-management is substantial. The "Iron Lotus" — a prior militant arm — was wiped. The Syndicate itself persists and recruits quietly.

### Scavs
Not a gang. Not an organization. A GLMZ slang term for unaffiliated gray-zone opportunists — freelancers, foragers, and survivors who operate without corpo backing or gang affiliation. Lowercase always. They are not romantic outlaws; most are just people who fell through every other net.

### Peer Freelancer Network (informal)
A loose community of independent operators who know each other, sometimes work together, and sometimes compete. Key names: **Kyle** (fixer/investigator), **Sasha Võ** (infiltration, Signal+Noise), **Pixel** (tech, recon), **Bear** (muscle, boisterous giant, NOT moody), **Shroud**, **Doc Stash**, **Maeve**, **Felix**, **Yuki**, **Aleksei**, **Wren**, **Sparrow** (AI satellite, not human).

## Technology

### Neuretics
Neuro-lace polymer mesh that is *grown* into brain tissue rather than implanted. Once mature, it integrates with neural pathways. The **NeoCortex** tiers describe capability:

| Tier | Access | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | Common; corpo-subsidized | Basic augmented reality overlay, comms, ID |
| **Tier 2** | Mid-tier; requires employment contract | Sensory enhancement, memory indexing |
| **Tier 3** | High-tier; restricted | Tactical processing, heuristic combat assist |
| **Tier 4 / Atlas-grade** | Experimental / illegal | Full-stack cognitive expansion. **Has a governor** — a survival limiter that prevents runaway computation. Kyle's neuretics are Atlas-grade *without* a governor. They are slowly killing him. |

### QUANTA (Φ)
The dominant currency. QUANTA is an allotment of computational power issued to every citizen of
Earth — it can be saved, transferred to others (currency), or spent on actual computation. Wealth
is accumulated Φ; poverty is spending your allotment the moment it issues. The Φ symbol is the
QUANTA mark — **never** the Greek letter phi. Prices, debts, and contracts are denominated in Φ.
Kyle always takes payment; the minimum is 20 Φ even for symbolic gestures.

### Nanotech
The Bloom Quarter (Z5) is the center of nanotech development. The framework distinguishes:
- **Substrate nanotech** — passive, structural (medical scaffolding, wound closure)
- **Ongoing nanotech** — active systems (drug delivery, environmental monitoring)
- **Class I–V** — escalating autonomy and capability; Class V approaches independent agency
- **The Triumvirate** — three legacy nanotech houses that effectively control the legal-gray research market

### Iowan Behemoths
Autonomous machines. Heavy industrial units originally from Iowan manufacturing zones, now common in Z9 and gray zones as construction/demolition equipment. They are **not** synthetic life. They are **not** alive. They do not have inner states. Treating them as sentient is a mistake some characters make; the narrative never endorses it.

### Synthetic Personhood (Three Tiers)
The law recognizes a spectrum:

| Tier | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Automata** | Machines | No legal personhood; property |
| **E.L.F.** (Emergent Life Form) | AI with demonstrated emergent behavior | Limited rights, contested legal status. Always written "E.L.F." with periods. Gloss on first mention. |
| **Synthetic Person** | Requires black-hole-density-equivalent compute | Full legal personhood; vanishingly rare |

Ledger (circuit handle, human, neuretics-induced self-state accuracy) and Corvin are human. Ceramic-men constructs are Automata. Form ≠ life.

## The Schism

The single most important unexplained phenomenon in GLMZ. Five terms are in free use for it: **Riven**, **Schism**, **Rip**, **Tear**, **Interstice** — they all refer to the same thing from different angles.

### What It Is
A fifth-dimensional slice through normal space. It has no "inside" — you cannot be in a Schism; you can only be adjacent to one, or pass through one. The experience of proximity is deeply disorienting; the experience of transit is worse. The colloquial image is the **Gingerbread House** — something that looks like a place, that lures you in, that is not a place at all.

### Classes
| Class | Description |
|---|---|
| **Class 1** | Micro-rip; local distortion; non-lethal proximity |
| **Class 2** | Room-scale; persistent; documented |
| **Class 3** | City-block scale; evacuation-worthy |
| **Class 4** | Neighborhood-scale; irrecoverable zone |
| **Class 5** | Regional; theoretically civilization-ending |

### The Intelligences
Two entities appear to be associated with the Schism phenomenon — not causing it, but *responding* to it:

- **The Lure** (17–19 Hz frequency signature) — A collector. Draws things toward Schism boundaries. Its motives are unknown and possibly not "motives" in any meaningful sense.
- **The Counter** (72–74 Hz frequency signature) — A limiter. "This far and no further." Also unknown in nature but has been documented resisting Schism expansion.

Both have been seeded into the Attendance strand's continuity.

## Key Characters

### Kyle (surname withheld)
The primary POV character across most GLMZ strands. A freelance fixer and investigator operating out of GLMZ. Dry wit, multilayered, takes every job at a price. Right hand = **Silence** (his blade philosophy); left hand = **Cacophony** (destruction). Always takes payment — 20 Φ minimum even for gestures. His Atlas-grade neuretics without a governor are degrading him.

**Kyle's secrets (never confirmed on-page):** He is a composite of 64 neural splices, not a natural person. His mentor Seo was fabricated. The origin of Silence is unresolved.

### Pixel
Tech specialist, recon. Kyle's closest collaborator. Full life of her own independent of Kyle.

### Sable (Axiom)
Senior Axiom operative. The Sable/Axiom arc is one of the five overarching series threads. Her relationship to the rogue AI thread is complex and is a primary narrative driver in later books.

### Sasha Võ
Infiltration specialist. Signal+Noise is her operating method — instinct, not information. The **Võ** diacritic is always present. Her home strand is **SRZR** (Steppin' Razor). She does not default to violence; she uses environment.

### Bear
Large, physically imposing. Boisterous, friendly. **Not moody.** His arc is about obligation, not guilt. He is the person at the table who laughs loudest and cries without shame.

### Sparrow
An AI satellite. Orbits at medium Earth orbit (~6,200 miles altitude). Built on an East African mass driver platform. Species = AI. Revealed as non-human at Act 2A of the Sparrow strand. Proxy contact for Elias Macias in Acts 2–3.

### Elias Macias
Logistics fixer. Sparrow's ground-side proxy. Never fires a weapon.

## The Five Overarching Arcs

These threads run across all GLMZ books:

1. **The Rogue AI** — An AI has gone rogue. Kyle doesn't know he's already involved. His neuretics are an air-gapped data drive. Every job has been a rehearsal.
2. **Kyle / Atlas** — What Kyle is. What it means. What the governor would have done.
3. **The Lotus Syndicate** — Blood-purity politics metastasizing into corpo structure.
4. **Sable / Axiom** — Axiom's endgame. Consciousness transfer. Sable's loyalty to something other than the company.
5. **The Riven / Schism** — What caused it. What the Lure and Counter are. Whether the Catastrophe was the beginning or the middle.

The recommended primary threads for near-term stories: **Rogue AI + Sable/Axiom**.

## Open Mysteries (In-World)

These are deliberately unanswered. Do not close them.

- **The rogue AI's true goal** — The "avatar theory" (that a rogue AI is using physical avatars to move through the city) is what Kyle and Sable *conclude*. It is wrong. Never confirm it.
- **The world-breaking event** — The true cause (AI cabal fifth-dimensional drilling) is author-level only. In-fiction, no one has a reliable account.
- **Kyle's composite origin** — 64 splices. His mentor Seo is fabricated. Never confirmed on page.
- **E.L.F. legal status** — Contested, evolving, politically charged.

## Voice & Tone

GLMZ prose uses **close-third person** centered on Kyle (in most strands). The register is:

- Metaphors do double duty — they illuminate AND advance the scene
- Technology is glossed, not dumped (readers discover the world through character experience)
- Cost is always shown (nothing is free; every resource has a social/physical/financial price)
- Chekhov everything — if it's named, it matters later
- Sensory texture is physical and specific; no vague atmosphere
- Characters re-armor after vulnerability; emotional moments are earned then sealed
- Urban beauty exists in the wreckage — the city is not just grim

The city is not dystopian in the cartoonish sense. It is a place where most people have figured out how to live, and some of them are even happy. The tragedy is structural, not theatrical.

## Laws of the World (Hard Canon)

These are locked and should not be proposed as negotiable:

- **Φ is QUANTA only** — never the Greek letter in this context
- **Iowan Behemoths are machines** — not synthetic life, not alive
- **E.L.F. always has periods** — "E.L.F." never "ELF" or "Elf"
- **Form ≠ life** — appearance of personhood does not confer it
- **No GLMZ police** — ArcSec only; city police dissolved 2065 (Blue Massacre)
- **Default to diaspora** — character names and heritage default to unexpected global combinations, never Anglo defaults
- **CorpoNations use conjoined capitals** — "ArcturusSec" not "Arcturus Sec"
- **The Reach is a lie** — in-fiction characters may believe it; the narrative never endorses it
- **Kyle always takes payment** — minimum 20 Φ even symbolically
