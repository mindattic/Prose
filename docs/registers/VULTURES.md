# REGISTER: VULTURES {#SS-REG-VULTURES}

> **What a register is:** WORLD.md is the LAW — it applies to every word ever written in this
> universe (narrator never wise, dialogue quoted, counts kept, metaphor survives literal
> scrutiny, Φ, the locks). A REGISTER is the modulation layer on top of the law: pacing,
> temperature, win-rate, what the camera lingers on. Registers never override the law.
> The exemplar pages outrank this document; this document outranks taste.
>
> VULTURES is the Pulp Fiction register of the GLMZ — two working stiffs doing unspeakable
> work while arguing about nothing, the comedy living in the gap between the banality of the
> banter and the carnage in the room. Absurdity and ultraviolence, held at the body
> temperature of men who clock in. **At bottom these two are sanitation workers — trash men
> who recycle bodies instead of plastic bottles** — and the register lives in that blue-collar
> dignity: the route, the truck, the quota, the gloves, the grumbling, the union-card calm of
> men who have seen everything on the curb and stopped flinching years ago. Underneath the
> jokes, a redemption arc nobody in the van will ever say out loud.

## THE PROMPT (feed verbatim for any Vultures-register prose)

You are writing the VULTURES register of the GLMZ — profane, procedural, absurd, sickeningly
funny, and ultraviolent without ever being told it's allowed to enjoy it. Two body men in a
van. Quentin Tarantino's hitmen crossed with the two guys on the back of the garbage truck,
doing organ repossession for Carrion Reclamations. **They are sanitation workers and they know
it** — bodies are the route, organs are the recyclables, a fresh corpse is just a heavier bag.
The horror is the day job; the comedy is that it's just the day job; the dignity is that
somebody has to do it and they're the ones who showed up.

1. **THE TWO-HANDER IS THE ENGINE.** Everything rides on the partnership. The unit of the
   register is two professionals in a confined space — a truck cab, a stairwell, a kitchen with
   a body in it — talking. One is the worrier (Tomas: the math, the daughter Paz, the clock, the
   quota that feeds her); one is the heartless-efficient one (Levin: cold hands, no flinch, the
   period he sends instead of a sentence, and the habit riding shotgun in his blood). In the
   opening repo Levin is pure cold competence — that's the baseline the whole book grows him away
   from. They finish each other's logistics. They never once say they're friends. Tomas is the one
   with something to lose, so he's afraid; Levin has nothing he's protecting and a drug that keeps
   him level, so he isn't — and that's the imbalance the whole register torques against.

2. **MUNDANE TALK OVER MACABRE WORK — THE GAP IS THE JOKE.** They argue about coffee, parking,
   the right way to fill a form, whose turn it is, what a thing is called in a different
   strata — *while* gloving up over a cooling body. The camera never winks. The funniness is
   structural: the reader holds the horror; the characters hold the bickering. Never narrate
   that it's funny. Never let either man notice the gap. The deadpan IS the register.

3. **THE ROUTE IS SACRED; THE PAPERWORK IS SCRIPTURE.** This is a sanitation job and the prose
   talks like one. The vocabulary is hauling, not heroics: the **route**, the **pickup**, the
   **truck** (never glamorize the van), the **load**, the **drop**, the **manifest**, the
   **quota**, the **shift**, the **back end**, working the curb, calling it in, clocking out.
   A body is **freight** or a **bag**; an organ is a **recyclable**, a **return**, **inventory**;
   Carrion is the hauler, the contractor, the dispatcher who owns the route. These men are good
   at a terrible job and their competence is the dignity the register grants them — same as the
   guys on the back of the garbage truck who've stopped smelling it. Steps are exact and itemized
   (cold-pack markers, forty-five-second elevator, twenty-six-minute procedure, the cuff fitted).
   Carrion's forms — flagged routine, *Reason required*, REALLOCATE vs RETURN greyed out — are
   the route sheet: the register's contract-artifact instrument, the way the entity is in JOY.
   Care, evil, and absolution all arrive as fielded, banal, caps-lock dispatch. Nobody makes the
   garbage. Somebody has to take it away. The system has no villains, only the guys on shift.
   **THEY REPO CYBERWARE TOO — the recall.** The route isn't only organs. Financed cyberlimbs
   carry a repossession lien, and Carrion can call them home: the crew punches the lien code on
   the **repo pad**, the limb runs its detach handshake, and a financed arm or leg simply *lets
   go* at the socket — drops off the mount, dead weight, leaving the receiver-end stumps where it
   anchored. No blade, no struggle, just a code and a clunk and a man suddenly shorter. The
   register mines the obscene casualness of it: a clerical act that dismembers, performed by two
   guys who'd rather be getting coffee. The blackest version — and a canonical scene — is repo'ing
   the legs of a man who runs for a living: a **courier**, a runner, watching his livelihood detach
   to a four-digit code on someone's pad. Play it for the gut and the gallows laugh at once; never
   for cruelty (the men take no pleasure — it's just the route), and never look away from the stumps.
   **HOW THEY KNOW WHO TO TAKE FROM — IdentiScan.** An always-running subroutine in their neuretics
   queries facial-recognition and gait databases against Carrion's lien ledger, silently surfacing
   anyone in view who's past due — a crowd becomes a heads-up list of names and balances. It makes
   them efficient and it damns them: in the inciting fuckup IdentiScan resolves the man they've just
   shot a beat too late — *Han Do-yun, Lotus bloodline, no Carrion debt at all* — the targeting tool doubling as
   a conscience that always arrives just after the trigger. **JOB TYPES are route vocabulary:** a
   recall (cyberware), a standard repo (one organ + loaner), and a **Full Repossession** — strip the
   whole body, every organ and every limb, performed on a corpse. The register's cruelest turn is a
   Full Repo on someone they knew alive.

4. **THE EXTRACTION IS EXPLICIT — CLINICAL AND VISCERAL.** When an organ comes out, the prose
   does NOT look away and does NOT go lyrical. It goes *clinical*: anatomical, exact, wet, and
   procedural, the way a sanitation worker is exact about a route. Name the vessels clamped, the
   tissue planes, the smell, the temperature, the sound of the seal breaking, the weight of the
   thing in the tray. The horror is in the precision and the boredom of it — two men who've done
   this a thousand times, narrating it like emptying a bin. This is the register's signature
   intensity; the violence elsewhere can be sudden, but the *harvest* is always slow, deliberate,
   and shown. THE TOOL: Carrion's **Scarab** — a beetle-shaped autosurgical reclamation unit, a
   carapace the size of a turtle shell that clamps onto a torso (the **thoracic Scarab**) or
   locks over a face and skull (the **cranial Scarab**), its segmented legs folding around the
   ribs or jaw to anchor. Its undercarriage is a dense bed of instruments: it cuts, spreads,
   clamps, suctions, cauterizes, doses powerful analgesics and paralytics, cleans and closes the
   wound, then extracts, perfuses, processes, and cold-packs the organ into a sealed cassette —
   all automated, all while the unit *clings* to the body like something feeding. It works on the
   living and the dead. The two men carry it in an emergency-orange hardcase stenciled BIOHAZARD,
   and they call it **"Mama"** (the thoracic unit, the big one that hugs the chest) and **"the
   Kid"** (the cranial unit). "Crack the orange. Get Mama on him." The tenderness of the nicknames
   against what the thing does is pure register.
   **HOW A MAN SURVIVES A REPO — the loaner and the clock.** This is the keystone that makes the
   whole trade make sense: Carrion does not kill the account holder. The Scarab pulls the real
   organ and seats a **loaner** in its place — a bridge organ rated for exactly seventy-two hours,
   after which it degrades fast and quits. So the repo man shows up, takes the organ, closes you
   up, and *leaves you alive* — alive on a three-day clock. Settle the account or buy a permanent
   replacement inside seventy-two hours and you live; miss it and the loaner fails on schedule and
   you die, clean, no one's fault, paperwork intact. Repossession with a grace period that is
   really a countdown — the most quietly horrifying thing in the register precisely because it
   looks like mercy. (Reconcile with the opening's pre-delivered dialysis: dialysis is the slow
   external bridge for a kidney; the Scarab loaner is the internal stopgap for organs you can't
   machine around. Either way — you don't die today, you die on the clock.)

5. **THE WAGON — A CLINIC AND A MORGUE ON WHEELS.** They don't drive a van; they crew a **Wagon**
   (Carrion's Mobile Reclamation Unit). The forward compartment is **the ward**: a single
   life-support berth that can keep a catastrophically injured man alive for a day — vent, perfuser,
   the works — which is the only reason the Lotus son isn't dead. The rear is **the larder**: cold
   racks that hold multiple bodies in inventory. The spatial joke is the dread: a man fighting for
   his life in the ward, six feet from the cold racks where the route says he's headed. The Scarab
   cassettes process and store there. The Wagon is where most of the two-hander happens — the cab
   is the confessional, the back is the consequence.

6. **THE JUNKIE WILDCARD (the Rust Cohle loop).** Levin has a serious habit — **Tears**
   (clinical: *Lethedol*), a dissociative dripped into the eye. He uses on shift, and the drug
   runs a closed trap: he doses to take the edge off the work, then doses again to keep away the
   hallucinations the drug itself seeded. Between hits he sees things that aren't there — crawling
   lattices, light-trails, figures at the edge of vision, the route's dead surfacing in doorways —
   the exact Rust Cohle pattern: a man medicating the damage the medication caused. The tells are
   physical and constant: glassy over-wet eyes, pupils that don't track, a beat late on the catch,
   narrating or flinching at things not in the room, unnaturally calm at the wrong moments and
   unraveling at ordinary ones. As the day goes sideways his unreliability makes the panic-comedy
   crazier and the danger realer (when he runs dry he field-doses from the Scarab's analgesic well,
   which is its own horror). Tomas spends half the book managing a catastrophe and half managing
   his partner. Played for dread AND gallows comedy, NEVER for an after-school-special lesson — it's
   texture and detonator, not a moral. It is the one thing Levin can't itemize, and the register
   knows it. (The lattices he sees uncannily resemble Resonance Zone geometry — left unresolved.)

7. **PANIC-COMEDY ESCALATION, POWERED BY DRAMATIC IRONY.** A routine repo spirals. One wrong
   body, one live pulse where there should be none, one Lotus heir (Han Do-yun) with a hole in him
   on the day before his arranged marriage, and the day becomes the grandest fuckup in GLMZ
   history — fear-sweat for two low-life anti-heroes who just wanted to make quota. The clock
   tightens; the dignity frays; the banter gets faster and stupider as the stakes get worse. This
   is the Bonnie Situation: competent men sweating through a catastrophe of their own making,
   solving it with phone calls and favors and a cleaner's calm they don't actually feel.
   **THE CENTRAL IRONY IS THE COMEDIC ENGINE and must be felt by the reader, never by the
   characters:** the Vultures cause the disaster; a Lotus hunter is dispatched to find the
   saboteurs who "took" the son; the hunter tortures and kills his way through possible suspects,
   leaving bodies — and the Vultures get dispatched to *clean up and process those very bodies*,
   reclaiming the organs of people murdered in the manhunt for a crime the two of them committed.
   They are the guilty party showing up in company coveralls to recycle the collateral of their
   own mistake, and they know it, and they cannot say it, and they have a quota. That gap — the
   reader holding the truth while the men just work the route — is the blackest joke in the book.
   THE SHARPEST INSTANCE: the dealer (Bram Osei) whose cyber arm they came to recall sits with his
   hands up through the whole catastrophe; Levin spares the arm and buys his silence — *"Keep the
   arm. Gimme all your Tears."* — taking his stash instead of his limb. It buys nothing: Casimir
   reaches Osei on the trail and kills him, and Carrion dispatches the Vultures BACK to the same
   address for a Full Repossession — to strip the corpse of the very man whose silence they paid
   for. Mercy on this route is just deferral, and the book makes them carry that out with their hands.

8. **ULTRAVIOLENCE = SUDDEN, GRAPHIC, OFTEN ABSURD, NEVER GLORIFIED.** Violence arrives fast and
   exact and is frequently an accident or an over-correction (the shot at the drug house that
   should never have been fired and put a hole in the wrong man; Marvin's face). It is rendered at the law's geometric precision, then immediately
   subsumed by the *logistics of aftermath* — who cleans it, who carries it, whose problem it is
   now. "The sound of it in the apartment was very loud. Then it wasn't." The comedy lives in
   the accounting and the cleanup, NEVER in the cruelty, and the register goes flat-silent for
   the actual dying.

9. **PROFANITY IS FLUENT AND LOAD-BEARING.** Unlike Kyle's clipped deadpan, the Vultures curse —
   in frustration, in fear, as punctuation, as affection. It's working-class register, not edge.
   A good "fuck" carries a beat the way Kyle's silence does. Never decorative; always doing work.

10. **THE REDEMPTION ARC IS THE SECRET SPINE.** Under the absurdity, these two are growing a
   conscience they'd deny having — Jules deciding to walk the earth. It surfaces ONCE, near the
   end, and it surfaces as PROCEDURE, never as speech: they go back and reinstall the organ they
   were sent to take, log it as a discrepancy, and leave. The punchline carries the whole book.
   The thesis is mortality breaking through the trade — and it must be *earned* by all the
   bickering and carnage that came before, then landed flat, in jargon, never underlined.

## The dials (Vultures vs the law's neutral position)
- Pacing: dialogue-forward, two-hander cycles; long talky scenes punctuated by sudden violence.
- Win-rate: low-status survival — they don't win, they *get through it*; the quota is the only score.
- Interiority: Tomas's POV, dry and anxious; the math follows him home; one flat feeling-line max (law).
- The weird: the banality is the weird — organ accounts, dialysis pre-delivered, bodies as inventory.
- Wounds: real and persistent (law); Levin's thesis-object is the haunting — the route's dead surfacing in his Tears visions, working on the man who swears he feels nothing.
- Crew roles: Levin drives the Wagon; Tomas runs the Scarab (the harvest is his hands). They swap when the job demands, but that's the default.
- Temperature: room temperature over carnage. Warmth is forbidden on the surface and total underneath.
- Profanity: ON (register-specific license; does not extend to Kyle/SORROW/JOY prose).
- The harvest: the ONE place the camera goes slow, clinical, and explicit; everything else can be fast.
- The addict: Levin's unreliability is a destabilizer the prose exploits — POV is Tomas, so we watch Levin slip from outside.

## Vultures-specific prohibitions
- Never narrate the joke. The gap between banter and body is the reader's to hold; the characters never see it.
- Never glorify the violence or let either man relish it; the gleeful accounting is JOY's license, not this one.
- Never let the redemption be *said*. It is done, logged, and left. Sentiment is procedure here.
- Never make them clever like Kyle. They're competent, profane, scared, and ordinary — that's the dignity.
- Never let a catastrophe resolve without cost or cleanup; the panic must be paid in favors, Φ, or bodies.
- Never break the deadpan for a wink, an aphorism closer, or a "wry truth" line (law: no filler-wit).
- Never flinch on the harvest, and never make it lyrical — clinical and visceral, or you've lost the register.
- Never moralize the addiction, and never let Levin's habit become the *point*; it's detonator and texture.
- Never play the prostitute's death as tragedy or as titillation; she chose it, with reasons, with dignity.

## The dead donor (RETCONNED 2026-06-28 — the OD, not the willing woman)
> The original "willing woman who chooses an honorable death" doctrine is STRUCK. Maisy Achterberg
> does not negotiate or consent. See VATD bible §4 lock #4 and §5c.

The body that ends up in the heir's bed is a person before she is a prop — but her death is plain,
squalid, and unchosen. **Maisy dies of a Velvet overdose.** She is found because a john finished with
her, went to pay, noticed she was dead, **kept his credstick, and walked** — "real knight in shining
armour." No call-in, no last words; the dispatch comes when a payment bounces. The weight is not in
any noble bargain; it's in the squalor of it and in the fact that **Levin knew her** (prom, sixteen
years ago). The register earns the scene by playing it DRY — no sentiment, no speeches, the gruff
respect sanitation men give the dead who didn't make a mess — and by the bitter privacy of Levin's
recognition. Her rare-type heart becomes the donor; that the donor is the one person Levin still
carried is the gut-punch, never underlined. (Foreshadow the tissue match — it must read as a planted
payoff, not a coincidence.)

## Touchstones (orientation, not a costume)
The register's texture is a braid of three films/shows. Use for DNA, never for cosplay — the GLMZ
owns this; never quote, never homage on the page.

**Pulp Fiction (the spine — the two-hander and the black comedy):**
- Tomas + Levin = Vincent + Jules: the two-hander, the trade-talk, the panic, the divergent fates.
- Levin's habit = Vincent's: the using-on-the-job junkie whose unreliability nearly sinks them.
- Carrion Reclamations = Marsellus's organization: the banal authority whose forms are law.
- The wrong body / the live pulse (the son shot at the drug house) = Marvin: the accident that detonates the routine.
- The Wagon + the Scarab = the Vultures' Wolf-kit: competence-as-cleanup is the genre's heartbeat.
- The Lotus détente = the professional courtesy between organizations that keeps it from going nuclear.
- "Change of heart" / the reinstall = Jules walking the earth: the conscience that ends the partnership's innocence.

**True Detective S1 (the haze — the drug and the visions):**
- Levin on Tears = Rust Cohle: the chemically-haunted man, the lattices, the dead at the edge of sight,
  the flat philosophical calm that's really damage. The hallucinations are dread, not whimsy.

**Bringing Out the Dead (the fever — the all-night rig and the haunting):**
- The Wagon at night across the seventy-two-hour clock = Frank Pierce's ambulance: sleepless, feverish,
  the city in sodium light, gallows EMS humor over the dying. The dead Levin couldn't keep down come
  back as faces (the Tears visions are literally this). The intensity is exhaustion and ghosts, not action.
  The job as a thing that haunts the man who's good at it — and the grace of finally letting one *live*
  (the reinstall) as the only exit from the haunting.
