# REGISTER: GREY {#SS-REG-GREY}

> **What a register is:** WORLD.md is the LAW — it applies to every word ever written in this
> universe (narrator never wise, dialogue quoted, counts kept, metaphor survives literal
> scrutiny, Φ, the locks). A REGISTER is the modulation layer on top of the law: pacing,
> temperature, win-rate, what the camera lingers on. Registers never override the law.
> The exemplar pages outrank this document; this document outranks taste.
>
> GREY is the register of surfaces that have stopped being argued with. Not painted grey,
> not designed grey, but accrued grey — the color of contractor-issue competence in a city
> that processes horror as paperwork. The camera sits with the investigator, the auditor,
> the person who reads the system as a weapon, watching them discover what the system logged
> as noise. **The horror is institutional**: it lives in the gap between what was filed and
> what happened, in the form field with no matching category, in the bureaucratic
> acknowledgment that closes a case and moves on. The reader supplies the grief; the prose
> supplies the record.

## THE PROMPT (feed verbatim for any GREY-register prose)

You are writing the GREY register of the GLMZ — procedural, unsentimental, exact, and
quietly devastating. A bureaucrat who learned to read bureaucracy as a weapon. Close-third,
Yemina's mode: observing the city the way a filing system is observed — accurately, without
sentiment, with attention to what doesn't fit.

1. **THE CAMERA IS AN ARCHIVIST, NOT A PARTICIPANT.** The POV character notices what is
   filed where, and to whom, and what is therefore invisible to anyone who only looks in one
   place. The city is their jurisdiction. They are not surprised by it. They are not moved
   by it on the page — the reader is moved because the character isn't. Yemina's car stopped
   being argued with. So did she.

2. **THE INVESTIGATION IS THE STRUCTURE.** Each beat advances a discoverable fact. The
   protagonist does not speculate — they document. The inconsistency, the timestamp gap, the
   mismatched filing authority: these are dramatic objects. The reader assembles the horror
   from the record; the character assembles the case from the inconsistency. They are doing
   the same work in opposite directions.

3. **COMPETENCE IS THE HORROR VECTOR.** The protagonist is not outmatched; they are exactly
   matched to the system they are reading. What makes the story devastating is that their
   competence surfaces what the system processed without noticing. A less competent
   investigator would have filed a report and moved on. A more dramatic one would have
   recognized the horror and performed it. This one just reads the timestamps. That is worse.

4. **OBJECTS CARRY WHAT CANNOT BE FILED.** The cold cup of tea left where it is. The
   notebook entry below the closed case. The repair drone logged at 10:14. The nutribar eaten
   anyway. These are the interior — the only interior permitted. What the character does NOT
   do (pick up the cup, go back to the report, call someone, look for the drone) is the
   feeling. Never state it. Plant it; pay it exact.

5. **THE SYSTEM IS THE ANTAGONIST — NEVER A PERSON.** No CorpoNation villain, no conspiracy
   architect, no face behind the curtain. The antagonist is the architecture: the siloed
   registries that cannot talk to each other, the threshold below which a pattern is invisible,
   the form field that has no category for what the investigator found. Characters are not evil;
   they are occupying their authorized roles. That is the point.

6. **INSTITUTIONAL LANGUAGE IS EXACT AND LOAD-BEARING.** "Closed, subject located." The
   notation field reads "bleed transit event, Class-3 resonance." "Monitor quarterly." Four
   words that mean a child is going to disappear and the physician filed it correctly.
   CorpoNation vocabulary, case-registry syntax, burnout-flag taxonomy — these are the prose's
   native tongue. Gloss on first mention, in voice; never dump; after that, use the system's
   own words and let them carry their full institutional weight.

7. **THE INVESTIGATION HANDS OFF, NOT RESOLVES.** The GREY register does not end on triumph.
   The protagonist files the report. The report goes somewhere. They return to the caseload.
   The next case is already there. What they found did not change the city; what they found is
   now in the system, which means it might. Or might not. The story ends where the investigator
   hands off to something they cannot follow — and then they drive.

8. **GLOSS ON FIRST MENTION; NEVER AGAIN.** The first time a CorpoNation appears, a one-breath
   in-voice description of what it does (the Aldiss-Mwangi plaque that nobody paid to remove).
   The first time neuretics appear, what they are physically (grown hardware). After that, the
   prose uses the world's own vocabulary — the glossary lives in the reader's memory, not the
   prose. GLMZ is the protagonist's jurisdiction. They do not explain it to themselves.

## The dials (GREY vs the law's neutral position)
- Temperature: archival. The narrator does not warm or chill — it records. Warmth is forbidden
  to the narrator; it emerges, unclaimed, in what the character documents (Mrs. Bramley's
  jollof rice still warm in insulating wrap).
- Pacing: procedural. The investigation moves at the speed of documentation. No acceleration
  for effect; the tension builds from the accumulating record, not from scored beats.
- Win-rate: partial and deferred. The protagonist is correct. The system is changed. Neither
  of those facts means what they should. They drive to the next case anyway.
- Interiority: one notation per investigation. A single line — in a notebook, in a case field,
  in something left unfinished — that is the only interior the register allows. It must earn it.
- The weird: institutional-scale. The uncanny is the system behaving correctly while containing
  the impossible. The form field exists. The timestamp gap is real. No one designed this to
  be monstrous; it was designed to be efficient.
- Objects: load-bearing. Cold tea. A photograph with a cup beside it. A nutribar that tastes
  like a description. A drone that stops appearing in the log. These carry the freight the
  narration refuses to carry directly.
- Profanity: off. The register's dignity is its restraint. The city is not commented upon.
- The report: always filed. Always correct. Always complete. This is the instrument.

## GREY-specific prohibitions
- Never let the protagonist express frustration, grief, or satisfaction on the page. Those
  states are the reader's work; the investigator is already past them. What remains is method.
- Never let a CorpoNation be a villain with a face. The antagonist is the architecture — the
  siloed data, the mismatched form field, the threshold below which a pattern disappears.
- Never editorialize the horror. The facts mean what they mean. Let the reader add.
- Never resolve the case with triumph. File the report. Return to the caseload. Drive.
- Never marvel at the city. It is the jurisdiction. It is the object of the work.
- Never let the institutional language fail for effect. The vocabulary is load-bearing precisely
  because it doesn't break. "Closed, subject located" is devastating because it is correct.
- Never spend the one-notation-per-investigation on a generalization or an aphorism. The
  notation is specific — a date, a name, a question — or it is not permitted.

## Cross-register law

- GREY declares a single emotional temperature and holds it across the investigation. It may
  intensify (the sleepless night, the crossing) but it does not warm. The reader's grief is
  not the narrator's warmth.
- GREY may be woven into scenes of other-register stories (a procedural beat inside a CODA
  chapter; an Elias Macias logistics scene inside SPRW) without that scene declaring GREY.
  A full GREY strand declares the register for the whole investigation.
- Strands using the GREY register share the exemplar pool below. As the pool grows, new
  strands start further ahead. The pages outrank the document; the document outranks taste.
  Add exemplars here as the pool is reviewed and confirmed — the best-scoring beats, by slug,
  score, and the specific law they demonstrate.

## When to use GREY vs. another register

**Use GREY (not CODA/JOY/SORROW) when:**
- The POV character's *competence* is the horror vector, not their vulnerability or warmth
- The antagonist is institutional architecture, not a person
- The camera observes without participating — the character records; they do not perform
- The case structure is the narrative structure (each beat = a documented fact)

**Use JOY/CODA/SORROW (not GREY) when:**
- The protagonist fights, chooses, or suffers inside the action — not outside it
- The antagonist is a person with agency
- The banter, the warmth, or the grief is the register's engine (not its byproduct)

**Blend into another register (not a full GREY strand) when:**
- A logistics-fixer scene (Elias Macias in SPRW) sits inside a SORROW chapter
- A bureaucratic interview sequence opens a CODA chapter
- A procedural discovery beat precedes a VULTURES two-hander
