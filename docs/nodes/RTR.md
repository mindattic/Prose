# NODE BIBLE — Read the Room (RTR) {#SS-RTR}

> **Purpose.** The dry spine of *Read the Room*: a post-show encounter in a Milwaukee dive
> club that puts a mind-reading girl and a noise-rock singer on the same side of a robbery
> gone violent. When prose and spine disagree, fix one in the same change.
> See [[../BIBLE]] for universe laws.

---

## 0. What this story IS {#SS-RTR-0}

A heist-in-reverse thriller with a psychic B-plot. Fenris, a three-piece noise-rock/industrial
band out of the Pilsen corridor, celebrates a good show in their green room at the Velvet Tine
(Milwaukee, The Low, Zone 4-adjacent). Their usual supplier, Dayo Ferreira, arrives with
compounds and company — among them Faith Larson, a 24-year-old Milwaukee bakery worker who
was at the barrier for the whole set and doesn't yet know she is a Read (a natural psionic receptor).
Faith takes Candor (empathy amplifier), which unlocks her ability at full aperture. She picks up
the hostile surface thoughts of a robbery crew prepping to breach. She pulls Ethan Wolfe, Fenris's
lead vocalist, out the bathroom window seconds before the breach.

What follows is a two-thread story: Faith and Ethan running through Milwaukee's Low while she
narrates the crew's minds; Mona Dominique (bassist, band manager, Ethan's sometimes-lover)
playing dead in the green room and eventually becoming Koss's leverage call. Ethan goes back.
Faith comes with him. He upgrades through four weapons (demolition hammer → sidearm → SMG →
Fenris Ballistics Howl FB-7 Wolfpack combat shotgun) as Faith runs a real-time psionic feed of
enemy mind-state. Koss shoots regardless. Faith takes the hit. The ending is hers.

**This story IS:**
- A genre action story grounded in character. The gun work earns the ending.
- Faith's origin story — the first story in which she discovers what she is.
- The story where Ethan Wolfe finishes his four-year song.
- A full-aperture portrait of Reads in GLMZ: how rare they are, how dangerous that is.

**This story is NOT:**
- A Kyle story. Kyle does not appear.
- A heist story. The criminals are competent professionals, not caricatures.
- An explanation of the Reads: this story shows; it does not explain.
- A death story for Faith. She gets shot. She lives.

## 1. Setting {#SS-RTR-1}

**The Velvet Tine** — a dive club on Milwaukee's Zone 4 lower terrace, 40–60m altitude,
deep in The Low. Named for the neon antler silhouette on its facade, half-burnt out for years
and never replaced. The main room holds 200 standing. The green room is a reinforced concrete
box attached to the rear: rusted door, a lamp on a chain, a couch that has held hundreds of bad
decisions, equipment cabling on every flat surface. The side entrance is a fire-code door that
Koss's crew pries open with a modified pry bar — the Tine's owner hasn't replaced the lock
catch in three years.

**Milwaukee in 2226** — deep GLMZ administrative zone, not the dense core but not the buffer
fringe either. Eigenlift towers rise from converted industrial blocks. The Low here is ungoverned
in the classic sense: no corpo patrol contract, no Continuity Office presence below 80m.
Pulse signal is strong enough for neuretic communication. The streets at 2AM in this zone are
quiet, purposeful, and indifferent to small violence.

## 2. Canon Drugs (GLMZ, 2226) {#SS-RTR-2}

Three compounds feature in Beat 3. All are legal-gray in the current GLMZ regulatory framework
(Schedule III or unscheduled, depending on district).

**Glass** — a translucent wafer pressed to the inside of the wrist; absorbed transdermally in
90 seconds. Induces deep cognitive clarity: every decision feels like the only possible
decision, consequences visible, no second-guessing. Artists and corpo strategists both use it.
The clarity is real; the inevitability is the chemical lie. Comedown is second-guessing
everything the following morning.

**Fade** — a ceramic capsule, thumb-sized, cracked and inhaled. Dissolves the boundary between
experienced and imagined. Music on Fade becomes narrative, becomes a story you are inside.
Forty-minute duration. Fashionable in the arts underground for a decade. People on Fade at
concerts report "hearing the song as a story I've always known." Ethan takes Fade; it gives
his fear a slightly rehearsed quality during the chase.

**Candor** — a soft gold sublingual tab. Amplifies whatever empathy circuitry the user has.
You feel what the people near you are feeling — not strongly enough to lose yourself, just
strongly enough to know. Musicians swear it makes them play better. Ethically debated in 2226:
can you consent to sharing your emotional state with someone who's taken Candor? Faith takes
Candor. For an ordinary person it would produce gentle ambient emotional awareness. For a Read
it does something else entirely.

## 3. The Reads {#SS-RTR-3}

**Reads** (GLMZ underground term): individuals with natural psionic-adjacent neuretic
sensitivity — they receive surface thoughts and strong emotional states of people within range.
Not telekinetic. Not projective. Pure reception. The range is approximately 30–50 metres for
surface thoughts; stronger emotional states (fear, intent, acute grief) arrive from further.

Reads cannot transmit thoughts. They cannot plant suggestions. They can only hear.

The GLMZ establishment does not acknowledge Reads officially — the category blurs too
uncomfortably with Schism consciousness research and the political consensus that the Schism
was caused by psychic disruption (the Reach disinfo frame, SS-LAW-Reach). On the street: Reads
are either sought after as intelligence assets by criminal operations and gray-market corps,
or they are feared and avoided. There is no reliable test for the ability. Reads themselves
often attribute their perceptiveness to intuition, social intelligence, or strong drug
reactions for years or decades.

**Faith's ability** — she is a strong Read, approximately top-decile of the known (unofficial)
population. Her range under normal conditions is ~25 metres. Under Candor (which amplifies
empathy circuits), her range extends to ~60 metres and the signal is significantly cleaner.
The Candor hit is what triggers full aperture for the first time. She has always known she was
"too perceptive" — friends have found her unsettling without being able to name why — but
tonight is the first time the signal is unambiguous.

**What Reads cannot do** — in this story: Faith cannot read intent further ahead than the
moment before an action (she reads Koss pulling the trigger ~0.5 seconds before it fires; this
is not fast enough to get fully out of the way). She cannot read encrypted neuretic thoughts.
She cannot turn off the signal.

**The danger** — if Koss's crew knew what Faith was before the story began, the night would
have gone differently. Any criminal operation that learns a Read was present will want to
find them afterward. This is the seed of what Faith's life becomes. For now, nobody but
Ethan knows what she is.

## 4. Characters {#SS-RTR-4}

### Ethan Wolfe (lead vocal, songwriter)
White Hispanic (Spanish-European descent), mid-thirties. Silver-templed close-cropped hair.
Slim, post-show disheveled. Carries genemod wolf fangs — the lateral incisors replaced with
tapered canine geometry, quality work that has always looked like it was there.
His stage presence is intensity without aggression: he holds still and the room leans in.
Offstage he's quieter than people expect, the kind of quiet that comes from having spent too
much on three hours of performance.

**Arc:** Self-identified soloist in a band → the man who goes back for his people → the man
who finally finishes the song.

**The song** — Ethan has been working on a closing track for four years. He has the verses. He
has the melody. The last line won't come because he suspects it's about Mona and if he sings it
it becomes true and if it's true he can't have the band be the thing. Faith reads the truth
underneath: the last line is not about Mona. He doesn't know who it's about yet.

**Register** — economical. Dry. Gives you less than you want and then gives you exactly what
you needed. Not a warrior, not a fighter; he has no training. He is fast and angry and he cares
enormously, which is different.

**Lock** — he does not initiate physical violence against an incapacitated person. Koss goes
down and Ethan stops. He is not executing anyone tonight.

### Mona Dominique (bassist, band manager)
Ukrainian-Portuguese, late twenties. Subdermal light-strip forearm implant — pulses in sync
with bass frequencies, goes flat white when she's still. Runs Fenris's logistics (bookings,
finances, touring agreements) in addition to playing. Practical, organized, the one who knows
where everything is.

**Arc:** Surviving → leverage → steady voice under extreme stress.

**The dynamic with Ethan** — they've slept together twice. The band is the wall they both
agree is more important. Mona has made her peace with this better than Ethan has; she knows
the song is coming and she is ready to not be the person it's about.

**Lock** — she makes the neuretic emergency call the second she's free. Professional even
bleeding and shaking.

**The playing-dead beat** — Dayo falls on her when he goes down. His weight on her chest is
the thing that saves her; Koss reads her as another casualty and moves on, then recalculates.
Her steadiness when Koss addresses her is real: she has simply decided to do whatever keeps
the maximum number of people alive. She is the most competent person in the room under
pressure.

### Faith Larson
Scandinavian (Swedish-Norwegian), 24. White. Grew up in Racine, GLMZ periphery. Works at a
bakery in Milwaukee — not circuit, not polished, the flour-whiteness on her wrist gives her
away. Dayo found her at the barrier during the show and brought her back. She had never been
in a green room before.
She has the compact quality of the Jennifer Sky visual lock (see entity record): small, blonde
bob cut, open-faced, the kind of bright-eyed attention that reads as harmless.
She is harmless. She is also the most dangerous person in the room.

**Arc:** Bakery worker who doesn't know what she is → the girl who reads the room in both senses
→ the girl who lives.

**Register** — dry humor, quick. She has the conversational tempo of someone who has already
processed what you're about to say by the time you say it, which she has. She doesn't use
this maliciously. She uses it to keep things moving.

**Lock** — she does not reveal to Koss's crew that she is a Read. Ethan is the only person
outside the band who knows by story's end.

**Lock** — she steps into the shot line because she reads Koss pulling the trigger and moves
wrong: into it rather than out of it. This is not heroism; it's a half-second misread of a
spatial situation while simultaneously processing a man's decision to kill. She is not trying
to take a bullet for Ethan.

**The ending** — she knows the last line of Ethan's song. She doesn't tell him. She reads the
thought underneath his worry and she understands that the song isn't finished because he
doesn't know what he feels yet. She reflects it back to him — not the content, the fact of it.
Then she lets him go find it.

### Rill Nakashima (drums)
Japanese-Korean, 29. Speaks very little. Completely present when behind a kit; politely absent
the rest of the time. Takes Glass on party nights. Under Glass tonight: precise about nothing
in particular, which is his factory default.

**Function** — grounding element. He's the one who cooperates immediately when the crew
breaks in (hands up, quiet), which keeps the situation from escalating further before Mona can
go still. He's freed mid-fight and stays down.

### Dayo Ferreira
Nigerian-Brazilian, early forties. Slim, tailored matte-black jacket that costs more than the
club's PA system. Speaks little; the goods do the talking. Has been supplying Fenris and
similar acts for a decade. Not a villain. Operates at the intersection of the entertainment
economy and the gray market — a position that involves both luxury and danger as matter of
professional fact.

**Lock** — he survives. He gets shot in the shoulder and shoulder-adjacent ribs, which is
bad, but he regains consciousness before the paramedics arrive. He does not see Faith's
ability clearly; he is unconscious for the relevant moments.

**Future hook** — Dayo Ferreira is the most visible possible loose end: someone shot his
people, and he will want to know by whom and why. He is a future-story entity.

### Koss (Kostas Mirković)
Handle: Koss. Serbian-Greek origin, mid-forties. Runs a crew that targets soft-money
operations in The Low — musicians, artists, dealers after payday shows. Professional, calm,
the quiet kind of dangerous. His register has no performance in it.

**Lock** — Koss made the decision to shoot before the standoff began. This is not impulsive.
This is operational: he cannot leave live witnesses who can describe his voice and his
physical presence, and he has made that calculation before walking into the room. Faith reads
this in the standoff beat (Beat 13) and it is what makes her tell Ethan *now*.

**Lock** — Koss is stopped, not killed. Ethan uses the shotgun but does not execute.
(The story does not require a body to be satisfying.)

### Nkechi
Dayo's other companion tonight. Loud, funny, already in motion from the moment she walks in.
Hides under a table during the breach. Survives. Does not look at where Mona is hiding, which
is the most courageous single thing anyone does in this story and it is not noted on the page.

## 5. Beat Spine (14 beats) {#SS-RTR-5}

**Beat 1** — The weight of a good show. Post-show green room. Establish Fenris, the Tine, the
dynamic between Ethan and Mona, Rill's quiet. The crowd still audible through the walls.

**Beat 2** — What Dayo brings. His arrival, the compounds, the two women. Faith's entrance.
The room warming.

**Beat 3** — Candor. The party. Drug descriptions in full. Faith and Ethan beginning to talk.
The room at its warmest.

**Beat 4** — Stray thought. Faith catches the first alien thought. Then a second. Then a third.
She goes still in the middle of a laugh.

**Beat 5** — The bathroom. Faith extracts Ethan. She tells him what she heard. He follows.

**Beat 6** — Out the window / The breach. They exit seconds before Koss's crew enters. The
B-story begins: Mona plays dead under Dayo.

**Beat 7** — The chase. Faith steers them through Milwaukee's Low. Ethan watching her
decisions.

**Beat 8** — What are you. They stop. He confronts her. She can't explain it. He's scared. He
checks his neuretics and ignores it twice.

**Beat 9** — Mona plays dead (B-story). The green room under occupation. Koss identifies her.
She makes the call.

**Beat 10** — The call. Ethan hears Mona's voice, flat and steady. He decides. The line:
*"What scares you more — me knowing what you're thinking, or a bullet in the head?"*

**Beat 11** — The approach. Faith narrates enemy positions. Ethan finds the demolition hammer.
The weapon ladder begins: hammer → sidearm (fire escape man).

**Beat 12** — The inside pass. Faith runs the real-time read. Ethan works through the room:
sidearm → SMG → Fenris Ballistics Howl FB-7 Wolfpack on the bar.

**Beat 13** — Koss. The standoff. Faith reads him and goes quiet. The shot. Faith on the floor.

**Beat 14** — The ending. She reads him. She laughs at the wrong time. She tells him what she
sees. She goes out. Three months later: front row, blonde bob, she already knew the words.

## 8. Voice and Register {#SS-RTR-8}

**POV** — split. Faith and Ethan share A-story POV (alternating). Mona owns the B-story beats.
The narration is close-third throughout: no omniscient intrusion, no named emotion.

**Faith's register** — quick, observational, dry. Her narration notices what other people's
bodies are doing before it names what they feel. She describes thoughts she hears in the same
flat register as physical facts: *"He's thinking about the door. He's been thinking about the
door for two minutes."*

**Ethan's register** — economical. Understates. The prose earns his fear by showing the exact
moment he stops showing it — which is more frightening than showing it.

**Mona's register** — professional, precise, internal. She does not editorialize. She catalogs
and acts.

**The drug register** — Fade (Ethan) gives his fear a slightly-too-scripted quality during the
chase. Things feel like they're happening in the order they were always going to happen. This
is the Fade. It does not impair him physically; it gives the chase a slightly dreamlike texture
that the prose can use.

**Lock** — no phones. All communication via neuretics sub-vocal. Mona's "call" is a neuretic
relay. The one exception: Koss hands Mona a physical relay device (his crew uses analog for
operational security — paranoid about neuretic intercept logging).
