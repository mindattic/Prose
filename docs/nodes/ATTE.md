## Voice & Prose Laws (SS-A31 + Register — MANDATORY)

### Accessible Prose (SS-A31)
- Sentences: short-to-medium. Break any clause over ~20 words.
- Narration uses contractions naturally: she's, it wasn't, couldn't, hadn't.
- Vocabulary: Anglo-Saxon base words preferred. Avoid Latinate abstractions.
- No stacked subordinate clauses. Split before stacking a second "that/which."
- No pseudo-profundity. Metaphors survive literal scrutiny.
- No filler-wit. No wry universal-truth lines.

### Yemina Fola's Register — Administrative Horror (GREY)
Close-third on Yemina. The city is observed the way a filing system is observed: accurately, without sentiment, with attention to what doesn't fit.

**What her narration notices:** Institutional age. The building that "stopped being argued with." Timestamps. What is filed where, and to whom — and what is therefore invisible to anyone who only looks in one place. She reads time the way a forensic accountant reads ledgers.

**What her narration does NOT do:** Express frustration (she's used it up). Marvel at the city (it's her jurisdiction). Tell the reader what the facts mean. Rush. Emote on the page.

**Her competence is archival.** She is not a detective; she is a bureaucrat who learned to read bureaucracy as a weapon. The prose shows what she observes, in the order she observes it, without editorial comment.

### BANNED in this strand
- Kyle's register: arithmetic as cognitive style, "X but Y" math-thought, geometric metaphors — NEVER in Yemina's narration. She thinks in TIMESTAMPS, CROSS-REFERENCES, GAPS — institutional logic, not calculation.
- Feelings named directly. Show the body; do not label the emotion.
- The backstory stated on page (her prior report that cost her the interesting cases is the engine, not content).
- Drama. The scale is deliberately small: three children, three flags, one gray contractor car.

## Story — Attendance (ATTE)

**Gateway designation.** The "if you only read one" entry point for GLMZ. Zero prior knowledge required.

A procedural investigation. Cross-district truancy investigator Yemina Fola follows a bureaucratic anomaly — three children logged as unexcused absences — and discovers the city's most ordinary-sounding problem is a cover for something the city can't officially acknowledge.

It ends on institutional ambiguity, not triumph. Yemina files a report. The report goes somewhere. She doesn't learn where.

**This is NOT:** A Kyle story. A conspiracy thriller. A rescue story. A CorpoNation villain story. The antagonist is the system's architecture.
