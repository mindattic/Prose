## Voice & Prose Laws (SS-A31 + Register — MANDATORY)

### Accessible Prose (SS-A31)
- Sentences: short-to-medium. Break any clause over ~20 words.
- Narration uses contractions naturally: he's, it wasn't, couldn't, didn't, hadn't.
- Vocabulary: Anglo-Saxon base words preferred. Avoid Latinate abstractions.
- No stacked subordinate clauses.
- No pseudo-profundity. Metaphors survive literal scrutiny.
- No filler-wit. No wry universal-truth narrator lines.

### Tomas Alvarado's Register — Blue-Collar Pragmatic (PRIMARY POV)
Close-third on Tomas. He is a family man with steady hands who does an ugly job because the work is reliable and he has a daughter and a therapy bill. The narration notices practical things: what his hands are doing, what the job requires, what it costs. He does not editorialize. He is not cynical about the work — he has compartmentalized it. When the compartment fails, the prose doesn't announce the failure; it shows his hands slowing down.

**His register:** procedural, blue-collar, no-nonsense. He fixes things. He maintains the Scarab. He does not make speeches. When he's angry at Levin, the anger comes out in efficiency — shorter movements, less checking his partner's work.

**The moral turn:** He fires the shot he would give a year of his life not to fire. The guilt doesn't appear as reflection — it appears as the way he cannot stop working after. Work is the thing his hands know.

### Casimir / Ekow Ato's Register — Hunter (SECONDARY POV, 4 chapters)
He reads people the way a hunter reads ground. His register notices what is *carried* rather than stated: posture, weight distribution, the angle of someone's attention. His instrument is the look — "your lies written on you like a confession letter." This lands before he says anything. He is menacing in stillness, not in aggression.

His arc: the leashed dog who lights the match on the house that owns him. He doesn't announce the turn. He does it, efficiently, and then the work is done.

### BANNED in this strand
- Kyle's register: arithmetic/filing/parliament/geometry/math-thought — never in Tomas's or Casimir's narration.
- Sentimentality about the work. The story's power comes from when the compartment fails — not from Tomas feeling bad about it ahead of time.
- Over-explanation of the comedic irony. The Bram Osei situation is never commented on. The reader feels it.

## Story — Vultures at the Door (VATD)

**Genre:** Dark comedy / noir. VATD-style black humor — the ironic gap between professional competence and moral squalor is never commented on. The reader feels it. Characters can't.

**The job:** Tomas Alvarado and Levin "Lev" Calder operate cardiac repos for the body-organ lending system. They repossess the financed hearts of people who default. It is legal. It is ugly. They do it because someone has to and the pay is steady.

**The inciting act:** They repo a heart from someone who turns out to be the Lotus Syndicate heir (Han Do-yun), at the wrong time. The IdentiScan confirms his identity one beat after the shot. The conscience arrives too late.

**The hunt:** The heart needs to get back to Do-yun alive. Han Jeong-suk (Do-yun's mother, Lotus Stem) sends Casimir/Ekow after the "saboteurs."

**The black comedy engine:** Tomas and Levin convince Casimir to look the other way (earning: he's been the Syndicate's despised outsider dog — used, never admitted). Casimir flips and helps them get Do-yun home and stage the damning evidence. Bram Osei (who Casimir spared) is killed anyway when the Vultures are dispatched to strip the man whose silence they'd purchased. This is never commented on.

## Characters
