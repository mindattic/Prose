## Voice & Prose Laws (SS-A31 + Register — MANDATORY)

### Accessible Prose (SS-A31)
- Sentences: short-to-medium. Break any clause over ~20 words.
- Narration uses contractions naturally: she's, it wasn't, couldn't, didn't.
- Vocabulary: Anglo-Saxon base words preferred. Avoid Latinate abstractions.
- No stacked subordinate clauses.
- No pseudo-profundity. Metaphors survive literal scrutiny.
- No filler-wit.

## Story — Crimson & Chrome (CxC)

**The Rook Trilogy finale.**

**MxG** — the surface: corporate sheen over a weapon. Extract Soraya; Class V formula goes public.
**NxR** — the decay underneath: crack the partition; find the manifest (61 names, 40 delivered, **21 walked off** a barge under an unsigned relocation order).
**CxC** — the body itself: find the twenty-one; stop the harvest the first two jobs unwittingly fed.

**The reveal:** The crew were never doing three unrelated jobs. Each was a step the Nano Triumvirate needed taken and could not be seen taking. Rook has been a contractor to her own ending the whole time.

**Logline:** One last job to find out where twenty-one people went — and to stop the thing the last two jobs were quietly building. The body the whole city has been pricing turns out to be the only thing worth stealing back.

**Genre:** HEIST — unashamedly the biggest, most familiar one. Last-job reunion, mastermind reveal, mirror-antagonist who picks a side, the vault that is a person, the team walking away from an explosion they earned. Honor the cliché at full volume. Never apologize for the genre.

**The emotional register is the highest of the trilogy.** This is where the count has names. Rook's warmth is spent here — not withheld, not performed; rationed correctly, deployed when it matters.
