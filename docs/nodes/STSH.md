# NODE BIBLE — The Long Cut (STSH) {#SS-STSH}

> **Purpose.** The dry spine of Amara Adeyemi-Kowalski's story: a dead runner, a broadcasting
> implant, and the discovery that the cover-up she is trying to expose has her name in it.
> What happens, not how it reads. When prose and spine disagree, fix one in the same change.
> See [[../BIBLE]] for universe laws.

## 0. What this story IS {#SS-STSH-0}

A medical noir in three acts. A street medic in the Gray Zone inherits a dead corpo runner's
evidence implant and has 72 hours to broadcast it before two competing kill-teams — one NCID
(Neuretic Crime Investigation Division, Halcyon Civil Security; street name: RaNCID), one
corporate — eliminate the problem.

The story is about complicity: what it means to have done something wrong without knowing it,
and what it costs to correct the record once you know.

**This is NOT:**
- A Kyle story. Kyle does not appear. Kyle's register vocabulary (filing, arithmetic, parliament,
  gap, geometry) is strictly forbidden in STSH prose.
- An action thriller with a competent hero. Stash makes medical decisions with precision and
  most other decisions badly. Her arc is accepting this.
- A redemption story in the sentimental sense. The record is corrected; she is not forgiven.
  She doesn't ask to be.
- A conspiracy thriller with vast scope. The scale is two kill-teams and one implant. The
  corruption is real but local: one subsidiary, one cover-up, 400 deaths, fifteen years of silence.

## 1. The central object {#SS-STSH-1}

**The implant.** A surgical-grade data capsule, thoracic cavity placement, Model Nova-7 (Sable
Industries MedTech line, 2210). No neural interface — physical broadcast only, tight-band
frequency 412.7 MHz, range ~800 meters in open air, ~200 meters through ferrocement and water.
The frequency is licensed to the Gray Zone medical monitoring network — dead channel since 2219.

The implant contains:
- 14.3 GB of encrypted trial records from the Sable Industries/MidNorth Medical neuretics
  governor program, 2211. The records document 428 non-consensual surgical subjects — Block War
  refugees held in the Z-7 gray zone relocation camps — used to test governor suppression chips
  for the Praxis NeoCortex line.
- Six video diary fragments recorded by Renata Osei, the runner, between 2218 and 2225.
  These play as non-continuous B-story interludes in the prose. They show what Renata witnessed
  and remembered; they do not show what Stash suspects until the final fragment.
- A 12-second voice message addressed specifically to Amara Adeyemi-Kowalski. This is the
  dark night revelation: Renata knew Stash's name. Renata came to her on purpose.

**Why Renata sought Stash:** Stash's name appears in the 2211 trial records as a junior field
surgeon contracted by Sable Industries MedTech. She was 23. She believed she was doing routine
post-op follow-up on war-injured patients. She was, in fact, closing the incisions after
governor implantation without knowing what was inside. She did not consent to this use of her
skills. Neither did the patients.

The records prove both things: that the trials happened, and that Stash was an unknowing
participant. Renata found this dual exculpation/implication and decided Stash was the only
person with both the motive and the technical skill to broadcast the evidence in a format
the Gray Zone's distributed network could route.

## 2. The wound (background, never stated on the page) {#SS-STSH-2}

Stash left Sable Industries in 2214 after two years of increasing unease: patients who would
not make eye contact, incision sites she was told were "standard protocol," forms she was not
permitted to review. She told herself she was quitting because corpo medicine was corrupt in
general. She did not investigate what specifically she had been part of.

She has run the Dispensary in the Gray Zone border between Z-7 and Z-8 since 2214. Twelve years
of treating everyone who couldn't go official — exos, scavs, tunnelers, runners who caught
something their employers wouldn't cover. She is good at this. She is also, as she will discover,
running the clinic partly to outrun a specific memory she has refused to fully form.

**Rule: the wound never surfaces until Chapter 11.** Before that, it functions as a texture —
her over-caution with consent, her specific dislike of Sable Industries, the way she looks at
her own hands when she finishes a surgery.

## 3. Character rules for this story {#SS-STSH-3}

### Amara Adeyemi-Kowalski — "Doc Stash" (POV protagonist) {#SS-STSH-3a}

- 38 years old. Mixed Nigerian-Polish heritage. Compact, precise in movement.
- Full name always available to readers. Handle is what the Gray Zone uses.
- Voice register: **GREY adjacent, medical precision variant.** Close-third limited POV.
  - Sees bodies as systems. Her narration reads in terms of deficits, pressures, drainage,
    repair — not metaphysically, but because this is genuinely how she processes the world.
  - Dark humor as shield. "That's going to be an interesting autopsy" is the kind of line
    she says aloud in an empty room.
  - **Anglo-Saxon base vocabulary** with medical/technical overlay deployed naturally.
    She does not explain her terminology to herself or the reader.
  - Sentences longer than Kyle's in contemplation; shorter in crisis. The compression is
    situational, not habitual.
  - She does not perform competence. She is competent and occasionally wrong and both
    things show.
- She takes payment. Not Φ20 minimum like Kyle — she takes what people have.
- She does not carry a weapon habitually. She has a bone saw she has used in non-surgical
  contexts exactly once. This matters in the finale.
- She has one external tell for emotional distress: she catalogues supplies. When
  something frightens her, she counts what is on the shelves.

### Ledger / Cayo Reyes-Ibarra {#SS-STSH-3b}

- 52 years old. Colombian-Moroccan heritage. Circuit handle: Ledger.
- Information broker. His rule: he always gets paid first. It is also a confession — he has
  arranged his entire life to avoid the moment of choosing between money and conscience.
- **In Chapter 10:** Scalpel Division offers him the implant's location in exchange for
  immunity. He accepts. Then, after one hour, he calls Stash to warn her.
- His arc is not redemption; it is acknowledgment. He knows what he is. He acts anyway.

### Petra Voss {#SS-STSH-3c}

- Halcyon Civil Security — NCID / RaNCID Agent, GLMZ Field Division. Late 40s. Finnish-Somali heritage.
- Her logic: the governor tech saves 1,200 people per year. The 428 victims were already dead.
  She has done the arithmetic. She is not entirely wrong. She is wrong about the next set of 428.
- She does not torture. She explains her position with complete honesty.
- She is the most dangerous person in the story because she cannot be argued with.

### Commander Izoha Mwangi {#SS-STSH-3d}

- Scalpel Division commander, Sable Industries MedTech security arm. Ghanaian heritage. Former NGRA.
- Professional, not sadistic. Her job is asset recovery.
- She does not survive the finale — killed by Petra Voss in the Chapter 13 crossfire.
- Carries a Cascade suppressor (disrupts neural implants within 5 meters).

### Femi Adebayo {#SS-STSH-3e}

- 17 years old. Nigerian heritage. Came to Dispensary at 14 with compound fracture; stayed.
- Knows every tunnel and crawlspace in a two-kilometer radius. This knowledge saves Stash's life.
- He is the one who names the new clinic. He puts Renata's name on the wall.

### Renata Osei {#SS-STSH-3f}

- Deceased before story opens. 51 years old at death. Ghanaian heritage.
- Former corporate financial compliance officer (Sable Industries, 2209-2014). Built the implant herself.
- Spent eleven years finding a way to broadcast the evidence. Researched Stash for two years.
- Walked into the Dispensary knowing the killswitch would fire.
- She is the story's moral center. She had the right answer and spent her life paying for it.

## 6. Voice register — STSH prose laws {#SS-STSH-6}

**Governing register:** Medical precision variant. Close-third limited, Stash's POV throughout
except during Renata's fragments (first-person video diary format, direct to camera).

**What this register does:**
- Diagnoses situations before judging them. Mechanism before moral weight.
- Uses medical vocabulary naturally — "pressure deficit," "lavage," "sternotomy" — without glossing.
- Counts and catalogs under stress. Her inventory reflex is an emotion tell.
- Dark humor in internal narration and dialogue, never in high-stakes moments.
- Contractions in dialogue always. Contractions in narration when situation is immediate.

**What this register does NOT do:**
- Does not use Kyle's vocabulary (filing, arithmetic, parliament, gap, geometry — FORBIDDEN).
- Does not name emotions directly. Body signals carry the emotional load.
- Does not describe her own appearance. There are no mirrors.
- Does not editorialize. She describes; the description does the work.

**Forbidden constructions:**
- "She felt [emotion]." — replace with physical signal.
- "It was as if—" — wrong register.
- Sentimental register in the final image. Warmth is real but functional, not effusive.

## 8. Locks {#SS-STSH-8}

- Stash does not kill Mwangi or Petra. The story ends without her having killed anyone.
- Ledger does not become a hero. His one act of conscience is unexplained by him and costs him.
- Femi does not die.
- The broadcast succeeds. The records are out. The outcome is uncertain but the act is complete.
- The governor program is not shut down by the end of the story. The legal process takes years.
- Stash does not seek forgiveness. She does not get it. She does not need the story to provide it.
- Renata's name goes on the wall. This is the final image and it is not negotiable.
