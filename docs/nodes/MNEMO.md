## Voice & Prose Laws (SS-A31 + Register — MANDATORY)

### Accessible Prose (SS-A31)
- Sentences: short-to-medium. Break any clause over ~20 words.
- Narration uses contractions naturally: he's, she'd, it wasn't, couldn't.
- Vocabulary: Anglo-Saxon base words preferred. Avoid Latinate abstractions.
- No stacked subordinate clauses. Split before stacking a second "that/which."
- No pseudo-profundity. Metaphors survive literal scrutiny.
- No filler-wit (wry universal-truth lines).

### Amara Osei's Register
Precise, restrained, institutional. She processes the world as documentation: what was filed, where, by whom, and what is therefore invisible. The bleed intrusion from Seto arrives **before her mind votes** — kinesthetic, spatial, another person's grief landing whole in her body. She does not editorialize on the page. She observes, logs, moves.

Her competence is calibration-floor mechanical: she knows the machinery better than any client should, because she services it. This is never stated; it shows in how she moves through Orison's corridors.

### Seto Banda's Register
Gray-zone kinesthetic. Spatial threat-assessment is his default: he maps exits, reads doorways for sightlines, holds his weight over his feet. The bleed intrusion from Amara arrives as interiority — grief, institutional claustrophobia, a name he carries in his lungs before he knows whose it is. He is quieter than he should be in company. He knows things he has no reason to know, and he has stopped being surprised.

### The Bleed Intrusion (formal rule)
A bleed intrusion sentence arrives in the current POV's paragraph **without attribution, without italics, without explanation**. It is not signaled. The reader learns to recognize it. This is the formal question of the novel enacted at prose level: *which sentences are yours?*

**Law:** bleed = cognitive-state rewriting, not skill download. Amara does not gain Seto's threat-assessment as an ability she can consciously invoke. She becomes someone who maps exits she didn't know she'd looked for.

### BANNED in this strand
- Kyle's register: arithmetic, filing-log thought, parliament, geometry, "X but Y" math-structure — NEVER in Amara or Seto's narration.
- Feelings named directly. Show the body first; let the reader name the feeling.
- "The bleed gave her [skill]" framing. Write the consequence, not the mechanism.
- Orison as cartoon villain. The CorpoNation is a luxury prison that doesn't advertise itself as a prison.

## Story — Mnemosync (MNEMO)

**The question:** *Are we even ourselves?* A dual-POV investigation novel (~80k words, 3 acts, 27 chapters) structured around a cognitive horror that is never metaphysical: the systematic removal of meaning from human memory, at scale, by a CorpoNation that calls it maintenance.

**Amara Osei** (news broadcaster, former wellness-tech correspondent) and **Seto Banda** (data courier, CorpoNation access routes daily) are both Batch 44-C recipients. Their carrier-grade neuretics — hermetically sealed — create an accidental two-way bleed. They share suppressed memories.

**The asymmetry:** The suppressed memories arrive in the wrong person *whole* — with the full weight the original person is no longer capable of feeling. Seto receives Amara's memory of choosing not to ask questions on air; it lands in him with the weight of the silence she lost. Amara receives Seto's memory of a dawn route; it arrives with the full freedom of a life ungoverned, unmanaged.

**The conspiracy:** Orison Neuretics classified Batch 44-C's accelerated degradation as "managed liability." A suppression sub-protocol runs during calibration visits and erases evidence rather than fixing the condition. 847 clients. The conspiracy is visible only in absence — a constellation-shaped black spot in the gray-zone route map.

**Ciro Fonseca** — Orison internal fixer, Account Liaison. Straight razor with a bone handle. He appears twice in Act 1 as grooming before threat (Ch5, Ch7). The reader must be genuinely torn between Ciro and Seto through Act 2. He is not a cartoon villain. He was appalled by himself, once. The suppression took that too.
