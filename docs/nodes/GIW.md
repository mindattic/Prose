## 0. What this story IS {#SS-GIW-0}

The story of Soren becoming a Champion.

A volunteer soldier posted to Mossland, first campaign, Hebrides mossland — wet and cold and
old enough to have been fought over before anyone alive can remember. He perceives through the
thin-membrane without apparatus. This should not be possible. Dame Lyra reads the post-action
report and issues a standing order.

The rest of the story is what her standing order costs him.

It is:
- An alchemical frame: Lyra is the alchemist, Soren the prima materia, each deployment one
  stage of the Magnum Opus toward the Philosopher's Stone (the Champion).
- **The story of what it costs to be someone's most important experiment** — to be noticed by
  power for reasons neither party can name, and deployed again and again into the places no
  living person should survive.

**This is NOT:**
- A redemption story. Soren has not done wrong; he has been deployed.
- A chosen-one prophecy story. Lyra chose him at a field station, early in the war, for reasons
  she has seven theories about and hasn't confirmed. No prophecy. No destiny.
- A love story between Soren and Lyra (not explicitly).
- A Kyle story. Different universe. Different laws.

## 1. Name {#SS-GIW-1}

**Soren is Soren. Always.**

No M-number. No designation slash. His name is his from the beginning and it does not change.
The Houses track him by name and rank in their service records. That tracking system is theirs.
The name is his.

**The arc of the series** is: tracked soldier → Champion (the Houses can no longer hold or
designate him; he requires a new kind of name, his own, that belongs to no House record).
Maewen the Threader has a name like that. Soren doesn't yet. That's the arc.

## 2. The alchemy frame {#SS-GIW-2}

- **Dame Lyra** = the alchemist. Certified Transmutation researcher and Knight-rank practitioner;
  physically shaped by many successful infusions; loyal to the House and the procedure because
  both have given her everything she is. Has been issuing the standing order since at least his
  forty-first campaign.
- **Soren** = the Prima Materia (the base substance worth refining).
- **The Champion** = the Philosopher's Stone — the product of many infusions over many years,
  body warped by the work into something the House can no longer hold or designate.
- **Each deployment** = one stage of the Magnum Opus.

The alchemy frame is not metaphor. In the Caul, Transmutation IS alchemy: directed biological
change through Catalyst infusion, administered by certified practitioners, producing controlled
ascendance over time. Dame Lyra is a researcher in that discipline. Soren is her most interesting
candidate — anomalous perception at thin-membrane sites before any formal infusion. She wants
to know what the Transmutation does to someone who is already, somehow, further along than he
should be.

**Why Lyra keeps choosing him:**
Four recorded instances of anomalous perception at the boundary — not what the Scrying apparatus
captures, but what Soren perceives without it, before any infusion. This is not supposed to
happen. It suggests a natural sensitivity that, under Transmutation, might produce something she
has only theorized. She doesn't tell him this. He doesn't ask. She keeps sending him to the
strange places because those are where her data is.

**The arc endpoint:**
Many infusions over the arc of the story. Each deployment earns the next. Each infusion further
from baseline. He will become one of the old things: a Champion. Whether he considers this a
victory is the open question of the series.

## 3. Memory doctrine {#SS-GIW-3}

- **Soren does NOT have amnesia.** Memory accumulates and BLURS. Distant campaigns lose
  resolution like looking through water. Recent ones bleed into each other.
- **The smell of yggdra smoke** is his most stable carried anchor. Present across all campaigns.
- He knows his own record from documentation, not clean memory. He cannot confirm what is his
  vs. constructed from someone else's account of a campaign he was part of.
- **He does NOT ask existential questions.** He has accepted what he is. The doing is a
  condition, not an action.

## 4. Character rules {#SS-GIW-4}

**Soren (POV):**
- Long view, not detached — small things hit harder because the big things don't.
- Death is not dramatic. Specific moments are.
- Cost shown, never stated: name the material loss. Never "it was painful."
- Dame Lyra is the anchor. Not romantic necessarily — the one constant across many campaigns.
- **The inventory:** after every loss of consciousness (head wound, Sphere-proximity event,
  field surgery), he runs through the same sequence before opening his eyes: which hand, which
  foot, which eye. She has it in her notes. He knows she has it. Neither mentions it.

**Dame Lyra:**
- **House Ophiuchus.** The Vigil Seat — oldest installation in the Caul, northern Ridge
  country, practitioners who have been on the long watch longest.
- **Rank: Knight, closing on Paladin.** Certified Transmutation researcher and practitioner.
  Title "Dame" is the Knight-rank prefix.
- **Physical form (Transmutation-shaped):** Six feet five inches currently, lean and
  well-proportioned — not a mass build, a functional one. Each major battle she survives
  earns another post-action infusion. She is still growing. The number is not the ceiling.
  She is recognizably human. She is not baseline human.
- The alchemist. Reads Soren like her own document.
- She has seven theories and hasn't confirmed any.
- Her Interview ritual: established protocol across many campaigns. Yggdra smoke. Old Rite
  hands. Questions about what he perceived near the membrane. She doesn't tell him what she's
  building from his answers. He doesn't ask. That's how long they've been doing this.
- **Transmutation has been overwhelmingly positive for her.** She is not performing loyalty —
  she is genuinely loyal, because the House and the procedure gave her what she is. She believes
  in Transmutation because her experience of it has been one long ascendance with no meaningful
  setback. This makes her advocacy genuine rather than cynical, and her certainty about Soren's
  candidacy hard to argue with. She can tell him it works. She cannot promise he will survive
  the first dose. Both are true at the same time.
- She does not sentimentalize the relationship. He is her most important experiment.
  That doesn't preclude care.

**Sergeant Osei:**
- Executes Oathless fighters. "The third army."
- The moment establishes that Houses do not recognize combatants outside the system.

## 5. World mechanics for this universe {#SS-GIW-5}

> Full world facts live in [[../universes/CAUL]]. This section records story-specific
> applications and sensory anchors.

**Scrying** = observe a parallel Sphere's technology, copy the design, build it locally.
Nothing crosses. The engineers of the source Sphere never know. Like pressing a key into a mold.

**The Houses** = the political powers of the Caul. Organized around control of Scrying
installations — whoever controls observation controls what gets manufactured. The Living War
is fought over Scrying rights. Seven Houses, operating as 2–3 coalitions at any given moment.

**Myrmidons** = the Houses' professional soldiers. Named, ranked, tracked in House service
records by name and current posting. Death is permanent. A Myrmidon who dies does not return.

**The Oathless** = those outside the Houses. Third army; former Myrmidons and civilians who
swore no oath or broke it and kept walking. Not bandits by nature — survivors. Houses execute
them as unlicensed combatants. They know House tactics, know the terrain, and have nothing
left to lose.

**Thin-membrane sites** = geographic zones where the barrier between Spheres is thin. Anomalous
phenomena; incursion risk; valuable for Scrying. Most soldiers feel unease near them. Soren
does not. He perceives things in them that the apparatus hasn't captured.

**Scrying data blocks** = the cold bureaucratic notation the Houses use to record Sphere
observations. Interrupts medieval prose with industrial precision. Never dramatize them —
they are receipts, not revelation.

**Transmutation** = the guided body-refinement procedure. Certified practitioners administer
Catalysts (primarily Xerum 525 / Red Mercury) to willing recipients as a loyalty reward.
80% lethal on first infusion. Survivors begin physical ascendance. Multiple infusions over
years = Knight → Paladin → Champion. Botched or unsanctioned = catastrophic mutation.
Monster Meat is the same mechanism without guidance.

**Transmutation rank ladder:**
- Myrmidon → Knight (Dame for women) → Paladin → Champion
- Rank = infusion count + distinguished service; visible in physical form

**Dame Lyra's standing order** = she has been requesting reassignment of Soren to her research
station across many campaigns. He never sees the order. He just gets transferred.

**The orientation inventory** = Soren's tell: after any loss of consciousness, he runs through
the same sequence before opening his eyes. Which hand, which foot, which eye. A soldier's
habit. Lyra has it in her notes.

**Burnt ozone** = the smell at thin-membrane breach events. Physics-wrong air.

**Yggdra smoke** = Soren's most persistent sensory anchor across campaigns. The hardwood is
everywhere in Caul — weapon stocks, construction, furniture — but the particular sweet rot of
yggdra smoke is his through-line the way Silence is Kyle's through-line.

**Scrying operators** = long-term installation staff. Decades of experience at specific
apparatus and Sphere access points. Some receive low-dose Catalyst infusions tuned for
observation work. Institutionally bound through expertise, not supernatural mechanism.
**The vigil watcher** = a long-watch operator stationed at a thin-membrane site. Accumulated
presence. Not dramatic. The nod says everything.

**Bheur** = what the Houses say waits for the dead. Whether it is real is unknowable — death
is permanent and no one returns to report. Some Scrying operators claim to glimpse it in
visions gone sideways. It is feared. That is enough.

**The Prisoner** = the great mystery of the Caul. Extracosmic; never confirmed. The four
instances where Soren reports something noticing him at the membrane: this is what Lyra's
seventh theory is about.

**Champions** = the extreme end of Transmutation — many infusions over many years, awarded to
the winners. Bodies warped into battle-ready forms no House can designate or contain. Named,
not tracked. No House owns them. Houses court them.

**Bheur Incursion Predators** = the enormous bird-things. Native to Caul. Not from Bheur —
physical, biological, wrong. Wingspan measured in stories. They don't choose sides. A battle
in their territory draws them.

## 6. Narrative locks {#SS-GIW-6}

1. **The Prisoner thread.** Four recorded instances of anomalous perception at the membrane.
   The fourth is the inflection point. Lyra says "it implies something." Soren says "it implies
   the Prisoner." Neither confirms. The Prisoner is NEVER confirmed in-universe.
2. **Scrying data blocks = cold bureaucratic notation interrupting medieval prose.** The
   uncanny made mundane. Never dramatize the data blocks.
3. **Soren does not ask existential questions.** The doing is a condition, not an action.
4. **Yggdra smoke follows him.** The last beat of any arc ends with it.
5. **The orientation inventory.** After every loss of consciousness. Always.
6. **Death is permanent.** (SS-LAW-19.) A Myrmidon who dies is gone. No return.
   The theological debate about Bheur is unresolvable precisely because no one comes back.

## 8. Register and voice {#SS-GIW-8}

**Buehlman (Between Two Fires), not Gibson.** Medieval-formal register + clinical military
data intrusions (Scry catalogue blocks). Close third, Soren's POV.

**Negative Transmutation scenes — specific register:**
Body horror + grief + romantic tragedy + the dread of watching someone you love become a
stranger in their own skin while they're still trying to be who they were. The horror is the
transition, not the endpoint. Write the last moment of recognizable self, not the monster.
The transforming soldier apologizes. They use the wrong word, catch themselves once, stop
catching it. The people around them grieve before the person is gone.

- Long view, not detached — small things hit harder because the big things don't
- Sentences earn their weight; no glib clipped lines (dialogue only)
- Death is not dramatic. Specific moments are.
- Scrying data blocks = cold bureaucratic notation interrupting medieval prose
- Cost shown, never stated

**Visual language (LOCKED):** FFVI + Hebrides. Muted brown-green-gray, cold stone. Airships =
industrial dark military. No clean heroic color. The Houses' war-machinery looks like something
between medieval siege equipment and a WWI field camp — because that is what it is.
