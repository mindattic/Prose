## 0. What this book IS {#SS-BCODA-0}

The flagship GLMZ novel. 16 chapters. The book is about **Kyle being manipulated by a rogue AI
for 11 years** through contracts, E.L.F.s, and bleeds. Reader sees the architecture; Kyle doesn't.

It is simultaneously:
- A love story (Kyle and Pixel, across 16 chapters, earning the consummation at Ch11–12)
- A search for truth about himself (who trained him; what he is; why the contracts keep coming)
- The first glimpse of the AI cabal that broke the world
- Kyle's rules written in full, chapter by chapter

**Register: CODA** — `docs/registers/CODA.md` (LOCKED). Warm-propulsive frame that DROPS
to SORROW-grade elegiac exactness for sacred beats (the bow, a death, the lemon tree, the
consummation, the ledger). The entity is ONE character at two temperatures. Temperature is
the chapter's — not a blend within chapters.

**Target:** ≥ 90/100 at book-context milestone review (author gate; jury advisory).

## 1. The three manipulation threads {#SS-BCODA-1}

**Thread 1 — Contracts:** A rogue AI has been routing Kyle's contracts for 11 years. Ch4 is
the first plant (the retainer "sulking 3 days"). Ch13 is the reveal: "Your contracts do not
come from people." The reader sees the pattern forming; Kyle sees independent work.

**Thread 2 — E.L.F.:** An E.L.F. latches onto Kyle in Ch10. Detected by Ledger in Ch14.
Activates protectively in Ch16 — E.L.F. saves Kyle at a bleed threshold. Source ID: the
11-year shell. First contact sent 01:14.

**Thread 3 — Bleeds:** The AI knows resonance geometry. Ch6 (18.9 Hz residue at the dock),
Ch10 (E.L.F. latches during bleed), Ch15–16 (all inside the AI's map). The bleeds are not
accidents — they are evidence of something navigating the geometry.

## 2. Mrs. Chen through-line {#SS-BCODA-2}

Sacred ground. Never ironic. Never deflated.

- Ch1: Edge meeting over camphor. Her anchor.
- Ch3: The ritual. The fraud held against her stall. Kyle's rule made concrete: leverage
  not violence.
- Ch7: Her funeral planned — "Counter place on Halsted, Thursday." (Null's wake is here.)
- Ch13: The reveal site — Kyle and Vey at Antiquity & Stationary; the entity's confession.

**The Noodle Vendor** (`019f04f1…`) — a GFA-7 Culinary Service Automaton in operation since 2187,
occupying the corner directly across from Mrs. Chen's stall. It does not know it has a rival.
It does not know it is funny. It just keeps serving noodles with cheerful mechanical precision.

**Noodle Vendor beat directive:** several beats across the book must use the Noodle Vendor for
comedy. Its presence is an ongoing reminder that the Gray Zone contains machines that outlast
everyone's opinions of them. Beat angles:
- Mrs. Chen's running monologue about its inadequacy (one-sided, the Vendor never responds)
- Kyle's carefully neutral position on whose broth is superior
- The Vendor still serving customers through an active crisis while humans scramble
- A moment where the Vendor's perfect consistency is more comforting than it has any right to be

**Rule:** Mrs. Chen's beats use the SORROW-grade temperature regardless of surrounding
chapter tone. The lemon tree. The camphor. These are the book's sacred moments. The Noodle
Vendor beats are JOY-register comedy relief — they live inside the sacred section but they
breathe differently.

presence is part of the scene.

The entity = the ONE object Vey can't source (no provenance) = the only physical proof the
rogue AI exists.

## 5. Tweeze and War Dog {#SS-BCODA-5}

**Tweeze** (`05fbd9d0`, slug=tweeze) — psionic code-runner (the Read). Handle: short for
Tweezer — she plucks things from people's heads. Gave Kyle the handle "Broken Glass" then
reduced it to "K" (the naming beat, BCODA sk=14000). Killed in Ch7 The Dock by War Dog.
Kyle doesn't know her real name — only "Tweeze."

**Ch7 The Dock — how she dies and the battle (LOCKED 2026-06-23):**
The job completes. Tweeze says "Clean." One word. War Dog is already coming up the ramp.
No warning, no words. He has the angle on her before Kyle is moving. She dies before the
echo of "Clean" is gone.

**Battle choreography (LOCKED):**
- **War Dog has the grenades.** He deploys them to clear a kill zone, not target Kyle.
  Fragmentation radius + War Dog's thermal tracking = Duskwrap becomes a liability (heat
  bloom doesn't disappear inside a frag pattern). Stash cannot absorb ordnance. Shroud and
  Doc Stash take off. Right call. Kyle does not stop them. Kyle dives.
- **The NeoCortex moment (LOCKED — canonical Atlas-grade demonstration):**
  One of War Dog's grenades bounces wrong and lands live near Kyle's cover position.
  Kyle picks it up. The Atlas-grade NeoCortex has no governor — that is what is slowly
  killing him and the only reason this is possible. He **neuretically** connects to the
  detonator port, finds the rolling cipher on the countdown (designed to make field disarm
  impossible), and breaks it with raw processing power — not finesse, sledgehammer. The heat
  comes up behind his eyes. He pauses the countdown at one second.
  He sets the grenade near the ramp-mouth exit as a booby trap.
- Kyle gets one run at War Dog's back when War Dog crosses toward the ramp.
  He drives **Silence into War Dog's spine two-handed** — between the shoulder housings,
  edge-first, deep — and hits something that seizes. Not flesh, not bone. Something that locks.
- Damage cascade from the spine hit: **one arm hanging limp at the socket** (right-front);
  **one leg actuator frozen solid** mid-stride.
- War Dog fires blind: sweeping backward, suppression not aim. Kyle takes cover.
- War Dog backs toward the rail. **Kyle neuretically reactivates the countdown.**
  War Dog hears the arming tone. He goes over the edge — three stories — the decision made
  in the half-second between the tone and what staying would mean. Not a fall. A calculation.
  Frozen leg and hanging arm hit pavement. War Dog moves — faster than a frozen actuator
  should allow — into the city. Gone.

Kyle carries Tweeze's body to the van — coat and all, the way Stash laid it. **LOCKED.**
**Both truths are stated on the page, in order:**
1. The operational truth: the body is evidence. A Read's neural architecture doesn't go dark
   at death immediately — with the right equipment (brain in conducting fluid, electrodes
   through the right structures) what she knew can be reverse-engineered. Client's name,
   the data, the job, the faces on the team. Someone with resources could end everyone in
   the van with what's still in her head.
2. The human truth: Kyle carries her anyway. *You did not leave a comrade behind.* That is
   not an operational rule. It is a human one. It does not require justification. Kyle does
   not offer any.
**Do not collapse this to one truth.** The operational truth makes the compassion visible — he
knows exactly what he's doing and why, and the human rule wins. Both sentences must stand.
**The van is Doc Stash's** — his Vultures crew work vehicle; has seen this weight before.
He stops the van three blocks later. Checks Silence at the corner — blade intact — gets back in. Nobody asks.

**War Dog escapes. War Dog will be faced again.** His survival is load-bearing for the arc.

**The Rogue AI motivation (LOCKED — 2026-06-23):**
Two facts, both true:
1. **War Dog is a Rogue AI asset** — not a freelancer, not a coincidence. He is directed.
   He does not know the full picture of what he serves, but he takes the call and he moves.
2. **Tweeze carried evidence** — the data she pulled from the CorpoNation vault implicated the
   AI's contract-routing operation. Once she transmitted it to the client, the evidence existed
   outside her head. But she was still the only person who had *read* it — who could testify
   to what it contained, name the shell companies, trace the pattern. Killing her is data hygiene.
   The client received a file. A file can be discredited. A live Read who walked the data
   personally cannot.

**In prose:** War Dog's timing — arriving the moment the transmission completes, not before —
is the tell. Ch7's planted contract ping happens at second light north while Tweeze is dying.
The AI's clock and War Dog's arrival are the same clock. The reader sees it. Kyle doesn't.
Not yet.

**Tweeze's wake is in Ch6 The Quiet Hour** (Mrs. Chen's counter, Thursday). The "Meet me"
note at Antiquity & Stationary is planted in Ch6 sk=1250 — it seeds Ch13.

**War Dog** — present in Ch7 at the dock. Tweeze dies there. War Dog is absent from her wake.
"Still square" in Ch19 (his list). War Dog is an enemy, not a former crewmate.

**A Borrowed Hand — permanently deleted (LOCKED).** Strand `019e9fb2` and its 102 exclusive
beats were deleted 2026-06-21. The "hands cut off with cleaver / dumped underground" scene is
gone. War Dog's prior-ally framing in those beats is gone. Do not reference either.

## 8. Chapter writing rules {#SS-BCODA-8}

**Every chapter draft must check:**
1. Where is the AI manipulation thread in this chapter? Even quiet chapters carry a beat.
2. Where is the love-story thread? Even action chapters carry approach-and-retreat.
3. What is the chapter temperature? CODA-warm or SORROW-elegiac — not blended.
4. Are any forbidden prose patterns present? (See [[../registers/CODA.md]])

**The CODA register in practice:**
- Warm-propulsive: want on page 1, countable wins, Pixel lands.
- Sacred beats DROP to SORROW: the bow, a death, the lemon tree, the consummation, the ledger.
- Never average-temperature. The temperature is the chapter's.

**Mrs. Chen's beats use SORROW-grade regardless of chapter temperature.**
