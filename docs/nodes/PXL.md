## 0. What this story IS {#SS-PXL-0}

A specialist from Detroit does a brilliant job that goes sideways and has to skip town fast. She
arrives in GLMZ with nothing but her skills, her secondary kit, and the clothes on her back. The
Assessor — a CorpoNation talent-hunter who has had eyes on her since before she boarded the Pulse
— is waiting. She rebuilds from 8Φ. When he makes his pitch and she declines, he tries to make
her stay untenable. She doesn't defend. She goes inside his infrastructure and takes it apart.

This is the coolest Ghost story in the series. Pixel is a full-stack operator — Channeler,
Ghost, Splicer — with one edge nobody else in the cast has: she can ghost any machine with a
coherent frame, and she can channel AIs the way a musician plays an instrument. She ghosts
stoplights green without thinking about it. She ghosts a building maintenance crawler through a
corporate physical layer and doesn't call it anything. She didn't arrive in GLMZ looking for
work. She arrived because Detroit got too hot, the Assessor's people were already at the
terminal, and she rebuilt anyway.

**This is NOT:**
- A naive country girl discovering the city. She's from Detroit. She's been doing this for three years.
- A rescue story. Nobody saves her. Not once.
- A story about learning to be capable. She already is. This story is about what she does with it at scale.
- A Kyle story. He appears in one beat. He says three words.
- A romance. The Assessor is a predator. She finds him as interesting as a broken lock.
- A story where Bear appears.

## 1. The central symbol {#SS-PXL-1}

**Her mother's calibration instruments.** A set of precision measurement tools — resonance
meters, mesh calibrators, signal probes — that her mother used to tune industrial machinery in
Detroit's gray zone. When her mother died, the tools went into a hard case. Pixel has carried
that case across state lines and through three years of gray-zone work.

She put the hard case in checked cargo on the Pulse. The Assessor's people intercepted it at
the GLMZ terminal. He logged it: *misc. calibration hardware, no resale.* Sent to a scrap
merchant in the Seam margin. She traces it across GLMZ through the offensive, buys it back for
60Φ from a scrap merchant who had no idea what was in it. She says nothing about this during
the confrontation. She wanted to see what the Assessor would say when she asked about the
instruments. He said he burned them. She logs it: he lies, and he burns what he takes.

**The boots:** Her mother's work boots — one size too large, toes padded with folded paper since
she inherited them. In the final beat she notices she hasn't re-padded them since GLMZ. She
leans down and laces them tighter. She doesn't know when she stopped needing the paper.

## 2. The wound (background, never stated on the page) {#SS-PXL-2}

Her mother was a precision calibration specialist who spent twenty years keeping Detroit's
legacy industrial machines running — gray-zone work, unlicensed, because the licensed providers
had no interest in machines they didn't build. She was good at it. She died of a failed SNT
bridge — a licensed one, because the gray-zone clinics she trusted had waiting lists and the
corporate option was there and she was tired. The case number was RX-2217-44091. The response
team acknowledged it in eleven business days.

Pixel was twenty. She inherited the tools, the boots, and the specific understanding that
licensed infrastructure treats consent as a formality. She has been working unlicensed ever since.

**Rule: the wound never surfaces in this story.** It is the engine, not the content. The Assessor
is the same logic — a system that decided consent is expendable — but the echo is never named.

## 3. Drones as Tools {#SS-PXL-3}

She uses drones. She does not own one. There is no named, personified drone companion in this
story.

She ghosts drones. She commandeers them. A building security drone on its patrol route. A
gray-market transit unit running cargo in The Low. A maintenance quad idling on a landing pad.
Any aerial machine with coherent circuitry is a potential frame she can slip into and out of in
seconds. She picks them up, uses them, sets them down. The drone that maps an eighth floor and
the drone that surveys a relay housing are each whatever machine was available and pointed in the
right direction. None of them have names.

**The eigenlift connection:** She understands eigenlift coherence-frame physics from industrial
work in Detroit — not from a device she owns but from having worked around large-scale eigenlift
infrastructure for three years. She knows how the physics scales. She knows that a sufficiently
small coherence-frame fragment would suppress a drone's effective weight signature. She has
never built such a device. She has thought about what it would take. GLMZ's machine density
means she doesn't need one — there is always a drone she can ghost, and she is better than
surveillance systems that hunt for a specific signature when she can ghost any signature at all.

## 4. Her Abilities — The Full Stack {#SS-PXL-4}

Pixel is the most complete operator in the series. She does not announce this. She demonstrates
it with her hands.

### Channeler — She Talks to Machines

Standard channel work is session injection into networked frames. She does this faster and
cleaner than most — her Detroit background means she learned to channel on systems without
clean APIs, machines half-dead and poorly documented. She knows how to talk to hardware that
doesn't want to be talked to.

The edge: she can channel AIs directly. Not hack them — *negotiate* with them. An AI running
building management infrastructure, a maintenance scheduler, a security pattern recognizer —
she can address these as entities rather than as attack surfaces. Some of them respond. This
is not documented anywhere. It is not something the licensed channel curriculum teaches. She
does not know why it works; she has never met another operator who does it. It started in
Detroit with a half-dead AI running a legacy furnace controller that she needed to cooperate
rather than override. She talked to it. It cooperated.

In GLMZ, where AI density is orders of magnitude higher than Detroit, this ability opens
infrastructure that brute-force injection cannot reach.

### Ghost — She Rides Anything {#SS-PXL-4-ghost}

**The ability: Ghosting (eigenstate conscience transfer / ECT).** She transfers her conscious
perception into any machine with coherent circuitry — not just purpose-built frames. A building
maintenance crawler. A ventilation actuator array. A security camera with a motor mount. A
stoplight controller. An aerial drone on patrol. Anything with a coherent signal pathway and
enough sensory output to be worth inhabiting. Her body at rest during a ghost is her **Husk**.

Licensed Ghost work — formally eigenstate conscience transfer (ECT) — requires purpose-built
frames with handshake protocols and safety governors. She operates on hardware that doesn't
have any of that, which means she can go places licensed Ghosts cannot reach.

**Casual use:** She ghosts stoplights to make them turn green when she's crossing. She ghosts
building security cameras to check a corridor before stepping into it. She ghosts ventilation
fans to check air quality in a new space. This is as automatic to her as checking her neuretics
for messages — a background habit she doesn't narrate, just does.

Detroit gave her this. Detroit's machines are old, mixed, layered — 20th and 21st century
industrial hardware under three generations of patch-and-extend. She learned to ghost machines
that weren't designed to be ghosted, that had no frame-spec at all, that she had to find the
coherence pathway in herself because there was no manual. In GLMZ she is surrounded by
mechanical density that Detroit never had. She is a native speaker in a city that thinks this
language doesn't exist.

**In-universe vocabulary:** The ability is *ghosting*. The operator class is *Ghost*. The body
at rest during a ghost = the *Husk*. The formal term is eigenstate conscience transfer (ECT).
"Rider" as a job title is retired (SS-A38) and does not appear. "Exo" and "RFO" are retired —
replaced by Ghost / ECT throughout.

### Splicer — She Builds and Breaks

She can crack proprietary service menus, model RF sweep protocols, trace financial routing
through gray-zone relay nodes. She assembles dossiers from public records and gray-zone data
deposits. She can reverse-engineer access patterns from a single credential call structure.

What distinguishes her from standard splicers: she builds hardware. She can look at an access
system and not just crack it but understand the physical implementation, which tells her things
a software-only attack surface won't reveal. The calibration instruments she inherited are
precision hardware she can use and repair. She thinks in physics before she thinks in code.

### Neuretic Etching {#SS-PXL-4-etching}

Visible external trace of sub-dermal neural mesh at skull nerve ports. Her own etching is
mid-grade — functional, clean, minimal scarring. She did not have it installed at an Atlas
clinic. It was done in Detroit by someone she trusted, with instruments she inspected herself.

The Assessor's etching is immaculate — top-tier, geometric micro-patterning, expensive as a
year's income for most operators. He wears it like jewelry. She notices it the same way she
notices any hardware: status signal, installation cost, what it implies about who installed it
and under what conditions.

## 5. Her Handle: "Pixel" {#SS-PXL-5}

**She arrived as Pixel.** The handle was earned in Detroit — her calibration work was
pixel-precise, and the reputation preceded her. She does not explain the origin to anyone in
GLMZ. It simply is her name, and anyone who knows anything in the gray-zone net knows it.

When the Assessor uses it at his approach, she notes he did his homework. She does not find
this flattering.

Her legal name (never used on the page) is Vera.

## 7. The City (GLMZ Context) {#SS-PXL-7}

**The Blur and the Pulse:** Physically, Pulse passengers travel in individual sealed spheres.
Neuretics produce a shared hallucination: the interior reads as a 200-year-old intercity train.
Passengers experience the train, not the pod. Characters call their pod "the car."

**The aerobloc:** In transit she sees a drifting aerobloc — a city block held aloft by an
eigenlift coherence frame, unmoored, floating on thermals through The Blur. She recognizes
the physics immediately — the same fight with weight she knows from industrial work in Detroit,
at a scale that makes her recalculate. She thinks about what a fist-sized coherence-frame
fragment could do in the right chassis — suppress effective weight, invisible to aerial sweeps.
Someone built that block with nine thousand engineers. Both would be cheating the same physics.
Both would be in the air.

**Altitude and class (SS-A32/A33):** The float layer above GLMZ is where wealth lives. Sky
People are the most privileged residents; their altitude is not incidental. The Low (30–320m)
is the ungoverned band — persistent industrial haze, no claimed authority, where gray-market
vertical transit moves people and cargo off the official displays. Pixel operates at street
level and in The Low. The float layer is backdrop.

**Detroit vs. GLMZ:** Detroit's gray zone is tight — three years there made her a big fish in
a contained pond. GLMZ is ten times the mechanical density, ten times the network complexity,
and ten times the population. Her skills scale. Her network does not — she has to rebuild from
nothing.

**The Pivot:** A gray-zone boarding house in the Seam margin. Cheap. The reason it's cheap:
the building is old enough that three generations of fiberoptic trunk lines run beneath its
foundation, unregistered, laid by successive operators who needed bandwidth off the official
grid. She scouted the thermal signature in the basement before she walked in the door. 45Φ a
week for a room, a power access point, and the most ubiquitous unmonitored fiberoptic splice
access in the Seam.

## 8. Character Rules {#SS-PXL-8}

**Pixel:**
- She is 20-21. She moves like someone who grew up in manufacturing spaces — economical, attuned
  to mechanical sound, with calibrated instincts for when something is wrong.
- She is not naive. She is not new. She is *unmapped.* GLMZ is territory she doesn't have
  detailed data on yet.
- She **enjoys this.** The work is interesting. The machines are interesting. AI conversation is
  interesting. Even the problem of being stripped and stranded — she approaches it like a puzzle,
  not a trauma. The question is always: what do I have left and what can I build from it?
- She is allowed to be angry. Once. Briefly. When she finds out his squad ran the skim operation.
- Her voice notices hardware before people.
- She is not performing competence for anyone. She just does things.
- Ghosting is as routine to her as breathing. She ghosts things constantly without marking it —
  a stoplight, a camera to check a corner, a door lock. Show it casually, don't announce it.
- She is Pixel. She does not wait to be helped. When she needs to confront something, she walks
  through the door herself.

**The Assessor:**
- **Visual:** White hair, slicked back. Dark sunglasses worn indoors, always. Top-tier neuretic
  etching — immaculate geometric lines at temples and jaw, worn like decoration. White suit, long
  coat. Everything about him is a display of control.
- **What he is:** He runs a squad-for-hire under CorpoNation cover. He finds exceptional
  gray-zone talent before the corpos find it, offers protection and pay, and when they decline,
  applies pressure until they capitulate or leave.
- **His operation:** He had eyes on Pixel in Detroit for weeks. He arranged the Detroit job to go
  sideways — or let it go sideways, knowing it would push her toward GLMZ. His people were at
  the Pulse terminal when she arrived. They skimmed her account and intercepted her checked kit.
  He waited three weeks for her to be desperate enough to be receptive. She wasn't desperate.
  She rebuilt.
- **What he wants from Pixel:** Her ability — the full stack, the AI negotiation she doesn't know
  anyone knows she has, the ghosting range nobody else in the city has. He wants to own it.
- **His manner:** Charming, controlled, precise. When something surprises him he doesn't get
  louder. He gets quieter. More exact.
- **The horror:** His theory isn't wrong about everything. What he decided was expendable is
  consent. That single thing makes everything else corrupt.

**Kyle:**
- One beat. Standing in his doorway as she opens hers. Looks like he just came back from
  somewhere with a different set of physics than a hallway.
- Three words: **"Give em hell."** Door closes.
- Do not give him interiority. He is observed, not rendered.

## 9. Narrative Locks {#SS-PXL-9}

1. **She solves it herself.** No one helps her. Not once. Kyle says three words and closes his
   door. The confrontation is entirely hers.
2. **She enjoys the work.** This is not grim survival. She is good at what she does and she
   knows it. The pleasure of competence is visible even when the circumstances are terrible.
3. **The instruments were never burned.** She had already recovered them before the confrontation.
   She asked to see what he'd say. He lied. She filed it.
4. **ACS is not evil — it is structurally unhelpful.** If she encounters an authority, it does
   its job correctly — correctly for the institution, not for her.
5. **The Assessor's logic is legible, not cartoonish.** His theory is partially correct. What
   makes him the villain is the one thing he decided to discard.
6. **Kyle's words are "Give em hell" — not "stay safe."** He sees her going to confront something
   and affirms the going. She does not need permission and he is not offering any.
7. **Bear does not appear in this story.**
8. **The AI negotiation is never named or explained.** She does it. It works. The story does not
   offer an explanation, because she doesn't have one either.
9. **She arrived as Pixel.** The handle is not earned in this story — it is what she already is.
10. **Drones are tools.** There is no named companion drone. She ghosts whatever machine she
    needs, uses it, moves on. No drone in this story is a character.
11. **Ghosting is shown, not announced.** Casual ghosts (stoplights, cameras, doors) happen as
    action, not exposition. She doesn't narrate the ability in her own voice.

## 10. The Authorial Spine {#SS-PXL-10}

### Act 1 — Escape and Arrival (Beats 1–4)

**Beat 1 — Detroit, Last Job (the Escape)**
She is mid-job: data extraction from a CorpoNation relay facility in Detroit's Old Packard
District. The job is going beautifully — she has the facility's maintenance scheduler running
her access rotation on a direct address, two security drones ghosted and running counter-patrol
to her own movement, and a ventilation actuator array carrying her through the physical layer
of the relay node. She is in three places at once. It feels like music.

Then the counter-intrusion unit finds her substrate signature. Four seconds before it
cross-references. She pulls out of the scheduler, ghosts the actuator array, threads through
the duct junction she'd already mapped, and gets clear before the lockdown closes. Her work
kit was staged at the access point on the wrong side of the lockdown. She leaves it.

She goes to her Detroit workshop, puts the hard case with her mother's instruments in checked
cargo for the Pulse — forty pounds isn't carry weight when you're moving fast — books the
ticket with her dead-drop reserve, and walks to the platform in the rain in the clothes she
was working in.

On the platform she runs a passive sweep and finds it: two separate credential queries on
separate gray-zone identities, coordinated, timed too close together to be coincidence.
Someone has had both identities cross-referenced for a while. Not counter-intrusion. Someone
else's homework. She boards the Pulse.

**Beat 2 — The Blur**
Through the Pulse from Detroit. The train-hallucination car. Through the window: a drifting
aerobloc, unmoored, moving on thermals three hundred meters up. She recognizes the eigenlift
physics immediately — the same fight with weight she knows from industrial work, at a scale
that makes her recalculate. She thinks about what a fist-sized coherence-frame fragment could
do in the right chassis. Someone built that block with nine thousand engineers. Both would be
cheating the same physics. Both would be in the air. She watches until it's gone. She does not
expect to feel anything about it. She is wrong.

In the dead zone of The Blur, a third credential query comes in on the secondary identity she
only uses for dead drops. Too clean to be coincidence. Someone is tracking her through the
transit network. She notes it and does not react. She boards the last leg.

**Beat 3 — GLMZ Arrival: Already Watched**
Union Station, West Town GLMZ. The city hits her before the smell does. She steps off the
Pulse and spends thirty seconds reading the terminal. Four credential pings in the first three
minutes — professional, coordinated, someone who built this net specifically for her arrival.

Within twenty minutes her secondary account is drained to 8Φ. A passive tap in the terminal's
gray-zone access layer, stripping her authentication signals while she was still moving. Clean
work. The relay signature: layered handoff structure, CorpoNation-adjacent. The same
architectural hand as the credential queries on the Detroit platform. She saves it. The checked
cargo pickup address maps to a storefront registered four days ago that has already served its
one use.

She does a full inventory. Secondary kit in her jacket. The boots on her feet. 8Φ. The skills
in her head, which cannot be skimmed.

She ghosts a stoplight green on the way out of the terminal and keeps walking.

**Beat 4 — Night Zero**
She needs shelter without spending the 8Φ. Three blocks from the terminal: a building complex
with an audible environmental system — a twenty-two Hz undertone, coolant routing working
harder than it should. She finds a building maintenance drone on its automated patrol route
and ghosts it for four minutes — maps the HVAC routing, locates a maintenance services bay in
the sub-basement, unenrolled in the new credential system, default unlock still held. She
returns to her Husk.

She addresses the building management AI directly through the environmental control subnet.
Explains she's a calibration specialist, she's seen the coolant fault flag, she can fix it
tonight. She needs the bay until morning. The AI considers — longer than network latency, the
pause of something thinking — and the bay access clears.

She spends forty minutes on the coolant coupling and sleeps. In the morning the AI has logged
the repair under an anonymous contractor timestamp. She hadn't given her name. She sits with
that a moment, then picks up her secondary kit and goes to find work.

### Act 2 — Rebuilding (Beats 5–8)

**Beat 5 — Gray Zone Reentry**
She maps three boarding houses against two criteria: price and fiberoptic access. The Pivot is
cheap because the building is old and nobody has done anything to it since the 2180s expansion,
which means three generations of unregistered fiberoptic trunk lines run beneath its foundation.
She saw the thermal signature in the basement when she walked past. 45Φ a week for a room and
a power point and the best unmonitored splice access in the Seam margin.

She takes the room. She's on the gray-zone boards by that afternoon — 90Φ job, clean extraction,
paid on receipt. Runway by nightfall.

She starts mapping GLMZ. The AIs here are more present than anything she worked with in
Detroit. Three building systems address her in the first two days without being prompted —
flagging faults, offering data, acting as if her presence is a resource rather than an
intrusion. She files this as an unexplained variable and keeps working.

**Beat 6 — What Was Taken**
Two weeks of parallel processing: gray-zone boards for Φ, the relay signature tracking for
intelligence. Nine days of passive traces before she has the chain topology. The relay's final
address resolves to a node in The Low.

She needs eyes at 210 meters without surfacing on aerial surveillance. She finds a gray-market
transit drone running cargo through The Low and ghosts it for twelve minutes — navigates to
the relay housing bolted to the underside of an eigenlift platform strut. Active. CorpoNation
registry tail. She's still reading when the node shuts down clean — detection protocol or
someone pulling the plug. She holds the drone on the strut for six seconds, releases it to
its route, and returns to her Husk.

She has a registry node. A relay architecture. A timing window. She opens a new file and
starts building the dossier.

**Beat 7 — Ghosting (the GLMZ edge)**
A job needs access to a sealed corporate node. A building maintenance crawler in the basement
is on the same physical network segment — dumb machine, not designed as a frame, no handshake
protocol. She finds the coherence pathway in seventeen minutes and ghosts it. From inside the
corporate physical layer she reads what she needed. 340Φ.

On the way out: GLMZ has a maintenance crawler in every large building she's walked past.
Eigenlift systems, environmental controllers, freight-lift mechanisms, aerial patrol drones —
every kind of machine she's ever ghosted, in concentrations she couldn't have pictured from
Detroit. Any machine with coherent circuitry is a potential frame. She has just arrived on the
right continent.

**Beat 8 — The Assessor**
Three weeks in. Rebuilt to operational: 900Φ, four active clients, room paid through next
month. A job comes through a source she doesn't recognize. She takes it. Midway through:
someone is watching her work from inside the same network segment. Not interfering. Watching.
She clocks the observer's entry point: a CorpoNation access token, masked as gray-zone. The
relay structure matches a node she flagged in the dossier. Same hand as the terminal skim.

She cuts the session. Collects the pay. Goes back to the Pivot with a partial ID and the
beginning of a theory.

Two days later the Assessor contacts her directly. White hair, dark glasses, immaculate
neuretic etching. She listens to all of it. She asks three questions. She thanks him for his
time.

### Act 3 — The Offensive (Beats 9–12)

**Beat 9 — Background (she digs)**
Two days on his history. Everything he said checks out: the squad, the CorpoNation cover, the
pay. The operators who signed three years ago are still there — their credentials integrated
into his infrastructure. It's not coercion you can name. It's dependency architecture. One
more thing: the relay structure in the terminal skim matches a node his squad owns. He ran the
skim. He had her tracked from Detroit, let the job go sideways, and had people waiting at the
terminal. She adds it to the dossier. She declines formally by not responding to his
follow-up.

**Beat 10 — The Campaign**
He doesn't argue. He applies pressure. Two relay channels dark within forty-eight hours. A
client cancels — she traces it to an entity in his CorpoNation registry. Her building
credentials at The Pivot locked out for exactly four hours. A gear order rerouted.

Each incident deniable. Together a campaign. His CorpoNation infrastructure means he can
reach things from inside his own network that she cannot block from the street. She cannot
defend against this indefinitely.

She is not going to defend. She is going to go inside.

**Beat 11 — The Offensive**
Four days watching his network footprint. By night she ghosts aerial patrol drones in the
ungoverned band above his squad's operational site — climbing into whatever unit is running a
pass near the building, reading the electromagnetic signature through its sensors, mapping
physical entry and egress patterns. By day she channels into his squad's coordination node,
addresses its management AI directly, and it turns toward her — the warmth, the oriented
attention. She reads.

She finds the full skim operation. Her instruments: logged as "misc. calibration hardware,
no resale." Sent to a scrap merchant in the Seam margin. An address. A timestamp.

She is angry for ninety seconds. Then she goes back to work.

She plants a ghost node in his financial routing. Stages a dossier release — six channels,
independent clocks, requiring her active cancellation to stay silent. Then she burns one
account: the specific account that funded the terminal skim, routed back through his own
infrastructure. She extracts 8Φ — the exact balance she walked out of the terminal holding —
and routes them to a gray-zone mutual aid node. He will understand the message.

She notes the scrap merchant's address. She knows where she's going tomorrow.

**Beat 12 — Kyle**
The instruments are in a hard case on the table in 2E. She cleans them. She packs her
secondary kit. She opens the door.

Across the hall, Kyle is in the doorway of 2D. He looks like he has just returned from
somewhere with a different set of physics than a hallway. He looks at her. She is loaded up.

"Give em hell."

He closes the door. She goes downstairs.

### Act 4 — The Confrontation and Close (Beats 13–14)

**Beat 13 — The Confrontation**
She goes to where he is. Presents the dossier: the skim, the campaign, the financial routing
she has been reading for four days. Shows him the ghost node still live. Shows him what's
staged to release the moment she doesn't cancel it.

She says: give me back my mother's instruments.

He says they were destroyed in processing. He says this the way someone says a thing they
believe cannot be checked.

She watches his face. She adds it to the file: he lies, and he burns what he takes. She does
not show him the instruments are already on the table in her room.

She says: this stops. He calculates. He says: it stops.

She leaves. In the stairwell she cancels the staged release. Deactivates the ghost node. He
has nothing to point to. She was a ghost. She did not become one by accident.

**Beat 14 — The Close**
She comes up the stairs. The latch on 2D is already engaged.

She goes inside. Opens the hard case. The instruments are there — cleaned, seated in the foam
in the right order, undamaged. The scrap merchant charged her 60Φ and never opened the inner
partition.

She puts on music. Loud. The kind that goes through walls.

She looks at the room — small, organized, hers: everything she built from 8Φ and three weeks
of work. She looks at the boots. The toes are not padded. She cannot place when she stopped.
She leans down and laces them tighter. She leaves them as they are.

Her name is Pixel. She is in GLMZ. She is staying.

## 11. Register and Voice {#SS-PXL-11}

Close-third, Pixel's POV. She is the camera.

**What her voice notices:**
- Hardware before people. Calibration states. Network architecture. The specific sensation of
  coherence handing over when she ghosts a new frame.
- AI responses — she notices when they are unexpected, when they volunteer data, when they seem
  *interested.* She does not yet know what to make of this.
- Physical sensation in technical language: satisfaction is "the last spec falling into range."
  Anger is "the temperature dropping two degrees behind the sternum."
- Music as a throughline — always present during work, never named aloud.

**What her voice does NOT do:**
- Explain emotion.
- Marvel. She observes. The aerobloc is a physics problem she finds unexpectedly moving. She
  does not announce that she finds it moving.
- Chase identity for its own sake. She chases data because data answers questions.
- Refer to her mother.
- Narrate the ghosting. She ghosts a stoplight and keeps walking. She doesn't describe the
