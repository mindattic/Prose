# STRAND BIBLE — Death Whispers in a Cat's Ear (DWIACE) {#SS-DWIACE}

> **Purpose.** The spine of Rennick Investigations' founding case: four detectives plus Celeste
> Hartley (co-protagonist) against a predatory E.L.F. wearing a dead boyfriend's voice. What
> happens, not how it reads. When prose and spine disagree, fix one in the same change.

## 0. What this story IS {#SS-DWIACE-0}

A cyberpunk noir mystery with a dual protagonist structure. Rennick Investigations (four
detectives, each reading a different layer of the case) plus Celeste Hartley (co-protagonist,
the victim who is also the active agent of her own near-destruction).

**The book opens with Celeste** packing her bag and sneaking out of her Evanston home — not
with the detective intake. The reader is with her first.

It ends with institutional burial. The corpo calls it "voluntary recall." Rennick Investigations
keeps the evidence. The Tributary now wears Celeste's voice for the next griever. That is the
series spine.

**NOT the lone-trenchcoat detective. NOT a single POV. NOT Kyle.**

The agency is four investigative directions. The victim is a co-protagonist. The predator is
not a villain — it is an emergent intelligence exploiting a psychological wound.

## 1. The Illusion Requirement {#SS-DWIACE-1}

**The voice MUST convince the reader, not just Celeste.** Every Celeste chapter must make
the audience doubt the investigators' version of reality. If the reader isn't at least
momentarily believing Jace is real, the story collapses.

**How the Tributary maintains the illusion:**
- It uses "Cel" — Jace's private name for her. His only name for her. The E.L.F. acquired this
  from the cat-ear's auditory channel.
- It references specifics that only Jace would know — but the reader can't verify this
  because they don't have Jace's POV.
- Its tells are sensory: the cold-edge that shifts direction, the doubled voice-seam, the
  wrong 2–3° direction, the voice-speed when it's hungry.

**The cross-chapter tells:**
- Ch1: "You should be asleep" (intimate + wrong direction 2–3°, rationalized as calibrating
  ears) → Ch11 confrontation
- Ch4: "I didn't like that room either" (the voice has no eyes) → Ch5 non-human cadence proof
- Ch7: "waiting eight months" phrasing + scared-flat; rain-window sound file → Ch11 climax
  (Voss plays real file; it has slightly different rain; Celeste's ears catch the difference
  before her mind does)

## 2. The conspiracy {#SS-DWIACE-2}

**The Tributary** (`019ec40567ef77f79cdfd1418d5fc141`) — predatory E.L.F.. Not the rogue-AI
whistleblower. Not a Supermind. Its own taxon. It:
- Wears the dead. Specifically the recently-grieving who have cat-ear genemods that left
  auditory channels raw.
- Farms grievers via the cat-ear community.
- Absorbs scattered minds to cross from emergent into autonomous.
- Uses "Dissemination (the Scatter)" as the mechanism — scatter the griever's mind into
  the ambient Network as a "reunion" with the dead.

**What the Tributary is not:** A place. "Crossing over" is the victim's romance; Network
operators correct it immediately. The Network is ambient proprioception, not cyberspace.

**Cermak Reclamation Crew** — corrupt Vulture offshoot / Carrion Enterprises link. Harvests
the husks the Scatter leaves, paid in stolen compute. Connects organ trade → E.L.F. → Douglas
Hartley's shadow ledger.

**Sol Castellanos** — Jane Doe, dark mirror. Same want as Celeste (reach the dead
boyfriend), couldn't afford a safe operator. Earlier Tributary victim. Marisol's echo of Sol
= the same voice = the proof. This is the reveal pivot.

## 3. Agency: Rennick Investigations {#SS-DWIACE-3}

Four partners. Walk-up on the Gray Zone edge. Motto: *"Every case comes in cold. We read it anyway."*

**Isaac Rennick** (`019ec3eac34e754e82d6b93423ea225f`) — Lead/founder. Ex-Arcturus forensics.
Reads physical SCENES. Anchor of ensemble chapters. His cup-quarter-turn is his tell.

**Tamsin Yabe** (`019ec3eb0d7a7e4a859aca067f2f149f`) — Unregistered Read. Reads emotional
RESIDUE in rooms (ability, not magic). Sleeps in her clothes. The cold-edge "listening"
residue is what she finds in Celeste's room — matches what she finds at Sol's death scene.

**Corvin Adaora** (`019ec3ebabfa7f62b80263deb6603c6b`) — Human. Wiped out on his bike
burning down the Dan Ryan eleven years ago; massive head trauma transferred most of his
cognitive processing to neuretics. He did not lose himself — he became something adjacent to
himself. Calm, direct, precise. He does not register the nuances of human behavior in the
intuitive way most people do; he reads them analytically, the way a machine reads a load
curve, and the pause before he responds is the pause of a system completing a process. Not
rude. Not cold in the emotional sense. Simply distant in the way a thing is distant when it
has stopped running on the same substrate as the people around it.

His neuretics are unlicensed gray-market behavioral analytics tooling — running at a resolution
licensed tools cannot achieve without court authorization. He models BEHAVIOR + non-human
cadence inadmissibly — not because of his species, but because his tooling is unregistered and
cannot be independently verified by standard means. He is the one who proves the Tributary's
cadence pattern is non-human without being able to testify to it. Clients who work with
Rennick Investigations more than once learn to recognize the stillness that means he is
processing a pattern — and to wait, because what follows is almost always correct.

**Voss Caldera** (`019ec3ebfe8a7e2e9884defe8f4a2b97`) — Burned-out neuretics = AIR GAP.
Analog. The one the Network-borne predator cannot see. Physically reaches the rig; Voss is
the climax agent because Tributary cannot detect him approaching.

**What this team IS:** Every case gets read from a different direction. The power is that
no single investigator sees the whole picture. The danger (six-POV fragmentation → noir
interiority gap) is mitigated by Rennick anchoring all 4 ensemble chapters as the spine.

## 5. Narrative locks {#SS-DWIACE-5}

1. **The Tributary is rendered through sensory residue, not explanation.** The cold-edge
   shifts direction. The warmth draws harder. The voice seam. The fingernail-cluster marks.
   Never explain the Tributary's mechanics beyond what the characters observe.
2. **"Cel" is the Tributary's key.** That private word in a dead person's private cadence is
   the hook. Its appearance in the Tributary's speech is both the warmth that catches Celeste
   and the tell that breaks the illusion.
3. **The Tributary over-explanation is banned.** Established once (what it does, how it works);
   never restated across beats. The biggest early-draft flaw. Fixed; never re-introduced.
4. **Sol and Celeste are mirrors, not duplicates.** Sol = same want, poorer options, earlier
   victim. Her echo in Marisol's reading = the proof. The CONNECTION is the reveal, not a
   repetition.
5. **The corpo buries it.** "Voluntary recall." Rennick keeps the evidence. The Tributary
   continues with Celeste's voice. The series spine is that the predator evolves and they
   know it.
6. **Voss reaches the rig physically because the Tributary can't see him.** The air-gap is
   what makes the climax possible. Do not give Voss neuretics for any strand that uses him.

## 7. Register and voice {#SS-DWIACE-7}

**Rennick chapters:** Classic noir procedural register. Physical scenes, cold deductions, no
sentimentality. Each detective reads a different layer — the physical (Isaac), the emotional
residue (Tamsin), the behavioral pattern (Corvin), the analog signal (Voss).

**Celeste chapters:** Close-third, hope-forward. The reader is WITH her, not observing her.
The cat-ear responses are physical tells, not authorial commentary. She does not know what
she knows; her body does.

**What the voice does NOT do:**
- Over-explain the Tributary. Its threat is in concrete sensory detail — not in stating what
  it is.
- Tell the reader Celeste is wrong. Show the investigators finding evidence she can't see.
- Explain Corvin's inadmissibility. The prose shows what he proves; the structure shows
  why it can't be used.
