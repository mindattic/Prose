# The Vigil's End — Story Bible (VIGL)

## Logline
A Liturgy Templar with thirty years of faith pursues stolen Ocipheus memory-relic across the full width of the Cauld, discovers what the Liturgy actually is, and refuses the Canon's writ in the Sinter quarantine zone — losing everything she built her identity on.

## Register
*The Blacktongue Thief* + *Between Two Fires* (Buehlman). Dark fantasy. Earthy, physical, dangerous. Gallows humor as armor. The world is trying to kill you and has been for a long time. Violence is quick and unglamorous. History is felt in the body, not the intellect. No false heroism.

## Characters

### Dame Lyra M-101 — POV Protagonist
Thirty-year veteran Liturgy Templar, Ocipheus Station archive keeper. Three Transmutation infusions — permanent, each consented to with full knowledge of the cost. ~6'5", wide through the shoulder. True believer: Ocipheus IS her identity. She does not have an identity beneath this. The Relic theft is the first crack in a structure she has never needed to examine. Her arc: true believer → erosion → refusal → not-yet-anyone.

### Rowe (Edvard Rowe) — B-plot POV (flashback), then party member
Pulled through the membrane from Sphere 31 — a textile mill worker on a Thursday morning in autumn. The intake oath was administered before he had any language for it. He didn't agree. Seven months in Vierna infantry before deserting. Four words of Cauld when found: factory, smoke, morning, gone. By the channel crossing he can testify in full sentences. He is what Scrying actually costs.

### Kressida Haun — Party member (subplot carrier)
Atrament battle-rig pilot, BR-7. Fused to her rig in the Schmalburg raid — anti-armor round drove the power-coupling housing into her spinal-adjacent neural pathway. Cannot survive surgery to remove it. Atrament classified her as a logistics problem. She deserted. Her rig sensors see the membrane boundary as a resource pressure gradient. She does not get her surgery. The book ends with her still in the rig.

### Tavardo Leal (Tavar) — Party member
Lacerta intelligence operative. NOT a captain. Does NOT have a ship. Knows where a Liturgy vessel (Eirene-7) is moored two hours west of Sal Vento. His own codes, not the Houses'. Has other reasons for helping that he never states.

### Orim Zebulun — Party member
Old man, Calyx origin, no House, sixty years of minor Scrying. Was at Ocipheus Station completing a verification contract the night of the theft. Scried the south passage for the thieves and once more after because something felt wrong. Tells Lyra everything. The book's living history. His scry is refused at Forge Hearth — first time in sixty years. Honest face of complicity: knew about the intake and the slave labor for forty years and continued taking the contracts.

### Vega — B-plot POV (4 Scribe chapters)
Lyra's younger sister, mid-twenties. Liturgy canonical Scribe. Copies and notarizes Liturgy canonical orders. Has been processing Canon Prelacze's authorizations for eight months and understands the pattern. She is the canonical witness: her notary seal on the Sinter aperture event is the first instrument Prelacze cannot authorize away. The night before Lyra left, they drank together, laughed, she did an impression of the Canon's correspondence style; Lyra left a note ("back in three days, don't eat the station bread").

### Owain Caerglas — Pallor Knight, antagonist
Two infusions. Theft squad leader. When Lyra has him down, he names Canon Prelacze and says: "I thought I was doing right. I don't know anymore." He is who Lyra was at his stage. She leaves him alive.

### Canon Immaculate Tomasz Prelacze — Villain
Liturgy Canon. Commissioned the theft. Wants the Relic at the Sinter aperture to surface evidence that the Liturgy's founders had foreknowledge of the Year 1079 Sinter catastrophe — to position himself as the reformer who faces what the old Canon hid. Cannot control what the Relic actually shows. He chose Lyra specifically: a Templar with a thirty-year loyalty record is useful cover. She holds the evidence; not him.

### Sgt. Ardea Marten — Ocipheus soldier
Lyra's lead soldier. Killed in the Schmalburg raid pulling Rowe from a crossfire. Thirty seconds. Death is permanent.

## Subplot
Kressida Haun's relationship with her rig — parallel to Rowe's language acquisition; both about what happens when you can no longer separate yourself from the thing the system did to you.

## Theme
What are you loyal to when you find out what the thing you loved actually is?

## Key Structural Decisions
- Resolution: MIXED — external success (Relic recovered, writ refused) + significant cost (Ardea dead, Lyra's standing destroyed)
- Moral polarity: AMBIVALENT — no House is the villain; the system is; Owain and Lyra are mirror images at different stages
- Ending: AVALANCHE — Canon confrontation arrives before Lyra has time to plan; she acts from who she has become
- Epilogue: NONE
- Escalation: institutional theft → illegal border crossing → pursuit/combat → Schmalburg (death + fusion) → Forge Hearth (cosmic horror) → testimony (personal devastation) → Pallor confrontation → Sinter aperture (moral climax)
- The mobilization is NOT resolved at the end — the book ends with the Liturgy's military build-up still in place
