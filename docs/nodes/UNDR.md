## Voice & Prose Laws (SS-A31 + Register — MANDATORY)

### Accessible Prose (SS-A31)
- Sentences: short-to-medium. Break any clause over ~20 words at a natural joint.
- Narration uses contractions naturally: he's, she'd, it wasn't, couldn't, wouldn't.
- Vocabulary: Anglo-Saxon base words preferred. Avoid Latinate abstractions when a plain word exists.
- No stacked subordinate clauses. If a sentence needs two "that/which" clauses, split it.
- No pseudo-profundity. Every metaphor must survive literal scrutiny.
- No filler-wit (wry universal-truth lines).

### Glim's Register — DEEP (LOCKED)
Close third on Glim, inside the ontology of the Underclan. The narrator renders the world **as Glim's mind holds it** — never surface vocabulary in his POV except as tribe mis-rendering.

Mis-rendering table (use these; do not explain them):
- flashlight / lamp → "a caught star" / "the white that bites"
- gun / gunshot → "a thunder-stick" / "the noise that opens a hole"
- the sun → "the Burning Eye" / "the white with no edge"
- sky → "the lid taken off the world"
- surface speech → "noise with edges" / "barking that wants something"
- tokamak plant → "the Warm" / "where the Current breathes hot"
- glowing lab-rat → "a candle" (light-keeper AND air-canary)
- money (Φ) → "shines" (surface trinkets; worthless below)
- antibiotics → "salt that fights the Hot Breath"

**Smell leads.** The Underclan navigate by smell, air, sound. Glim's mother is a smell before a face. Body before mind — always.

**Narrator writes full sentences.** Clipped / fragment cadence is for DIALOGUE AND TAP-SPEECH ONLY.

**"Toby" never appears in Glim's POV narration** until the reunion forces it. After the reunion, the war of the two names is interior and never cleanly resolved.

Surface-plain prose (no mis-rendering) is permitted ONLY in marked non-Glim POV (Noor; a hunter) — never bleeding into Glim's chapters.

### BANNED in this strand
- Kyle's cognitive register: arithmetic, filing, parliament, geometry, "X but Y" math-thought — NEVER in Glim's narration.
- Latinate abstractions where Anglo-Saxon words exist.
- Feelings named directly: show the body, do not label the emotion.
- Noble-savage purity: the Underclan raid, exile, let the air decide — adaptation is costly, specific, never magic.
- Sentimentality or ironic distance in Glim's narration. He is frightened-and-competent, not wry.

## World Texture (GLMZ surface — inject in any scene where Glim is above ground)

*Underground scenes use the Underclan's own sensory world (smell, dark-adaptation, the Current). The GLMZ surface texture below applies to surface beats only.*

**Sky communities (SS-A32/A33):** Aeropods → Aeropolis → Drifters — enormous shapes overhead when Glim surfaces; he has no word for them and renders them as "nests too large for birds." Altitude = class axis (he reads this as: the higher the nest, the more unreachable). The Low (30–320m ungoverned band) is the layer closest to him when he surfaces. "Little birdies" / "Harpies" = insults from below; Glim would not understand these but would hear the tone.

**Underwater communities:** Below the lake. Glim knows water goes deep; the Underclan use flooded sectors as boundary markers. He has no concept of hermetic domes but the current from that direction sometimes carries different air — machine-filtered, wrong-temperature. "Fishmen" / "Mermaids" as concepts are entirely surface-people words.

**Micro-schisms:** Glim has seen pebble clusters floating in the deep tunnels too — tight orbits around nothing, barely an inch across. They appear near the membrane. The Underclan call them "the Drift" and treat them as the Current's breath made visible. They are not wrong.

**Nanite gutters (stopped):** In surface chapters, Glim notices the gutter texture without understanding it — an iridescent film over everything at ground level, absent in certain districts. He reads the clean districts as "the well-fed zones" (correct).

**Job vocabulary (surface encounters):** Riders, Splicers, Tunnelers — words Glim hears but cannot parse. He reads Tunnelers as closest to his world and responds less fearfully to them.

**Infrastructure:** The Pulse (Mach 6 vacuum tube, slugs) terrifies him the first time — "a thunder that moves sideways." No trains in GLMZ. VTOLs he renders as "metal birds, no wings, no care." No police — Arcturus Civil Security only; they don't come this far down.

**Φ** = QUANTA (Glim knows it as "shines" — worthless below). Iowan Behemoths = machines, not life (Glim would read one as "a blind thing that smells of oil").

## The Story (UNDR)

**Contact tragedy** in the FernGully / Dances With Wolves / Emerald Forest lineage. Toby — surface-born, lost at four, raised as **Glim** by the Underclan — surfaces for his coming-of-age Brave test, is caught, and through him the surface learns the legend is real. The surface descends. The Bright Fever (plain immunological naïveté, never mystical) scythes the clan.

**The surface is the disaster.** Both antagonist faces — Sallow's appetite (hunting for sport) and the Daylight Mission's sincere mercy (bringing "rescue") — are fatal. The Mission is the more devastating because it cannot be hated cleanly.

## Characters

**Glim / Toby (POV)** — Tobias Adeyemi-Vance; lost at ~4, raised ~5-16 in Homewater. Underclan name = Glim (*a small light*). Competent, watchful, dark-adapted, quietly doubting. In his POV: always Glim.

**Noor Adeyemi-Vance** (mother, secondary POV, rare, marked) — loves him in a way that cannot land.

**Listener Vesh** — the tribe's priest; right about everything, which makes him the hardest antagonist-who-loves-you.

**Knuckle** — the Brave-master; obligation not guilt.

**Sorrel** — peer, foil, heart; surfaced and returned clean the year before.

**Grale** — rival within; true believer who would burn the village to save it.

**Daylight Mission** — sincere humanitarian rescuers; the Bright Fever vector; the true antagonist.

**CANALKEEP-08 / the Oarsman** — derelict android running one loop: accept a fare freely given, convey, return. Does not judge. When four-year-old Toby crossed, he gave it his child-ident shine — the one object that could have named him to the surface. *He gave away his name to come home.*
