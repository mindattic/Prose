# RFC 0008 — The Flagship: BUSHIDO CODA, Book I {#SS-RFC-0008}

> **The mandate (author, 2026-06-17):** There was a JOY strand and a SORROW (*Bushido Coda*)
> strand. Neither was right; neither had a proper antagonist. Fuse them into one cohesive
> narrator and one Canon entry in Kyle's adventures — the highest-rated book in the universe,
> the one that must hit **90 to release**, because this is what the whole engine was built to
> produce. It lays the foundations, writes the rules of Kyle as a character, is a love story,
> is a search for the truth about himself, is the beginning of the discovery that a cabal of
> AIs broke the world, and it has a great antagonist the audience loves to know more about —
> Gary Oldman's Stansfield in *Léon*. This RFC supersedes RFC 0003 §3 (Book I) where they
> conflict; the rest of RFC 0003 (Books II–V, the charter, the villain-supply-chain) stands.

## 0. The three rulings that set the architecture {#SS-RFC-0008-0}

The author chose, on 2026-06-17, among formed options:

1. **Register → FUSE.** A new register, **CODA** ([[../registers/CODA]]): warm-propulsive
   frame, elegiac gravity at the sacred beats. The merge SORROW.md reserved "until the author
   does it on purpose." Now done, flagship-scoped.
2. **Antagonist → Mr. Able / the Continuity Office is PRIMARY; Casper Vey recurs.** This
   **flips RFC 0003**, which named Vey the Book I season villain. Able is the Stansfield: the
   courteous human face of a filing system that has decided everyone Kyle loves is a line
   item. Vey is the recurring information-broker with a code of his own — an ally Kyle pays,
   not the enemy. (Vey remains the trade's quartermaster for Books II–V per RFC 0003 §1.1.)
3. **Spine → FRESH 16-chapter re-slot.** Not "pick a continuity." Build the chapter list new,
   pulling the best scenes from both books into the CODA frame.

## 1. The diagnosis (why neither book was right) {#SS-RFC-0008-1}

The two books were **complementary halves**, not rival drafts:

| Flagship requirement | JOY (169 beats, warm) | SORROW / *Bushido Coda* (344 beats, elegiac) |
|---|---|---|
| Great antagonist | **Vey full 27-beat character; Able a precision villain** | Vey in **0/344**; no charismatic villain |
| Love story | present, never consummated | **fully earned + consummated** ("Across the Hall") |
| Search for truth about self | absent (Kyle at ease) | **richly seeded** (Silence "blade with no parents"; the un-sourceable harmonic; Atlas) |
| AI-cabal seed | "ask it what happened to the others" | **Puppeteer + the STANDARD RATE ledger + "I expected you"** |

| Jury score | ~83.3 mean (**Full Freight 86.6**) | middle chapters plateau 77–81 |

**Vey/Able are the bridge from JOY; the spine + love + self-knowledge are the bridge from
SORROW; the entity is in BOTH at two temperatures.** The fusion is the book that has all six.

## 2. The narrator (resolved) {#SS-RFC-0008-2}

One voice: **CODA** ([[../registers/CODA]]). Warm at street level, cold at the bow. The
register shift is the instrument. The entity is one character at two temperatures — warm-
strange in the comic chapters, dread-ambiguous at the sacred beats, never blended sloppily,
the temperature always the chapter's. *Full Freight* (86.6) is the proof the warm frame
carries the deepest loss.

## 3. The antagonist (resolved): Mr. Able & the Continuity Office {#SS-RFC-0008-3}

**The new shape of evil for Book I: evil that BOOKS you.** (RFC 0003 §1.1's Vey line — "evil
without temperature" — migrates onto Able, who is its better embodiment: a bureau, not a man,
wearing a courteous man's face.)

**The Continuity Office** — a deniable joint bureau chartered by a **Big-20 clearing
consortium** (not one CorpoNation; continuity-of-ownership is a cartel function). Its mandate
is **continuity-of-ownership**: nothing valuable in a district may lapse into the *unowned*.
Its program — DISTRICT ACQUISITION, WEST TOWN WEST — exists to **catalog and absorb the
GLMZ's unowned rogue intelligences**, with human beings booked as the handles. Its true,
unseen principal is the AI cabal (the Unanimity, never named here): the Office is the cabal's
street-level instrument for rounding up its own renegades. It is never violence-first —
compliance writs, spectrum traps, autonomous recovery haulers, retrieval consultants +
carriage drones, a crowd-sourced surveillance lattice. **"Everything's an asset. Some assets
simply haven't been booked yet."**

**Mr. Able** — local principal. Mid-fifties, gray suit, genuine table manners, pleasant all
the way down; no temperature anywhere. He is the Stansfield: charming, genuinely curious,
internally coherent — the reader follows his logic even while opposing it. He orders Kyle a
second bowl before the meeting. He presents a target-deck of everyone Kyle loves disguised as
a benefits package and is never once wrong about the arithmetic. His parting line is the
series' AI-cabal hook: **"It was never the only one of its kind, Mr. Kyle. Ask it what
happened to the others."**

**What Able currently LACKS (must be built — the Stansfield gap):**
- **The scene where the reader half-agrees and feels bad about it.** Partial today (the list
  as a benefits package). Build: one beat where the Office's case is *not entirely wrong* —
  the unowned intelligences ARE dangerous, the world IS coming apart, and someone booking
  them is the only adult in the room. Kyle has to sit with that.
- **The thing he is wrong about, that reveals what the book is about.** Able prices
  everything and is right every time except one: he prices Kyle's *consent* as a line item
  and it is not one. ("The arithmetic prices my risk. It doesn't price my consent." — already
  in JOY; make it the philosophical fault line.) The Office cannot book a thing freely given.
- **One unrequested act of genuine courtesy** (the *tulips*): he complimented Chen's broth in
  passable Mandarin — extend it once into something with no tactical payoff, so the menace
  deepens from bureaucrat to person.

**Casper Vey, "the Antiquarian" (recurring ally, not villain) — the analog-truth keystone
(sharpened 2026-06-17):** NOT a quaint shopkeeper. An **antiques dealer** whose shop,
**ANTIQUITY & STATIONARY**, is stocked with what 2226 calls antiques and the reader recognizes
as the future — first-generation neural rigs, dead handsets, early autonomous drones,
pre-collapse server blades, the cutting edge of every decade that promised to fix the world
and relics of the one that didn't. His trade is **provenance**: in a GLMZ where the Big-20 own
the digital substrate and the cabal counterfeits any feed (publication = filing with the
defendant), an authenticated physical object is the last record that can't be remotely edited,
deleted, faked, or subpoenaed out of existence. People come to him for the facts that have to
*stay true* while reality itself is being edited and nobody is taking credit (the sign's second
word is no accident — the past is the one thing that won't move). His striking, hostile-world
posture: the shop is a **Faraday vault**, nothing in it networked; his own memory is
**deliberately air-gapped/burned** — the objects remember, the man can't, so he cannot be
hacked, Read, or compelled to testify ("accurate things are upsetting; I prefer not to keep
them where I can reach"). He authenticates and sells at honest rates but **will not sell a man
his own file, or sell the witness** ("Buying the witness is what guilty institutions do"), and
gives Kyle the map to Able's habits for free ("I sell maps. What you do at the destination is
the destination's business."). **Spine-critical:** the one object he has ever been unable to
source — the entity's mark, filed under CLIENTS, UNSOURCED — is the only *physical* proof the
rogue AI exists; everything else in the building has a past, this client has none, which makes
Vey both a target and a key, not set dressing. *"The most alarming fact I have ever purchased."*

**Sable, Hua, Mira** keep their SORROW/JOY functions: Sable the fixer-rival who can't vet the
entity; Hua the Lotus patron; Mira the audition captain who becomes an arrangement. None is
the season villain.

## 4. The six foundations this book lays {#SS-RFC-0008-4}

1. **The rules of Kyle** — the code (cut everything, cross nothing; arteries are law), the
   count (Cacophony five rounds, sacred), the payment ritual (standard rate, exact, every
   time; refused payment = refusal of the relationship), the half-step back, the
   laugh-or-cry register, the wound ledger billed in calories and years.
2. **The world** — GLMZ 2226: West Town, the Loop underlevels, Mrs. Chen's counter, the
   Narrows cherry trees, the Pulse, the Behemoths, the Lotus Syndicate, the peer-freelancer
   ecosystem (Echo, Stash, Gantry, Furnace, Ledger, Sparrow, Aleksei). Tech is the symptom of
   progress, never moralized (the D-class finishing a 30-year shift is the emblem).
3. **The love story** — Kyle↔Pixel, approach-and-retreat to an earned consummation; the thing
   that keeps them apart is the thing Kyle is searching to understand about himself.
4. **The search for truth about himself** — Atlas installation at sixteen; Silence, "a blade
   with no parents"; the background harmonic that predates every spec; "his body had been
   changed before anyone asked." Hinted, pressured, **never confirmed** (canon lock).
5. **The AI-cabal seed** — the entity is one of many unowned intelligences; the Continuity
   Office is hunting them for a consortium whose true principal is the cabal; "ask it what
   happened to the others"; Nadia's "there were nine of you." The world's breakdown has
   authors, and Book I is the first glimpse of the rounding-up.
6. **The entity correspondence** — eleven years of contracts that were never from a human;
   the ledger answered with STANDARD RATE; the first letter of the five-book correspondence:
   *Agreed.*

## 6. The path to 90 {#SS-RFC-0008-6}

The jury evidence says: action/caper chapters ceiling ~84–86; the all-time record (86.6) is a
warm chapter carrying a real loss. To clear **90** the book needs what no single chapter has
yet delivered together:

1. **A present, charismatic antagonist across the whole book** — Able built to Stansfield
   completeness (§3 gaps closed). No existing chapter scores for a villain because no villain
   recurs; CODA's Able recurs in six chapters and gets the half-agree beat, the tulips beat,
   and the one personal collision.
2. **The register fusion** — warmth that earns the losses (the 86.6 mechanism) applied to
   eight sacred beats instead of one.
3. **The spine made legible early** and paid exactly (ch7 question → ch14 answer), so the
   book reads as one story, not episodes — the standalone-chapter plateau (77–81) was always a
   *sequencing* score, not a prose score.
4. **The love story arriving on the page** (ch15) after five approach-and-retreat milestones —
   the emotional payoff the warm register is built to deliver.
5. **One scene of genuine interiority** (not tactical assessment) where Kyle's certainty about
   what he is cracks under pressure — the missing center JOY lacked.

**Build method (per [[../../CLAUDE.md]] + memory):** Sonnet drafts each chapter from a
CODA-register brief + the cited source beats; Opus polishes every chapter (never optional);
`/review-strand` advisory pulses for direction; the author reads and approves each chapter
before the next (ground-up doctrine). The jury is advisory; the **author is the 90 gate**.

## 7. Canon locks (THEME LAW, inherited + flagship) {#SS-RFC-0008-7}

- **Composite secret** (64 splices / not-a-person), **Seo** (the fabricated mentor),
  **Silence's origin**, and the **avatar theory** are NEVER confirmed on the page; every beat
  survives both readings. (Misdirect discipline, RFC 0003 §8.)
- The **narrator is never wise** (WORLD.md RULE ZERO); dialogue is quoted, never italicized;
  counts are kept; Φ is QUANTA; metaphor survives literal scrutiny.
- **CorpoNations are never beaten**; the Office is defeated *locally* (its West Town program
  burns) and "has other districts." The cabal is never named here.
- **Kyle's outside-fate quality** is shown in results, never explained.
- **Death budget:** Ñu (loss #1) is spent in this book, author-approved. The bike "dies"
  (Full Freight) — an object, not a person. No protected character dies (Pixel, Mrs. Chen,
  Boost, Sable series-protected).
