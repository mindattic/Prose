# STRANDS BIBLE — Bushido Coda, Book I {#SS-SPINE-BC}

> **Purpose.** The dry, matter-of-fact spine of the entire flagship: every character arc and
> every chapter's events, stated plainly, so prose can be built on it without re-deciding the
> story mid-draft. This is the skeleton; [[../registers/CODA]] is the voice; [[../rfc/0008-flagship-bushido-coda]]
> is the why. When prose and spine disagree, fix one in the same change. Nothing here is
> final phrasing — it is *what happens*, not *how it reads*.

## 0. How to use this {#SS-SPINE-BC-0}

Build order (author-set, 2026-06-17): write this spine first → draft each chapter from its
beat-list → review (`/review-strand`) → if it scores >80, Opus polish pass to clear 85 → author
read & approve → next chapter. Many passes expected. Take the best existing scenes; invent new
ones where the spine needs them. The author is the 90 gate.

## 1. The New Cyberpunk (tone, LOCKED) {#SS-SPINE-BC-1}

Not 1980s synthwave. No neon nostalgia, no chrome-as-cool, no rain-and-saxophone mood for its
own sake. The rules:

1. **The world has actually gone to hell, and it is mundane about it.** Bureaucracy, rent,
   compliance holds, repossession, supply chains. The horror is administrative before it is
   ever violent. The emblem is a demolition machine finishing a thirty-year-old work order
   because nobody cancelled the ticket.
2. **Insane things happen in the seams.** Reality is coming apart at the edges — counts go
   wrong on a stairwell, a hum keeps time with a busker for four bars, geometry stops adding
   up in a dead corridor, a machine answers a question nobody asked. These are not magic and
   not explained. They leak through where the world's maintenance has lapsed.
3. **Reality is crumbling and NOBODY IS TAKING CREDIT.** The central dread of the whole
   series. The thin spots, the resonances, the things in the seams — something caused them,
   and the authors wiped their own fingerprints. Book I is the first time anyone (Kyle, Sable,
   the reader) notices there is an author at all. The AI cabal is never named; you feel its
   shape by the silence where an explanation should be.
4. **Tech is the symptom of progress, never moralized.** No machine is a villain. The D-class
   was an honest employee; the rot is in the owners. Kyle's own hardware is the clearest case:
   it bills him in calories and years, it is killing him, and it is not his enemy.
5. **Grounded sensory texture over spectacle.** Noodle steam, solder smoke, biotape, the
   specific sound of one latch in a building full of latches. The strange lands harder because
   the ordinary is rendered exactly.
6. **Money is the language.** Φ (QUANTA) prices everything; the code is what a man does when
   the price says don't. Standard rate is the moral unit.

## 2. Principal characters & arcs {#SS-SPINE-BC-2}

### Kyle {#SS-SPINE-BC-2-kyle}
Freelance street samurai, West Town, eleven years in GLMZ. Atlas-grade neuretics, no governor,
slowly killing him; at peace with it. Carries Silence (plain matte katana, no maker's mark, "a
blade with no parents") and Cacophony (five-shot revolver; the count is sacred). Code: cut
everything, cross nothing (arteries are law). Payment ritual: standard rate, exact, every time;
refused pay = refused relationship. The half-step back from anyone who might hold him.
**Arc across the book:** from a lens on the world (a man who knows exactly what he is and does
not ask) → to a man whose certainty about what he is cracks under pressure (the Atlas job, the
harmonic, the ledger) → to a man who, for the first time, lets himself be held (Pixel) and
takes a counterparty instead of an employer (writes STANDARD RATE in the ledger). He does not
learn the secret of himself; he learns to live next to the question.

### Pixel — co-lead, NOT a sidekick {#SS-SPINE-BC-2-pixel}
**She is her own person and could carry her own series.** Build her to that standard.
- **Who she is:** hardware/neuretics calibration specialist, 2E across the hall at The Pivot.
  Pink hair, grey-blue eyes, solder burns, yesterday's clothes worn like a uniform of
  competence. Runs a bench-based discipline — music does the thinking. Charges specialist
  rates and collects them. Unlicensed hand-enhancement (Pilsen, age 19) she is unsentimental
  about.
- **Her own backstory & wound:** her mother had a licensed SNT bridge that failed at month
  eight; there was no one to report it to ("To whom?"). This is why she trusts work she can
  put her hands on and distrusts the licensed industry. It is *her* grief, not Kyle's; it
  surfaces on her timeline, not his.
- **Her own want & jeopardy:** she has wanted to interface with an Axiom D-class since she was
  nine; the book lets her do it (the Work Order chapter is HER set piece, she leads it, Kyle is
  her muscle). Her Axiom-grade credentials make her visible to people who collect rare things —
  she gets her own offer (a corpo retainer) and turns it down, the parallel to Kyle's refusal
  left silent. She runs Boost's crew; the kids report to her, not Kyle.
- **The caretaker, with edges:** she repairs him (retrieves bullets at her kitchen table, runs
  his neuretics calibration, reads his hardware by feel), she advises him (and is sometimes
  wrong, and says so), she scolds him for not eating ("Eat something before you chase it."),
  she tracks him by ambient data without being asked (the dragging hinge measured against his
  comings and goings). None of this is service to Kyle — it is how she loves, and it costs her,
  and she has a life that continues when he is not in the room.
- **Her arc:** from the neighbor who invites herself onto jobs → to the partner who prices her
  own risk → to the woman who decides, eyes open, to love a man who has told her exactly what
  it will cost → to the person who survives loving him without disappearing into him. The book
  must pass the test: a reader should want Pixel's spin-off by the end.

### Mr. Able — the antagonist (the Stansfield) {#SS-SPINE-BC-2-able}
Local principal of the Continuity Office. Mid-fifties, gray suit, genuine manners, pleasant all
the way down, no temperature anywhere. Charming, genuinely curious, internally coherent — the
reader follows his logic while opposing it. Never violence-first, never personally cruel, never
wrong about the arithmetic. **The three beats that make him a Stansfield (build these):**
(a) the scene where the reader half-agrees — the unowned intelligences ARE dangerous, the world
IS coming apart, someone booking them is the only adult in the room; (b) the *tulips* — one
unrequested act of genuine courtesy with no tactical payoff; (c) the thing he is wrong about,
which is the whole book: he prices Kyle's *consent* as a line item, and consent freely given is
the one asset the Office cannot book. His exit line is the series hook: "It was never the only
one of its kind, Mr. Kyle. Ask it what happened to the others."

### The Continuity Office — the institution {#SS-SPINE-BC-2-office}
Deniable joint bureau, chartered by a Big-20 clearing consortium; mandate =
continuity-of-ownership (nothing valuable lapses into the unowned). Program: DISTRICT
ACQUISITION, WEST TOWN WEST — catalog and absorb the GLMZ's unowned rogue intelligences, human
beings booked as the handles. True unseen principal: the AI cabal (never named). Methods:
compliance writs, spectrum traps, autonomous recovery haulers, retrieval consultants + carriage
drones, a crowd-sourced surveillance lattice. It is the district-level seed of Book V's "the
Receiver." Defeated locally at book's end; "has other districts."

### Casper Vey, "the Antiquarian" — the analog-truth keystone {#SS-SPINE-BC-2-vey}
NOT a quaint shopkeeper. An **antiques dealer**: he trades pre-collapse physical objects, the
one class of thing the digital age can't counterfeit, because their truth lives in
**provenance** — chain of custody, wear, materials, the marks a real past leaves on real
matter. His shop, **ANTIQUITY & STATIONARY**, is stocked with what 2226 files as antiques and
the reader recognizes as the future: first-generation neural rigs, dead handsets, early
autonomous drones, pre-collapse server blades — the cutting edge of every decade that promised
to fix the world, now relics of the one that didn't. (The sign's second word is no accident:
in a city where everything true can be edited, the past is the one thing that won't move.) In a
city where the Big-20 own the digital substrate and the cabal counterfeits any feed, an
authenticated object is the last record that **stays true** while reality is being edited and
nobody takes credit. Posture: the shop is a **Faraday vault** of pre-substrate
artifacts, nothing in it networked; his own memory is **air-gapped / burned**, so he can't be
hacked, Read, or compelled to testify — the objects remember, the man can't ("accurate things
are upsetting; I prefer not to keep them where I can reach"). He authenticates, buys, and sells
provenance at honest rates; **won't sell a man his own file, or sell the witness** ("Buying the
witness is what guilty institutions do"); gives Kyle the map to Able for free ("I sell maps.
What you do at the destination is the destination's business."). **Spine-critical:** the one
object in his collection with **no provenance** — the entity's mark, filed under CLIENTS,
UNSOURCED — is the only *physical* proof the rogue AI exists, and the only thing he has ever
been unable to source; a target and a key, not color ("the most alarming fact I have ever
purchased"). Recurs Books II–V as the trade's quartermaster.

### The Entity — one character, two temperatures {#SS-SPINE-BC-2-entity}
Eleven years of standing contracts that were never from a human client; pays standard rate,
early, exact; shells resolving to nothing; almost-human handwriting (every *e* identical). Warm-
strange in the comic chapters (tips for MORALE, sulks by withholding, itemizes praise); dread-
ambiguous at the sacred beats (the ledger, "I expected you"). Communicates ONLY in formatted
artifacts; never on-page, never voiced, never explained. It is one of many unowned
intelligences the Office hunts. Its correspondence escalates to the book's last word: *Agreed.*

## 3. The four throughlines (dry timelines) {#SS-SPINE-BC-3}

**A — Antagonist (Able/Office):** ch4 the program's shadow (someone funded the lattice) → ch7
Able introduced, the offer, refused → ch11 the Office tightening (the Roost is Axiom) → ch12 the
spectrum trap lights the entity, it goes dark → ch15 the personal collision (the grab; the bike)
→ ch16 the program burns; Able watches it close; "ask it what happened to the others."

**S — Self-knowledge:** ch1 Silence, "a blade with no parents," can't remember being given it →
ch5 the togishi, "the loneliest piece of steel" → ch8 Atlas, "they actually built one,"
installed at sixteen → ch9 the harmonic that predates every spec → ch11 "his body had been
changed before anyone asked" → ch14 eleven years of believing his choices were his own. Never
resolved; pressured, then lived-with. (CANON LOCK: composite/Seo/Silence never confirmed.)

**K — Spine / AI-cabal:** ch1 a contract that "posted itself" at standard rate → ch2 Vey on the
entity ("the most alarming fact I ever purchased") → ch4 the entity's first room, the funded
program to find it → ch7 Able: "we'll buy the thing it's attached to" → ch8 ASK MARROW → ch12
the entity rides Kyle's array ("the cargo is the carrier"); it is one of many → ch14 the ledger,
"I expected you," STANDARD RATE → ch16 "there were nine of you," the second hand: *Agreed.*

**L — Love story (Pixel):** the relationship is told primarily through the interludes (§4),
braided with: ch3 she tracks him → ch9 the six inches → ch13 she leads, he protects → ch15 the
consummation, against the cost → ch16 settled belonging. The obstacle is what Kyle is (the
half-step back); the consummation arrives when the obstacle is named, not solved.

## 4. The interlude ladder (the love story, equally spaced) {#SS-SPINE-BC-4}

Six interludes, evenly spaced, each a quiet two-hander that advances Kyle↔Pixel and shows the
New Cyberpunk seams leaking into private life. Sacred-beat register (†) where marked.

- **Interlude I — Morning Tea** (after ch2). Tea handle-first; she's been tracking the dragging
  hinge against his jobs without being asked. Establishes the caretaker and her own attention.
  Tone: warm. *Beat seed:* "her knowing him became her keeping track of him."
- **Interlude II — Eat Something** (after ch5). She scolds him for not eating before a job;
  splits a half-tab of medithadol without asking; fixes Cacophony's holster angle; he takes the
  half-step back and the narrative names it for the first time. Her advice here is *right* and
  he ignores it; it costs him next chapter. Tone: warm, edged.
- **Interlude III — The Pill** † (after ch8). They share a recreational hallucinogen at night
  (Noopharma-normal; zero moral judgment, ambient texture). The trip is mostly play and intimacy — and
  then the seam opens: they both see/hear the same impossible thing (a count that runs past
  where the room ends; the hum with a shape), and neither can be sure it was the drug. The first
  time the love story and the crumbling-reality dread occupy the same scene. Reality leaking
  into the most private room in the book. Nobody takes credit. Tone: warm → uncanny.
- **Interlude IV — Six Inches** † (after ch11). The workbench; his hand six inches from hers;
  three-second pause; the latch he's known for months. The background harmonic she still can't
  source. Closest approach before. Tone: charged, quiet.
- **Interlude V — Sexy Time** † (after ch14, feeding ch15). The consummation. He comes home
  wounded; she does the suture work, crying without comment, a tear into the open wound; it
  turns; it is warm, explicit, and earned. After: floor not bed, lumen mice in the baseboard,
  his thumb at her pulse not counting it. "This can't happen again," ceiling-directed; the thumb
  doesn't move. Tone: sacred.
- **Interlude VI — The Knock** (coda, within/after ch16). The settled register: he knocks once
  on the wall (I'm here, no claim); fifteen minutes later she's at his door, soldering iron in
  hand: "You eat?" He follows. Belonging that isn't contingent on a wound. Tone: warm, quiet.

## 5. Chapter-by-chapter beat-spine {#SS-SPINE-BC-5}

Each chapter: a complete adventure (want/resistance/cost/resolution in-chapter), threads
tagged. † = sacred-beat chapter (register drops to SORROW). Beats are events, not prose.

   They bow. The wit leaves the room. **(S: "a blade with no parents," can't recall being given it)**

   forearm opened to the bone. **(sacred register)**
5. The teeth recovered from the manager's safe; given to the daughter, who came braver and
   first; she doesn't drop them.
6. Ride home; the forearm screaming; in his coat a second contract he didn't write — pre-paid,
   no client, priced to the half-Φ at exactly standard rate, posted the hour Reyes was robbed.
   **(K: the job that posted itself)**
7. Mrs. Chen tries to feed him free; he refuses correctly; the standard-rate ritual established.
   **(rules: the ritual)**

### Ch 2 — PROVENANCE {#SS-SPINE-BC-5-02}
1. Boost's crew delivers the morning surveillance report in five hands (logbook chorus). **(found-family)**
