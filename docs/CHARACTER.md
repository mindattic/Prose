# The Character Doctrine {#SS-CHAR}

> "Everyone is the main character." — Yoshinori Kitase, on *Final Fantasy VI*

This is a binding craft law for every Universe in the engine. It sits beside the
[Bible](BIBLE.md): the Bible governs *what is true*; this governs *who the people are*.
It exists because a story is remembered for its **people**, not its places, its tech, or
its set pieces. A reader forgets the heist and remembers the thief. The world is the stage;
the characters are the reason anyone watches.

The empirical proof is in our own review data: strands whose cast reads as **functions**
("the netrunner," "the muscle") cap on the reader panel no matter how clean the prose or how
tight the plot. The same panel rewards a cast of **people**. Craft at the sentence level cannot
buy back a character who does not want anything.

---

## §0.5 — The Relational Law: interpersonal interaction is the 90+ lever {#SS-CHAR-relational}

A cast of *people* (§1) is necessary but not sufficient. What lifts a strand from the high-80s to
**90+** is **what passes between them** — genuine human interaction, good, bad, or indifferent. More
plot events don't do it; more polished prose doesn't do it. *People acting like people toward each
other* does. This is the secret sauce of the whole enterprise: **humans interacting with humans in a
human way, in the middle of an uncaring world.**

**Every scene with two or more people is a relationship under pressure**, carried on two channels at
once — and BOTH must be on the page:
- **Verbal** — what is said, what is pointedly *not* said, the answer to the question under the
  question, deflection, teasing, the small dry thing chosen over the large true one, status moves,
  repair attempts, the apology that arrives as an insult.
- **Non-verbal** — body and proximity, eye contact and its refusal, the better cup handed
  handle-first, a thumb on a pulse, who touches whom, who steps back, what the body does that the
  mouth contradicts.

**Rules:**
- The exchange must do real **relational work**: shift power, deepen or fracture a bond, expose or
  conceal, forgive or wound. An exchange that only conveys information is dead weight.
- **Subtext over statement.** Characters rarely name the feeling; the reader feels it through
  behavior (see VOICE: tenderness arrives as objects and gestures, never as statements).
- **Continuity of the bond:** every exchange leaves the relationship changed, and the change carries
  forward into later scenes.
- **Indifference counts.** Two people failing to connect — or not caring to — is interaction. Render
  it; don't skip it.

This is the craft target the **InterpersonalDynamicsService** (verbal + non-verbal exchange tracking)
will measure and enforce, beside the affect→behavior and causality services. Proof case: in VATD the
story is Tomas's fury at Lev and how the heart-hunt mends them — not the heist.

## §1 — The Law {#SS-CHAR-§1}

1. **Every named character has a Drive and an Obstacle.** No exceptions for "minor" roles.
2. **The Drive is a want with a reason** — a goal the character is moving toward and *why*
   it matters to *them specifically*. "Money" is not a Drive until you know what the money is
   *for* (Locke calls himself a treasure hunter to avoid saying *grief*).
3. **The Obstacle is two things at once**: an external force (the Empire, the corp, the
   antagonist) **and** an internal one (the character's own denial, rage, guilt, walls, fear).
   The internal obstacle is the more important of the two. The best characters fight
   *themselves* on the way to fighting the villain.
4. **No two characters in a scene see it the same way.** Each carries a perspective the others
   do not have. If two characters are interchangeable, one of them is furniture.
5. **The antagonist obeys this law too.** Kefka (nihilism), Sephiroth (identity), Ardyn (a grudge
   older than the kingdom), Ultimecia (who is Rinoa's own future) — a villain without a Drive is
   a weather system, not a character.

A character who has a *role* but no *Drive* is a **gimmick**. A character who has a *quirk* but
no *want* is a **trope-fill**. Both are failures of this doctrine, regardless of how cool the
quirk or how necessary the role.

---

## §2 — The Test {#SS-CHAR-§2}

Before any character appears in prose, answer all four in one line each. If you cannot, the
character is not ready — write the answers first, then the scene.

- **Drive:** What do they want, and why does it matter to *them*?
- **Internal obstacle:** What are they wrong about, afraid of, or refusing to face? (This is
  Storr's *Sacred Flaw*; see [§6](#SS-CHAR-§6).)
- **External obstacle:** What/who stands in the way?
- **The thing only they see:** the belief, wound, or angle no one else in the cast carries.

If the honest answer to "Drive" is *"to perform their job on the team,"* you have described a
**function**, not a person. Go back.

### §2b — The Action Figure Test / The Cosplay Test (the bar for leading characters) {#SS-CHAR-§2b}

A leading character must clear a harder bar than §2: **would a reader want their action figure —
and would a fan cosplay as them?** These are the same test from two directions. The action figure
asks whether the character reads at a glance on a shelf; cosplay asks whether a real person would
spend a weekend *becoming* them — assemble the silhouette, carry the signature gear, strike the
pose, and have a room know exactly who they are without a name tag. A character worth cosplaying
is one a reader has already decided to inhabit. Both tests demand the same three things at a glance —

- **A silhouette** — a shape you'd know blacked-out: posture, build, the coat, what they carry.
- **A signature** — the gear *and* the move that are theirs and no one else's (the specific
  weapon, the tool, the tell).
- **A soul** — the Drive (§2), legible under the plastic; the reason the pose means something.

If you cannot picture the figure — its pose, its accessory, the one word on the box — the
character is not yet leading material. **This is the test that takes a strand from competent
(~85) to iconic (~93).** The reader panel does not fall in love with functions; it buys the
action figure, and it dresses up as the ones it loves.

Corollary: **the signature gear is half the silhouette, not set-dressing.** This is why the heist
series runs gear-dense — the Reibo flechette Rook *won't* draw in a crowd, Boiler's gray-market
Hwarang resonance driver, Scout's vent-rated crawlers, the Daewon machine pistol that marks
PEREGRINE as people who price a corpse as paperwork — each is *character* rendered as hardware.
Name the kit; it tells the reader who is holding it.

## §3 — Failure Modes {#SS-CHAR-§3}

- **The Function.** Defined by their job: the netrunner hacks, the demo guy blows things up, the
  planner plans. Fix: give the job a *cost* and a *why*. Vox is not "the netrunner" — she measures
  her own body in lost bandwidth and rebuilds on obsolete tech because unwatched is the only
  privacy she has left.
- **The Gimmick.** A memorable tic standing in for an interior (a catchphrase, a hat, a way of
  speaking). Fix: the tic must *express* a Drive, not replace it. Boiler genders structures
  female because he reads load the way other people read faces — the tic *is* the worldview.
- **The Trope-Fill.** A recognizable type dropped in to occupy a slot (the mysterious mercenary,
  the plucky kid, the cool loner). Fix: the type is the *door*, not the room. Cyberpunk *expects*
  these archetypes — that familiarity is our register (see [BIBLE](BIBLE.md)) — so the archetype
  is allowed, but it must open onto a specific wound. Shadow is "the mysterious mercenary" until
  the dreams reveal the partner and the child he ran from.

> **Cliché is not the enemy; the empty trope is.** Our heist Universe is *intentionally*
> Shadowrun/Cyberpunk-Red/Akira — the runner-vs-corp set piece is the pleasure, not the problem.
> A familiar *role* played by a specific *person* is the whole game. A familiar role played by
> no one is the failure.

## §4 — Depth Is Mandatory; Length Is Not {#SS-CHAR-§4}

A Drive can be one line. The error is **absence**, not brevity.

- **Optional / minor characters still pass the test.** Quina wants to taste the world — owned
  honestly, no pretense of more. Umaro fights out of loyalty to Mog. Mog wants to be more than a
  bystander. A single clean want is enough.
- **An absent interior is acceptable only as a deliberate artistic choice, never as laziness.**
  Gogo's blankness is *authored* — a Mime whose mystery is the point. If you cannot defend a
  character's lack of Drive as intentional, it is a hole.
- **Cool is not flat.** Squall and Lightning are *defined* by detachment — and their arc is the
  thawing. Restraint means depth **rationed**, not depth **missing**. A businesslike exterior is
  a door the reader must be able to feel the room behind. If the reader senses only the door,
  the character reads as cold; if they sense the rationed warmth underneath, the same coolness
  reads as *control*. **The depth must be present on the page, spent sparingly, never withheld
  entirely.** This is the difference between a character the panel calls "kept at arm's length"
  and one it calls "built from behavior, not backstory."

## §5 — The Canon Exemplars {#SS-CHAR-§5}

The *Final Fantasy* casts are the engine's reference corpus for this doctrine — chosen because
each game proves "everyone is the main character" with a wildly diverse ensemble. Distilled by
the *kind* of Drive each teaches:

| Drive pattern | Exemplars | The lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-discovery** | Terra, Cloud, Vivi | Identity itself is a Drive; "who am I" carries a story |
| **Atonement** | Locke, Vincent, Celes | A past failure becomes a compulsion; grief disguised as a job |
| **Revenge → growth** | Barret, Cyan, Hope | Vengeance is a starting Drive that must *transform* or the character stalls |
| **Duty / protection** | Edgar, Steiner, Gladiolus, Fang, Snow | Loyalty is a Drive — and "blind loyalty becomes immoral" is its arc |
| **Freedom** | Sabin, Balthier, Fran | Wanting *out* is as strong as wanting *something* |
| **Belonging** | Gau, Prompto, Eiko | The outsider who wants in; backstory (Prompto's origin) weaponized against the want |
| **Detachment → care** | Squall, Lightning, Amarant | The arc is learning to want at all; coolness is the *obstacle*, not the absence of one |
| **Facade over grief** | Setzer, Shadow | The devil-may-care mask is the tell, not the truth |
| **Cheer over guilt** | Vanille, Selphie | Upbeat as armor; the brightness hides the wound |
| **Owned simplicity** | Quina, Umaro, Mog, Gogo | A one-line want — or an *authored* blank — is legitimate |

Two structural rules these casts encode:

- **The villain is the protagonist of their own story.** Give antagonists the §2 test in full.
  Ultimecia (Rinoa's future), Vanille-as-secret-l'Cie, Ardyn's ancient grudge — the best
  antagonists are mirrors, not obstacles.
- **Found family beats individual heroics.** The FFVIII orphanage, the FFXV road trip, the FFVI
  diaspora — ensembles are remembered as *relationships*. Write the bonds, not just the roster.

## §6 — Engine Integration {#SS-CHAR-§6}

This doctrine is not an essay; it maps onto the data model and the review pipeline.

| Doctrine concept | Where it lives in canon |
|---|---|
| Drive (want + why) | `Characters.psychology → core_desires` |
| Internal obstacle / Sacred Flaw | `core_fears` + `blind_spots`; scored by `NarrativeScienceService` (Storr) |
| The thing only they see | `SpeechSubtext`, `NarrativeFunction` |
| Voice that expresses the Drive | `SpeechCadence`, `SpeechVocabulary`, `PsychologySecret` |
| External obstacle | the strand's antagonist + `CharacterRelationships` |
| Bonds (found family) | `CharacterRelationships`; render them in prose, not just the table |

**Process mandate.** Per the [New Story Workflow](BIBLE.md), no prose is generated before every
named character in the strand has `core_desires` (Drive) and `core_fears`/`blind_spots`
(internal obstacle) populated. A seeded entity with empty psychology is a function waiting to
disappoint a reader. The review panel is the enforcement mechanism: it reliably docks
function-casts and rewards person-casts, so this doctrine is not taste — it is measurable.

## §7 — GLMZ Application: The Heist Crew {#SS-CHAR-§7}

The *Magenta & Gunmetal* / *Neon & Rust* ensemble, written to the doctrine. Each line is the §2
test in miniature; these are the same people across both strands.

- **Rook (Inkeri Saarinen).** *Drive:* be worth following — keep the people who walk through the
  doors she opens alive on her arithmetic. *Obstacle (internal):* a heart she rations so hard it
  can read as cold; *(external):* a corp world that prices people. *Only she sees:* that
  leadership is a debt she pays to a partner, Wennick, who died on her call. (Lightning model:
  depth present, spent sparingly.)
- **Vox (Tem Okafor).** *Drive:* stay the unseen channel; keep her bandwidth-self intact.
  *Obstacle:* the graze that cost her channels; being named aloud. *Only she sees:* the network
  topology no one else can, and her own body as substrate for it.
- **Lace (Blessing Agwu).** *Drive:* be better at being other people than anyone alive; never be
  underestimated. *Obstacle:* not knowing which of her faces is load-bearing. *Only she sees:*
  social space as architecture.
- **Boiler (Mikkeli Väinämöinen).** *Drive:* the beauty of a thing under load, and the moment it
  finally goes. *Obstacle:* the delight isolates him. *Only he sees:* what every structure — and
  every person — is carrying that it was never rated for.
- **Scout (Remi Diallo).** *Drive:* to matter to the work without having to be in the
  room for it; the quiet she keeps for herself. *Obstacle:* the gap between jacked-in omniscience
  and jacked-out silence she has never tried to bridge. *Only she sees:* the world as coverage
  and approach vectors. *(Formerly the cast's blank-slate risk — this Drive is the fix.)*
- **Stave.** *Drive:* a clean way out of the work the debt keeps buying. *Obstacle:* the subject
  who did not come back. *Only they see:* every catastrophic outcome, pre-counted.
- **Soraya / "Ohara" (Halina Soraya).** *Drive:* solve any problem given enough preparation —
  and protect herself from the version of her that built the thing. *Obstacle:* that version.
  *Only she sees:* catastrophe as data.
- **Seun Adalemo.** *Drive:* execute clean terms cleanly. *Obstacle:* the widening gap between
  what he counts and what he is willing to leave uncounted. *Only he sees:* that he is Rook's
  mirror, on the other side of the same job.

The flagship solo, **Kyle**, is the contrast case: where Rook's coolness is *command*, Kyle's is
*wit over a wound* (personhood, mortality, the code). Both eat at Mrs. Chen's; the wary respect
between a crew-runner and a solo is itself a relationship worth a scene.

## §9 — Behavioral Consistency: A Few Rules Predict the Person {#SS-CHAR-§9}

A character is **followable** when a small set of stable rules predicts how they will react.
Give every named character **3–5 load-bearing behavioral rules** — the things that are true of
them in *every* scene. From those rules the reader learns to anticipate the character, and
anticipation is trust; once the reader can predict them, a genuine *surprise* finally means
something.

**The cardinal sin is whiplash.** A character who is timid one beat and reckless the next, who
has a different competence or a different voice whenever the scene needs it, who "wears a
different hat" to suit the plot — that character has no self for the reader to hold. Inconsistency
reads as the author moving a puppet, and it breaks the spell faster than any prose flaw.

**Rules are observable behaviors, not adjectives.** Not "brave" but *moves toward the sound,
every time.* Not "cool" but *counts before she commits and never raises her voice.* Examples
from the heist crew:

- **Rook:** takes the corner seat; counts exits/steps/money before she commits; states
  conclusions, not questions; rations warmth, never spends it in a flood.
- **Boiler:** narrates load-bearing physics mid-action; genders structures female; finds the
  moment of failure beautiful; asks the structural question no one else thinks to ask.
- **Vox:** answers the room a beat late; finishes sentences to agents no one else hears;
  measures injury in lost channels, not pain.
- **Scout:** speaks in a number or a direction; agreement is the cessation of movement; will not
  name the drones.

**System wiring.** These rules are not prose notes — they are data:

| Layer | Home |
|---|---|
| The 3–5 rules (the contract) | `CharacterBehavioralRules` |
| The behavioral model | `CharacterBehavioralMaps`, `CharacterPsychologyTraits` |
| Forecast a reaction from the rules | `predict_behavior` |
| Validate a written beat against the rules | `check_behavior` |

Prose that violates a rule is either an **error to fix** or an **authored arc to track**
(§10) — never an accident left on the page. `check_behavior` is the gate.

---

## §10 — Growth Is a Vector, Not Noise {#SS-CHAR-§10}

Consistency is not stasis. Characters *must* change — that is the arc: Squall thaws, Cloud's
false self collapses, Cyan moves through grief, Lightning learns to trust. But growth is
**directional and earned**, never arbitrary.

The test that separates growth from inconsistency:

- **Growth** proceeds from the character's Drive/Obstacle, is *triggered by a nameable event*,
  and *holds* once achieved. Lightning's hostility softening into trust is growth — caused,
  tracked, permanent.
- **Inconsistency** reverses for plot convenience or has no cause. A character brave in one
  chapter and timid two later, with no event between, is noise.

When a character grows, their behavioral rules **evolve with the arc** — but the evolution is
*logged* (a new rule supersedes an old one at a specific beat), not silently swapped.
`check_behavior` should flag an un-arced reversal as a defect, and pass a tracked, caused
change as an arc.

---

## §8 — The Mandate {#SS-CHAR-§8}

1. Run the §2 test on every named character before prose. Write the four answers into the
   character's psychology fields.
2. Treat the antagonist as a protagonist (§5).
3. Render bonds, not just rosters (§5).
4. Cool characters keep their depth on the page, rationed (§4).
5. Familiar archetypes are welcome; empty ones are not (§3).
6. When a strand under-scores on the panel and the notes say "functional," "flat," or "at arm's
   length," the fix is **this doctrine**, not more polish.
