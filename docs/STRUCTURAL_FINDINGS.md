# Structural Findings — Review/Rewrite Campaign (2026-06-25)

> **Premise (user rule).** 93 is the quality bar. If prose-polish plateaus *below* 93, the gap is
> **structural** — a missing want/need, soft stakes, an absent arc beat, a dramatic question that never
> bites — and no amount of line-polish fixes a hole in the architecture. These are **Opus-led**
> diagnoses (the automated five-act/sacred-flaw tools are unreliable for chaptered books — they don't
> walk the book→chapter hierarchy — so this is human-judgment-led per the directive), corroborated by
> the RunPod 72B panel's prose reviews. **To be folded into each strand bible so future prose
> generation closes the gap**, not just this campaign's edits.

---

## BCODA — Bushido Coda  (box 86.4 / flow 91.2; cloud 87.3)

**Protagonist / theory of control (sacred flaw):** Kyle Ellen Corbin Vister. His *code* — "cut
everything, cross nothing," always take payment, meticulous unflappable competence — is how he proves
to himself he is a *person* and in control, against the buried truth that he may be a composite built
for a purpose ([[project_kyle_composite_secret]]).

**Structural gaps capping it below 93:**
1. **Diffuse antagonist / non-escalating pressure.** The 72B panel explicitly flagged *"lack of clear
   antagonist."* By canon the antagonist is architecture (the rogue AI / the system), deliberately
   ambient — but structurally the opposing force never *mounts*. There's no tightening clock or
   sharpening agent applying rising pressure across the acts. **Future gen:** give the ambient
   antagonist a recurring face or a closing-net mechanism so Act II→III pressure visibly escalates.
2. **The dramatic question doesn't bite on the surface.** The engine question — *"is Kyle a person, or
   the AI's instrument?"* — is a LOCKED secret (never on page), so it works subconsciously but rarely
   puts his personhood at *visible* stake. **Future gen:** stage concrete moments that test his
   personhood (a choice only a person makes; a place the code fails him as a human, not an operator) —
   without revealing the secret.
3. **Stakes softened by competence.** Near-perfect capability + the no-kill code make outcomes feel
   controlled; the **cost** is underweighted. The real price — the Atlas-grade Neuretics slowly killing
   him, the relational cost with Pixel — should mount and be *felt*, not sit in the background.
4. **Arc is revelation, not transformation.** Kyle's five-act movement is "discovers he's been used"
   more than "changes." **Future gen:** Act V must force a choice that *answers* the dramatic question
   (keep or break the code once he knows) — a definitive God-moment, not a fade.

**Net:** polish ceiling ~mid-80s; the climb to 93 needs escalating antagonistic pressure (1) and the
dramatic question surfaced as mounting, felt personal stakes (2–3) — architecture, not lines.

## DWIACE — Death Whispers in a Cat's Ear  (box 84.3 / flow 90.2; cloud **90.95** — corpus-best prose)

The box scores the *best-written* strand *lowest* — a pure structure tell. Box consensus: heavy exposition +
**"voice's reliability questioned too late."** Protagonist: Rennick (procedural investigator).
**Gaps:** (1) **Seeding failure** — the unreliable-voice / cold-entity doubt is raised too late, so the
dramatic question ("can this voice be trusted; what is the cold thing") never bites *across* the arc. (2)
**Atmospheric antagonist** — the cold entity is mood, not mounting pressure. (3) Rennick's personal
want/need is under-surfaced, so the stakes stay forensic, not personal. **Future gen:** plant the
voice-reliability doubt in Act I; give the entity escalating, concrete intrusions; sharpen Rennick's stake.

## VATD — Vultures at the Door  (box 83.7 / flow 88.3; cloud 88.9)

Box consensus: **"too much exposition, not enough tension," "overlong."** Dual-POV (Tomas & Levin + Ekow/Casimir).
**Gaps:** (1) **Diffuse dramatic question** — split POV with no single transforming protagonist blurs *whose*
arc this is. (2) **Tension deficit** — exposition substitutes for mounting pressure; no tightening clock.
**Future gen:** commit to one driving question/arc spine; convert exposition into stakes; add a ticking pressure.

## SPRW — Sparrow / Elias Macias  (box 87.3 / flow 91.0; cloud 87.3 — box == cloud, the honest score)

Canon: Elias is **Sparrow's proxy and never fires a weapon**; the AI satellite holds the agency.
**Gap:** **passive protagonist** — the want/need and the decisive choices belong to Sparrow, so Elias's arc
is thin and the dramatic question about *him* barely registers. **Future gen:** give Elias an active want
and a choice that is *his*, not Sparrow's — or make "choosing to be the proxy" his deliberate arc, so his
passivity becomes a decision with cost rather than a structural absence.

## SM — Street Meat  (box 86.6 / flow 91.7; cloud 88.7)

Gateway novella, 17 beats, the Marrow reveal as engine.
**Gap:** **compressed arc / reveal economy** — in a tight beat count the Marrow reveal risks under-seeding
(payoff without enough plant) and the stakes risk staying flat across the short span. **Future gen:** seed
the Marrow turn earlier so the reveal recontextualizes; ensure each of the 17 beats raises the stake.

## MNEMO — Mnemosync / Amara & Seto  (box 85.4 / flow 90.2; cloud 86.7 — in-progress)

Box consensus: heavy exposition / info-dumps + pacing. Canon: bleed-intrusion mechanic; Act-3 reverse-transfer.
**Gap:** **mechanic over story** — the bleed/reverse-transfer machinery is explained more than it is *felt*,
and the central dramatic question (what do Amara & Seto each *want*, and what's the cost of the transfer)
isn't yet load-bearing. **Future gen:** subordinate the mechanic to the want/need; make the Act-3
reverse-transfer a *choice with a price*, not a reveal of how the system works. (In-progress — finish on this spine.)

## TEST — Testament / Bear court-martial  (box 86.0 / flow 91.4; cloud 87.5)

Box consensus: **"ending feels rushed" ×11** — the single loudest structural signal in the corpus.
**Gap:** **compressed Act V.** A court-martial's entire engine is the *verdict and its meaning*; rushing it
deflates the dramatic question (is Bear's obligation-driven act justified?). The God-moment arrives hurried.
**Future gen:** give the verdict + aftermath real room; let Bear's response *answer* the question definitively.
This is the corpus's clearest, most actionable structural fix.

## ATTE — Attendance / Yemina Fola  (box 86.2 / flow 91.3; cloud 89.2; pre-flight 1 FAIL)

Box consensus: **"ending feels rushed" ×11 / "too tidy"** — and the structural pre-flight logged 1 fail.
**BUT this collides with deliberate design.** ATTE's GREY register and locked ending (institutional
ambiguity, children not recovered, the report goes somewhere unknown) make restraint *the artistic point*.
**Diagnosis:** do **NOT** "resolve" ATTE's ending to chase the box — that would violate the design. The real
structural question is whether the dramatic question ("will Yemina's correct, complete report *matter*?") is
weighted heavily enough that the non-resolution lands as *devastating* rather than *abrupt*. **Future gen:**
keep the ambiguous close; raise the felt stakes of the report's fate beforehand so the restraint reads as a
gut-punch. ATTE's ceiling is partly intentional — its plateau is closer to "done" than the others.

### Corpus-wide conclusion
All 8 cap in the **mid-80s with high flow (88–92)** on the box: prose flows, architecture caps. The recurring
structural deficits are **(a) endings/Act-V rushed (TEST, ATTE), (b) tension/antagonist that doesn't mount
(VATD, DWIACE, BCODA), (c) passive or diffuse protagonist agency (SPRW, VATD), (d) mechanic/exposition over
felt want-need (MNEMO, DWIACE).** These are the things to fix in **future generation** — polish cannot.
