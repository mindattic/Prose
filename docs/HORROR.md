# HORROR — Universe Craft Rules {#SS-HORROR}

> **Scope: HORROR universe stories only.** Universal prose rules live in **docs/CRAFT.md** (Base
> layer) — clarity standard, scene architecture, sentence/voice rules all apply here unchanged.
> This file adds what GLMZ/SCRY/SOURCE don't need: the specific discipline of **sustained,
> unresolved dread** — how to escalate wrongness without collapsing it into either a monster
> manual or a tidy answer.

---

## 0. What HORROR is {#SS-HORROR-0}

**HORROR** is StreetSamurai's contained-horror fiction universe. Unlike GLMZ (one continuous
cyberpunk metropolis) and SCRY (one continuous fantasy world), HORROR is **anthology-shaped**:
each book stands in its own real-world-adjacent setting, with no requirement of shared
geography, timeline, or antagonist across books. What every HORROR book shares is not a world —
it's a method: one clean transgression against a rule of reality, escalated through specific,
technically credible detail, never fully explained.

The flagship exemplar is **QRT** — an amateur-radio operator being reconstructed, pattern by
pattern, by something that was never listening to the signal at all.

---

## 1. Ambiguity Is Structural, Not Decorative {#SS-HORROR-1}

The antagonist force in a HORROR book is never given a taxonomy, an origin, a name, or a stated
goal. The story may let the protagonist construct a hypothesis — even a rigorous, technically
grounded one — but the narrative itself never confirms it. A hypothesis that turns out to be
half-right (true enough to explain some events, wrong enough to leave the protagonist exposed
when it fails) is more effective than either a fully-explained mechanism or a shrug.

**Hard rule:** if a beat's prose states the antagonist's nature as narrative fact rather than
protagonist inference, that beat has failed HORROR's core discipline — flag and fix it, don't
publish it. Confirmation is death for this universe the way an internal-understanding ending is
death for GLMZ.

---

## 2. Escalation Through Small Wrongnesses {#SS-HORROR-2}

Before any overt reveal, the prose must accumulate specific, checkable wrongnesses — details a
careful reader (and the protagonist) could verify and that are almost, but not quite, right. A
detail that's *completely* alien reads as fantasy; a detail that's *subtly* wrong (a callsign
correct except for one digit, a signal report the protagonist would give but didn't) is what
makes it dread rather than spectacle. Escalation curve: verifiable-detail wrongness → pattern
recognition → the protagonist's own controlled experiment → the experiment's result exceeding
what the protagonist's hypothesis predicted.

---

## 3. The Observer-Effect Discipline {#SS-HORROR-3}

**Locked mechanic available to any HORROR book that wants it (originated in QRT):** an uncanny
phenomenon that cannot occur while the protagonist is actively, attentively observing the exact
channel they *think* is compromised. This isn't invoked as hard science — the protagonist may
reach for the language of quantum observation because it's the framework their own technical
literacy hands them — but the story never adjudicates whether that framework is literally true.
Its narrative function is to manufacture false safety: the protagonist tests the wrong layer,
confirms the phenomenon can be suppressed by watching *there*, and stakes their safety on a
hypothesis that only holds for the channel they happened to test. The turn is discovering the
leak was never in the observed channel at all.

---

## 4. Endings {#SS-HORROR-4}

HORROR does not default to GLMZ's avalanche-no-epilogue or SCRY's permanent-death convention.
The default HORROR ending is the **cold/ambiguous close**: the immediate threat drops off-page,
time passes without incident, and then one final concrete detail confirms the thing never
stopped — it simply stopped needing the protagonist's participation. No epilogue processes the
horror into meaning; the last image is procedural and understated, not operatic.

---

## 5. Technical Authenticity Is the Credibility Engine {#SS-HORROR-5}

Ambiguity only works if everything adjacent to it is exactly right. When a HORROR book is built
around a specific subculture or technical practice (amateur radio, analog recording, a trade),
get the jargon, procedure, and equipment correct to the letter — Q-codes, PTT discipline, key
clicks, harmonic signatures, logbook conventions. A reader who knows the domain and catches a
factual error stops trusting the ambiguity everywhere else in the book.

---

## 6. Violence and the Body {#SS-HORROR-6}

Where HORROR includes physical harm, `docs/CRAFT.md`'s body-true standard applies unchanged —
physical consequence, not spectacle. HORROR's inflection is that dread from *wrongness* usually
carries more of the book's weight than dread from violence; a HORROR book is not obligated to
include on-page violence at all.
